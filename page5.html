#210. Harga Emas Dunia dan Antam Terkoreksi, Begini Proyeksi hingga Akhir Tahun

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Reporter: Vivit Dewi | Editor: Avanty Nurdiana

KONTAN.CO.ID – JAKARTA. Harga emas dunia melemah pada perdagangan Kamis (29/10/2026). Tekanan juga terlihat pada harga emas batangan Antam di dalam negeri, di tengah ekspektasi kenaikan suku bunga Amerika Serikat (AS) dan kekhawatiran inflasi.

Melansir Bloomberg, Kamis (29/10/2026) pukul 21.52 WIB, harga emas dunia berada di US$ 4.032 per ons troi, turun 0,86% dibandingkan perdagangan sebelumnya.

Pelemahan harga emas terjadi setelah Federal Reserve (The Fed) mempertahankan suku bunga acuannya. Namun, dua pejabat The Fed berbeda pendapat dan mendukung kenaikan suku bunga sebesar 25 basis poin. Perbedaan pandangan ini turut memperkuat ekspektasi pasar terhadap peluang kenaikan suku bunga pada Desember mendatang.

Tingginya harga energi juga menjadi perhatian pasar. Pasalnya, kenaikan harga minyak berisiko mendorong inflasi dan membuat suku bunga bertahan tinggi lebih lama. Kondisi ini menekan daya tarik emas karena logam mulia tersebut tidak memberikan imbal hasil bunga.

Sejalan dengan itu, analis mata uang & komoditas Ibrahim Assuaibi mengatakan, konflik di sejumlah kawasan berpotensi berlanjut hingga tahun depan.

“Perang di Timur Tengah, di Eropa Timur antara Rusia dan Ukraina, kemudian antara Amerika dan Iran, kemungkinan besar akan berlanjut sampai tahun 2027 dan memengaruhi harga emas,” ujar Ibrahim dalam acara open house Traze Andalan Futures, Kamis (29/10/2026).

Sementara itu, harga emas batangan Antam juga turun. Mengutip situs Logam Mulia, harga emas Antam pecahan satu gram dibanderol Rp 2.559.000 pada Kamis (29/10/2026). Harga ini turun Rp 11.000 dibandingkan perdagangan Rabu (28/10/2026) yang berada di level Rp 2.570.000 per gram.

Meski harga emas tengah terkoreksi, Ibrahim masih melihat peluang penguatan hingga akhir tahun. Ia memproyeksikan harga emas dunia bisa mencapai US$ 4.300–US$ 4.400 per ons troi pada akhir 2026. Adapun harga emas batangan di dalam negeri diperkirakan menyentuh Rp 2.700.000 hingga Rp 2.800.000 juta per gram.

#52. Smartbird's CEO talks about converting a shoe company into an AI player

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Before Smartbird was powering large language models, it was making shoes.

The CEO of the AI infrastructure company, Nadia Carlsten, spoke about the transition with chief correspondent Ben Bergman on Thursday at Business Insider's The Long Play.

Its roots are the sustainable sneaker company Allbirds, which skyrocketed in the late 2010s. The shoes were particularly popular among the Silicon Valley crowd. Allbirds went public in 2021 before the market turned on many direct-to-consumer businesses. The stock quickly fell, and it sold to American Exchange Group in March for $39 million.

"The short answer is the shoe business wasn't doing that well," Carlsten said.

The company then sold off its footwear business and shed most of its workforce, rebranding as Smartbird. The AI pivot skyrocketed the stock and was briefly up 800%, though it has since come back down. Carlsten, who became CEO in June, said she had never worn those wool shoes.

"The goal of the company was to do something valuable and meaningful. And for me, I knew I wanted to do AI infrastructure," she said.

After looking at the emerging AI infrastructure sector, she believed with the company's transformation, it was the "right place at the right time" for her to join.

In the five months since she took over, Carlsten said the experience can be described in one word: exciting.

"Never in my life thought that I would be the CEO of a former shoe company and managing what is essentially a startup." Since taking on the role, she has focused on building out a new team, scouring new technologies for the company to look into, and producing a new pipeline of customers.

While the stock price has gone down since the initial pivot-induced spike, Carlsten does not regret taking on the challenge. She is betting on the company's long-term potential.

"The stock market is going to do what the stock market does," she said. "What I care about is making sure that we're putting the company on the right path, and that we're able to build it quickly enough and well enough that we have a sustainable business that investors will love. So what happens in the short term? Not that important. What happens in the long term is very important."

When asked why the company decided to reinvent itself in the public eye instead of beginning as a private start-up, Carlsten countered with a question: "Why not?" she said, "It will come back up."

Having to reinvent the company from scratch can be challenging, and the public's presence is clear, she said. Yet, Carlsten said reinvention is inevitable for companies.

"This is a very public reinvention of the company, and people are watching," she said. "I think every company needs to reinvent itself every now and then."

When asked why the company is fully remote, Carlsten said most of the work at Smartbird is done virtually or at data centers, meaning an office would not be of much use.

"We spend a lot of time actually building things, which I actually love. So if my people are sitting in an office, something's wrong," she said.

Carlsten said that although her competitors are building similar AI infrastructure, the way Smartbird is going about it is unique.

"For us, it's about the right infrastructure for the right company at the right time," she said. "Our business model allows us to work more closely with customers, but it also is a lot less capital intensive."

Smartbird is looking to work with companies that have yet to explore implementing AI in their systems, she said. As more companies outside the tech realm look to adopt AI into their businesses, they have the choice to build infrastructure themselves or outsource it, and Smartbird wants to be the one to do it for them, she said.

"We'll do the infrastructure part. We'll let you focus on the actual business," she said.

The most common misconception Carlsten said she has encountered in the business is that it's all about GPUs. She said the company does a lot more than just run GPUs.

"It's really about having a holistic system that a customer can use to do AI," she said.

Another rumbling that's taken over the tech company discourse is whether there's an AI bubble and if it will pop. Carlsten said that depends on how people go about defining the bubble.

Excitement in the sector means funding might be going into companies that may not prosper in the future, but this is something that happens with the arrival of new technologies, she said.

"Every time there is a transformational technology, this happens," she said. "Even if we assume that there is a bubble, that doesn't mean that the technology is not good. It also doesn't mean that the adoption of the technology is going to go away."

#62. Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick's management style: No chief of staff, no 'toxic' discretionary pay programs

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Strauss Zelnick is relentlessly focused on making "the best entertainment on earth." There's no confusion about that mission among his employees, he said.

"I have 13,000 colleagues," the Take-Two Interactive CEO said at Business Insider's The Long Play event in New York City on Thursday. "100% of them can and will tell you exactly what I just said."

His management philosophy — "establish a mission, establish a strategy, and promulgate collective culture" — is about to get its biggest test in years.

Take-Two is the publisher behind popular games like "Red Dead Redemption" and "NBA 2K," and, on November 19, they'll launch a new "Grand Theft Auto." The wildly popular series hasn't had a new mainline installment since 2013 and has delayed its launch twice in an effort to meet the studio's exacting standards. GTA 6 is central to Take-Two's forecast for at least $8 billion in net bookings for fiscal 2027.

Zelnick has run the company since 2007. To align employee incentives with Take-Two's big expectations, he believes compensation should be based on a formula aligned with the company's mission — not left to manager discretion.

"I personally believe that discretionary compensation programs are toxic," he said, adding that they create "politics and get you the wrong results, especially over time."

"So, we're all formulaic," he said. "You perform, the company performs — you get paid. You perform, the company doesn't perform — you don't get paid. Company performs, you don't perform — if you still have your job, you get paid."

Zelnick also said he has no chief of staff, is available on Slack 24/7, and shares his cellphone number with employees.

"If you're not president of the United States, you have no reason to have a chief of staff," he said.

#1. Binance 'potential security issue' sees $2.54 billion frozen by the likes of Circle, Tether

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Several crypto firms, including Circle Internet Group and Tether, on Thursday froze $2.54 billion of funds tied to a potential hack on cryptocurrency exchange Binance.

The world's largest crypto exchange by daily trading volumes didn't explicitly confirm that it has suffered an exploit.

"Our teams are aware of reports of a potential security issue and are actively investigating," a Binance spokesperson said. "Rest assured that user funds are completely safe." Binance also said on X that Binance Alpha trading had been temporarily suspended, and that "the current verified total frozen is $2.54 billion."

Binance founder and former CEO Changpeng Zhao said in a post on X that user funds were safe and that he expected all customer funds to be fully covered. Zhao, who no longer runs the exchange, also said the incident appeared to be a hack.

Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire said Thursday evening in a social media post that the company had frozen more than 800 million in USD Coin associated with the incident. USDT stablecoin issuer Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino also said in on X that his firm had frozen more than 1.5 billion of its stablecoin that may have been related to a potential breach.

Later, Binance co-founder Yi He said in a statement that the XRP and RLUSD-tied firm Ripple helped halt the movement of $200 million of funds linked to the possible attack.

But those frozen funds could represent only a fraction of the ones impacted. Crypto data provider Arkham Intelligence linked nearly $6 billion in digital assets — including bitcoin, ether, solana and various stablecoins — to 55 wallet addresses it has linked to the potential exploit.

Arkham identifies, tracks and flags crypto wallets by analyzing publicly available blockchain data. It also solicits tips from its community members.

Exploits are not uncommon in the crypto space. In 2025, Bybit suffered roughly $1.5 billion in losses due to a hack. More recently, Bitget lost more than $350 million worth of digital assets in a cyber theft incident. Over the past several years, bad actors have also drained billions of dollars in crypto from decentralized exchanges.

Bitcoin — the most widely followed cryptocurrency in the world — slid more than 3% following the executives' statements, breaking below $80,000. Ether fell nearly 4%, while solana shed about 3%.

Shares of Circle also tumbled about 5% in after-hours trading on Thursday.

#5. I’ve Sat on 23 Boards. Here’s What Founders Get Wrong When They Hire a CEO and Step Back

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

You're viewing the US website. Looking for the Site edition?

A six-time CEO on making the move from founder to chairman work.

Opinions expressed by Entrepreneur contributors are their own.

As a six-time CEO who has served on 23 corporate boards, I’ve watched the same story unfold in nearly every sector. An ambitious founder builds a business, reaches the point where they want to step back from daily operations, hires a CEO and moves into the chairman role, assuming the hardest work is behind them. Six months later, the relationship starts to fracture.

The founder feels the new leader is moving too slowly or making different choices, while the CEO feels micromanaged and stripped of authority. Once that trust breaks, the transition meant to unlock the company’s next chapter becomes a source of friction and lost momentum.

Having navigated this handoff from both sides of the table, I call this dynamic “the chairman’s gap”: the gray zone between providing governance and interfering. Staying on the right side of that line doesn’t happen by accident. It requires unlearning the operational habits that made you successful in the first place.

When founders ask me how to make the move from founder to chairman, they usually expect a tactical playbook on board meetings or governance structures. The mechanics matter, but I’ve found the real challenge is psychological.

As an operating CEO, your daily survival depends on answering “how.” You inspect details, solve bottlenecks and drive execution. As chairman, you have to give up ownership of the “how” and focus on the “what” and “why”: setting strategic direction, securing capital and holding your CEO accountable for results.

I put this into practice when I co-founded a luxury vehicle business. I knew from day one that I wouldn’t run daily operations, so I brought in an industry expert I had mentored for five years as president and CEO. Even with decades of executive experience, I still had to train myself to stay in my lane.

If you’re wondering whether to hire a CEO, the real question is whether you’re prepared to let someone else run the business differently than you would. If our CEO evaluates an opportunity differently than I would, my job isn’t to override that decision. It’s to support the culture of accountability we built together. Different isn’t wrong; it’s just different. When a chairman accepts that there are multiple routes to a goal, the partnership thrives.

Adopting that mindset is essential, but making it work day to day requires clear structure. Most executive friction comes down to a simple oversight: Neither leader defined where their authority starts and stops on day one. Working well with the CEO you hired means setting explicit rules of engagement long before you hand over control.

Notice what’s missing: managing inventory, dictating daily workflows or going around the CEO to direct staff. With our CEO, I stay informed through a simple routine: a formal monthly financial review, dual authorization on major transactions and quick high-level updates. That gives me clarity without putting my hands back on the wheel.

Knowing the difference between constructive oversight and interference is a critical skill for any chairman. From the outside, asking questions in a meeting or reviewing performance metrics can look the same either way. From the inside, they reflect opposite approaches to leadership.

Oversight focuses on outcomes and alignment: Is the team hitting its targets, protecting its values and managing risk? Interference focuses on personal preference: Why was that email phrased that way? Why wasn’t the meeting run the way I used to run it?

When chairmen interfere, it usually comes from an emotional attachment to old routines. Early in my board career, whenever I spotted an inefficient process, my instinct was to jump in and fix it.

I quickly realized that doing so undermines the CEO’s confidence and destroys accountability. When you spot an issue as chairman, your job is to raise it with the CEO in an advisory conversation and leave the execution entirely in their hands.

Mastering this discipline is what unlocks the real value of the chairman role. Giving your CEO room to execute frees you to focus on strategy and scale your impact without burning out on 80-hour workweeks.

But that success depends on one non-negotiable boundary. Before you sign a contract, post the job or even think about handing over control, ask yourself: Am I truly ready to let go, and will I trust the new CEO?

If the answer is yes, hand over the keys completely. Trust the leader you chose, hold them accountable for outcomes and give them room to drive. But if you hesitate, even for a moment, don’t hire a CEO. The biggest threat to a company’s next chapter isn’t a new executive making an operational mistake. It’s a chairman who refuses to get out of the driver’s seat.

Pengakuan tersebut menegaskan bahwa fasilitas FL Technics Indonesia di Bali telah memenuhi standar keselamatan dan kualitas perawatan pesawat yang ditetapkan regulator penerbangan Uni Eropa. Sertifikasi ini juga memperkuat posisi perusahaan dalam persaingan industri MRO internasional.

Chairman FL Technics Indonesia Martynas Grigas mengatakan, perusahaan akan segera memulai pekerjaan perawatan untuk operator penerbangan asal Uni Eropa. Kedatangan armada berikutnya juga telah dijadwalkan pada November 2026.

#232. Bakal Cabut IUP Mangkrak, Pemerintah Diingatkan Tak Semua Sengaja Ditelantarkan

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Penulis: Chelsea Anastasia, Chelsea Anastasia | Editor: Avanty Nurdiana

KONTAN.CO.ID - JAKARTA. Kebijakan pemerintah untuk mencabut izin usaha pertambangan (IUP) yang lama mangkrak perlu mempertimbangkan alasan minimnya aktivitas nyata suatu usaha pertambangan. Sebelumnya, Presiden Prabowo Subianto menegaskan bakal mencabut IUP usaha yang tidak menunjukkan aktivitas selama lebih dari 2 tahun. Menurutnya, masih banyak konsesi yang telah diberikan selama bertahun-tahun tetapi tak kunjung dikerjakan. Direktur Eksekutif Pusat Studi Hukum Energi dan Pertambangan (Pushep) Bisman Bakhtiar menilai, pemerintah memang mempunyai kewenangan untuk mencabut IUP, tetapo kewenangan ini harus dilakukan dengan selektif dan hati-hati.

Baca Juga: Laba Bumi Resources Minerals (BRMS) Anjlok 36,86%, Produksi Emas Diharap Naik di 2027 Menurutnya, kebijakan ini tentu berpeluang menertibkan konsesi yang ditelantarkan atau hanya dijadikan objek spekulasi, sehingga dapat dialihkan kepada pelaku usaha yang lebih siap dan produktif. "Namun tantangannya, pemerintah harus membedakan penelantaran yang disengaja dengan kendala investasi agar tidak menimbulkan ketidakpastian hukum," jelasnya kepada Kontan, Kamis (29/10/2026). Pasalnya, Bisman menyebut terdapat beberapa alasan yang dapat menjadi penyebab mangkraknya sebuah usaha pertambangan yang sudah mendapatkan izin, mulai dari teknis hingga non teknis. Di antaranya, persoalan keterbatasan modal, harga komoditas yang belum ekonomis, proses eksplorasi yang panjang, persoalan lahan, perizinan lingkungan, hingga infrastruktur. "Jadi nggak ada aktivitas itu belum tentu berarti perusahaan sengaja menelantarkan konsesinya," terang Bisman. Karena itu, ia mencermati, pemerintah perlu melakukan evaluasi secara objektif dengan berbasis data, mempertimbangkan realisasi investasi dan kendala perusahaan, serta memberikan kesempatan klarifikasi kepada usaha tersebut. "Jadi waktu dua tahun itu bisa menjadi ukuran evaluasi, jadi jangan sampai langsung cabut," ungkap Bisman.

Baca Juga: Apjatel Minta Ada Dialog soal Kabel Udara, Soroti Kepastian Usaha Operator Ia juga mengingatkan proses pencabutan perlu sesuai regulasi, yakni harus sesuai prosedur dan tahapan. Mulai dari permintaan klarifikasi, peringatan, kemudian pencabutan. "Itu pun harus dilakukan dengan transparan dan dasar hukum yang jelas. Sebab, kalau asal cabut, justru tidak baik bagi iklim usaha, kepastian hukum, dan kenyamanan investasi," tegas Bisman.

#83. The CEO behind Grand Theft Auto has an 8-part longevity routine for shredded abs and sharp decision-making. Here's why peptides aren't part of it.

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Media mogul Strauss Zelnick spends his days hyper-focused on making the most creative, innovative, best video games in the world.

As the CEO and Chairman of video game-maker Take-Two Interactive, he's readying to release Grand Theft Auto 6 next month.

At home though, he doesn't play games. He prefers to spend his time working out and staying healthy for a long time.

At Business Insider's "The Long Play" event on Thursday, Zelnick said his formula for healthy aging is both simple and effective.

While he calls biohacker Bryan Johnson, the man behind the "Don't Die" movement, a friend, he said trying to live forever is a pretty silly strategy.

"There's almost no evidence that anything anyone is doing will increase your lifespan," he told Business Insider's Senior Leadership & Workplace Correspondent, Sarah Needleman. "How many of us really want to increase our lifespan from like 96 to 101? Look, that is not on my bucket list, thank you very much."
