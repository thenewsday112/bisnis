#3511. Six concept vehicles from Stellantis exploring the future of car design

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

A Fiat that converts from a family car into a pickup and a Peugeot with a glass-bubble roof were among six concept cars unveiled by Stellantis at the 2026 Paris Motor Show, as captured in this video carousel by Dezeen.

Automotive corporation Stellantis unveiled the six concepts at a preview event in Meudon, outside Paris, on the eve of the motor show, offering an early look at the designs before the exhibition opened to the public.

The concepts explored different approaches to future car design – from reworking historic models to experimenting with colour-changing paint and illuminated bodywork.

Among the cars presented was Fiat’s Multiplay, which combines the proportions of a family vehicle with the functionality of a pickup.

Measuring 4.45 metres long, the concept has a sliding rear section that converts its six-seat-plus-one configuration into a four-seat vehicle with an open cargo bed.

Inside, a front passenger seat called the Sunseat folds into a lounge-style position beneath the panoramic roof, while a modular Petsenger seat can accommodate a pet, a child seat or storage.

The concept was finished in bright green, with a pink floor and red and pink upholstery inspired by the colours of a watermelon.

Citroën revisited its 1948 2CV with a compact electric concept featuring an orange-red body, grey steel wheels and yellow accents.

Inside, yellow tartan upholstery echoes details of the original model, while a fabric sunroof and removable storage basket replaced some of the conventional fittings found in modern cars.

The concept also features a detachable radio called Radioën, which doubles as a portable speaker, as well as door compartments designed to hold eggs, echoing the practical requirements of the original car.

Peugeot presented its RCZ Vision concept, a low-slung coupe with a sculpted body and a glass roof with a distinctive double-bubble profile that extends from the bonnet towards the rear.

Slim horizontal headlights reinterpret the brand’s three-claw motif, while a rear spoiler deploys only when the car is moving, keeping the bodywork smooth when parked.

Inside, a square steering control called Hypersquare replaces a conventional steering wheel and transparent micro-LED displays provide driving information while reducing the visual prominence of screens.

DS Automobiles’ F3VER concept features a compact body finished in Cosmic Blue paint that changes from vivid blue to violet depending on the light.

Its two-tone finish wraps around the rear of the car, while vertical Y-shaped headlights frame the front emblem.

At the rear, 16 LED panels sit within a sculptural light assembly informed by the vaulted roof of Paris’s Grand Palais.

Opel’s STX Concept is an electric estate car with a low roofline, sharply creased bonnet and sloping rear. Its front grille, known as the Vizor, incorporates vertical animated lights around an illuminated Opel emblem.

The rear bumper features extendable aerodynamic elements, while glass light strips run from the rear side windows into the roof.

Cameras replace conventional wing mirrors and three-section alloy wheels are designed to improve aerodynamic efficiency.

Alfa Romeo’s Cuorerosso concept car explores the brand’s future sports-car design through a streamlined body, active air intakes and slim aerodynamic mirrors.

Its rear window echoes the shape of the brand’s triangular front grille, while the interior is organised around the driver, with controls concealed in the central console until needed.

Stellantis was formed in 2021 through the merger of Fiat Chrysler Automobiles and France’s PSA Group.

Its portfolio includes Alfa Romeo, Citroën, DS Automobiles, Fiat, Opel and Peugeot, among other automotive brands.

#439. The Sky Today on Wednesday, October 28: The Moon hangs with Uranus

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Looking for a sky event this week? Check out our full Sky This Week column.

The Moon passes 5° north of Uranus at 6 A.M. EDT this morning. You’ll find our satellite hanging above the Pleiades (M45) in Taurus, roughly 45° high in the west around 6 A.M. local daylight time. Together with Uranus, the Moon and Pleiades form a triangle in the sky that slowly sets as twilight approaches.

If you want to view Uranus, you’ll need some optical aid — technically its magnitude of 5.6 puts it within naked-eye range, but the bright Moon nearby will wash it out. You can find the ice giant 5° to the lower left of the Moon as they sink, or about halfway along a line drawn from the Pleiades toward the upper left and ending at the bright star Aldebaran.

Magnitude 0.9 Aldebaran (Alpha [α] Tauri) is typically envisioned as the eye of Taurus the Bull. The red giant is the 14th brightest star in the sky, shining with a luminosity more than 400 times that of the Sun from a distance of roughly 67 light-years. Despite its brightness, its surface temperature is quite cool, which results in the reddish color. Compare its orangey-red hue to that of Mars in the east.

#7. Luxury cruise passengers stranded in Cambodia due to soaring fuel costs

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Villa Vie Odyssey was more than 750 days into its voyage around the world when it came to a stop in Cambodia

A luxury cruise ship on a years-long voyage around the world said it suspended operations due to extraordinarily high fuel costs, leaving passengers stranded in Cambodia.

The US-based operator of Villa Vie Odyssey said it was working with passengers on disembarkation plans and "temporary living arrangements".

It is unclear how many were on board but the nearly 200m (656ft) vessel can carry up to 650 passengers. It departed Belfast in October 2024 for a "continuous voyage around the world".

Global fuel prices have soared since the Iran war started earlier this year. The ship's operator said the monthly fuel cost per cabin in parts of Asia has risen to as much as $8,000 (Â£6,000) from $1,800.

The company said it had taken "significant steps to reduce fuel consumption, including slowing the itinerary and spending more time in port". But those measures turned out to be insufficient to "offset the extraordinary increase in fuel costs".

"Passing the full increase on to residents is not a realistic solution," it said.

The company would spend the next 60 days on "restructuring and recapitalisation", though "no outcome is assured", it said.

There were reportedly around 200 passengers on board when they were ordered to disembark in the Cambodian port city of Sihanoukville, a once sleepy beach town that has gained global notoriety in recent years as a hub for scam centres.

The Villa Vie Odyssey's voyage was expected to span 3.5 years and cover nearly 150 countries, according to its website.

Its departure had faced delays as the ship underwent repairs, leaving passengers stranded in Belfast for months and costing the company millions of pounds.

The ship was more than 750 days into its circumnavigation before announcing its suspension of its journey on Tuesday - which means its passengers would now be stranded in Cambodia.

Cruise passenger Holly Hennessey and her cat, Captain, have now found themselves in Cambodia

"It was not on my bingo card of places where I'd be spending a lot of time," one passenger, Holly Hennessey, told the BBC from Cambodia.

"We're all in different hotels; there are a number of shipmates in the hotel that I'm in. We're just waiting."

The Villa Vie Odyssey is equipped with several bars and restaurants, a pool deck and a pickleball court, according to its website.

Permanent residencies in its cabins range from $120,000 to $440,000. These cabins are also also available for rent from $3,500 a month.

Last year, the company launched a lifetime residence programme for people to spend their retirement years at sea.

In 2024, days after US President Donald Trump won his second term, Villa Vie Residences announced a four-year "Skip Forward" package. Its CEO told Fox Business at the time that it was the "perfect product for those who said they'd leave the country if 'XYZ' wins the election".

Hennessey said the ship's residents had earlier been asked to consider paying a fuel surcharge as global prices soared, and that their itinerary had been "severely curtailed". Some people decided to disembark and travel on land, she said.

But some, like Hennessey, have decided to stick around in Cambodia in the hopes that the Villa Vie Odyssey will set sail again soon.

"Until they say we are done - done done - I am not giving up. I could never duplicate this lifestyle. The idea of going home? It would be so trite," she said.

"Cambodia's not the worst place to hang out."
