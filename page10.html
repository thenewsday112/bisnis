#151. More companies like Microsoft are using buyouts. Should you take one?

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Microsoft is trying to cull its workforce by dangling an offer that might be hard for some employees to refuse. This week, the tech giant announced voluntary buyouts for 60,000 people, which is more than a quarter of its workforce. 

Leadership has framed this as a way for employees to have more agency over their future at the company. “The Voluntary Exit Offer reflects an important part of how we’re evolving our approach to change: creating greater flexibility and, where possible, more ways for employees to shape their next chapter,” chief people officer Amy Coleman wrote in a blog post. For employees who were already entertaining the idea of an outside opportunity, she said this could present “a new choice to make that transition now with support.”

It’s not the first time Microsoft has offered buyouts to trim its head count. Earlier this year, the company used another euphemism to nudge thousands of employees to leave. The “Voluntary Retirement Program” was intended for older, more senior employees who may have been planning to retire soon; according to The Wall Street Journal, Microsoft extended offers to about 8,600 employees and more than 30% accepted.

Voluntary buyouts have become an attractive option for companies looking to cut jobs and reduce costs without resorting to explicit layoffs, which draw headlines and heightened scrutiny. Mass layoffs can also significantly impact morale, something major employers like Microsoft are all too familiar with. Just this year, Microsoft faced its share of criticism for eliminating 3,200 jobs in its Xbox division, targeting some veteran staffers who had spent decades there; the company also made thousands of cuts across other departments. 

There are all kinds of euphemistic terms for this strategy, which has become a more popular approach to workforce reductions in corporate America. Just this week, Skydance—the new company formed from the merger between Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery—proposed buyouts to a number of employees (in addition to adopting a five-day return-to-office mandate that had already been implemented at Paramount). Companies like Google and Verizon have also deployed voluntary buyouts to soften the blow when they need to become leaner. 

As the public backlash over AI intensifies, companies may want to avoid the negative attention they attract when they make sweeping changes to their workforce. But they also run the risk of losing top talent when they make buyout offers—and it isn’t always clear how many people will choose to leave. (According to The Wall Street Journal, Microsoft anticipates that a much smaller share of people eligible for buyouts will accept this time, compared with the 30% who took the offer earlier this year.)

For certain employees, a voluntary exit can, in fact, present an opportunity. But it’s not always clear when it makes sense to take a buyout. 

As Fast Company has reported, companies sometimes target highly paid, experienced employees in the hopes of replacing them with cheaper labor. When presented with a buyout offer, experts say it’s important to think carefully about what it could mean for your career and where you see yourself next.

“The best-case scenario for a voluntary layoff is either stepping straight into retirement or having a new job already lined up,” writes Fast Company contributor Emily Guy Birken. “Then your severance package becomes an unexpected pot of gold in the path you were already walking. Unfortunately, most of us aren’t that lucky.” 

If you weren’t planning to leave prior to the buyout offer, it can be helpful to assess your future career prospects. Since companies usually give employees some time to make a decision about a buyout, you could put out feelers to get a sense of what the job market is like. A generous severance offer may be alluring, but it also means you’ll have to find a new job. 

If the severance offer is not compelling enough to consider, Birken points out that you’re in a position to try and negotiate a better package. “Learning that your employer is offering voluntary layoffs is stressful, but it doesn’t mean the company has all the cards,” she writes. “The organization wants something—a reduction in force—which means you have leverage.” 

The most important consideration, however, may be whether you think you’ll eventually lose your job either way, since voluntary exit programs can also just be a sign that layoffs are on the horizon. Sometimes a buyout offer is primarily a way to manage optics and give employees a false sense of control—only for a company to turn around and fire them a few months down the road.

#155. This chart shows how much Google's head count surged in the AI boom

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

While AI may be driving some companies to slash their head count, Google's is soaring.

When Google reported its Q3 earnings on Wednesday, it said it had 204,757 employees for the quarter ending September 2026. That means Google added roughly 10,000 people to the payroll since the end of March.

The uptick in head count runs counter to another trend across the tech industry, where some of Google's peers are slashing their workforces. Meta has been on a cost-cutting odyssey for several years, while Microsoft recently offered voluntary buyouts to 60,000 employees.

Some of Google's head count growth can be attributed to mergers and acquisitions. On March 11, Google said it had completed its acquisition of cybersecurity firm Wiz, which had roughly 2,000 employees.

In January 2023, Google announced it would slash roughly 12,000 roles — around 6% of the workforce at the time — and has since made several smaller rounds of layoffs, Business Insider has reported.

While the head count has grown somewhat steadily since Q2 2024, it has accelerated since the end of 2025. Google also reported Wednesday that its quarterly revenue grew 25% from this period last year.

#164. Some Polymarket user funds stolen by hackers; exchange referring matter to U.K. authorities

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Some users of prediction market platform Polymarket's international exchange had funds stolen from their accounts due to a hack of an unnamed third-party provider.

Polymarket has contacted law enforcement and pledged to reimburse any affected trader. It's unclear how many users were impacted. The company's U.S. platform was not compromised.

Traders first reported on social media platforms that they were seeing unauthorized withdrawals from their accounts on Thursday afternoon. In response, the company halted trading on its international platform.

Polymarket said that it has fixed the issue on its end, and that trading resumed at 3 p.m. ET. It told users later in a chatroom on Discord that users who had less than $10,000 taken from their wallets will have funds restored by Oct. 30, and those with funds greater than that figure withdrawn will have to sign a release form to receive their restoration.

A person familiar with the matter told CNBC that the issue stemmed from a hack of DebugBear, a website performance monitoring platform. DebugBear in a post on X said that it was impacted by a security incident on Thursday. The person familiar with the Polymarket outage said that the company referred the matter to U.K. law enforcement, as that is where DebugBear is based.

DebugBear didn't immediately respond to a request for comment.

An analysis of publicly-available data by two analytics firms — Betting Intelligence and Polysights — provided to CNBC said that an account on Polymarket Thursday withdrew at least $2.7 million across 710 other wallets.

'This is certainly a significant and new form of breach that we've seen on Polymarket," said Dan Zimmermann, the chief intelligence officer at Betting Intelligence. "The timing right before the midterms ...is certainly a wake up call for folks."

Polymarket's international prediction market exchange operates as a decentralized finance platform on the Polygon blockchain and is not regulated in the U.S.

The disruption comes just days before both the company's international and U.S. exchanges are expected to see heightened volumes during the U.S. midterm elections on Nov. 3.

Back in June, a similar incident happened, when Polymarket said a third party vendor had been "compromised" and that the company would refund affected users.

#222. Rare Stanley Druckenmiller post sends crypto-linked stock surging

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The post appeared seemingly out of nowhere. Stanley Druckenmiller, the legendary hedge fund manager who brought the Bank of England to its knees in the 1990s, and whose annual returns are the envy of Wall Street, posted a simple message on an otherwise sparse X feed announcing his involvement with a new type of decentralized exchange. The post caused bedlam on social media and moved shares of a leading crypto treasury company and its token.

TradeXYZ, the primary developer of perpetual futures, or "perps" markets, on exchange Hyperliquid, announced a partnership Thursday with Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) to bring its popular perpetual futures onshore into the U.S.

Shortly after the two said they are seeking regulatory approvals, Druckenmiller said on X that he is "excited to be an investor in trade(xyz)," and that he is "looking forward to to using their innovative product to enhance my investment process." Representatives for Druckenmiller confirmed the statement to the Financial Times.

Options traders pounced on the closest publicly-traded play to the news: Hyperliquid Strategies (PURR), the company that invests in the HYPE crypto token that powers the Hyperliquid exchange.

More than 111,000 options traded on Hyperliquid Strategies Thursday, more than six times the 30-day average, as underlying shares surged 19% to the highest in three weeks. The flows leaned bullish, with almost 86,000 calls and under 27,000 puts trading. Almost four times as many calls were likely initiated by buyers than puts, and of the top 10 trades, only one involved put-buying, Cboe LiveVol dat show.

"We are thrilled to see ICE partnering with Trade.XYZ to bring the markets and liquidity of Hyperliquid to American investors," David Schamis, CEO of Hyperliquid Strategies, said in a text. "ICE is a cornerstone of traditional finance and the ideal partner."

Schamis added that the deal shows demand for perp-futures trading and is a step forward toward regulated trading of the asset class.

In August, President Donald Trump said in a surprise comment to reporters that regulators were working to bring the exchange onshore.

The most popular trades by volume in PURR were selling the 13-strike put expiring Dec. 18, and buying the 13-strike call expiring Nov. 6. Taken together, the two resemble a "risk reversal" strategy, a bullish trade looking for a short-term continuation of the rally, paired with long-term confidence the stock won't be below $13 by year-end.

#165. Nubank, Latin America's largest digital bank, is turning its eyes toward the US — where foreign rivals have failed

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nubank is the largest digital bank in Latin America — now it's turning its eyes towards the US.

Cristina Junqueira, the co-founder and US CEO of Nubank, sat down with Dan DeFrancesco, executive editor and anchor of Business Insider Today, at Business Insider's The Long Play, to talk about the digital bank's expansion on Thursday.

Since its founding in 2013, the fintech bank has pushed for financial inclusivity in Latin America by providing access to banking and financial services through its mobile app. Headquartered in Junqueira's home country of Brazil, the simple, low-cost, and digital-first approach discarded the need for physical banks, which are at times harder to reach in parts of Latin America — a key part of its success in the region.

It all started with a purple credit card with no fees.

"We had this vision that data, design, technology, completely transforms people's relationship with money," she said.

Junqueira previously worked as an executive at Itaú Unibanco, the largest private-sector financial giant in the region, also headquartered in Brazil and now a direct competitor of Nubank. Today, it is the largest company in Brazil based on primary banking relationships, and two-thirds of the country's adult population are customers, she said.

The digital bank is set to expand into the US after opening a branch in September, operating under the name Nu. This comes after expansion into Mexico and Colombia in 2021.

"We always saw the thesis as a global one," she said. "Everywhere in the world, people want more convenience. People want more transparency. People want simpler experiences managing their finances. People still get like paralyzed by complexity through their financial lives."

Entering America's crowded banking sector is a risk — but for Junqueira, entering no country goes without risk.

"The US is a very hard market, but I haven't found easy ones either," she said. "Building a bank is hard everywhere, so you might as well go for the biggest payout."

In the US, she said getting a bank account is still "surprisingly hard" and normally requires you to go to a physical branch, while Nu offers an easier alternative to that, she said. Foreign digital-first banks have tried to bring their platforms to the US before and failed. German fintech N26 and UK fintech Monzo both tried to expand their businesses to the US, and ultimately withdrew from the market.

The American consumer is unique, which is why Junqueira aims to take a different approach than her competitors to earn trust and scale the platform. She said most other companies started with debit cards, while Nubank is the first to start with credit cards.

Starting people out with a debit card "requires you to build a lot of trust with those customers because you're asking them to deposit their money with you," she said. "In our case, all the countries that we started, we started with credit. This is a very different relationship because you're the one trusting the consumers," she said.

Some Latin American immigrants who already live in the US are responding positively to Nu as it enters the market.

"I've been positively surprised not only by the amount of people, sure, but Brazilians, Mexicans, and others are here and have been waiting for this," she said.

Junqueira also pointed to emerging AI technology and how it could impact the company and the banking sector. She said the company is "heavily leveraging AI," integrating it in credit underwriting, customer support, and product development.

Brazil's presidential election on Sunday made headlines as President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva unexpectedly won a runoff against Flávio Bolsonaro, who fled the country to the US after he was under investigation for alleged corruption and money laundering. When asked about how she reacted to the big market sell-off in response, Junqueira said volatility is part of operating in a country like Brazil, but having 13 years of experience in the country has helped them learn they are going to run the company for the long run.

"We got to focus on the fundamentals, and the reality is, last quarter we turned over a billion dollars in net income," she said. "Markets don't like surprises, right? But we manage our business regardless of what happens."

The founder said one of the hardest things Nu will have to do in the US is build a brand that connects with users on an "emotional" level. Junqueira's dream scenario, five years from now, is to find evidence of that connection in the US.

One of the ways Nubank is attempting to spread its brand is through a recently signed deal with the Mercedes F1 team. Junqueira pointed to fans' passion for specific teams, as they follow the championship around the world, which was part of why they wanted to take part.

"We got a lot of visibility out of that, and we're working with them on brand activations," she said. "We're producing very interesting activations for consumers that help us amplify the branding fast, and that really help us bring this partnership to life."

Another huge part of the digital bank's brand is gender equality. In 2021, Nubank set a goal for 50% of Nubank's leadership to be women by 2025. Junqueira said today that the company's workforce is over 45% women, with many in leadership positions. In the four countries where they operate, all top leaders are women. Junqueira said the biggest challenge of being an executive while raising a family is not having the flexibility to juggle the demands of both roles.

"The reality is, as hard as we have to work, I think the hardest part is not having enough flexibility," she said. "It has been wonderful for us to have that type of opportunity, for us to really be able to tap into this part of the workforce that often struggles to have this type of career opportunity."

#170. 66 major housing markets now see falling prices

Based on ResiClub’s analysis of the Zillow Home Value Index, nationally aggregated U.S. home prices are up 1% year over year between September 2025 and September 2026. That’s a month-over-month deceleration from the +1.3% YoY shift between August 2025 and August 2026.

After contracting over the past year, the number of America’s major regional housing markets seeing year-over-year declines is rising again, as the already soft national housing market sees another burst of softening amid the recent run-up in long-term yields and mortgage rates.

  • 54 of the nation’s 300 largest housing markets (i.e., 18% of markets) had a falling year-over-year home price reading in the August 2025 to August 2026 window.
  • 66 of the nation’s 300 largest housing markets (i.e., 22% of markets) had a falling year-over-year home price reading in the September 2025 to September 2026 window.

Home prices are still climbing a tad year over year in pockets where active inventory remains well below pre-pandemic 2019 levels, such as certain areas in the Northeast and Midwest. In contrast, some pockets in states like Texas, Florida, Washington, and Colorado—where active inventory exceeds pre-pandemic 2019 levels by a solid clip—are seeing either material corrections, modest home price pullbacks, or simply flat pricing.

Looking ahead, the big question is how much of a burst of softening the nationally aggregated housing market will see, now that the 30-year fixed mortgage rate has jumped back above 7% amid an already strained affordability environment and a post-boom cyclical cooling period. To keep an eye on it, ResiClub recommends closely monitoring local active inventory over the next several months.

Many of the housing markets seeing the most softness—where homebuyers have gained the most leverage since the pandemic housing boom fizzled out—are primarily located in Sunbelt regions or the Mountain West.

Many of these areas saw even greater price surges during the pandemic housing boom, with home price growth outpacing local income levels. As pandemic-driven domestic migration slowed and mortgage rates rose in 2022, markets like Tampa, Florida, and Austin, Texas, faced challenges, relying on local income levels to support frothy home prices.

That Sunbelt softening was further compounded by an abundance of new home supply in the Sunbelt. Builders are often willing to lower prices or offer affordability incentives to maintain sales, which also has a cooling effect on the resale market. As a result, some buyers who might have previously opted for existing homes are instead choosing new construction with more attractive deals—which added further upward pressure to resale inventory growth over the past few years.

Of course, while 66 of the nation’s 300 largest metro-area housing markets are seeing year-over-year home price declines, another 234 are seeing year-over-year home price increases. However, most of the places posting “nominal gains” are seeing gains below income growth and consumer price growth, which economists call “real” (inflation-adjusted) home price declines.

#160. Colon cancer rates in the U.S. are on the rise for the first time in 20 years. Here’s who’s most at risk

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

If you’re overdue for a colonoscopy, let this be your reminder: Colorectal cancer rates are increasing for pretty much everyone.

While word has gotten out about rising colorectal cancer rates among younger adults, a new study found that rates are climbing across all age groups up to the age of 69. Researchers from the University of Chicago found that the overall incidence rate for this type of cancer—the second-leading cause of cancer-related deaths in the U.S.—rose for the first time in more than 20 years, according to a study published earlier this week in NEJM Evidence.

The age-adjusted incidence rate of colorectal cancer rose from 36.7 per 100,000 people in 2022 to 38.3 in 2023, the study authors found. By comparison, the incidence rate in 2000 was 55.2. The incidence rate among people between the ages of 45 and 49 rose nearly 13% from 2019 to 2023, the sharpest increase among any age group—although that’s likely driven by more people in this cohort undergoing screenings. In 2021, the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force lowered the recommended age for a first screening from 50 to 45.

“That means that screening is doing as we intend it to do—it’s catching cancer earlier,” Caitlin Murphy, the lead author of the study and a professor of pediatrics and public health sciences at the University of Chicago, said in a statement. 

But earlier screening doesn’t explain why the incidence rate is also rising among older adults between the ages of 65 and 69, as they’re about 80% up to date with screenings and historically benefitted from this detection method. The results suggest that unknown risk factors may be offsetting the previous gains from screening—and the study’s authors say they still need to understand why that’s happening.

“In terms of understanding the root cause, I think our study shows that we can consider that whatever might be causing colorectal cancer in younger people is now causing it to increase in older people as well,” Murphy said.

THEORIES FOR THE ROOT CAUSE

Finding that root cause of colorectal cancer remains the biggest mystery for researchers to unravel. Earlier this year, new evidence showed that colorectal cancer is now the leading cause of cancer-related deaths among men and women under the age of 50. Meanwhile, nearly 160,000 Americans will be diagnosed with colorectal cancer this year, including both colon cancer and rectal cancer, according to estimates from the American Cancer Institute.

Theories for what causes colorectal cancer are wide ranging. A recent study found that faster biological aging could be a factor that’s associated with an increased risk of several early-onset cancers, including colorectal cancer. The authors of this latest study plan to explore another possible culprit: environmental chemical exposures.

Working with a toxicologist from Emory University, the team from the University of Chicago will measure thousands of environmental exposures to see which ones warrant further research, then test those chemicals on organoids, which are specialized models that can grow colons in a dish.
