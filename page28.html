#96. China elevates a military general to the Politburo and seeks to strengthen party discipline

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Chinese President Xi Jinping, center left and Zhang Shengmin, Vice Chairman of the Central Military Commission second from right attend a ceremony in honor of fallen national heroes at the Monument to the People's Heroes to mark Martyrs' Day at Tiananmen Square in Beijing, Wednesday, Sept. 30, 2026. Ng Han Guan/AP hide caption

BANGKOK — China's top leaders on Thursday elevated the only remaining general on the commission that oversees the country's military to the Politburo, the top governing body of the Communist Party.

The leaders also adopted a resolution on strengthening self-governance, discipline and anti-corruption in the party, as they seek to extend their grip on power.

During a four-day closed-door meeting, top Communist Party leaders deliberated on and adopted resolutions that will guide the party in the coming years. The meeting comes about a year before President Xi Jinping is widely expected to secure his fourth five-year term as the party's leader.

The party elected Gen. Zhang Shengmin, who currently serves as vice chairman of the Central Military Commission, to the Politburo. Xi had earlier purged two of China's top generals, leaving Zhang as the commission's only member besides Xi himself. It also left the Politburo without any representatives from the People's Liberation Army.

Zhang also holds a position overseeing internal discipline for the military, serving as deputy secretary of the CMC Commission for Discipline Inspection.

The party said in a summary of the resolution that the tasks facing it were "increasingly arduous," such as "uniting and leading the people to safeguard national sovereignty, security, and development interests."

It said it must step up its self-governance, which involves rooting out corruption and maintaining ideological purity.

The party should, "with an eye on enhancing its long-term governance capacity, maintain its progressive nature and purity, and its close, flesh-and-blood ties with the people," according to a summary of the resolution from the official Xinhua News Agency.

Xi has championed anti-corruption since coming to power in 2012, and has sustained a crackdown on officials for more than a decade. However, analysts say the focus now also includes underperforming officials.

Party members also reviewed the earlier purges of top members and military officials, confirming the personnel changes.

The plenum meeting revealed wider-than-expected purges, according to analysis by Neil Thomas, a senior fellow at Asia Policy who studies Chinese politics. He calculated an attendance rate of 74 percent, the lowest since the times of former leader Mao Zedong, who ran political purges regularly.

#730. Russia to extend diesel export ban, use quotas to fulfil US deal, sources say

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

FILE PHOTO: A general view shows the Lukoil refinery in Volgograd, Russia, April 22, 2022. REUTERS

Oct 29 - Russia will extend its diesel export ban until the end of the year while allowing some companies to ship fuel under a quota system to meet its new export commitment to the US, two people familiar with the discussions told Reuters.

Moscow and Washington agreed a diesel supply deal after Presidents Vladimir Putin and Donald Trump spoke by phone earlier this month. The energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine announced alongside the agreement has allowed Moscow to increase fuel production. At the same time, fuel shortages earlier this year have prompted the government to maintain tight controls on oil product exports.

Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said on Tuesday that three tankers carrying Russian diesel to export markets were already under way and that a fourth would sail on October 28.

Novak also said Russian refineries were recovering quickly from repairs. The sources said refinery utilisation had risen 25% at the end of October from the start of the month after drone attacks on facilities subsided.

Under the current diesel export ban, the government permits limited exports through a quota system. Those quotas will quadruple from October levels to 2 million metric tons, or about 0.5 million barrels per day, in November, the sources said. Shipments under existing intergovernmental agreements will also continue.

The Energy Ministry did not respond to a request for comment.

The current ban on diesel exports is due to expire on October 31.

In October, the government exempted several refiners from the ban and granted them export quotas totalling 0.5 million tons, the sources said. A new government order expected in early November would expand the list of exempt refiners and set new export limits. If the quotas are used up quickly, the government could consider increasing them further, the sources added.

Nominal diesel production in Russia exceeds domestic consumption by about 40%. As refineries return to normal operating rates, they will need export outlets to prevent a domestic glut. REUTERS

#809. Fears of rent hikes after chancellor hits landlords with higher property tax

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Please refresh the page or navigate to another page on the site to be automatically logged inPlease refresh your browser to be logged in

Experts warn that Labour’s Autumn Budget has ‘made life harder for renters’ who are already facing an extra £700 on their average annual bill

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

Tenants have been warned to expect higher rents following Labour’s decision to hike property tax for landlords at the Autumn Budget.

Chancellor John Healey confirmed that rental income will be subject to a further two percentage point tax increase from April, which will add to an identical announcement made by his predecessor Rachel Reeves last year.

The tax surcharge on property income will be four points higher from April 2027 as a result, in a move the National Residential Landlords Association (NRLA) said had “made life harder for renters right across the country”.

Under current forecasts, rents are already expected to rise by four per cent over the next year – adding £700 on to the average annual bill for tenants.

This could now grow even higher as a result of Labour’s tax increase, with the Office for Budget Responsibility (OBR) warning that it risks “leading to a steady rise in rents”.

Delivering its economic outlook alongside the Budget, the spending watchdog said that the increased tax burden – alongside other housing policies – will reduce rental supply and cause remaining landlords to push up their prices.

Chris Norris, chief policy officer for the NRLA, said: “When the last Budget announced a rise in income tax on property income, landlords told us clearly how they planned to respond.

“Research for the NRLA by Pegasus Insight found that 46 per cent of landlords planned to increase rents over the next 12 months as a result, with around a third (35 per cent) planning to increase rents by more than previously planned. A third (33 per cent) said they planned to sell one or more properties,” he told The Independent.

“It is deeply disappointing that, rather than heeding those warnings, the chancellor has chosen to raise the tax again by a further two percentage points. As the Office for Budget Responsibility has confirmed, hiking taxes will drive up rents and drive down supply.”

Other experts have given more conservative estimates of the impact of the change on renters.

Hannah Aldridge, senior research and policy analyst at the Resolution Foundation, said price rises are “likely to be small as rents tend to track earnings and landlords seek the highest rent they can charge”.

She added: “The fact that their tax burden has gone up, doesn’t change the amount that tenants are willing to pay for the property (unless supply becomes more constrained). There will likely be some pass through to rents but most of the tax rise will affect landlords’ margins rather than rents.”

Estate agents have also cautioned that while tax increases and new regulations under the Renters’ Rights Act will prompt some landlords to evaluate their portfolios, the full effects may take time to be felt.

Lucian Cook, head of residential research at Savills, told The Independent that the policy is “another reason why some landlords will reconsider whether or not they want to continue in the buy-to-let sector, or whether they want to rationalise their portfolio”.

“That is likely to mean that it adds to the squeeze on the supply of rental property in the market,” he added.

“The knock-on implications of that... is likely to mean that there is less choice available to tenants when they come to look for a property to be able to rent. And that would also suggest that it puts some continued upward pressure on rental values.”

Following the Budget, several housing groups also expressed disappointment that the chancellor did not address concerns around frozen housing benefit rates. Campaigners have called for the government to relink the Local Housing Allowance (LHA) to market rates, warning that low-income renters are having their budgets squeezed.

According to analysis by the Resolution Foundation, the shortfall between housing benefit rates and the bottom 30 per cent of local rents recently reached a record high of 23.3 per cent – equal to £170 a month for a typical family living in a two-bedroom home.

Holly Williamson, chief executive of Generation Rent, said: “Homes are the foundations of our lives, so it is hugely disappointing the government has maintained the freeze of Local Housing Allowance. When housing benefit doesn’t cover the rent it forces families into impossible decisions, traps them in poverty and leaves them vulnerable to homelessness, with councils picking up the pieces.

“This decision also brings into question the government’s goal of ending rough sleeping, as it will mean there are very few affordable long-term homes for people currently sleeping rough.”

A nicotine ban has come into effect in Scotland, meaning under 18s will no longer be able to purchase certain products

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

Scotland will ban any under-18s from buying all nicotine and herbal smoking products in an effort to crack down on the number of children and young people smoking.

The tougher new rules, taking effect on 29 October, will make it illegal for children under the age of 18 to buy pouches, strips and pearls, and any other herbal products that can be smoked.

Anyone selling nicotine products will need to possess a valid registration by April 29, 2027, meaning Scotland will be the only country in the UK that will know where such products are being distributed.

Retailers already registered to sell tobacco and vaping products must update their registration if they intend to continue doing so, as existing registrations will not automatically cover them.

Scotland’s fixed penalty notice scheme for tobacco and vaping offences is also being extended to cover nicotine and herbal smoking product offences, alongside additional offences.

The Scottish Government has been urging retailers to review their age verification processes, staff training and registration details before the new rules took effect.

The tougher legislation comes as part of the Tobacco and Vapes Act 2026, and will also mean vending machines can no longer stock such items. Discounts on the sale of any nicotine products will also be outlawed.

Public health minister Maree Todd said: “We want to protect future generations from nicotine addiction and we know that nicotine products, particularly pouches, are starting to gain popularity among young people.

“By banning the sale of these products to under-18s, the Scottish Government is taking a crucial step in protecting people’s health. This in turn is helping people across Scotland to live longer, healthier lives.

“Requiring tobacco and vape retailers to register their business to sell nicotine and herbal smoking products will also provide a strong foundation for effective regulation and enforcement of the sale of these products in Scotland.

“I want to thank all of the retailers who have already registered and remind others they must do so before the deadline next April.”

Scotland’s chief medical officer Gregor Smith said: “Nicotine is a highly addictive substance that can lead to dependence and withdrawal symptoms including depression, difficulty concentrating and insomnia.

“Whilst vapes may have a role to play in assisting addicted people to stop smoking, it is very clear to me that those who do not smoke should never start using nicotine products, and this especially applies to children and young people.

“I welcome these new age-of-sale restrictions on nicotine and herbal smoking products, which will help reduce the health harms associated with nicotine addiction and address health inequalities in Scotland.”

“There is only one team that matters in this White House, and that is the team focused on serving President Donald J. Trump and the American people,” White House spokeswoman Danielle Alvarez said in a statement. “The president’s team remains strong, united and focused on delivering his America First agenda.”
