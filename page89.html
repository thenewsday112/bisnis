Google and OpenAI now build answers as interfaces. The decision generative UI forces on a business looks like the one hotels made with booking.com.

In May, I wrote that generative UI ends the page as the destination. I still think generative UI goes there, but five months later it is clearly early. Google and OpenAI now both build answers as interfaces, and neither has shown a website’s data inside one. The decision generative UI would force on a business is already visible, though, and it looks a lot like the one hotels made with Booking.com.

Generative UI means the AI builds the screen for each answer, with charts, buttons, maps or a small tool, instead of returning a block of text or a list of links.

Google’s generative UI was live globally in English in AI Mode, the chat-style tab in Google Search, by Aug. 19, 2026. Google first brought it to AI Mode with Gemini 3 on Nov. 18, 2025, and at I/O on May 19, 2026, promised it to everyone. Google’s Search post from I/O says Search “can design custom layouts, assembling components (like interactive visuals, tables, graphs or simulations),” and that these capabilities “will be available for everyone in Search this summer, free of charge.”

Google’s I/O post says how generative UI is built: “We’re bringing the power of Google Antigravity and the agentic coding capabilities of Gemini 3.5 Flash right into Search.” Antigravity is Google’s tool for AI that writes code. Google’s November 2025 post says Gemini codes the interactive tools in real time and adds them into the response.

Google’s August 19 post confirmed the rollout: “Generative UI capabilities have launched globally in English in AI Mode” and “have started rolling out as well in AI Overviews,” the AI summaries at the top of Google results. The examples in Google’s August post are study aids, “custom tools and simulations to help you see complex topics in action.”

The I/O example that does use outside information is for a later feature: Small apps people will be able to build inside Search “in the coming months.” Google’s example is a fitness tracker: “Search will code it for you, tapping into fresh, real-time sources including reviews, live maps and local data like the weather.” Google’s I/O post does not say whose reviews.

OpenAI launched Intelligent UI with GPT-6 on October 7. ChatGPT answers can now “include graphics, tappable buttons, forms, charts, and interactive experiences you can use directly in your conversation.” OpenAI started rolling it out to most paid ChatGPT plans that day, and to the Free and Go plans the next day.

OpenAI says ChatGPT shows these screens while the answer is still being written. ChatGPT puts its screens together from ready-made parts, while Google says Gemini writes the code for its interactive tools on the spot.

OpenAI’s examples are a Sunday roast timed alongside the recipe, a road trip on a map, the central limit theorem, a bill splitter and a retirement calculator. OpenAI’s post closes on “Instead of people adapting to software, software will adapt to people.” OpenAI’s Intelligent UI announcement mentions web search but says nothing about websites, sources, or links back.

The examples in Google’s and OpenAI’s announcements are built from what the AI already knows. None of them shows a store’s product, a hotel’s rooms, or a local business’s hours inside a generated interface. Google’s November 2025 post says “all responses have prominent links to high-quality content across the web,” which puts links next to the generated interface and says nothing about a business’s own data inside one.

I have not seen it either. I have seen rich results, the search results with extras like star ratings or prices, but I have no idea if that was generative UI.

Whether anyone is using generative UI is also an open question. Google’s June 3 post, updated August 31, puts AI Overviews at “over 2.5 billion monthly active users” and AI Mode past 1 billion monthly users. OpenAI says more than 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week. Those are the audiences of the products, and neither company has said how many people see a generated interface. One ChatGPT Plus user reported on OpenAI’s forum that their account showed GPT-6 but no Intelligent UI. That is a single report.

Generative UI is something that would keep the user with Google longer, or even all the way. And it is something that others, OpenAI, are now doing. It is not just Google, it is all platforms.

If you know the Backrooms, the internet horror story about endless empty office rooms that A24 turned into a movie in 2026, generative UI is the friendlier version. The rooms are nicer, there is a calculator in every one, and you are still not supposed to leave.

There is a second reason, and this part is my guess. On Alphabet’s earnings call on July 22, Sundar Pichai said Google has started training Gemini 4, its next model. Asked by an analyst how Google would close the gap in coding, he answered: “There are areas where we have acknowledged we need to improve. Coding and agentic coding is an example of that, and I think the teams are very, very focused on it.” On September 30, he showed an early version, Gemini 4 Argon, on X, and said it performs at the top level in “software engineering”, based on Google’s own tests. Google says Gemini codes the tools inside generative UI, so a Gemini that is better at coding builds better tools.

A business facing generative UI has to decide what to do about Google traffic. Wait for the booking or the order to come via Google, a version of what Booking.com does, or work on other channels. Or do both.

What a business would pay Google for those bookings, I do not know. My hunch is that there is zero chance Google is not thinking about how to monetize generative UI. It would be a commission, or paid placements, or both. It would basically be the Booking.com business model on a search results page.

The paid-placement half already exists. At Google Marketing Live, its yearly advertising event, in 2025, Google said, “We’re expanding Ads in AI Overviews, and testing Ads in AI Mode,” so paid placement inside Google’s AI answers was already a product by 2025. A commission on bookings made inside a generated interface is not something Google has announced.

This is me talking about what I think will happen. Nothing Google or OpenAI has shown puts a website inside a generated interface yet. The choice between waiting for Google to send the booking and building the channels a business owns does not need to wait for that.

#1231. The 25 Best Halloween Movies for People Who Hate Horror Movies

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Relax, there isn’t a jump scare in sight.

Who doesn’t like getting scared? Apparently, a lot of you. While Halloween is undeniably the season for watching the scariest movies ever made, sometimes, it’s a bit much. Plus, let’s be real. The world is plenty scary already. So, what is there to do when you want to get festive for Halloween but can’t stomach another jump scare? Luckily, we have a list of movies just for the occasion—and the right audience.

The last few years have seen an explosion of genre hybrids that blend horror with action, comedy, even romance. We’re talking movies like Send Help, a Sam Raimi joint starring Rachel McAdams as a woman stuck on a deserted island with her shitty boss, and Companion, a 2025 hit where Sophie Thatcher plays a “companion bot” who wakes up to her own programming. Action junkies can join in too, with movies like Na Hong-jin’s Hope—maybe one of the coolest alien disaster movies we’ve seen in a long time—and Sinners, Ryan Coogler’s red-hot Oscar darling that made vampires even more cool. There are also gems like Beetlejuice and Practical Magic, as well as vintage classics like The Rocky Horror Picture Show.

The temperature has cooled. The sun has set. The popcorn is ready. Binge these not-so-scary horror movies that are perfect for everyone, and we truly mean everyone, this Halloween.

You know exactly who to call. Released in 1984, Ghostbusters was a game-changer, being one of the first truly successful cross-genre movies that also found an unexpected audience in children. Bill Murray, Harold Ramis, and Dan Aykroyd star as disgraced academics who turn their expertise into a business: catching pest-like ghosts in New York City. Later joined by Winston Zeddemore (Ernie Hudson), the Ghostbusters fight to save Manhattan when the ancient Sumerian (not Babylonian) deity Gozer rises up. Admit it: The theme song is still stuck in your head.

Don’t you hate it when your sister's boyfriend dies and then you use your witchcraft to resurrect him but accidentally possess him with an evil spirit instead? Classic! Practical Magic is a new-school favorite and a solid Halloween choice that gives you both Sandra Bullock and Nicole Kidman.

Ten years after his last film The Wailing, Na Hong-jin came roaring back to theaters with his sci-fi monster epic Hope. Set in a rural Korean village, a police chief and his gun-toting rookie deputy must defend the people when a rampaging beast tears shit up. While Hope nosedives into a bizarre science fiction epic in the second half, the first half is some of the most visceral and thrilling action we’ve seen in a long minute.

Rarely can a modern remake outshine whatever other movie it’s trying to emulate. But in one rare instance, Zack Snyder did the impossible with his brawny 2004 remake of George Romero’s Dawn of the Dead. Essentially a modern retelling of Romero’s anti-consumerist classic, Sarah Polley stars as a nurse who holes up with other survivors at a Milwaukee shopping mall during a sudden zombie outbreak. While there are a few tension-riddled scares—this is a zombie movie we’re talking about here—Dawn of the Dead is a sun-drenched blockbuster where Zack Snyder’s signature brand of action is the real attraction.

Oh no! We’re stuck on an island with Rachel McAdams! Someone save us! (But please, take your time.) In this rip-roaring black comedy from none other than Sam Raimi, a die-hard Survivor fan (McAdams) gets stranded on a remote island with her pampered billionaire boss (Dylan O’Brien). While there are elements of horror sprinkled throughout the film—including a pretty dark turn for one of the leads—Send Help is a pure crowd-pleaser that is more concerned with exploring gender politics and power dynamics than trying to rouse up a few scares.

Talk about being on the bleeding edge. Companion stars Sophie Thatcher as Iris, who discovers—to her horror—that she isn’t a living person but a “companion robot” for pathetic Josh (Jack Quaid). Suddenly, a carefree weekend getaway with friends turns into a nightmare as Iris fights to break free from her programming. Essentially a slasher flick where we follow (and root for) the “monster,” Companion trades suspenseful terror and jump scares for a few solid laughs.

Roddy Piper is here to kick ass and chew bubblegum, and he's all out of bubblegum. The pro wrestling legend stars in this iconic 1980s sci-fi satire from John Carpenter, in which a man sees through some special sunglasses how the world is actually ruled by aliens. Imagined by Carpenter as a commentary on Reagan-era America, They Live is only scary in how timeless it remains.

Somehow, a grotesque body horror thriller with mesmerizing creature effects and 5,500 gallons of fake blood made it all the way to the Oscars. So, if the Academy of Motion Pictures Arts & Sciences can stomach it, so can you. In Coralie Fargeat’s festival darling, Demi Moore stars as has-been Hollywood star Elisabeth Sparkle who turns to a secret black market drug that can transform her into a younger, more beautiful version of herself (Margaret Qualley). What begins as Elisabeth’s grasp for relevancy becomes a fight for her survival as her two sides sabotage one another until everything comes to a phenomenally phantasmagoric finale. Beware, and behold, the “Monstro Elisasue.”

Michael Keaton stars as an mischievous demon with perverse humor and wild gags in this 1988 horror comedy about a “bio-exorcist” who won’t stop terrorizing a family (Alec Baldwin, Geena Davis, Winona Ryder, Catherine O'Hara, Jeffrey Jones) until they say his name three times. With Beetlejuice, it’s harder than it sounds.

Ryan Coogler worked his magic over Sinners, and it worked. The hit vampire blockbuster sizzled the summer 2025 box office in its story about twin brothers (both played by Michael B. Jordan) who finally come home to Mississippi after serving in World War I and bootlegging for the mob during Prohibition. On the first night of their very own juke joint, vampires come knocking. Sinners ain’t for the children, but even wimps can tolerate a few of its scares when it tells such a profoundly moving story—about brotherhood, about community, about culture, about dreams.

In 2024, Jane Schoenbrun unleashed their sophomore feature I Saw the TV Glow to critical acclaim. The praise is well-deserved. In this movie about millennial nostalgia, the terror of something sinister at play is palpable. Justice Smith and Jack Haven co-star as childhood friends who bonded over their favorite ‘90s television show, The Pink Opaque (think Buffy meets Ghostwriter). Years later, Haven’s character Maddy returns out of nowhere and begins swearing The Pink Opaque is real. An elaborate metaphor about queerness and the terror of never living your true self, I Saw the TV Glow is a deeply emotional film under a cloud of hazy dread.

When An American Werewolf in London was in production, financiers thought writer/director John Landis's script was too scary to be a comedy and too funny to be a horror movie. It's that kind of balance that makes the movie so great decades later. David Naughton stars as an American backpacker who is bitten by a wolf and is taken in by a pretty English nurse (Jenny Gutter). Though its final parting shot is tragic, the road to the end is paved with equal parts horror and humor. The first transformation sequence is quite scary, but the rest of the movie is a riot.

If there's one foolproof Halloween movie, it's Hocus Pocus. Bette Midler, Sarah Jessica Parker, and Kathy Najimy all star as a trio of witchy sisters who are resurrected on Halloween night. The campy nineties classic is also directed by Kenny Ortega, who helmed Disney’s High School Music franchise.

Could a horror movie also be sad? Of course it can, and you can look no further than Jacob’s Ladder. Directed by Adrian Lyne, this genre classic from 1990 stars Tim Robbins as a Vietnam veteran whose post-traumatic stress disorder have left him with bizarre hallucinations. Stirring and provocative, Jacob’s Ladder sacrifices jump scares in favor of a persistently anxious atmosphere that induces a steady level of dread throughout—and a final reveal that hits like a punch to the gut.

Jordan Peele did it again. After his first two movies, 2017’s Get Out and 2019’s Us became critical and commercial hits, the director came back to the big screen with his awe-inspiring 2022 sci-fi horror Nope. An alien movie disguised as an awe-inspiring monster flick, Nope stars Daniel Kaluuya and Keke Palmer as Hollywood horse wranglers who begin chasing after an ominous entity in the sky. A tribute to cinema and an exploration of man’s futile attempts to control nature, Nope should be a “Yup” to everyone, even those who don’t “do” horror.

“It can't rain all the time.” This grunge classic from 1994, based on the comics by James O'Barr, stars Bruce Lee's son Brandon Lee in his final film role. Lee plays Eric Draven, a rock star who is murdered on Halloween night along with his fiancée. One year later, Eric returns to the world of the living, now imbued with supernatural powers, to carry out his revenge.

Ever see a zombie movie with yakuza hitmen and samurai warriors? In 2000, underground auteur Ryuhei Kitamura made his feature-directing debut with Versus, which combines zombie horror with badass martial-arts action and John Woo–like gunplay. Set in a haunted forest, the movie sees a runaway prisoner clash with gangsters while they all fight to survive an endless horde of zombies.

When Guillermo del Toro's Crimson Peak bombed in theaters in 2015, one of the most cited reasons for its demise was that it just wasn't scary enough. That’s because it was mistakenly advertised as a haunted-house horror movie, when Crimson Peak is in fact a tragic Gothic romance, with Mia Wasikowska playing an aspiring author who moves into a dilapidated English mansion with her husband (Tom Hiddleston) and her sister (Jessica Chastain).

In 2009, when the world was smitten with Twilight's sparkling vampires, Korean auteur Park Chan-wook unearthed richer ground in the genre with his erotic horror Thirst. Song Kang-ho stars as a priest who is turned into a vampire and slowly begins to fall in lust for the wife of his childhood best friend. A vampire romance like nothing you've seen before, Thirst bites in places where it counts.

At the height of her Transformers fame, Megan Fox subverted expectations and the male gaze in this millennial classic from director Karyn Kusama and writer Diablo Cody. Fox stars as a demonically possessed high school student, Jennifer, who uses her sex appeal to draw in and kill her male classmates. Jennifer's bookish best friend (Amanda Seyfried) takes it upon herself to stop her reign of terror.

Before the hit series on AMC, Anne Rice's Interview with the Vampire was made into a gorgeous piece of Gothic cinema from Neil Jordan. Based on Rice's 1976 novel, the film version stars Brad Pitt as a plantation-owner-turned-vampire in 1791 by the charismatic Lestat (Tom Cruise). Pitt's Louis recounts his many years to a journalist in contemporary San Francisco. Hence the title. Though the movie deviates from the book quite a bit, it still found acclaim and reignited the appeal of vampires until a certain other author, Stephanie Meyer, came along.

If the title The Addams Family doesn't immediately make you want to snap your fingers and sing along, then you clearly aren't familiar with either this movie or the old TV classic it reimagines as must-see entertainment for any Halloween enthusiast.

Come up to the lab and see what's on the slab... A staple of the midnight-movie scene, The Rocky Horror Picture Show is the definition of a cult movie that finds new life every Halloween. (Fun fact: Its original New York City premiere on Halloween 1975 was scrapped due to low ticket sales.) A tribute to the sci-fi and horror movies of yesteryear, The Rocky Horror Picture Show famously stars Susan Sarandon, Barry Bostwick, and, of course, Tim Curry as the alien scientist Dr. Frank-N-Furter. Problematic? Sure. Fun? Oh yes. Shiver with antici ... pation.

Based on the Neil Gaiman novel, Coraline—a stop-motion masterpiece from the legendary Laika Studios—follows a lonely 11-year-old girl who wanders into an alternate universe where everything seems close to perfect. Too close. Too perfect. Naturally, there’s something much darker and more horrific going on. For all its cartoon whimsy, Coraline has just enough underneath it to send a solid shiver down your spine.

If you've seen the TV show (and who hasn't?), you've got to see the movie that started it all. Released in 1992, Buffy the Vampire Slayer stars Kristy Swanson as an all-American Valley Girl cheerleader who reluctantly accepts her destiny to become a vampire hunter. Also starring Donald Sutherland, Paul Reubens, Rutger Hauer, and Luke Perry, along with the film debut of Hillary Swank, Buffy the Vampire Slayer may not be as era defining as the TV show it later inspired, but it's still a killer good time.

#1227. These Are The 7 Best Fruits to Lower Blood Pressure, According to Experts

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Meet the top picks and what experts have to say about each one.

Women's Health may earn commission from the links on this page, but we only feature products we believe in. Why Trust Us?

High blood pressure can creep up on you, leading to symptoms like chest pain, headaches, and dizziness. And since the condition is linked to more serious health complications like heart attack and stroke, it’s important to stay on top of your numbers.

Blood pressure management is complex, and usually involves making a slew of lifestyle changes, including adjusting your exercise routine, tweaking your diet, and even taking medication. But when it comes to those dietary adjustments, consuming fewer packaged and ultra-processed foods and “replacing them with whole foods and antioxidant-rich foods like fruits can be helpful,” says Jessica Cording, RD, author of The Little Book of Game-Changers.

Meet the experts: Scott Keatley, RD, is co-owner of Keatley Medical Nutrition Therapy. Jessica Cording, RD, is the author of The Little Book of Game-Changers. Keri Gans, RDN, is a dietitian and host of The Keri Report podcast.

There's some research to back up these claims too: A 2022 study in the Journal of Clinical Hypertension monitored their blood pressure at home in over 2,200 participants ages 50 to 65. They self-reported how often they ate bananas, apples, pears, and citrus fruit (response options were: not every week, once or twice a week, three to four times per week, and at least five times per week), and those who ate those fruits more frequently had significantly lower systolic home blood pressure.

Ahead, dietitians break down the best fruits that may help lower your blood pressure to regularly add to your plate.

The big draw with bananas is their potassium content; one medium-sized banana delivers about 375 milligrams of potassium, per the USDA, which helps you meet your daily goal of 2,600 milligrams of the nutrient, according to the NIH.

Why does this matter? Potassium “balances sodium in the body and relaxes blood vessel walls, directly lowering [blood] pressure,” explains Scott Keatley, RD, co-owner of Keatley Medical Nutrition Therapy. Having some sodium in the body is important, but too much will cause your body to hold onto water, according to Harvard Health. When that happens, the amount of fluid in your blood vessels increases and raises blood pressure along with it.

Blueberries contain anthocyanins, which are plant compounds that improve blood vessel flexibility and support nitric oxide production, says Keri Gans, RDN, a dietitian and host of The Keri Report podcast. Nitric oxide helps relax the smooth muscles in the blood vessels, helping them widen so blood can flow more freely, decreasing blood pressure, she explains.

This green fruit is loaded with vitamin C—59 milligrams, to be exact. (That's over half the daily recommended goal for women, which is 75 milligrams, per Mayo Clinic.) Vitamin C “enhances nitric oxide availability," Keatley says. Again, nitric oxide helps to relax the smooth muscles in your blood vessels, lowering blood pressure along with it. It also helps to relax the blood vessels, making it easier for blood to flow through and lowering blood pressure as a result.

Like kiwi, grapefruit is rich in vitamin C, as one half of a grapefruit has 39 milligrams of the nutrient, per the USDA. “Compounds [like vitamin C and potassium] in grapefruit can improve the elasticity of blood vessels and reduce oxidative stress, which helps the circulatory system function more efficiently and lowers blood pressure,” Keatley says.

Grapes are loaded with polyphenols, which are naturally-occurring chemical compounds found in plants, Cording explains. These support the elasticity of your blood vessels and reduce oxidative stress in the body, lowering the risk of inflammation—which is also a risk factor for high blood pressure, Gans adds.

Watermelon contains an amino acid called L- citrulline. It boosts the production of nitric acid in your body, helping to widen those blood vessels and lower your blood pressure, Keatley says.

Like bananas, avocados pack a potassium punch—576 milligrams is in 100 grams, or 3/4 of a cup, per the USDA—to help with blood pressure management, Keatley says. They may also help with weight management thanks to the fact the contain healthy fats that will keep you full and satiated—and that can indirectly help to reduce blood pressure, he says.

Remember: High blood pressure is complex, and consuming more fruit alone is unlikely to make it to drop. However, eating more nutritiously can support your other efforts to bring it down. If you have high blood pressure, it’s important to work closely with your healthcare provider to come up with a comprehensive plan to help turn things around.

NASA has officially ended the mission of its Swift space telescope, after a private satellite failed in its bold bid to rescue the aging spacecraft.

That rescue mission was led by the Arizona company Katalyst Space Technologies, whose LINK spacecraft tried to meet up with Swift this summer to boost its orbit.

That didn't work out, however, so Swift continues its fall back toward Earth. The telescope is expected to reenter Earth's atmosphere "in the next few weeks," NASA said in a statement on Friday (Oct. 23).

That statement served primarily to announce the end of the Swift mission after more than 20 very productive years.

The telescope launched to Earth orbit in November 2004, tasked with studying gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) — the most powerful explosions in the cosmos. Swift was designed to operate for two years, but it continued studying the high-energy universe for more than two decades.

"In that time, Swift played a major role in advancing our understanding of the changing universe and was part of a revolution in astrophysics that showed how critical rapid-response observations are to the entire community," Shawn Domagal-Goldman, director of the Astrophysics Division at NASA Headquarters in Washington, said in the statement.

NASA had hoped to keep Swift going even longer. In September 2025, the agency awarded a contract to the Arizona company Katalyst Space Technologies to rescue the observatory, which was falling back to Earth thanks to atmospheric drag.

Swift has no thrusters capable of counteracting this drag. So Katalyst built a refrigerator-sized spacecraft called LINK, which aimed to rendezvous with Swift, grapple it with three robotic arms and boost it to a higher orbit, allowing the observatory to continue its science work.

NASA and Katalyst stressed from the start that success was far from guaranteed. After all, LINK's mission was unprecedented: No private spacecraft had ever captured and boosted a satellite that wasn't designed to be serviced in this way. And the rate of Swift's orbital decay demanded a rapid timeline: LINK launched on July 3, a little over nine months after Katalyst won the $30 million NASA Swift-rescue contract.

LINK was not able to execute its very challenging mission. About three weeks after liftoff, it suffered problems with its propulsion system and orientation-maintaining reaction wheels, which caused it to start spinning at an unexpected rate. These issues prevented LINK from capturing and boosting Swift, but the private craft did manage to get within 7.5 to 9.3 miles (12 to 15 kilometers) of the observatory and photograph it.

"From the beginning, this was a high-risk, high-reward mission," Domagal-Goldman said in a statement on Sept. 25, the day that LINK fell back to Earth.

"And while we'll be sad to see Swift's mission come to a close, we knew this boost effort would be worthwhile either way — advancing U.S. spacecraft servicing technology, challenging us to meet unprecedented mission timelines, and testing how we operate satellites to extend their time in low Earth orbit," he added.

Swift — which was officially renamed the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory in 2018, after its first principal investigator, who died a year earlier — gathered its last scientific data on Oct. 20, according to NASA's Friday update.

During its long and storied career, the space telescope observed more than 2,000 GRBs. And it did other work, too, studying more than 1,400 supernova explosions, as well as eyeing asteroids, comets and black holes and conducting an all-sky survey in X-ray light. The mission's data has contributed to about 7,000 published scientific papers to date, according to NASA officials.

And that number is sure to keep rising far into the future.

"Swift leaves us with an extraordinary archive of multiwavelength observations that will fuel new discoveries for decades more," Domagal-Goldman said in the Oct. 23 statement.

Mindfulness can’t always change the outcome when life gets hard, but showing up with loving awareness can ease suffering and deepen our connection to each other.

Life is hard, and it might get harder. So, where could mindfulness or loving awareness fit in when survival feels tenuous and ultimately temporary?

Recently, I listened to a good friend sharing painful accounts of his health crisis, the loss of his sister to cancer, and how his work is suffering as a result. It all sounded pretty grim. I could sense his anxiety and distress, so I told him that sometimes I cheer myself up with this image: It’s like we’re on the Titanic and we’re all going down, but it’s ok because we have our arms around each other as we sink into the ocean.

After a moment of stunned silence, my friend said, “Is that your idea of comforting me?”

After a moment of stunned silence, my friend said, “Is that your idea of comforting me? That we’re on the Titanic as it’s sinking!!?” And then, because he knows I practice mindfulness, he added: “Is this your idea of mindfulness?”

I paused to reflect for a moment and replied, “Well, I guess I’m being mindful that this is my thought, and that by acknowledging it I can gently and kindly be present to whatever it brings up. Besides, I’m focusing on the part that we all have our arms around each other.”

I mean, c’mon. Yes, there is so much unhinging crap that we have to wade through every day. And most of us have very little control over important things that affect our safety and well-being, so it makes sense that uncertainty brings up worry and anxiety.

So, how could mindfulness hope to solve this? Good question!

For decades, all my plans to drink more water, practice the piano, and regularly go to the gym never seemed to gain traction. But I’ve kept returning to a mindfulness practice, even though it can be irritating to become more aware, like when I realize I’m judging, or that I just want to keep judging or gossiping, or stay impatient or demanding. It’s easier, often, just to carry on with the slime.

It can be irritating to become more aware, like when I realize I’m judging, or that I just want to keep judging or gossiping, or stay impatient or demanding.

But then, much to the chagrin of that part of me that just wants to scream into people’s faces, years of practicing mindfulness cause me to stop and look at whatever strong thoughts and emotions I’m laying myself open to and ask this question: “Will leaning into hate and divisiveness take me closer to, or further from, who I really want to be?”

Not to mention that rage-venting also does NOT make those bad feelings go away. Actually, it usually makes them worse. The ragey-ness supports a dark narrative, blocks nourishing positive interactions, and cuts me off from more complicated emotions, the ones I could see, welcome, and learn from if I had enough calm and presence.

So, a mindfulness question: What meaning arises when we show up with loving awareness…no matter what’s happening?

My mother lies dying in front of me. There are no words left to exchange. I hold her hand quietly for hour upon hour. She does not appear to be conscious, but I figure, you never know. I hold her close, no cell phone scrolling, just loving presence. I hope this might help her feel that she is safe and surrounded by caring love…wherever she is.

I have a thought: I am ferrying her from this side to the other. And I don’t want her to be afraid, since nothing can stop her from going. Every once in a while, I tell her that everything is OK, and that she can go anytime she needs to.

At the end, at her end, there was nothing to do. There was only loving awareness. “Hello, Mom. Fran. Frances. You’re not alone. Do you feel my hand? Love is here with you. And, we are taking that ferry together, as far as I am allowed to go.”

I could not stop my mother from dying. Not only that, I’ve not been able to stop the climate crisis, or prevent so much gut-wrenching harm.

Sometimes loving awareness might mean finding your voice, or your courage, and taking action against harms. Sometimes it might come down to bravely bearing witness to others’ suffering. Mindfulness might not change an outcome, but it can reduce suffering by increasing loving connection.

In fact, in my experience, that is a superpower.

You might meet people who practice mindfulness and still aren’t very nice. Well, mindfulness isn’t necessarily going to immediately make any of us a nicer or more loving or less judgy person. That requires choosing to take the actions that loving awareness illuminates, over and over again.

Practicing mindfulness can help us step back and hold ourselves in loving awareness before we lose our cool. And by using a mindful approach, we can learn to break habits like viewing everything and everyone as the enemy. We can learn how to intentionally engage with our vagus nerve to help regulate the body’s stress response. It can be as ordinary as extending your out-breath, a simple technique for helping the body stabilize.

When we are already feeling rocky, automatic thoughts and fears often trigger a threat response, appropriate when there’s danger to escape from. But without mindful awareness, we risk remaining caught in that response long after it’s helpful. When we get stuck in a dark narrative, when we don’t accept the invitation that loving awareness offers us, we can end up unintentionally zapping ourselves with adrenaline and cortisol, amping up our stress over and over, which you may have noticed is very depleting.

When we practice loving awareness, we are inviting ourselves to notice whatever shows up, without judgment. This might include how unpleasant it can feel to sit in so-called silence. This means having space for all the wildness, and choosing to hold it all with love. That’s the power of loving awareness.
