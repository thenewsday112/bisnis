#1045. Netanyahu’s party retains lead with 97.3% of ballots counted — election committee

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

TEL AVIV, October 29. /TASS/. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud party remains in the lead following the October 27 parliamentary election, with 97.3% of ballots counted, according to preliminary figures released by the Central Elections Committee (CEC).

Likud is projected to win 27 seats in the 120-member parliament, followed by Yashar, led by Netanyahu’s main opposition rival Gadi Eisenkot, with 21.

The bloc comprising Likud and the far-right and Orthodox parties that support Netanyahu and make up the current governing coalition also retains its lead. It is projected to secure 53 seats, compared with 48 for the opposition. Arab parties are set to win 19 seats, although neither Likud nor the opposition is expected to be willing to form a coalition with them.

The CEC is expected to complete the vote count by the end of October 29 and publish the final results no later than November 4. Once the results are available, President Isaac Herzog will begin consultations with representatives of the parties elected to the Knesset before tasking one of its members with forming a new governing coalition.

A coalition needs at least 61 Knesset seats to form a government, a threshold no political bloc has yet reached. The leader of the party with the most seats usually becomes the candidate for prime minister. However, the president may also task a politician whose party did not win the most votes with forming a government if that politician can assemble a coalition.

The first candidate will have 28 days to form a government, with a possible two-week extension. If that attempt fails, the president will give another lawmaker two weeks to form a coalition, without the possibility of an extension.

If no lawmaker succeeds in forming a stable coalition, new elections will be called after 90 days. Netanyahu will continue to lead Israel’s government until a new cabinet is sworn in.

Spending on welfare is set to cost more than 11% of the UK’s economic output in 2026.

LONDON – British finance minister John Healey said the government would reduce spending on welfare benefits as he sought to address one of the big public spending challenges that remain after his first budget announcement.

“We have to bring the benefits bill down. You don’t do that by crude cuts,” he said in an interview with the BBC.

Healey made no commitment on when welfare spending would fall when asked whether it would happen before the next election, which is due no later than mid-2029.

Speaking later to reporters, Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the government was “working flat out” to get the welfare bill down as quickly as possible.

Healey’s tax and spending plans offered some short-term support for households hit by the surge in energy prices caused by the US-Iran war, financed partly by higher taxes on banks, savers and landlords.

But the budget did not address long-term questions such as how to raise defence spending to 3% of economic output, offset inflation’s impact on public spending and tackle the growing welfare bill, which includes pensions as well as support for people deemed too ill to work.

Spending on welfare is set to cost more than 11% of economic output in 2026.

The opposition Conservative Party and some bond investors say that reveals a lack of will in Burnham’s Labour Party to lower borrowing.

However, markets reacted broadly favourably to Healey’s budget and 10-year government borrowing costs touched a one-month low on Oct 29.

Healey focused on the problem of unemployment among young people.

“This system is unsustainable,” he said. “I feel a moral duty to deal with these youngsters (who) have been written off.”

Healey said the government would help young people out of work who would be required to take opportunities on offer.

Eduin Latimer, senior research economist at the Institute for Fiscal Studies think tank, said that approach might save money “but it’s much more uncertain than just directly cutting entitlements or eligibility and it’s likely to give you savings over a longer timeframe, if you do deliver savings”.

A government review into young people out of employment, education or vocational training and another into the disability benefits system are due to be published in November.

Healey drew criticism from the left-leaning Resolution Foundation think tank for spreading too thinly his flagship spending measure – a £100 (S$170) reduction in energy bills for 20 million households to offset some of the sharp increase in bills caused by the US-Iran conflict.

“Even those families receiving help (will be) facing higher energy bills than they did during the last energy price shock,” its chief executive Ruth Curtice said.

Typical energy bills this financial year are set to rise to £1,750 a year after government support, 4% higher than after Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine, the think tank said. REUTERS

The project involves developing the Nyinahini deposit in the Ashanti Region in the southwest of the country, with resources estimated at more than 100 million metric tons of bauxites, Rusal said. Children under 16 will be blocked from using the biggest social media sites next year, but experts are concerned some young people will be able to bypass the restrictions.

More than 55 million people use social media in the UK and the regulator Ofcom has shared the best methods of identifying which users are under 16.

The ban is due to come into force in March 2027 and will stop children from accessing the ten biggest platforms including TikTok, Instagram and Snapchat.

A similar ban was introduced in Australia last year, but children have managed to find their way around the rules and download social media apps.

However, checking social media users' age can prove challenging because many 16 and 17-year-olds do not have credit cards, driving licences or passports — it can also be hard to verify age based on appearance.

That’s why Ofcom has recommended some tougher measures to identify which users are under age.

The regulator has suggested four methods that could be “highly effective” at age checking. These are:

Ofcom explained credit card checks, mobile network operator checks and email estimation are only effective for over 18s. It added that self declaration is not considered “highly effective” at any age.

Many adults are likely to have to confirm they are not under-age, but it’s not yet known how this will be enforced.

Ofcom said there would be a "large number of accounts" that social media companies would need to deal with, according to the BBC.

It’s “inevitable” some under-16s will get around the checks, Ofcom has warned.

The regulator added that social media firms could reduce the risk by making it easier to report suspected underage accounts or by using technology that estimates a user’s age from their online behaviour.

It also stressed some facial recognition technology could be inaccurate by several years, meaning some users would need to undergo a second check.

Ofcom chief executive Dame Melanie Dawes said: “This ban will shape the future of children’s lives online so it’s important to get it right.

“It can work if social media firms do highly effective age checks properly, and we’ve set out ways that could be achieved.

She added: “We should expect that motivated children will seek to bypass the restrictions – just like alcohol laws can’t completely eradicate underage drinking – but there’ll be ways for platforms to mitigate this risk.”

The regulator said it was developing draft guidance for social media firms that would open for consultation when the Government’s plans come before Parliament.

The government’s ban follows a global change in attitude towards social media companies prompted by Australia’s own social media ban for under-16s in December last year. Countries such as Spain, Greece, Portugal and Denmark have all since decided to look at changing the age at which they can access social media.

A consultation for a social media ban, which ended on 26 May, was declared one of the biggest national conversations, with more than 116,000 responses submitted by parents, children and experts across the country.

The responses showed overwhelming public backing for tougher action. Not only did 90 per cent of parents support a ban, but the government also claimed two-thirds of young people agreed that children below 16 should not be allowed to use at least some social media platforms.

Finnish ⁠police have launched a criminal investigation into possible breaches ⁠of the country’s environmental ⁠laws ​at the site of a planned Google datacentre in rural northern Finland.

Authorities allege that Tuike Finland, a Google subsidiary, cleared and drained 330 hectares (815 acres) of forest at the planned site in Muhos before environmental impact assessments required by Finnish law had been completed.

Like other Nordic countries, Finland is attractive to tech companies seeking to build datacentres because of its relative geopolitical stability and abundance of land, but also because its cool climate should allow them to cut energy costs.

“Based on preliminary information, there have been ambiguities and ‌shortcomings in the drainage and water management at the construction site of Tuike Finland ​Oy’s datacentre project,” Finnish police said in a statement on Thursday.

“The criminal investigation will examine, among other things, circumstances related to these issues,” they added.

Finnish authorities are considering applications for a total of 145 new datacentres at various stages of planning, including a €13bn (£11bn) artificial intelligence infrastructure project by Google that the US company has called its largest ‌investment in Europe.

Earlier this month Finnish authorities ordered Google to suspend work at two sites – Muhos and Otanmäki, about 100km to the south-east – after the company failed to complete environmental impact assessments before clearcutting the land.

The Supervisory ⁠Agency, which oversees environmental permits and assessments, told Google to halt “preparatory ​measures altering the environment”. Campaigners estimate 250,000 trees have been felled at Muhos and 150,000 at Otanmäki.

High street discounter Poundland has filed to appoint administrators, threatening the future of around 12,000 workers.

Court filings show that the retailer has filed a notice of intention to appoint administrators on Thursday afternoon, which will give the business 10 working days of breathing space from creditors to secure a potential rescue deal.

It is understood advisers are still in talks with parties interested in buying the brand and swathes of stores.

The move means around 12,000 jobs are at risk. Poundland currently runs around 600 stores across the UK.

Poundland managing director Barry Williams told staff in a memo that the situation is “unexpected, unwanted and wholly avoidable”.

Following the filing, Mr Williams said: “Over the past two months, initially prompted by interest from several parties, we have been working on a formal sale process for Poundland.

“Despite enormous efforts it has not been possible to identify an acceptable offer for the business as a whole.

“The process has however attracted several established retail operators who have made formal offers, collectively these cover a significant part of the business, and we believe offer the best available prospects for shareholders, creditors and to preserve jobs.

“We recognise any sale process creates uncertainty and particularly when set against a challenging UK economic environment.

“We thank all stakeholders, and in particular our people for their support during this period.In order to bring stability, we have today filed a Notice Of Intention (NOI) to appoint representatives of AlixPartners as Administrators to the business.

“The filing of the NOI creates an immediate ten working day legal moratorium during which we will be working closely with our advisors to identify and deliver the best possible outcome.

“Our stores remain open as normal and we thank our staff, suppliers and customers for their continued support.”

The discount chain is due to enter insolvency after owner Gordon Brothers rejected a takeover offer from Irish real estate firm Martin Property Group, which management named as their preferred bidder earlier this month.

Gordon Brothers – which only bought Poundland last year for £1 – launched a sale process for the retailer at the start of September.

Wilko and The Range owner CDS Superstores, Poundstretcher owner Fortress and TG Jones owner Modella are all understood to be interested in buying parts of the Poundland business.

It is understood the retailer has appointed advisers at AlixPartners to oversee the insolvency process and assess offers to buy all or parts of the business.

Gordon Brothers had overseen a major turnaround programme at Poundland that included closing more than 100 shops over the past year in an effort to shore up its finances.
