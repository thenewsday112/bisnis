#4317. NYT Connections hints today: Clues, answers for October 28, 2026

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The NYT Connections puzzle today is not too difficult if you've got butterflies.

Connections is the one of the most popular New York Times word games that's captured the public's attention. The game is all about finding the "common threads between words." And just like Wordle, Connections resets after midnight and each new set of words gets trickier and trickier—so we've served up some hints and tips to get you over the hurdle.

If you just want to be told today's puzzle, you can jump to the end of this article for today's Connections solution. But if you'd rather solve it yourself, keep reading for some clues, tips, and strategies to assist you.

The NYT's latest daily word game has become a social media hit. The Times credits associate puzzle editor Wyna Liu with helping to create the new word game and bringing it to the publications' Games section. Connections can be played on both web browsers and mobile devices and require players to group four words that share something in common.

Each puzzle features 16 words and each grouping of words is split into four categories. These sets could comprise of anything from book titles, software, country names, etc. Even though multiple words will seem like they fit together, there's only one correct answer.

If a player gets all four words in a set correct, those words are removed from the board. Guess wrong and it counts as a mistake—players get up to four mistakes until the game ends.

Players can also rearrange and shuffle the board to make spotting connections easier. Additionally, each group is color-coded with yellow being the easiest, followed by green, blue, and purple. Like Wordle, you can share the results with your friends on social media.

#1795. YouTube calls on the court of BookTubers (including Haley Pham) to celebrate the latest ACOTAR release

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The fae have found a new court on YouTube.

The latest installment of Sarah J. Maas‘ bestselling romantasy series A Court of Thorns and Roses dropped today, and YouTube appears to have partnered with publisher Bloomsbury to muster BookTube for the release.

“YouTube is here to help fans celebrate the release with special ACOTAR experiences on YouTube, new content from your favorite creators, and a custom Shorts effect that reveals which Court you’d be a part of,” Samantha Rose, who’s listed as a contributor for YouTube’s official blog, wrote in an announcement post.

For those unfamiliar, ACOTAR has been a BookTube (and BookTok) darling for years. The series follows 19-year-old Feyre Archeron, who, after killing a faerie wolf out of desperation during a hard winter, finds herself in peril with the fae royal court.

A Court of Splintered Harmony is the series’ sixth book, and delves deeper into existential threats faced by human and fae alike.

YouTube’s bid for readers’ attention includes bringing YouTuber/author Haley Pham (who, btw, is married to Ryan Trahan) on for an episode of its in-house creator profile series Channeling.

Pham famously churned through the first five ACOTAR books in just one week, chronicling the marathon in a BookTube vlog. Now her episode of Channeling peeks into her own author career, taking viewers to a fantasy-inspired photoshoot that has Pham looking court-ready in chainmail.

YouTube’s also getting a little BuzzFeed with a quiz that sorts viewers into their destined fae court. Instead of the usual landing-page, multiple-choice-answer format, though, YouTube makes people open Shorts and create a video to get their results.

Taking the quiz means opening Shorts on mobile, hitting the ‘Create’ button, and using an effect called “ACOTAR Quiz.” Then you have to start recording while you answer the quiz’s questions–and finally, you’ll be sorted.

It doesn’t seem like people have to post their quiz-taking Short, but the effect is definitely geared for sharing. We’re curious to track its results, and plan to reach out to YouTube for any resulting data.

Other ACOTAR x YouTube touches include a special Starfall progress bar icon on Maas-related videos (you can see it in Pham’s Channeling episode) and, more tangentially, an animated, themed background for Google Search.

As for why YouTube’s plucking this particular page, it cited data from NRG that showed 69% of 14-to-44-year-olds in the U.S. watched videos from “a niche community or online subculture” in the last 12 months.

“YouTube offers a unique home for communities–such as BookTube–to connect and dive deep on their interests,” Rose wrote.

We’re not sure the legion of ACOTAR fans constitute a “niche community” anymore, but either way, YouTube is clearly in good with the fae.

#4332. AI slows growth for Infosys and keeps it afloat at the same time

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Predicts slower growth as savings from automation hit price of extended deals

Indian tech services giant Infosys has mixed news about AI.

In its most recent quarterly results late last week, the company revealed that it’s racked up 29 percent sequential growth in AI revenue, which now delivers 10.5 percent of Infosys’ incoming cash.

Those are far nicer numbers than the 1.5 percent year-on-year revenue growth Infosys reported for its second quarter.

“Growth was below our expectations. Volume was softer, and price increases were lower than anticipated amid high competitive intensity,” chief financial officer Jayesh Sanghrajka told investors. One reason for lower price increases was that customers expect Infosys to charge less when it uses AI, and those cost savings are now “baked in” to its multi-year deals.

CEO and managing director Salil Parekh also pointed to AI as one reason the cost of services is falling.

“I think what we see today is there is definitely some of our peers who are putting, I would say, more intense effort into the pricing pressure,” he said, “but it is also across the board because of the environment and also the discussion on AI productivity. If you are talking with clients today on large programs, it is a three or five-year outlook.”

That leaves Infosys dependent on new work from AI to boost revenue growth, while AI also makes it hard to keep growing revenue from existing customers.

Infosys won’t go low to win deals and intends to hold its margins at around 21 percent, Sanghrajka said. He sought to prove that point by citing “a large deal that we walked out of, or we did not pursue because it did not meet our return expectations.”

To help preserve margins, the company is conducting “Project Maximus” to find efficiencies within its own operations. AI-powered tools to win more sales are also in the mix.

The company sees difficult months ahead. Sanghrajka said the retail and high-tech industries have slowed their services spending and acknowledged that the second half of Infosys’s financial year is usually its slow period.

The company tried to convince investors that they have little to worry about, pointing to $8.8 billion of new deals won in the first half of its financial year – 68 percent of which were net new customers – as a sign that Infosys’ fundamentals are sound.

But the company also offered less optimistic guidance about its future. In its Q1 results, Infosys predicted full-year revenue growth of 1.5 to 3.0 percent. In Q2, it revised that prediction to 1.0 to 2.0 percent.

Infosys’s share price didn’t dive after that news but is already down over 25 percent in the last twelve months. And Bloomberg reports that investment analysts have downgraded the company, suggesting they feel its prospects are poor. ®
