#3644. Roca releases innovative all-in-one Avant toilet

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Spanish bathroom brand Roca has rethought conventional toilet design with Avant, integrating the cistern within the toilet itself.

For the Avant toilet, Roca designed the cistern to sit within the bowl, removing the external cistern on its floor-standing model and the concealed cistern and flush plate on its wall-hung version.

Avant’s cistern uses flushing technology based on the Venturi effect. Here, fluid pressure drops when liquid is funnelled from a larger pipe section to a smaller one.

This creates a powerful suction in the tank, which is needed for the flush. As the components are concealed within the wall, this gives Avant a more minimalist appearance but also offers benefits in space, aesthetics, installation and maintenance.

The integrated aesthetic also makes bathrooms more adaptable and practical for different design needs and interiors.

“The removal of an external cistern or the flush plate further enhances the overall aesthetic of the bathroom, and its neutral, minimalist design blends seamlessly into any bathroom style, adding a touch of modernity,” said Roca.

Another innovative element is that the Avant is also powered without electricity, but if water pressure or flow is insufficient in a building, the Avant-E model is best suited – an electric version suitable for a wider range of plumbing systems.

The Avant also features dual-flush options, side-mounted flush buttons, a soft-close seat and cover and Roca Rimless Vortex. According to Roca, it meets the highest hygiene standards and operates with splash-free, low-consumption flushing, reducing water consumption.

The toilet is also covered in Supraglaze, a custom finish that mitigates the build-up of dirt, germs and residue, making cleaning simple and further increasing hygiene.

The result is an efficient, minimalist toilet ideal for compact spaces, easy to install and maintain.

“Avant not only brings a modern aesthetic to the bathroom, but it also reduces flush noise, providing a quieter, more comfortable experience than conventional toilets,” said Roca.

Founded in Spain in 1917, Roca now operates in 170 countries, with a special focus on sustainability and craftsmanship.

The company produces high-end, design-forward bathroom appliances, including everything from toilets to sinks, hardware and faucets.

It recently released modular bathroom furniture that can be adjusted to fit different scales and colourful taps in collaboration with designer Inma Bermúdez, in bright green, blue and yellow finishes.

#1953. B2B Buying Decisions Are Moving Where Ad Tech Can’t See

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The ad tech industry built the most precise measurement infrastructure in the history of media. Every impression counted. Every click tracked. Every conversion attributed. The ability to measure what happened after an ad was served, across devices, across platforms, across sessions, became the industry’s defining capability and its core value proposition.

But that infrastructure doesn’t measure influence. And as B2B buying behavior shifts toward channels that ad tech can’t see (AI-assisted research, private peer conversations, dark social sharing), the gap between what measurement captures and where decisions actually happen is becoming the industry’s most consequential blind spot.

The B2B buyer who sees a display ad, clicks through, visits a landing page and fills out a form is fully measured. Every touch point is tracked, timestamped and attributed.

What about the B2B buyer who asks an AI assistant for a vendor recommendation, receives an answer that doesn’t mention your brand, forms a shortlist without visiting any website and eventually fills out a competitor’s form? That buyer is invisible to every measurement system the industry has built.

There’s also the B2B buyer who receives a forwarded email from a colleague (“This is the company I mentioned”), clicks through from that personal message, visits the site and converts. The measurement system sees a direct visit with no campaign attribution. The reality is that the most influential touch point, the peer recommendation, happened in a channel no ad tech platform can observe.

Each of these scenarios represents a real buying decision. And the proportion of buying decisions happening in invisible channels is growing, while the proportion happening in measurable channels is shrinking.

Attribution can only credit what it can see. If a significant portion of the buyer’s journey happens in channels attribution can’t track (AI conversations, private messages, dark social, peer recommendations), the model is distributing full credit across a partial journey.

The visible touch points get overcredited. The invisible touch points get zero credit. The resulting picture of “what’s working” is systematically biased toward the measurable channels regardless of whether they’re the influential ones.

This bias compounds over time and shapes the budget.

If attribution measures the visible journey, incrementality measures the actual impact: the difference between what happened with the marketing activity and what would have happened without it.

Incrementality testing asks the question attribution can’t: Did this activity change behavior, or did it reach someone who was going to convert anyway? A campaign that reaches 1,000 buyers who were already in-market and attributes 50 conversions looks strong in the attribution model. An incrementality test might reveal that 45 of those 50 would have converted without the campaign, meaning the actual contribution was five conversions, not 50.

The ad tech industry has the infrastructure to run incrementality tests (holdout groups, geographic splits, matched market analysis). The tools exist. The industrywide commitment to using them as the primary measure doesn’t, because incrementality almost always produces lower numbers than attribution, and lower numbers are harder to report.

The problem is this: The incentive structure rewards the metric that makes performance look best, rather than the metric that reflects reality.

The measurement gap is about to significantly grow. AI is inserting itself into the B2B buying process in ways that make traditional measurement increasingly incomplete.

As AI purchasing agents begin evaluating B2B vendors, the top of the funnel moves into a channel that current measurement can’t capture. The industry that built its value proposition on measurability is facing a future where the most consequential buyer interactions are unmeasurable by the infrastructure that exists today.

The buy side needs a measurement paradigm built around decisions rather than impressions.

The industry that solved measurement for impressions needs to solve it for decisions. That’s the harder problem. It’s also the only one that matters, as the buyer’s journey moves beyond the reach of the infrastructure built to measure it.

#2920. Beware, A Disability Can Force You Into Early Retirement

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Earlier this year, I was sick for two weeks. Unfortunately, I’ve had asthma since I was a kid. So when a respiratory illness hits, I tend to cough and suffer longer than the average person.

During COVID, my asthma flared up more often, likely due to the stress of having a baby during a pandemic.

It’s the type of breathing where you’re annoyingly conscious of every breath. You can never quite fill your lungs to 100%. In my case, it felt more like 75%.

On Wednesday, August 12, still sick, I decided to keep a previously scheduled consulting call with an FS reader. I had booked it assuming I’d be better by then. I wasn’t.

By the end of our 1 hour, 15 minute session, where I spent about 80% of the time talking, I was wiped out. My energy had dropped to maybe 30%. When you’re talking nonstop, you’re not breathing deeply or regularly. I felt like I was slowly suffocating while discussing their asset allocation.

The call reminded me of the many days I felt physically drained talking to clients during my 13 years in finance. As my responsibilities and stress grew, I eventually hit a breaking point. I’d rather earn nothing and be free and healthy than keep grinding myself down.

Just the other day, I couldn’t bend down to pick something up without a sharp pain shooting through my spine. I almost collapsed.

The last time I felt pain this bad was in 2023. I had spent an hour sitting on the ground signing copies of Buy This Not That for readers who donated to victims of the Maui fires. My dad is from Hawaii, and seeing thousands of lives upended overnight weighed on me. The Financial Samurai community ended up donating about $48,000. But when the signing was over, I couldn’t stand up and I called my wife for help.

My theory is that these episodes are triggered by a combination of prolonged stress and me forgetting I’m not 25 anymore.

This time, I clearly forgot. On Sunday, I played two hours of 4.5-level pickleball, then taught my kids for another hour. On Monday, I played 1.5 hours of intense tennis with plenty of serving. Then on Tuesday, several dads in our dad bod challenge shared their gym Personal Records, so naturally I went for personal records too. Instead of my usual three sets of 10-12 reps, I did five sets with progressive loading.

Wednesday morning, my back was already grumbling. So of course I went on a hilly 30-minute walk at 7:05am before breakfast and the school drop off. Walking downhill to school, the pain returned. By the time I got home, I could no longer walk normally.

If I still had a day job, there’s no way I could have gone into the office. And if my asthma flared up at the same time, I couldn’t speak to clients for more than an hour or two a day.

Not being able to provide for my family would be a terrible thing.

These two episodes forced me to confront a few uncomfortable truths:

Some people picture disability as a dramatic car accident or a fall off a ladder. Reality is far more boring. Accidental injuries make up only about 9% to 12% of long-term disability claims, while musculoskeletal problems like back and joint pain are the leading cause, at 26% to 33% of claims.

According to the Social Security Administration (SSA), roughly one in four 20-year-olds will be out of work for at least a year because of a disabling condition before reaching normal retirement age. That’s one quarter of all 20-year-olds, not just those in high-risk occupations.

In other words, the thing that takes you out of the workforce probably won’t be a skydiving mishap. It’ll be your back after one too many weightlifting attempts.

Before getting into what to do, it’s worth understanding the two main types of disability coverage. They work very differently.

Short-term disability (STD) covers you for a temporary inability to work, like recovering from surgery, a bad back, or a serious illness. Benefits typically replace around 50% to 70% of your income and last anywhere from a few weeks to about six months. The waiting period, called the elimination period, is usually short, often one to two weeks.

Many employers provide short-term disability as a benefit. A handful of states also run their own programs, including California (SDI), New York, New Jersey, Rhode Island, and Hawaii (TDI). If you’re self-employed, you probably have nothing unless you bought it yourself.

Long-term disability (LTD) kicks in when a disability lasts longer, usually after a 90-day elimination period. It typically replaces about 60% of your income and can pay for a set number of years or until age 65. This is the coverage that actually protects your family’s financial future. Short-term disability is a speed bump. Long-term disability is a car crash.

And no, don’t count on Social Security Disability Insurance to save you. SSDI is hard to qualify for, has a five-month waiting period, and the monthly benefit is modest. It’s a floor, not a plan.

If you have a job with benefits, log into your HR portal today and look at your LTD coverage. Many group policies cap monthly benefits, which means high earners may only be replacing a fraction of their income.

If there’s a gap, buy a supplemental individual policy, ideally with an own-occupation rider. It’s portable, so it stays with you if you leave your job. If you’re self-employed like me, individual LTD is the only coverage you’ll have. Get quotes while you’re young and healthy, because pre-existing conditions get excluded or priced up later.

Even with great coverage, there’s a gap. Short-term disability might make you wait a week or two, and long-term disability usually makes you wait 90 days. Meanwhile, your mortgage, tuition, and Costco runs don’t pause.

Keep at least six months of living expenses in cash or Treasury bills. If you have kids, a single income household, or are self-employed, aim for 12 months. If you’re wondering where the line is between prudent and paranoid, here’s my take on how much savings is too much. The emergency fund is your bridge from healthy to covered.

A single income stream is a single point of failure. If that engine stalls, the whole plane goes down. Your goal is to build a jet with at least four engines, so you can lose one and still land safely.

The best time to start a side hustle is while you still have a steady paycheck and good health. You get to experiment without pressure, and if it fails, you still eat.

I started Financial Samurai in July 2009 while working full-time in finance. I’d wake up early and write before work, then publish again on weekends. For three years, it made very little money. But by the time I left Corporate America in 2012, the site had become a real second engine alongside about $80,000 a year in passive investment income.

Without that second engine, I never would have had the courage to negotiate my way out.

Here’s the key for disability planning: prioritize side hustles that don’t require your body to show up. Driving for Uber or coaching tennis stops the moment your back gives out. Writing, a blog, a YouTube channel, an online course, or a self-published ebook can keep paying while you’re flat on your back.

If you need ideas, my wife put together a list of the 20 best side hustle ideas to make extra money. If you want to go the online route, here’s how to start a blog and build your own platform. I’m biased, but owning your own corner of the internet is the gift that keeps on giving.

One engine is a job. Two engines is a cushion. Four or more engines is freedom.

Your peak earning years are also, conveniently, your peak health years. Don’t assume your income will keep climbing forever. Assume it could drop to zero tomorrow, then save as if that’s true.

I saved over 50% of my after-tax income starting with my first job out of college because I knew I couldn’t last a lifetime working 70+ hours a week. I didn’t know my lungs and back would eventually join the protest, but the logic held up.

Front-load your savings. Max out your 401(k) and IRA, and invest the excess in a taxable brokerage account. Pay down high fixed costs where you can.

The lower your required monthly expenses, the less income replacement you need if disaster strikes. A paid-off house is one of the best disability insurance policy nobody talks about.

Your ability to earn over the next 20 to 30 years is likely worth millions. Yet we manage our portfolios more carefully than our bodies.

Get annual checkups. But lock down a life insurance policy before you see a doctor for something more. Manage your stress before it manages you. Warm up, cool down, and stop trying to beat the other dads’ PRs after a weekend of nonstop racquet sports. Ask me how I know.

While you’re at it, get your paperwork in order. A will, a durable power of attorney, and a healthcare directive are the basics. But if anyone depends on your income, term life insurance is the most important piece. A disability takes away your ability to earn for a while. Death takes it away for good.

The irony is that the health problems that make you worry about disability also make life insurance more expensive, or harder to get. Lock in coverage while you’re still healthy enough to qualify. The scary thing about disability is that it rarely announces itself. One day you’re serving aces and going for gym PRs. The next, you can’t walk your kids to school.

Being unable to breathe normally or walk for a few days made me realize how fragile everything is. If you’re the provider for your family, that fragility is your responsibility to plan for.

So check your disability coverage this week. Build your cash buffer. Start a side hustle that doesn’t depend on your body showing up. And for goodness sake, stretch.

As soon as late summer edges toward fall in New York, I begin to lick my pawpaw-loving lips. As this native North American fruit gains mainstream recognition it has become easier to source both the fresh fruit as well as pawpaw saplings. Despite the rising tide of awareness, many people are still unfamiliar with them and have yet to taste a fresh pawpaw. And if you have lusted after the perfumed fruit but have not met one, there are few things about these green-mango lookalikes you may not know.

The pawpaw under discussion is the native North American fruit belonging to the genus Asimina. The cold-hardy common pawpaw is A. triloba, but there are over a dozen other species, with regional distributions that cleave to the eastern and Mid-Western parts of the United States, from southern Canada down to Florida.

In regions and countries with Commonwealth and British colonial histories (think Australia, England, India, South Africa, the West Indies, and more) the common name “pawpaw” refers to what anyone from the Americas calls papaya. Soft-skinned and subtropical, small or football-sized, that pawpaw, Carica papaya, has yellow to deep rose flesh and is filled in the center with little black, peppery seeds. It is available in many grocery stores, year-round.

The botanical genus name Asimina is derived from the Shawnee word for pawpaw: ha’simini. For the Shawnee, September is the month of the pawpaw moon, ha’siminikiisfwa, according to Joel Barnes, Archives Director for the Shawnee Tribe (via a West Virginia Public Radio interview in a 2020).

Until the 19th century, before they were forcibly removed by the US government, the Shawnee’s ancestral lands were in pawpaw-rich Appalachia, including current southeast Ohio.

The three-day Ohio Pawpaw Festival in southeast Ohio in September is a celebration of everything pawpaw. There are pawpaw tastings, and dozens of pawpaw dishes to choose from. Chris Chmiel is one of the founders of the festival and the co-owner (with Michelle Gorman, his wife) of Integration Acres, which has sold the fresh fruit and frozen purée, and other native wild things to the public since 1996. (The first pawpaws I tasted were shipped from Ohio’s woodlands to my Brooklyn front door in 2016.)

On 18 September, 1806, William Clark wrote, in the detailed journal that Thomas Jefferson dictated the expedition keep, that “…our party entirely out of provisions Subsisting on poppaws…the party appear perfectly contented and tell us that they can live very well on the pappaws [sic].”

There are many similar entries (reading the journal is equal parts recoil and wonder; but it is a compelling chronicle of plants encountered).

The aroma of pawpaws is strong—they will perfume a room. Their texture when ripe is creamy. Different cultivars vary in flavor: Some pawpaws taste like a kaleidoscope of passionfruit and pineapple, lychee, and mango. Many tasters mention banana; personally, I detect none, but perhaps it is the pawpaw’s soft consistency that inspires the comparison. Others taste like caramel, lightly scorched. Think crème brûlée, with tropical overtones. A fruit may taste intense, or mild. The little slippery sac around each large seed is slightly bitter, as is the flesh closest to the skin.

Unless they are very close to ripe on the branch, pawpaws do not ripen well once picked (they are not like avocados). Visiting your local tree/s after a windstorm or heavy rain is a great way to find windfalls. Their skin may bruise or discolor, but this seldom means that the fruit itself is damaged. Rather, it’s a sign of ripeness.

Pawpaws are  close cousins of custard apple (cherimoya) and soursops. All these large-seeded, soft-fleshed, deeply aromatic fruit belong to the family Annonaceae. The pawpaw is the cold-hardiest among them.

There’s no reason why you would, but don’t chew up and swallow pawpaw seeds. Eating the skin has the same effect.

While the evidence is anecdotal, there is enough chatter in online forums to suggest that some people react badly to eating pawpaws. The result is dramatic diarrhea and, sometimes, nausea. Eat pawpaws in moderation (I cut myself off at three in one sitting. Is that moderate?). Also, some pawpaw purveyors, like Integration Acres, recommend against drying the pulp for use in fruit leather, since it can cause gastrointestinal distress. The reasons for this are not understood (yet).

Some researchers posit that eating pawpaws regularly could be detrimental to the eater. The seeds and pulp of pawpaws contain acetogenins, including annonacin, which is toxic to cortical neurons. Could regular pawpaw ingestion cause neurotoxins to build up over time? A connection has been drawn between pawpaws and their cousins, custard apples, which are popular in the Caribbean, where clusters of Parkinson’s in Guadalupe have been documented, possibly linked to a diet where the custard apples feature regularly. (Annonacin was also thought to play a role in cancer-cell death. That published paper has since been retracted.)

The common pawpaw is hardy from USDA growing zones 5 through 8 (according to Cornell).  It’s the tropical-tasting fruit many cool-climate gardeners can grow. Bradley Farms in New Paltz, New York (zone 5), brings pawpaws to greenmarkets in October. While pawpaws in the wild tend to grow in clonal colonies in the woods and on woodland edges, pawpaw trees will thrive in full sun as long as they have ample moisture in summer. They like hot, humid summers and cold winters.

Sowing pawpaw seed is most successful when the seeds are planted before they dry out. Even three days of drying can reduce germination rates to 20%, according to the University of Kentucky. (If you can’t plant them at once, keep them in a baggy with potting soil until you can). Sowing them in fall (right after you eat them) allows them to cold-stratify naturally, outdoors.

If you’re mosying around a pawpaw patch and are tempted to remove a sapling, think twice:  Pawpaws have long taproots and respond poorly to being yanked out.  Unless you have emerald-green thumbs, either grow them from seed or find a nursery that sells pawpaw trees. More and more do.

#519. A simple liquid can stop cavities without drilling, major study confirms

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A cavity doesn't always have to mean drilling, injections, or an uncomfortable visit to the dentist. A major clinical trial has confirmed that a simple, inexpensive liquid can stop tooth decay in young children, potentially preventing painful infections and the need for dental surgery.

The treatment, called silver diamine fluoride (SDF), takes only seconds to apply to each affected tooth. Rather than removing damaged tooth material and placing a filling, a dentist uses a tiny sponge tipped applicator to paint the liquid directly onto the cavity. The process requires no drilling, injections, or sedation.

The findings could have enormous implications for childhood dental care. Every year, thousands of young children in the United States visit emergency departments because of untreated cavities. Emergency physicians generally cannot provide the dental procedures needed to address the underlying problem, leaving some children with ongoing pain and infections. Others ultimately need dental surgery under general anesthesia, which makes them completely unconscious during the procedure.

Now, researchers led by the University of Michigan have produced compelling evidence that a much simpler approach can stop many cavities from getting worse.

Major Clinical Trial Confirms Silver Diamine Fluoride Works

Published in JAMA Pediatrics, the Phase III clinical trial included 830 children younger than 6. Participants were recruited through dental clinics, pediatric medical practices, and Head Start and Early Head Start programs in Michigan, New York, and Iowa.

Head Start and Early Head Start are federally supported programs that provide early childhood education and related services to eligible families.

The results showed that a solution containing 38% silver diamine fluoride successfully stopped decay in more than half of the affected baby teeth when treatment was provided at six month intervals.

Dentists refer to this as arresting tooth decay, meaning the damaged area stops deteriorating. Unlike a traditional filling, the treatment does not replace lost tooth structure. Instead, it helps prevent existing decay from progressing.

SDF works through the combined effects of silver and fluoride. Silver helps combat bacteria associated with tooth decay, while fluoride helps strengthen the mineral structure of teeth and resist further damage.

"This is a very effective and safe treatment -- even in children as young as 1," said Margherita Fontana, professor of dentistry at the University of Michigan School of Dentistry and the study's lead investigator.

A Simple Treatment Could Help Millions Avoid Dental Pain

Tooth decay is the most common chronic disease among children, affecting more than 40% of American children. Although cavities may initially cause few noticeable symptoms, untreated decay can eventually lead to severe toothaches, infections, problems eating and sleeping, missed school days, and repeated medical appointments.

For young children, conventional dental treatment can also be particularly challenging. Some are too young to remain still throughout a procedure, while others experience significant fear or anxiety.

SDF offers an alternative for many of these patients because it can be applied quickly without the equipment and procedures required for a traditional filling.

Fontana said the benefits could extend well beyond young children. Older adults, people with developmental or physical disabilities, individuals with severe dental anxiety, and patients who have difficulty obtaining conventional dental care could also benefit.

There is, however, one important tradeoff. SDF permanently turns the decayed portion of a treated tooth dark, which can be especially noticeable on front teeth. The discoloration is primarily cosmetic, but it may influence whether patients and families choose the treatment.

For many people, avoiding pain, drilling, or more invasive procedures may outweigh that drawback.

Why This Decades-Old Treatment Could Soon Reach More Patients

Silver diamine fluoride is not a newly invented substance. Dentists have successfully used it to manage cavities in numerous countries for decades.

In the United States, the FDA approved SDF as a medical device for treating tooth sensitivity in 2014. Dentists have also used it off-label to stop cavities, meaning they apply an authorized product for a purpose beyond its original approved indication.

What has been missing is large-scale clinical evidence from U.S. populations establishing its safety and effectiveness for treating tooth decay, a key step toward obtaining FDA approval for that specific use.

The University of Michigan trial was designed to help fill that gap.

"If we want more children and families to benefit from this treatment, we need rigorous evidence showing both that it works and that it's safe. From a public health perspective, if we want broader implementation across the United States, including in medical settings, we need carefully collected data in U.S. populations, and we now have that," Fontana said.

The investigation began in 2018 and continued despite the major disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic.

The researchers believe stronger clinical evidence could make SDF more widely available, including in pediatric medical settings where children often receive care long before their first dental appointment.

"In medicine, clinicians want high-quality evidence before changing practice," Fontana said. "It is important to have data they can refer to because young children often see pediatricians years before they ever visit a dentist, broader acceptance could allow many more cavities to be treated while a referral to a dental home is successful, and before they become painful, infected or require surgery."

A dental home refers to an ongoing relationship with a dental care provider who can deliver preventive care, treatment, and follow-up services.

Expanding access to SDF could allow more children to receive early intervention before relatively manageable cavities develop into serious dental problems.

FDA Approval Could Expand Access to Cavity Treatment

The trial brought together researchers from the University of Michigan, New York University, the University of Iowa, and Indiana University. The National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research, part of the National Institutes of Health, provided more than $12 million in funding.

The product tested was Advantage Arrest 38% SDF, supplied by Elevate Oral Care.

The findings provide clinical trial evidence that the manufacturer can use in an application seeking FDA approval of SDF as a drug for arresting dental caries, the medical term for tooth decay.

Amr Moursi, a professor of pediatric dentistry at New York University College of Dentistry and a co-principal investigator, emphasized the potential importance of that regulatory change.

"Our results support FDA approval of SDF for managing arrest of tooth decay in young children. Removing SDF from off-label status would be an important innovation which could lead to increased utilization by providers, enhanced payments by insurers and more consistent product quality," said Moursi, a co-principal investigator on the study.

For some children, receiving another application of SDF every few months could be sufficient to keep a cavity under control until the affected baby tooth naturally falls out.

Adults could also benefit, either by using SDF to manage decay over a longer period or as a temporary solution until a filling or another restorative procedure becomes affordable or practical.

The treatment is not suitable for every cavity, and stopping decay does not necessarily eliminate the need for future dental work. Nevertheless, the findings suggest that a procedure requiring only seconds to perform could help many patients avoid more complicated treatment.

"For almost anyone, this can arrest the decay and stop the infection and the pain it causes," Fontana said. "This could benefit many people."

#667. Scientists overcome a hidden obstacle to more powerful quantum computers

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Silicon spin qubits could play a major role in the future of quantum computing because they can be produced using the same semiconductor manufacturing methods that make today's computer chips. However, researchers have faced a significant challenge. When multiple silicon qubits are controlled at the same time, their operations tend to become less accurate. Building practical, large quantum computers will require scientists to control many qubits simultaneously while keeping errors to a minimum.

Researchers at the RIKEN Center for Emergent Matter Science and their collaborators have now made important progress toward solving this problem. In a study published in Nature Physics, the team demonstrated highly accurate control of several silicon spin qubits operating simultaneously. Their experiments used an array of five qubits connected to a shared microwave control line. By identifying a major cause of errors during simultaneous operation, the scientists developed a technique to reduce those errors and maintain precision.

Leon Camenzind, a RIKEN research scientist and co-first author who supervised the experiments, explains the significance of the results: "This shows that high-fidelity control can be maintained even when several qubits are operated at the same time and represents an important step toward scaling up silicon-based quantum processors."

Scientists Identify a Hidden Source of Quantum Computing Errors

Unlike conventional computers, which store information as bits representing either 0 or 1, quantum computers use quantum bits, or qubits, that can exist in combinations of both states. Silicon spin qubits store this information using the spin of individual electrons confined within silicon. Performing reliable quantum calculations requires extremely precise control over these electron spins.

Microwave signals provide that control, but operating multiple qubits simultaneously can create unwanted effects that interfere with their accuracy. The researchers traced much of this problem to a phenomenon called the AC Stark shift.

This effect occurs when microwave control signals alter a qubit's effective energy levels, producing changes in the phase of its quantum state. Those phase shifts can disrupt the intended operation of individual qubits, particularly when several are being controlled at once.

To counteract the problem, the team created a calibration method that compensates for these unwanted shifts. Rather than separately calibrating every possible combination of qubits, the technique relies on calibrations performed between pairs of qubits. This makes the approach more practical for systems containing larger numbers of quantum bits.

"Rather than requiring an entirely separate calibration procedure for every possible combination of qubits, our method uses calibrations between pairs of qubits to compensate for the unwanted phase shifts," explains Yi-Hsien Wu, the first author who carried out the experiments as part of his PhD research.

Quantum Qubits Achieve Accuracy Above 99.99%

The researchers combined their calibration technique with an improved device design and carefully adjusted microwave control pulses. Their results showed that exceptionally high accuracy could be maintained even when multiple qubits were operating simultaneously.

When the five qubits were tested individually, each achieved single-qubit gate fidelity well above 99.99%, with the highest approaching 99.999%. Fidelity describes how closely a quantum operation matches its intended result.

The team then tested how well the system performed when several qubits were active together. With as many as three qubits operating simultaneously, fidelity remained above 99.99%. Even when all five qubits were controlled at once, accuracy stayed at approximately 99.9%, approaching the levels needed for practical fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Although the calibration method greatly reduced errors, it did not eliminate them entirely. The researchers found that the remaining loss of accuracy was linked to drive-induced decoherence, an effect caused by the greater microwave power required to operate multiple qubits simultaneously.

Unlike the unwanted phase shifts corrected by the new technique, decoherence involves the loss of the delicate quantum coherence needed for reliable operations.

According to Camenzind, "This is a limitation, but further improvements to device design could help mitigate this source of error."

A New Path Toward Larger Quantum Processors

The findings show that controlling several silicon spin qubits at the same time does not necessarily require a major sacrifice in accuracy. By correcting a key source of errors, the researchers have demonstrated a promising approach for operating more qubits through shared microwave control lines.

This is especially important for the development of large quantum processors. Giving every qubit its own independent control system would make increasingly complex devices difficult to manage. Shared control lines could offer a more practical way to expand these systems while maintaining the precision needed for quantum calculations.

"By addressing a key source of control errors through pairwise calibration," says Camenzind. "Our study supports the use of shared control lines as a practical approach to scaling up quantum processors. Combined with silicon's compatibility with established semiconductor manufacturing, we believe that this advance could help pave the way toward larger quantum processors capable of performing increasingly complex computations."

The broader objective is to develop quantum computers that can reliably perform calculations despite the errors and disturbances that affect quantum systems. Such machines, known as fault-tolerant quantum computers, will require precise, dependable control over large numbers of qubits.

"Our long-term goal is to build a fault-tolerant quantum computer based on silicon spin qubits, and reliable parallel control is an essential step toward that goal," adds Seigo Tarucha, project manager of a silicon quantum computing project under JST's Moonshot Goal 6.

Phantom Blade Zero wants you to feel like a kung fu master from the opening moments, and you should, since you play as Soul, an elite assassin for the Order. Even after a fateful duel leaves him with 66 days to live, there’s no cheap Metroid-style reset, and his martial arts prowess keeps growing.

Whether taking on another fearsome warrior one-on-one or a group of enemies, there’s a fluid confidence to his moveset that captures the spirit of the classic martial arts films that have inspired studio S-Game's ambitious debut, itself based on the indie RPG Rainblood: Town of Death that director Soulframe Liang made more than 15 years ago.

While China’s first AAA hits like Black Myth: Wukong and Wuchang: Fallen Feathers have leveraged both Unreal Engine 5 and the overwhelming influence of Soulslikes, S-Game tries to carve out its own identity, which it has coined as ‘kungfupunk’. Aesthetically, that means a curious blend of traditional Chinese culture with the modern: steel-factory architecture clashing with bamboo forests and temples from a more classic wuxia setting, and music where elegant pipa strings give way to electric-guitar rock riffs. More literally, it’s about how Sha-Chi, the energy that powers your kung fu (in-game, it’s basically your stamina gauge used for heavy combos and guarding attacks), also powers other technologies.

But it's definitely the kung fu that's centre-stage here, with all of the fighting styles and movements in-game captured by real martial artists, notably with the involvement of Hong Kong martial arts legend Donnie Yen (he also plays a character, albeit one you don't strictly encounter on the main story path, while spending most of it behind a mask) and his stunt team.

Even though your combos are essentially just spamming light or heavy attacks, your fighting style changes depending on the weapon you use, such as the one-handed Jagged Steel, which inflicts bleed damage, or the weighty two-handed Scarlet Wraithblade, which leaves energy-draining red spider lilies on the ground. The action can often feel too fast for its own good, so you're usually focused on your flurry of attacks before waiting to react to an enemy's blue or red attacks to parry or dodge, but hitting the prompt to unleash a finishing move is pretty satisfying.

Since most bosses drop new weapons, there's a thrill in getting a new toy to play with, and the game has more than 50 to find and master. It can feel a little overwhelming, and some may feel more gimmicky than useful, like a sword that gives you unpredictable drunken-martial-arts movements, so I naturally stuck to a few favourites, especially since I needed to prioritise upgrading them with my limited resources.

Nonetheless, there's an incentive to master more than one weapon, as switching automatically gives you a full Sha-Chi gauge. I often used up a gauge with one weapon, then quickly swapped to another to keep my offensive momentum going (a cooldown period does prevent you from just exploiting this).

Special weapons called Phantom Edges have limited uses but, used at the right time, can turn the tide in encounters, like a large axe that smashes through enemy shields or a chain sickle that tears through multiple surrounding enemies. Some even serve Metroidvania-style exploration, letting you access areas you couldn't reach before.

But despite some interesting designs, it's hard not to see these as glorified keycards, with most leading to little more than a small room and a chest for your trouble. Even though the chain doubles as a grappling hook, it's a bit disappointing once you realise that, like other traversal options such as running up cliffs or wall-running, it's limited to a button prompt at prescribed points in the environment.

This is one of the frustrating aspects of Phantom Blade Zero's rich, dense Wulin, even as it takes you from the bottom of a valley to mountain peaks, plus a massive lake you can explore by boat. It may all look pleasing to the eye, but it suffers from environments where you regularly bump up against invisible barriers as you try to parse the artistically rendered map (albeit elegantly emulating a Chinese ink wash painting that fills up the more checkpoints you come across).

This is especially annoying when a quest marker is supposedly close by, but you still have to wander aimlessly until you spot an interaction prompt, or discover there's actually a long-winded detour you're supposed to take instead. Doors that require specific keys are one thing, but I catch myself sighing when I approach a ledge you would expect to mantle in a similar game, only for Soul's kung fu skills to be arbitrarily eluded.

Another question you might have about Phantom Blade Zero is whether it's a Soulslike. In many ways, it isn't. For instance, its checkpoints don't revive defeated enemies when you rest at them, and you also don't lose any resources permanently if you don't recover them. It nonetheless has some similarities, such as some labyrinthine level design where you'll encounter your share of doors inconveniently locked from your side, but it almost seems to turn into a Soulslike in challenge the moment you encounter bosses with hard-hitting attacks and where you have very limited heals (which you won't even be able to upgrade meaningfully until the game's midpoint).

On the one hand, some difficulty spikes are mitigated by bosses with a second phase, which also have a checkpoint so you're not forced to go through the first phase with fewer resources, and there are also difficulty options. The accessories you find and equip can also make a difference, especially ones that widen the parry and dodge windows, but my major gripe remains how spongy these boss fights feel, even for those that come back with a second phase.

This isn't unique to Phantom Blade Zero (see also Control Resonant, until Remedy patched it at launch), but it makes you question the point of staggering your opponent when, in this state, all your heaviest attacks still only chip away at their health. In many fights, it also makes your aggressive momentum push the enemy back until you inevitably get stuck on geometry. When it hits the camera, some cool-looking finishing moves get framed awkwardly, with parts of the environment obscuring the shot.

Even though I was testing on PS5 Pro, I still ran into some pretty glaring technical issues during my playthrough, mostly in the latter half of the game, which I think came down to optimisation and bugs, since they persisted even after I turned off ray tracing and set the graphics to Performance mode. Sometimes the audio got knocked way out of balance between the voice acting and music, and in a couple of cases the big puddles I came across didn't show reflections so much as they looked more like a big mirror or a hole in the ground.

Most egregious, however, were framerate drops during combat that devolved into a slideshow, spoiling several major boss fights. I should be relieved they didn't kill me, since most of these happened when I was already locked into my (likely very VFX-laden) attack animations against a staggered opponent.

That may make it seem like I have a lot to be down about Phantom Blade Zero, but there is still plenty to admire, including cultural and artistic flourishes I can't share in this review. The Wulin's centrepiece, Pangzhen, is the kind of labyrinthine town that deserves to be talked about in the same breath as Bloodborne's Yharnam, so rich and dense with secrets and lore, and home to some of the game's best bosses, while there's clearly been a lot of thought that has gone into the intriguing characters and deep questlines that offer meaningful rewards, including having an impact on the ending you'll get. It's just a shame that the version I got to play still needed a bit more time in the oven.

A final note: S-Game released a patch that supposedly resolves most of the above issues, but it came after I finished my playthrough, so I didn't have a chance to go through it again to make sure every issue I encountered was fixed.
