#176. Microsoft Unveils Local AI Coding Agents and Sandboxed Environments for Developers

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Microsoft has just announced an exciting evolution in software development tools, specifically designed to enhance the efficiency of small business owners and developers. With the introduction of local models like the MAI Code 1.1 Flash and new capabilities in GitHub Copilot, Microsoft aims to empower small businesses with enhanced coding and automation functionalities that prioritize both choice and control.

As businesses increasingly rely on technology for their operations, the ability to effectively manage coding tasks has never been more critical. Small business owners often juggle multiple priorities, and streamlined tools can significantly enhance productivity.

GitHub Copilot is now set to automatically determine whether tasks should use local on-device intelligence or cloud-scale models. This seamless orchestration occurs behind the scenes, enabling developers to focus on their core tasks without the stress of managing complex infrastructure. “Rather than forcing developers to manage infrastructure decisions themselves, GitHub Copilot automatically coordinates local and cloud inference,” Microsoft explains in their recent press release.

For businesses running on NVIDIA RTX Spark Windows PCs like the Surface Laptop Ultra, this means enhanced performance right at the edge. Local operations can now benefit from powerful inference models that deliver a remarkable coding experience. Combined with Microsoft Execution Containers (MXC), which support secure agentic coding sessions, small business developers can automate tasks while maintaining security and confidence in their systems.

Small business owners can now leverage these new advancements in several practical ways. For instance, using GitHub Copilot’s intelligent Auto orchestration, developers can allow the platform to decide the best inference model for different coding tasks, optimizing for performance and cost. Alternatively, for businesses that require specific models or direct control over certain workflows, the ability to explicitly select a local model provides flexible options.

Innovative features such as dynamic memory management in local inference help reduce overhead, ensuring that even those with limited technical resources can effectively harness AI to enhance their coding capabilities. The focus has been placed on preserving the integrity and quality of the output, as small mistakes in code can lead to significant complications, especially for businesses that rely heavily on software solutions.

However, while the new tools present numerous advantages, small business owners should consider potential challenges. The technological requirements for leveraging local inference models include having the specific hardware capacity, such as the aforementioned Surface Laptop Ultra. While these machines are designed for performance, they come at a cost that might not fit every small business’s budget.

Moreover, there is a learning curve attached to adopting the latest AI-driven tools. Businesses may need to invest time and resources into training their teams to fully understand and utilize new functionalities effectively.

The sandboxing features, powered by Microsoft Execution Containers, introduce another layer of complexity. While sandboxing provides enhanced security for local coding by controlling access to files and network resources, it may require additional management to ensure that operations run smoothly without unintended disruptions to standard workflows.

Microsoft is optimistic about the benefits these upgrades can bring to the coding community. “The result is poised to be the next step in the HydraFusion vision: intelligent orchestration that spans not just multiple models but multiple compute environments including the edge,” they state. As businesses experiment with automation and more cohesive coding tools, it can lead to significant gains in efficiency, allowing them to compete more effectively in a tech-driven market.

In practice, an example of utilizing these new capabilities could involve setting up automated daily reports for local repositories. By combining the tasks of reading repositories, running tests, and generating HTML reports into a daily automation script, small business owners can save considerable time and reduce manual errors.

Microsoft’s push to integrate local models and sandbox tools marks a noteworthy advancement for small businesses looking to harness the power of AI in development while maintaining control over their coding environments. As these tools roll out, businesses can anticipate impactful changes that streamline their development processes and enhance productivity.

For those interested in diving deeper into these advancements, additional details can be found at the original post on Microsoft’s Command Line Blog.

However, fluctuations in crude oil prices, which remain above $100 per barrel, pose a challenge for further reductions.

For small businesses that rely heavily on transportation, these changing gas prices can directly impact operating costs. Analyzing the latest statistics can provide valuable insights into potential budgeting adjustments and operational strategies.

With trick-or-treaters expected to be out and about, encouraging safe driving practices will be beneficial not only for community wellbeing but also for businesses that seek to operate responsibly.

The data from the Energy Information Administration (EIA) indicates that gasoline demand has slipped from 8.98 million barrels per day to 8.38 million, signaling shifts in consumer behavior. Additionally, total gasoline supply has increased from 205.3 million barrels to 209.2 million, reflecting adjustments in production levels. For small business owners, these shifts may present opportunities to adjust logistics, particularly in industries such as delivery services or service-based businesses that require constant travel.

With overall gasoline production declining from 9.75 million barrels per day to 9.24 million, small businesses should prepare for potential price increases if demand rebounds sharply or if crude oil inventories continue to dwindle. As of Wednesday, the West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude closed at $102.78 per barrel, raising concerns regarding sustained price levels at the pump.

For businesses invested in electric vehicle (EV) use, charging costs remain stable at an average of 41 cents per kilowatt hour at public charging stations. This consistent rate offers predictability for those transitioning to electrification, which may help control long-term operational costs. The shift towards EVs could also reflect positively on brand image as consumers increasingly seek eco-friendly options.

The average differences across state lines present actionable insight for businesses operating in multiple locations. For instance, in states like California ($5.94) and Hawaii ($5.65), where gasoline prices are significantly higher, companies may need to account for increased travel costs when planning delivery routes or commuting for client meetings. Conversely, states like North Carolina ($3.42) and Georgia ($3.61), benefiting from lower gas prices, can provide more budget-friendly operational areas.

Small business owners should also weigh the state-specific costs of EV charging. States with the highest public charging costs — like Hawaii (55 cents) and West Virginia (52 cents) — might influence the decision-making process for businesses considering a fleet transition to EVs. Understanding local cost structures can assist in making informed financial decisions.

As drivers prepare for a busy travel weekend, AAA reminds everyone of the importance of road safety during this festive time. Employers may wish to communicate reminders to employees about safe practices while on the road, particularly if their work involves considerable travel.

To help manage fuel expenses, small business owners can leverage resources like AAA’s TripTik Travel planner, which provides updated gas and EV charging prices along various routes, allowing for better planning and potential cost savings.

With gas prices shifting and EV charging costs holding steady, small business owners should remain vigilant and strategic in their operational decisions. Staying informed about these trends empowers entrepreneurs to optimize their budgets and adapt to a rapidly changing economic environment. #265. Aon CEO warns AI data center insurance risks could threaten financing

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The race to build artificial intelligence infrastructure is creating enormous new risks that could threaten financing for the data centers powering the artificial intelligence boom, according to Aon CEO Greg Case.

In an interview with CNBC Wednesday, Case warned that clustering billions of dollars of equipment and computing capacity in a small number of locations could make some projects difficult to insure, with consequences for developers seeking financing.

"If you create them and you build them on top of each other, you've created concentration risk in a way in which it makes it more difficult to get insurance," Case said.

"If it's more difficult to get insurance, that means the financing structure is going to change completely. You can't access the debt markets," he said.

The potential financial exposure from operational disruptions is staggering, Case said.

"Data centers are literally going to measure business interruption at millions of dollars per minute," he told CNBC.

The warnings come as technology companies accelerate spending on data centers, electricity generation and other infrastructure needed to support AI. Those investments are creating opportunities for insurers, brokers and business risk consultants. They're also raising questions about whether traditional insurers have sufficient capacity to absorb the risks.

In its third-quarter earnings, Aon reported a record pipeline of data center projects, helping drive 7% organic revenue growth in its commercial risk business during the quarter. Adjusted earnings per share increased 10% to $3.36, while adjusted operating margin expanded 70 basis points to 27%. One basis point equals 0.01%, or 1/100th of 1%.

The company has developed specialized insurance facilities, or pools of capacity to cover specific industries or risks, to address growing demand, including a $10 billion data center facility and a $2.5 billion energy facility.

Managing the risks begins well before a policy is written, according to Case. Aon said it is working with developers to design and locate facilities, reduce the potential for business interruption and structure insurance coverage that can support financing.

"These are engineering conversations around understanding risk," Case said.

The surge in demand for sophisticated risk management is also central to Aon's pending $17 billion acquisition of USI Insurance Services, which is expected to close in the fourth quarter.

The deal will combine USI with Aon's existing operations and NFP, which Aon acquired in 2024, creating a roughly $6.5 billion middle-market platform.

Case said smaller companies face many of the same increasingly complex risks as multinational corporations, but often lack access to comparable analytics, expertise and insurance solutions.

"Our middle market clients have exactly the same challenges, exactly the same risks they face every day, and they deserve to have the content, the insight, the capability that the largest companies in the world have," Case said.

Most industry insiders and experts anticipate AI will make insurance brokerage and consulting more efficient. Asked why Aon is investing billions in acquisitions rather than exclusively expanding its technology capabilities, Case said the two are not mutually exclusive.

"This is about talent and technology," he said. "You get those both right. That's how you build the relationships that matter for clients."

Aon expects $395 million in annual earnings benefits from the USI combination, including $280 million in cost savings and $115 million in revenue synergies.

The acquisition will be financed with debt, and the company expects to prioritize reducing leverage over the next two years.

AI is creating opportunities for insurers to better understand vulnerabilities, even as the technology introduces new threats that could generate widespread financial losses.

Researchers disclosed two critical vulnerabilities in PHP, a programming language used by roughly 70% of websites, on Oct. 21. The flaws could allow hackers to remotely execute malicious code on vulnerable servers without authentication. AI played a significant role in discovering and analyzing the vulnerabilities through cybersecurity firms Calif and Google's Wiz, along with OpenAI.

Asked whether cyber risks are escalating faster than insurers can respond, Case said the industry needs to expand beyond its traditional capital base to meet growing demand.

"We have to keep up. We have to continue to evolve," he said.

Case estimates the global insurance industry currently has about $4.5 trillion in capital. He believes that amount must double or triple to meet the growing needs across cyber, AI, data centers and other emerging risks.

"Call it 5 trillion to 10 to 15 trillion," Case said, outlining his vision for expanding the industry's capacity and clarifying remarks he made in the company's second-quarter earnings call.

His strategy involves tapping institutional investors, pension funds, sovereign wealth funds and wealthy individuals to help finance risks traditionally carried by insurance companies and reinsurers. Case estimates those sources represent a potential pool of approximately $250 trillion in capital — a fraction of which could substantially expand the market.

Aon is increasingly positioning itself not simply as a broker connecting companies with existing insurers, but as an intermediary helping create new pools of capital to absorb complex risks.

"Not just being a market taker or market optimizer for our clients, but a market maker creating new markets to sort of drive and address risk," Case said.

That creates opportunities for alternative asset managers and private investors seeking returns from insurance-linked investments, although investors must be able to evaluate the potential for large and correlated losses.

Case said the critical ingredient is the ability to quantify exposures accurately enough to give investors confidence to commit their capital.

While corporate risk is becoming more complex, some commercial insurance prices are declining and companies are beginning to see some relief after years of rising premiums.

But, Case cautioned, this is not a prolonged period of lower insurance rates.

"Some are confusing that with it's a permanent soft market. Not the case at all," he said.

He said some corporate clients are using lower insurance prices as an opportunity to buy more protection rather than simply reduce spending.

"We have clients who literally, when they understand their exposure, even though the unit price might have been coming down, they completely restructure their program and buy more because they need more coverage," Case said.

The dynamic is helping Aon maintain growth despite softer pricing in commercial insurance and reinsurance markets.

#55. Your Website Redesign Might Be Solving the Wrong Problem. Here’s the Step Many Teams Skip.

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

You're viewing the US website. Looking for the Site edition?

Before approving the pages, features and platform, define what you expect customers to do differently when the new site launches.

Opinions expressed by Entrepreneur contributors are their own.

Most website design projects become specific very quickly. Teams discuss a content management system, navigation, landing pages, search, personalization, integrations and the other capabilities the site may need. By the time business or brand owners review the project, there may already be an impressive list of what the website will include. What is often less specific is what customers are expected to do differently, or more easily, because of the design.

That distinction matters because delivering every item in a design scope does not necessarily improve the business, particularly when attention shifts too quickly toward visual representation or functionality. A company can launch a polished, technically capable website while leaving important parts of the customer experience unresolved. The project may be complete, but the assumption behind it was never really tested.

A stronger design starts with a clear hypothesis.

“Generate more leads” is a business objective. “Improve conversion” is a metric. Neither tells a team what the website needs to change.

Consider a company selling a complex service that wants more qualified prospects to request a consultation. Its first instinct may be to shorten the form, make calls to action more visible or add more opportunities to book a meeting. Those changes can increase the number of people reaching the form without addressing why the right prospects hesitate in the first place.

They may be unsure whether the service fits their situation, what implementation involves or whether the company has experience with businesses like theirs. If that is the real barrier, making the call to action more prominent addresses the end of the journey while leaving the earlier uncertainty intact.

The hypothesis becomes more useful when it connects the two: If qualified prospects can establish fit and understand what working with the company involves before they reach the inquiry stage, more of them should be willing to begin a serious sales conversation.

Now the redesign has something meaningful to solve.

Once the expected customer change is clear, the feature list starts to look different.

The company may discover that another page template matters less than improving how services are explained. A sophisticated search function may be less important than helping visitors recognize which solution applies to them. Personalization may sound attractive, while stronger case studies, clearer proof and a more deliberate path through the site may have a closer relationship to the behavior the business actually wants to change.

Technology decisions also become easier to question. A new platform may offer dozens of capabilities, but capability alone does not make each one strategically useful. The relevant question is whether the technology enables an experience that supports the hypothesis or reveals a better way to accomplish it.

This is also where redesigns can uncover possibilities the original brief never anticipated. Existing customer data may allow a company to simplify an experience, an integration may eliminate information customers currently have to enter twice, or a content system may make it possible to present different levels of detail without creating separate journeys for every audience.

Technology stops being a shopping list and becomes part of the solution.

A useful hypothesis also allows for the possibility that the original idea may be wrong.

Many redesigns are evaluated in ways that make failure nearly impossible. The site launched on schedule, the pages were built, stakeholders approved the design, and technical performance improved. All of those things can be true while the customer problem remains.

If the hypothesis is that clearer information about fit will lead to more qualified inquiries, the business should be able to observe whether that actually happens. If inquiry volume increases but sales teams spend more time filtering poor-fit prospects, the website may have made taking action easier without improving the outcome.

That evidence should be allowed to challenge the original idea. The answer is not automatically another round of interface refinements. The company may need to change what information prospects receive, when they receive it or even which action the website encourages them to take.

Good design does not protect the first idea. It gives the business a better way to determine whether the idea was right.

User acceptance testing can confirm that the website works as intended and that people can complete the important tasks built into it, while usability research can reveal where people hesitate, misunderstand information or struggle to move forward.

Those forms of validation matter, but they do not answer the entire business question.

Once the site is in use, behavioral data should show whether the intended change is actually occurring. A/B testing can help compare alternatives when there is enough traffic to produce a meaningful result, but the metric should remain connected to the original hypothesis. More clicks are not necessarily better if they do not produce better conversations, purchases, applications or whatever outcome justified the redesign.

That distinction keeps iteration from becoming a series of isolated optimizations. Each change should provide stronger evidence about what customers need and whether the website is helping the business respond to those needs more effectively.

Every redesign eventually needs requirements, specifications, pages and technology decisions. The problem begins when those decisions arrive before the business has established what the new experience is supposed to change.

A hypothesis does not make the answer predictable. It makes the reason for building something clear enough to question, test and improve.

That is a stronger place to begin a redesign because the business is no longer approving a collection of improvements and hoping they add up to progress. It is making a deliberate claim about how a better experience should change customer behavior and using the redesign to determine whether that claim holds.

Most website design projects become specific very quickly. A company can launch a polished, technically capable website while leaving important parts of the customer experience unresolved. The project may be complete, but the assumption behind it was never really tested.

A stronger design starts with a clear hypothesis.

#98. IRS CEO Bisignano donates to Trump Accounts in his childhood Brooklyn ZIP code

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

IRS CEO Frank Bisignano has donated to Trump Accounts for certain kids in Brooklyn, the agency announced Thursday.

The Bisignano Family Fund donated $250 apiece to Trump Accounts of eligible children ages 2 to 8 living in 11234, Bisignano's hometown ZIP code, according to the release.

"My life was shaped by the people, schools, and neighborhoods in Marine Park, Mill Basin, Bergen Beach, and Flatlands," Bisignano said in a statement. "This donation is an investment in the children of Brooklyn, giving them a boost in their personal wealth building journeys."

Within that ZIP code, fewer than 10,000 U.S. citizens are under 10 years of age, according to CNBC's analysis of the most recent population estimate from the U.S. Census Bureau.

The contribution will be invested in a diversified index fund, the release also said.

Expanding access to Trump Accounts has been a Trump administration priority. In July, Bisignano was tasked with leading that effort.

The latest Trump Account news comes weeks after the Treasury Department auto-enrolled more than 60 million children.

Trump Accounts, also known as 530A accounts, are open to any U.S. citizen under 18 with a Social Security number. Children born from 2025 through 2028 may also claim a one-time $1,000 pilot program contribution from the Treasury.

Families must still activate their child's Trump Account to receive contributions if eligible, according to Treasury guidance.

Bisignano's commitment comes on the heels of news that Elon Musk plans to give every kid in America a share of SpaceX in 2027.

Altimeter Capital CEO Brad Gerstner, founder of Invest America and an architect of Trump Accounts, described Musk's gift as "the tip of the iceberg" for philanthropic gifts on CNBC's "Squawk Box" Oct. 21. Gerstner has committed about $35 million to help fund investment accounts for kids in his home state of Indiana.

More gifts are in the pipeline, Bisignano told CNBC's "Squawk Box" on Oct. 8. "They range from small to mega," he said.

After claiming an account, families can track deposits in the Trump Accounts app, which was designed in partnership with Robinhood.

"Trump Accounts is direct philanthropy, extremely efficient, direct to children...," Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev said on CNBC's "Squawk on the Street" on Oct. 28.

"Excited to see Elon donation," he said. "I think there will be many others as well."

The Treasury Department in September released temporary regulations allowing Trump Accounts to hold donated individual stocks, a shift from previous guidelines that permitted only diversified, low-cost index funds. The Trump administration said the move could pave the way for more multimillion-dollar donations, with a number of philanthropists poised to participate.

The latest donation comes just days before the midterm election, and Republicans fight to keep slim congressional majorities in the Senate and House.
