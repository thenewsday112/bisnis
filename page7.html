#57. Berlin's Elif Eralp under fire over Der Spiegel interview

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Left candidate Elif Eralp has sparked controversy after Der Speigel reported she was advised against mentioning a pro-Palestinian activist for fear of reprisals against her family.

Elif Eralp, the candidate on course to become the next mayor of Berlin, sparked controversy on Thursday after having unintentionally aired personal security fears that appeared to be linked to pro-Palestinian activists in the city.

The controversy came about based on internal remarks that were part of her interview with the German news magazine Der Spiegel.

Eralp, whose Left Party (Die Linke) came out on top in local elections in Berlin last month, appeared to suggest in remarks made during an internal authorization process for an interview that she gave to Der Spiegel that she couldn't make references to a particular pro-Palestinian activist out of fear for her own safety.

"I can't put myself and my family in danger for Der Spiegel," the magazine cites her as having written in a Word Document edit, which was inadvertently sent back to the reporters.

"What do we do now? Cancel the whole interview?" she is cited as having asked her press officer and other party colleagues, while stating in another remark, "I should never have agreed to this interview."

In the Der Spiegel interview, which was conducted on Monday in Eralp's office in Berlin's House of Representatives, the 45-year-old was asked about allegations of antisemitism that have been leveled at members of her socialist party.

Those accusations came about after at least two instances where people with questionable backgrounds showed up to celebrate after Eralp's party won elections in the city state last month.

For instance, the Left Party election celebration in the Berlin district of Neukölln was attended by a controversial pro-Palestinian activist associated with the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine (PFLP), which is classified as a terrorist organization by the European Union.

In the past, Ibrahim Ibrahim has described Yahya Sinwar, the former head of Hamas in Gaza, widely regarded as the mastermind behind the deadly terror attack on Israel on October 7, 2023, as a "martyr" and a "hero." He has since rejected accusations of antisemitism and of supporting terrorism or Hamas.

Asked by Der Spiegel whether she was referring to the Ibrahim incident, Eralp initially responded with a "yes." But she later returned the interview transcript to the magazine with the question crossed out and the comment regarding her and her family's safety.

In Germany, it is standard practice for an interviewee to review and approve direct quotations with media outlets before the interview is published.

In notes included in the approved version of the transcript, which Der Spiegel believes were left in by accident, Eralp wrote that Berlin police had previously advised her not to comment on specific individuals.

That Eralp chose to conceal her concerns publicly led some politicians to ask whether she was the right person to be the mayor of Berlin.

"There were reasons not to publish – Eralp's security concerns first and foremost," the magazine wrote. "But the political importance of her notes outweighed them. Der Spiegel believes that the public should know."

It added that the story begs the question: "Can a woman who is too scared to name a terrorist sympathizer in an interview be responsible for the security of all Berliners?"

Rival politicians weighed in, including from the Social Democrats (SPD) and Greens, with whom the Left Party is due to conduct coalition talks in Berlin next month.

"I would advise my party to distance itself from an alliance with the Left Party," said the SPD's Karl Lauterbach, who rose to prominence as Germany's health minister during the Covid-19 pandemic.

"Further talks about a coalition with this party should be out of the question for any social democrat," added SPD internal affairs spokesman Sebastian Fiedler.

Katharina Dröge, the leader of the Green Party's parliamentary grouping, asked in the Süddeutsche Zeitung newspaper, "Will [Eralp] continue to let herself be influenced by those in her own ranks who are close to criminal groups and who tolerate threats and intimidation towards their own lead candidate? Or will she manage to give a clear and tough response?"

The chairman of the Central Council of Jews in Germany, Josef Schuster, warned: "The SPD and the Greens have a duty not to hand governmental responsibility for Berlin and for the security of the Jewish community to the Left Party" and claimed the story showed that "the Left Party is incapable of distancing itself from terrorist sympathizers and antisemites."

Eralp hit back later on Thursday, slamming a "truly toxic debate," vowing not to be intimidated and promising a tough stance "against organized crime, against organizations that glorify violence, against violence, against terror."

She said she wouldn't comment on the specifics of the Der Spiegel story but insisted that she would "ensure the security of all Berliners."

Eralp also received backing from the vice-president of the German parliament and veteran Left Party colleague Bodo Ramelow, who criticized the Der Spiegel story as "more than questionable."

He said the reporting "targeted a person who feels vulnerable and made them even more vulnerable." He said internal communications had been used to depict the "anachronistic cliché" of a "weak woman."

#46. Justice for Maya women in Guatemala, girls back in school in India, restored land in Mali: The UN has named its 2026 SDG Action Award winners

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Champions of indigenous women's rights in Guatemala, girls' education in India, and restored farmland in Mali all featured among the winners of UN's SDG Action Awards.

A legal aid group for Indigenous women in Guatemala, a movement of village mothers in rural India, and a Malian entrepreneur who restores farmland have won this year's UN SDG Action Awards.

The winners were announced at the ninth Heroes of Tomorrow awards ceremony in Rome, Italy, run by the UN SDG Action Campaign. They were chosen from more than 7,000 entries from 190 countries.

Nine finalists were shortlisted for three categories: resilience, creativity and changemaker.

The Resilience Award went to the Women's Justice Initiative. Many indigenous women in Guatemala face high rates of violence, poverty and discrimination, made worse by language barriers and a lack of access to information. The initiative helps women and girls get access to justice and lead change in their communities. According to the organization, more than 16,000 women and girls have participated.

"I accept the UN SDG Action Award in the honour of the Maya Kaqchikel women and girls in Guatemala who are building a more just future for their communities. Ending violence against women requires all of us. Only together, we can build a future where every woman and girl lives with dignity and realizes her full potential," said Viviana Patal, the initiative's legal director.

The Creativity Award was given to Mothers of Courage, a grassroots campaign that aims to keep girls in education in rural India. Its organizers say more than 1.2 million people have joined and pledged to keep their daughters in education and to prevent early marriage.

"This award today belongs to all the mothers in those villages, all the women leaders, who are working hard to change the destinies of their daughters and their daughters-in-law," said Khushboo Awasthi, Mothers of Courage's chief curator.

"Together, let's create a world where no girl ever has to negotiate for her right to dream and to aspire and to thrive."

The Changemaker Award when to Malian entrepreneur Rokiatou Traore. Her organization, Herou Alliance, helps women and small farmers bring worn-out land back to life by planting trees alongside their crops. The trees also help farmers generate extra revenue, allowing them to sell products from moringa and other valuable trees.

"Receiving this award means a lot to me. It is a reminder of why I started this journey," said Traore. "I invite you all to join us, to plant more trees, to empower more women, and above all, to create a future full of opportunities for communities for today and for future generations."

The UN Trust Fund to End Violence against Women and Girls received an honorable mention. Speaking about the award, President of the Organization of Women's Freedom in Iraq Jannat Al Ghezi paid a tearful tribute to its founder Yanar Mohammed, who was assassinated outside her home in Baghdad in March.

UN Assistant Secretary-General Michelle Muschett said the finalists "remind us that the future is something we create together and that each of us has the power to help shape it and open doors for the next generations."

"When they tell you that one person cannot make a difference, prove them wrong," UN SDG Action Campaign Director Marina Ponti told the audience. "Change has always begun with people who refused to accept that the world could not be different. So be courageous. Be persistent. Be that change."

A panel of 20 judges from UN leadership, government, civil society, the arts, business and youth activism chose the winners. They included UN Development Programme Administrator Alexander De Croo, Oxfam International Executive Director Amitabh Behar, and Otto Group Vice President Tobias Wollermann.

The awards are run by the UN SDG Action Campaign, a special initiative of the UN Secretary-General. The campaign supports the UN Sustainable Development Goals, 17 targets adopted by member states that cover areas including poverty, hunger, education, gender equality and climate protection.

The awards are supported by Italy's Foreign Ministry and Germany's Ministry for Economic Cooperation and Development.

#34. 'Star Trek' star Patrick Stewart dies age 86

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

The Shakespearean actor landed the role of a lifetime as "Star Trek" Captain Picard. He passed away at age 86.

For Star Trek fans, he was the legendary Captain Picard; the British actor and director Patrick Stewart, longtime member of the  Royal Shakespeare Company, died on Thursday at the age of 86 following a "brief and poignant journey" with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia, according to an official statement on his social media accounts.

In Los Angeles, California, far away from the small English village of Mirfield where he was born, Stewart achieved world fame. He was 47 years old when he landed the role of a lifetime in the legendary science fiction series Star Trek: Playing Captain Jean-Luc Picard in "Star Trek: The Next Generation," he stepped into the shoes of the legendary Captain James T. Kirk, played by William Shatner.

Stewart brought a calm, measured and distinctly British presence that quickly made Picard a fan favorite and a cult figure in the Star Trek universe. He returned to the role decades later in the spin-off series "Star Trek: Picard," which ended in 2023, proving the character's enduring appeal.

As Captain Jean-Luc Picard, he led the crew of the starship Enterprise, a spaceship with the sizeable mission: "To explore strange new worlds. To seek out new life and new civilizations" — and most importantly, "to boldly go where no man has gone before!"

Stewart's interest in acting was sparked in the early 1950s when a teacher asked him to recite a work by Shakespeare. His passion for Shakespeare stayed with him until the end.

His parents were far removed from the glamor of the theatrical world. His father was a highly respected officer in the British Army during World War II, but at home, he was an abusive husband, and young Patrick had to helplessly witness repeated violence against his mother. Stewart wrote about his experiences with domestic violence in The Guardian in 2009. The stage, he confessed there, seemed to him "a far safer place" than reality; he saw acting as a way to escape his home life.

Patrick began acting at the age of 12. At 16, he left school early and, thanks to a scholarship, studied acting in Bristol, England. Along the way, he worked as a journalist. In 1965, he became an official member of the Royal Shakespeare Company, and remained with the theather company from 1966 to 1982. Such was his passion for the great English playwright and lyricist that he even shared his sonnets on his Twitter account during the 2020 coronavirus pandemic.

Stewart dazzled the theater world with his own stage adaptation of Charles Dickens' 1843 tale "A Christmas Carol." He played more than 30 roles himself. After its 1987 premiere in Stewart's hometown of Mirfield, the one-man show became a critics' favorite and a long-running hit on Broadway in New York.

In the same year,  Stewart went from being a stage actor to an international TV star. Star Trek producer Robert Justman offered him the role of Captain Jean-Luc Picard, one of the main characters in a new edition of the popular TV series "Star Trek: The Next Generation."

The successful Shakespearean hesitated. Until then, he had never worked in Hollywood. But his agent encouraged him to accept the role, Stewart recalled in a 2020 interview with the German newspaper Die Welt. "You can accept the offer as a start for now. It won't be a big deal, you can't revive a series as iconic as Star Trek anyway."

But his agent was to be proven wrong: Stewart led the Starship Enterprise through its galactic adventures for seven seasons and four full-length feature films. The TV series had astronomical success on Earth in a very short time. At first, the producers were at odds as to whether Stewart's British accent could really fit the role of commander of a US starship in the 24th century, but it soon became impossible to imagine the series without the subtly diplomatic Captain Picard, who often quoted Shakespeare while at the helm. It seemed as if all the time he had spent as a Shakespearean actor had prepared him to command the Enterprise, Stewart once remarked with a wink. When the cult series ended in 1994, he had ruled out ever returning to the Enterprise's command center.

In 2010, the actor was knighted by the Queen of England. Stewart considered it the greatest honor of his career to stand alongside acting greats such as Sir Laurence Olivier and Sir Alec Guinness.

Beyond the screen, Stewart has long used his voice for social and human rights causes. In recent years, he has drawn attention to issues such as violence against women, age discrimination and the protection of children.

In a June 2020 interview, "Playboy" asked the New York resident whether he was considering retiring from acting. "Never! That would mean giving up my life," replied Stewart, who was approaching his 80th birthday at the time. He was filming the second of three seasons of "Star Trek: Picard." The series ends with a final episode in which the retired Starfleet admiral makes his last appearance. In the series, Picard retires to his family vineyard in France but is drawn back into space to confront new threats to the Federation.

This series also showcases Stewart's affection for his pit bull, Ginger. In "Star Trek: Picard," Picard also has a pit bull, a loyal four-legged companion named Number One — a deliberate reference to Stewart's real-life love of the breed. For his efforts to combat prejudice against pit bulls and to support their adoption, Stewart was honored by the US animal welfare organization ASPCA, among others.

In 2024, he published his memoir "Making it So." In it, he recounts his passion for theater and his love of Shakespeare.

Now, at the age of 86, the British actor has finally departed for another galaxy. Farewell, Patrick Stewart!

#23. Germany's Alexander Zverev clinches tennis No. 1 ranking

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Alexander Zverev is the second German man, after Boris Becker, to clinch the No. 1 spot in tennis.

German tennis star Alexander Zverev is set to become the new world No. 1 in tennis, capping a successful year where he won two Grand Slams.

Zverev is the second German man, after Boris Becker, to clinch the top spot in the sport. Becker reached No.1 some 35 years ago.

Zverev replaces Italy's Jannik Sinner, who previously held the world's No. 1 spot.

Zverev got there by reaching the quarter-finals of the Erste Bank Open in Vienna this week.

He is also the second-oldest first-time No. 1 ranked player after Australian John Newcombe.

"(It is) very special. To have the feeling to be on the top of the world is amazing," Zverev said.

"It is different than winning a tournament or a title, but it's such a special feeling to know that the No. 1 is going to be next to your name when the new rankings come out."

Among German female tennis players, only Steffi Graf and Angelique Kerber have achieved this in the WTA rankings.

Zverev's career had been defined by getting close to Grand Slam titles but never quite reaching them, facing stiff competition from Sinner and Spanish talent Carlos Alcaraz.

The Hamburg-born also won a gold medal in the 2021 Olympics.

But his tennis career finally had its breakthrough this year, when he won the Grand Slam at both the French and US Opens.

Known for his consistency, he was just the ninth man to reach at least the semifinals at all four majors in the same season.
