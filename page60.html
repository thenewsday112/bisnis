#1697. Patrón embraces Día de Muertos for one-night-only comedy show

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Let Marketing Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The experience will put a spin on a traditional poetry form and include guest experiences and custom cocktails.

As advertisers continue to embrace experiential marketing, Patrón is inviting a select number of guests aged 21 and over to a comedy show which takes inspiration from the traditional Mexican literary form known as calaveritas literarias, satirical verses usually written for Día de Muertos that are centered around death. The event is meant to draw on Patrón’s Mexican roots and engage with the holiday in an authentic way while providing content that can be used to engage with consumers on social media

Niño de Rivera, the first Mexican woman to release a Spanish-language comedy special on Netflix, will be joined by Jesus Trejo, Chingo Bling, Cris Sosa and Jilberto Soto. The 75-minute show will be hosted by Xazmin Garza, an Arizona-based stand-up comic. In addition to preshow cocktails, the brand will appear in guest touchpoints throughout the venue. Those not in attendance can look up the cocktails on a dedicated landing page.

Tequila brands have often looked to put cultural touchpoints at the forefront of Día de Muertos marketing. Last year, Tequila Don Julio partnered with Mexican bakery Panadería Rosetta to host pop-ups in 12 cities that offered pan de muerto, a type of bread baked during the lead-up to the festivities. At night, the locations became tequila bars. During the COVID-19 pandemic, Jose Cuervo sent out ofrenda kits so people could set up temporary altars at home.

However, marketing around Día de Muertos, which takes place Nov. 1-2, has also proven fraught for some brands. An important cultural celebration tracing its roots back to Mexico’s pre-European colonization, the holiday is often lumped by U.S. retailers and marketers into the same category as modern-day Halloween. In 2020, Mattel found itself in hot water after being accused of cultural appropriation for its $75.99 Día de Muertos Barbie. Disney attempted to trademark the festival in 2013 ahead of the Pixar film “Coco.” The company dropped the effort following swift backlash.

Patrón may be attempting to engage with the holiday in an authentic way, rather than lumping it in with the Halloween season.

"Día de Muertos is a time to celebrate the lives and legacies of those who came before us, while honoring the memories and connections that continue to live on," said Roberto Ramirez-Laverde, global senior vice president, Patrón Tequila in press materials. "With La Última Risa, Patrón is bringing that spirit of remembrance to life on stage through an evening of comedy, craft and culture.”

Tequila has managed to avoid the same issues many other spirits are currently facing in the U.S. The spirit has seen sales rise since 2004, largely due to its popularity among the 24 to 34 age demographic, which has been drinking less overall. However, the market is showing signs of slowing growth, signaling it may be stabilizing.

Bacardi is a privately held company and does not release quarterly financial reports. It completed its purchase of Patrón in 2018, the deal valuing the brand at $5.1 billion. Prior to the acquisition, Bacardi held a minority stake in the brand since 2008.

#453. India works to rebuild its navigation constellation with upcoming launch

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

On Tuesday, Oct. 27, SpaceX will launch the Starlink Group 15-16 mission from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California at 12:45 a.m. EDT. The Falcon 9 will carry a batch of satellites for Starlink, SpaceX’s broadband internet constellation, and its first-stage booster is expected to land on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You in the Pacific Ocean.

On Friday, Oct. 30, SpaceX returns to the pad at Vandenberg for the Starlink Group 15-18 mission at 12:02 a.m. EDT. The booster will again target a landing on Of Course I Still Love You.

Later on Friday, Oct. 30, the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), India’s national space agency, will launch the NVS-03 navigation satellite aboard a GSLV Mk II rocket from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota at 2:21 p.m. EDT. NVS-03 is the third of five second-generation satellites for NavIC (Navigation with Indian Constellation), India’s regional satellite navigation system.

The first of the second-gen satellites, NVS-01, reached orbit in May 2023. But NVS-02, launched in January 2025, failed to reach its designated orbit after a thruster issue. A successful NVS-03 launch would bring NavIC back to the minimum of four working satellites needed to deliver its positioning service.

Closing out the week on Sunday, Nov. 1, SpaceX will launch the USSF-432 mission for the U.S. Space Force from Space Launch Complex 4 East at Vandenberg. SpaceX has not released details about the payload. The Falcon 9 booster will land on Of Course I Still Love You.

On Monday, Oct. 19, the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) launched its Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) mission aboard an H3-24L rocket from Tanegashima Space Center at 3:41 p.m. EDT. MMX aims to be the first spacecraft to land on the martian moon Phobos, collect samples from its surface, and return them to Earth. It is scheduled to reach Mars in August 2027. The mission is led by JAXA in collaboration with NASA, the French space agency (CNES), the German Aerospace Center (DLR), and the European Space Agency (ESA).

On Tuesday, Oct. 20, SpaceX launched its Bandwagon-5 rideshare mission from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station at 7:04 a.m. EDT, with the booster landing on A Shortfall of Gravitas. That night at 11:39 p.m. EDT, Chinese company Galactic Energy launched the Xiguang-4 01 satellite aboard a Ceres-1S rocket from the Haiyang Oriental Spaceport, a sea-launch facility off China’s Shandong Province.

Friday, Oct. 23, was the week’s busiest day. Rocket Lab kicked it off at 3:11 a.m. EDT with “We’re Not Hawking Around,” an Electron launch from Launch Complex 1 on New Zealand’s Māhia Peninsula carrying three satellites for HawkEye 360, a company that tracks radio signals from orbit to monitor ships, aircraft, and other activity. The China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation (CASC), the country’s main state-owned space contractor, followed with two launches: a Long March 6A carried Yaogan 50-03 from the Taiyuan Satellite Launch Center at 6:15 a.m. EDT, and a Long March 4B carried six more Yaogan satellites from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center at 3:25 p.m. EDT. That evening at 6:48 p.m. EDT, SpaceX launched Starlink Group 15-15 from Vandenberg, with the booster landing on Of Course I Still Love You.

The week closed on Saturday, Oct. 24, when CASC launched SatNet LEO Group 28 aboard a Long March 8A from the Wenchang Space Launch Site on Hainan Island at 10:03 p.m. EDT. The satellites are part of Guowang, China’s state-run broadband megaconstellation.

On Thursday, Nov. 5, SpaceX is slated to launch the Starlink Group 15-21 mission from Vandenberg at 12:14 a.m. EST, with the booster landing on Of Course I Still Love You.

T-Mobile's new T-Mix plans deliver tons of extras ranging from streaming to mobile discounts, but there are some caveats to know.

T-Mobile’s phone plans just got a facelift, and now it’s easier than ever to pick and choose the benefits you want. Announced on October 26, T-Mobile’s T-Mix is replacing the old Mobile Experience plans. The three new T-Mix plans are postpaid phone contracts with a core set of features and multiple “Picks” options that can be interchanged to add benefits, streaming services, gaming subscriptions, and device financing.

Also: I switched to Mint from T-Mobile to save money

These new plans aren’t available yet (they launch in early November, though T-Mobile hasn’t confirmed a specific date). Existing T-Mobile Experience 2.0 plans will not be available to new customers, but current customers on those plans will be allowed to maintain them.

T-Mobile’s new offerings let customers pick and choose which perks they want, rather than having specific benefits tied to certain plan tiers. T-Mobile’s previous Experience plan options provided fixed benefits, much like plans from Verizon and other carriers. This means that some customers had to level up (and pay more) to get exactly the perks they wanted, like Hulu or Netflix access, while other customers didn’t have an option to select a benefit that best fit them if they didn’t want the entire tier of services.

To compete in today’s market, carriers must be more adaptable than ever to retain their customer base. It’s not just about phone discounts, preorders, or the flashiest family plans anymore. Customers want to tailor carrier services in ways that save them the most, and T-Mobile is delivering.

T-Mobile says T-Mix offers no fixed bundles or wasted benefits, and offers customers the freedom to choose how the value built into a wireless plan works for them.

Also: How to unlock your Verizon, T-Mobile, or AT&T phone

The new T-Mix plans include a base set of high-speed data, hotspot data, video streaming speeds, and international options. There’s also a selection of picks that allows customers to choose perks like streaming services, game passes, or services like DoorDash or T-Satellite. Picks can also go toward financing your new phone.

T-Mobile’s plans will maintain a one-plan-fits-all policy for accounts with numerous lines. Individual family members won’t be able to maintain their own plan; rather, everyone must agree on a T-Mix base plan to begin with. Customers can select from Standard, More, and Loaded plans.

The Standard plan offers 50GB of high-speed 5G data, unlimited hotspot data at 3G speeds, unlimited international data while traveling in Canada and Mexico at 2G speeds, unlimited texting in more than 215 countries, and up to 480p streaming resolution.

The More plan offers unlimited data, 60GB of high-speed hotspot data, 15GB of high-speed data in Canada and Mexico, up to 5GB of high-speed data when traveling internationally, and up to 4K streaming resolution.

The Loaded plan includes data, up to 250GB of hotspot data, 30GB of high-speed data in Canada and Mexico, up to 15GB high-speed data when traveling, and up to 4K streaming resolution. The Loaded plan also allows customers to connect a cellular-enabled device (add a smartwatch or tablet) for only $5 per item.

Generally, all plans include unlimited texting in more than 215 countries, long-distance calling at $0.50 per minute, and T-Mobile’s Basic Scam Shield service. Financing is also comparable across plans. You can choose to finance a new phone using T-Mobile’s Classic 24-month term or the recently introduced Flex 36-month commitment.

Pricing for the Standard plan, with no fees or taxes, starts at $55 for a single line or $85 for two lines. Three lines cost $100, and each additional line costs $15. There is a 12-line maximum for each T-Mobile account. The More and Loaded plans will cost more per line, which you can compare in the chart above.

T-Mobile’s new Picks option gives you the ability to activate perks you’ll actually use, but there are a few things to note. There’s a certain number of picks you get on your account, and a strict timeline for changing them.

Picks operate like a form of currency, with different perks being assigned a pick value. Two picks can get you an Apple Music subscription or Google AI Pro, while one pick can nab you Netflix with ads. The most premium benefits cost three or four picks and are for services like ESPN Unlimited and Netflix without ads.

It’s important to note that you’re at the mercy of T-Mobile’s business partners, and these perks can change over time.

Thankfully, you can choose internal T-Mobile picks too. T-Satellite can be activated with one pick, for example. You can also skip your perks to get discounts on a new device, which could cost between one and three picks, though this option automatically sets yo up with the Flex 36-month financing terms.

Through June 2027, Apple TV is only worth one pick. That’s what I’d spend it on first, so I could enjoy shows like The Servant, Ted Lasso, The Morning Show, and more, without paying more than $55 for my one line.

The number of picks you get depends on the plan and how many lines it includes, though picks can be pooled for everyone’s benefit. Not using your picks won’t lower the sticker price of your plan, and you can’t pay to add benefits, either. Your picks still remain separate from other T-Mobile promotions.

As noted above, not using your picks doesn’t result in a discount on your plan. It’s just a missed opportunity. Picks can only be switched every 90 calendar days. That’s when customers should change their benefits to fit their next three months. However, if you want to change your pick before the next pick window opens, you could add select picks, like streaming, for a fee.

The Cloudsurfer 3 is built to make everyday runs feel smooth and effortless, even as the miles add up.

Just because you run doesn’t necessarily mean that you’re up on running shoe innovation. In recent years, there have been notable improvements in propulsion and efficiency, thanks to additions like carbon-fiber plates, smart sensors, and midsoles that promise high energy return. What’s more important than these buzzy words is: How do the shoes feel?

After all, you can have footwear designed by rocket scientists, but if you’re uncomfortable with every step, your run will be a slog, no matter the distance. You want your shoe to make the ride feel soft, which is why the On Cloudsurfer 3 stands out over so many others.

On running shoes have always been distinguished by their trademark hollow pods— the “clouds” that are specifically engineered to help create a softer underfoot experience and give the brand its unmistakable look. In the Cloudsurfer 3, On’s CloudTec Phase™ allows those pods to compress in sequence, helping absorb impact and create a smoother transition from heel to toe. This isn’t just another update to On’s running shoe line. With breakthrough Swiss engineering working beneath every step, the Cloudsurfer 3 delivers the brand’s softest ride yet.

An option for everyday and recovery runs as well as longer training sessions, the Cloudsurfer 3 combines CloudTec® Phase with On’s brand-new SurrealTM Superfoam. According to the brand’s internal testing, the pairing provides 15.5 percent deeper compression—and therefore more impact absorption—than the superfoam alone. The result is a softer landing that can help daily miles feel easier on the legs, particularly over long distances.

According to the same internal testing, the Cloudsurfer 3 is also predicted to deliver a 9.9 percent improvement in smooth-ride sensation. That figure reflects the way the reimagined CloudTec® Phase orientation enhances the rolling motion of the foot, helping each step flow more naturally into the next from landing to toe-off.

You’re also getting a newly engineered mesh upper in the Cloudsurfer 3, which provides a secure fit that adapts to your foot while still offering breathability and support. That breathability helps keep heat and moisture from building up, reducing the conditions that can lead to chafing, blisters, and hot spots.

Even the cushioning around the collar and tongue has been updated, so you get a cloud-like feel as soon as you slip them on. It doesn’t hurt that the Cloudsurfer 3 looks cool, too.

Together, these innovations give runners what they want from an everyday shoe: a natural feel, reliable performance, and lasting comfort. The result is a smoother ride, whether you’re going long or heading out for a quick training run.

That softer feel is a big deal, considering a 2025 study found that runners who perceived their shoes as more cushioned had a lower risk of running-related injury. When a shoe feels right, you’re less likely to think about every step—or adjust the way you’re moving to compensate for discomfort. That allows you to settle into your natural stride and focus on the run itself.

Enjoyment is a major factor, too. Wearing a shoe like the Cloudsurfer 3, with its focus on cushioning, responsiveness, and comfort, can be key to unlocking your best run yet—and enjoying yourself along the way. When that happens, even a hard run can feel softer, mile after mile.

Practical guidance, which includes how-to advice and tutoring, has been the largest topic every month since March 2025 and was 28.1% in September.

According to the release’s data dictionary, seeking information covers requests for facts, questions about products and services for sale, and recipe or cooking help.

Work-related messages made up 32.2% of all messages in September. Among these, the share of messages seeking information remained between 10.7% and 12.1% each month from July 2024 through July 2026, before rising to 13.9% in August and 15.2% in September.

The work activity post organizes work-related messages into 165 activities from O*NET, a standard framework that groups tasks shared across jobs. It presents the data by quarter, covering Q1 2025 through Q3 2026, across nearly 100 countries.

Across the 74 countries with data in every quarter, the 10 most common activities averaged 51.1% of each country’s classified work messages in Q3 2026, down from 56.0% in Q1 2025. The post describes that as work-related use becoming less concentrated in the most common activities.

The post’s graph shows “Getting information,” which is one of these 165 activities. In the United States, it represented 11.6% of work messages during the first quarter of 2025. It also showed that it made up 9.9% of work messages in the third quarter of 2026. These are based on a separate classification system and represent only one country (the U.S.), so they do not measure the same thing as “seeking information” globally.

Last September, I covered the OpenAI and Harvard paper on how people use ChatGPT. It reported that seeking information grew from 14% to 24% of ChatGPT usage in the year after July 2024.

Signals utilizes the exact same classifier prompts used by the research study, according to the provided data dictionary, and both categorize the same three categories of requests under “seeking information.” Signals’ graphs also show the percentages of the same time frame for seeking information; 16.7% in July 2024 and 18.3% in June 2025, which is the final month covered by the research paper.

The two writing series have fewer differences between them than the two seeking-information series. The percentage decreased by 12 points (from 36% to 24%) in the research paper while it decreased by 8.4 points (from 34.1% to 25.7%) in Signals during the same time frame.

Approximately 1.1 million conversations were selected by the researchers using the sampling method where they took only one message from each conversation. Each share was then averaged over 28-day periods. Signals selects 1 million messages per month. This release altered previous months to be consistent with that higher number. No reason was stated or explained within the release blog or the provided data dictionary regarding the difference in the seeking information percentages.

In OpenAI’s own monthly data on consumer ChatGPT messages, approximately twenty percent of all messages are to look something up, which is roughly equivalent to the percentage for “writing.” Product questions are included in this percentage, however because OpenAI combined all three categories (fact/recipe/product) into one category, it cannot be determined how many were each.

Signals does track the various topics of conversation that occur via ChatGPT; however it did not provide any information regarding how often ChatGPT accessed external sources such as websites or cited a site in its replies.

When citing percentages representing the use of ChatGPT, please reference the source and time frame. There was an almost six point difference between the 2025 paper and Signals regarding information seeking in mid-2025.

So far, each update in the Signals data dictionary’s changelog adds a quarter of data and revises earlier months. Because a figure from v3.0 may differ from the same month’s figure in a later version, be sure to note the version number whenever you include a Signals figure in a report.

#588. Scientists Discover That Eating Peanuts Every Day May Help Protect the Aging Brain

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A daily serving of peanuts may improve insulin sensitivity in the aging brain, potentially supporting memory.

Peanuts may offer more than a convenient source of protein and healthy fats. New research suggests that eating them regularly could help the aging brain respond more effectively to insulin, a hormone that plays an important role in memory, learning, and communication between nerve cells. As people grow older, the brain can become less sensitive to insulin, a change linked to age-related cognitive decline.

In a small clinical trial, researchers at Maastricht University Medical Center in the Netherlands found that eating unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts daily for 16 weeks improved insulin responsiveness in brain regions involved in memory. The study, conducted by the Institute of Nutrition and Translational Research in Metabolism (NUTRIM), was published in Food & Function.

“The hormone insulin plays an important role in the brain, where it supports memory and other cognitive processes. As people age, the brain may become less responsive to insulin, which has been linked to age-related cognitive decline,” says Dr. Peter Joris, the study’s senior author and an associate professor in the Department of Nutrition and Movement Sciences at NUTRIM, Maastricht University Medical Center.

The trial included 31 healthy adults aged 60 to 75. Each participant completed a 16-week period of eating peanuts and a control period without them. This randomized, controlled crossover design allowed researchers to compare each person’s results under both conditions.

During the peanut period, participants ate 60 grams (approximately two servings), or about 2.1 ounces, daily. Premeasured packets helped them follow the prescribed amount, which they could eat in one sitting, divide throughout the day, or add to meals. They were instructed to consume the peanuts in the morning or afternoon.

The changes in insulin responsiveness appeared in a cluster on the brain’s right side, encompassing parts of the lingual gyrus, hippocampus, parahippocampal gyrus, and posterior cingulate gyrus. These regions are involved in memory.

To assess the brain’s response, researchers administered insulin through the nose and measured the resulting changes in regional cerebral blood flow (CBF). They used arterial spin labeling magnetic resonance imaging (ASL-MRI), combining advanced imaging with a controlled dietary intervention lasting several months.

Earlier results from the 16-week peanut intervention, published in 2025 in Clinical Nutrition, showed a 3.6% increase in global cerebral blood flow, which supplies the brain with oxygen and nutrients, alongside a 5.8% improvement in verbal memory.

The team also assessed cognitive performance using the Cambridge Neuropsychological Test Automated Battery (CANTAB) and measured intrahepatic lipid (IHL) content, or liver fat, with abdominal MRI.

“Our study found that daily peanut consumption beneficially affected insulin responsiveness in memory-related brain regions in older adults,” says Dr. Peter Joris, the study’s senior author and an associate professor at Maastricht University Medical Center. “These findings suggest that enhanced regional brain insulin responsiveness may contribute to the cognitive benefits of unsalted, skin-roasted peanut intake.”

“Multiple nutrients present in peanuts, including unsaturated fats, dietary fiber, vitamins and minerals, and bioactive phytochemicals, could potentially contribute to the observed brain benefits,” adds Joris. “Peanuts are also a good source of plant-based protein and are especially rich in L-arginine. L-arginine is a key precursor of nitric oxide (NO), which has been reported to facilitate learning and memory performance.”

Reference: “Daily consumption of unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts beneficially affects regional brain insulin responsiveness in older adults: results of a longer-term randomized controlled trial” by Kerkhof, Lucia, Mensink, Ronald P., Plat, Jogchum, Nijssen, Kevin M. R. and Joris, Peter J., 30 September 2026, Food & Function. DOI: 10.1039/d6fo03208g

#2958. Kili Day 1 – The Adventure Begins

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Mon Oct 19 3.0 miles Climb 1461 ft From Lemosho gate (elev 7742ft, 2360m) To Mti Mkubwa camp (elev 8694ft, 2650m)

I am actually writing these blog entries after I have completed my Kilimanjaro trek. Every day, I took notes and filtered photos into albums as the basis for these posts. I hope you enjoy it and it gives you a better feel for what it is like to trek Kilimanjaro.

There are many different routes up Kilimanjaro. And there are seemingly countless tour companies willing to guide you on them.

I knew that if I traveled this far, I wanted to optimize my chances of summiting Kili. The longer routes provide more time to acclimate to the altitude. Online consensus landed heavily towards the Lemosho 8 day trek for its length and the beauty of the route. That was enough for me.

Now I just needed to choose the telling company. I ended up choosing climbkili.com for several reasons, and I’m happy with that decision.

Most companies also provide an option to add on a safari to the end of the trip. That was not a factor for me. My friend Becky is joining me after we finish for a few weeks of safaris and touring Africa.

I wanted to make sure that my tell was supporting the local economy, so I wanted to book with as locally owned company that abides by KPAP (Kilimanjaro Porters Assistance Project) to ensure the porters are treated fairly.

A couple things then tipped my towards climbkili. On summit night and they use a camp at 16,000 foot elevation instead of the far more commonly used camp at 15,200 feet. That would provide a little more acclimation time and shorten that final summit hike. As someone who has hiked the PCT, I didn’t need luxury. Climbkili seemed focused more on the basics than on luxury, so they weren’t as expensive as many other companies. Finally, they seemed well established.

Dave and Joel are brothers-in-law from Wisconsin. They are both in their 70s and have extensive experience with endurance activities: bicycle touring, cross country skiing, snowshoeing, ultramarathons. Jake and James are both in their 30s. Jake is from New York State and has done lots of outdoor and backcountry activities. James is from Toronto and had enthusiasm and a “let’s do it” attitude towards almost anything and everything.

Herment was our lead guide from climbkili.com. He grew up in the Kilimanjaro region. To me, he looked to be about 30 years old, so I was a little surprised when he said he had 20 years experience working on the mountain. Talking to him later, I found out he was in his mid-40s. His experience and leadership really showed throughout the trip.

Florian and Dismus were our two assistant guides. Florian had 10 years experience on the mountain, and Dismus had 37 years. Dismus is almost as old as me.

Me. If you are reading this, then you likely know that I am in my early 60s and hiked the PCT starting in 2025. I help lead a couple scout groups. I love being outside.

Hikers and Guides. front: Florian and Herment; back: Dismus, Dave, Jake (back), James, Joel, me

At 4PM the afternoon before our trek was set to begin (Oct 18), we had a meeting with our guides. Herment have an overview of the whole trek, including conditions to expect. He emphasized what gear we would need, giving us an opportunity to rent anything we didn’t already have.

It was here that I met Dave and Joel and heard that the other two trekkers would be arriving later that evening. I was glad I had arrived early and had time to adjust to the time zone and organize gear at a relaxed pace.

At 7AM, we were to meet in the hotel lobby ready to go. We could leave a bag at the hotel. After itemizing things, we could also leave important documents (like passport and vaccination records) and cash in the hotel safe. I carried copies of important documents on me along with photos of them on my phone.

When I dropped my duffel next to all the others, it looked very small. Herment seemed somewhat disbelieving that I had the needed clothes and gear, thinking I had possibly left something in the hotel room by accident. I showed him my sleeping bag, pad, and down puffy jacket. I guess my worries about my duffel being too big were in vain.

I met Jake, who arrived at 3am from Zanzibar. His flight was 5 hrs late, then it took a couple hours to get through paperwork at the airport. And just to further decorative him of sleep, the shuttle hit a donkey on the ride from the airport, damaging the radiator. He finally made it to the hotel after getting picked up by a van from another tour company.

I also met James, but he didn’t have nearly as interesting a travel story as Jake. He also didn’t have as much Backcountry experience and wasn’t as sure exactly what gear he should bring.

Driving along the road from Arusha towards Moshi, the van kept making quick stops, where a couple more crew members would hop on.

At one point, we stopped and pushed the broken down van (from Jake’s trip) off the road. I found it interesting that it had been in the road since last night, and traffic just went around it for hours on this 2-lane road. It reinforced my observations that people here just adapt to whatever happens around them.

We were told not to expect any large animals, but the van slowed for us to look at a giraffe that was having a meal at a tree right on the side of the road.

At our trailhead, Lemosho Gate (elev 7742ft, 2360m), it became clear that we had a support team of 15-20 people. Herment noticed Thimbleberry (my little plush pika) poking out of my pocket and seemed to like her. She would become a sort of team mascot, showing up in most of the group photos.

We had lunch at the trailhead before heading out on trail.

A common Swahili expression that everyone becomes extremely familiar with is “pole pole” (poh-lay poh-lay), meaning “slowly slowly.” It is a reminder to stay within our body’s current limits, allowing it to acclimate to the altitude instead of break down.

When they say pole pole, it seemed to me that they mean pole pole sana (very slowly). We hiked as a group with guides in the front and back. We were close enough together and going slowly enough that I often felt it was more difficult. I had to pay more attention to not stepping on the person in front of me than observing what was around me.

We got glimpses of a couple Colobus monkeys in a tree, but tree branches made it to difficult to get a clear photo.

Dave, Joel, myself, James and Jake at Lemosho Gate

Once in Mti Mkubwa camp (elev 8694ft, 2650m), they had hot drinks and popcorn for us. We stayed in the dining tent talking. Dinner was soup, bread, potatoes, and pasta with a meat sauce.

After dinner, they gave us a rundown on tomorrow. They will wake us up around 6 by bringing us a hot drink in our tent. We pack our duffel, eat breakfast, then hike. They also checked our pulse and blood oxygen in what would become a daily safety ritual.

The whole crew at Mti Mkubwa the next morning
