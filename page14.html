Polymarket temporarily halted trading on its international platform Thursday because of a security incident that compromised a third-party provider, the company said.

Trading was paused for about an hour and resumed Thursday afternoon. The company has promised to fully reimburse any affected traders.

The prediction market platform said it halted trading “out of an abundance of caution” after identifying and fixing the issue affecting its international platform.

“We’ll make this right and are fully reimbursing all affected traders,” the company posted on X once trading had resumed.

Polymarket said it referred the incident to a law enforcement agency in the United Kingdom.

Reports have linked the source of the incident to DebugBear, a UK-based company offering website performance monitoring tools.

DebugBear's own website was unavailable Thursday afternoon and the company posted on X that it was “impacted by an ongoing security incident.” It also advised clients who were using one of its products to remove the embed code from their websites. The company did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

It was not immediately clear to what extent traders using the popular prediction market platform were impacted, or the breadth of the impact. The company said anyone who was impacted “will be made whole.”

President Donald Trump was briefed by top military officials on options for Iran, as administration officials gave Arab Gulf states a heads-up that attacks would resume after the midterm elections, according to a report.

Trump met Thursday with the top U.S. commander in the Middle East, Adm. Brad Cooper, at the White House, along with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Dan Caine, sources told The Wall Street Journal.

U.S. officials have warned several Gulf Arab nations to be ready for a short, intense military campaign after November 3, with Trump looking to potentially target Iranian infrastructure and nuclear sites, two diplomats briefed on the matter told the Associated Press on Wednesday, and the Journal previously reported.

“We may do another little Epic Fury,” Trump said at a rally last week, referring to the heavy bombing campaign by the U.S. and Israel this past spring. “Or they’ll agree.”

Trump and Cooper rarely meet in person, meaning Thursday’s meeting could have been about significant military actions, according to the report. The Pentagon and U.S. Central Command, which Cooper heads, declined to comment to the Journal.

The diplomats stressed that Trump has not made a final decision on whether to carry out new strikes on Iran. However, Trump has said he is weighing new military action once the critical midterm elections are over.

The U.S. has not carried out a strike against Iran since early September, but the conflict has since spread, now including Iran-backed Houthi rebels attacking Saudi Arabia’s oil infrastructure and airports.

While recently campaigning for Republicans across the country, Trump has tried to convince voters that the war will end soon — while also threatening refreshed attacks after the election.

“Somebody asked me before, when will the war in Iran end?” Trump said in Lubbock, Texas, on Tuesday. “I said, ‘Whenever I want it to end.’ It’ll be very quick, but we have to have it end properly, with total and complete — a total statement: they will never have a nuclear weapon.”

The report comes amid increased chatter that strikes will resume after midterms, as the $47 billion war against Iran remains at a standstill without a ceasefire or peace deal in sight.

Connecticut Democratic Sen. Chris Murphy spoke out about Trump’s reported plans to resume the war with Iran after the election.

“Trump is reportedly planning to restart war with Iran after the election,” he wrote on X on Thursday. “Some of us have been briefed on the war plan. Republican strategist Karl Rove also said that he expects Trump to resume strikes post-Election Day, telling Fox News’ Brian Kilmeade Trump has “sort of signaled it.”

“If you look at what’s happening on the ground — two aircraft carriers in the region, a third one on the way, plus enough ground forces, plus enough air assets. If I were the Iranians, I’d be certainly thinking that’s what’s coming my way after the election,” Rove said on Wednesday.

#262. Palestinians injured, arrested in Israeli raids across West Bank

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

This comes as more than 3,300 Palestinians are forced from homes in Hebron in Israeli military offensive.

Israeli forces and settlers have escalated pogroms and raids across the occupied West Bank, attacking and injuring Palestinians, restricting their movement, carrying out arrests and demolishing homes.

This comes as a military offensive and siege on the Hebron governorate entered its seventh day – an operation that has displaced or besieged hundreds of Palestinian families and kept tens of thousands of students out of classrooms.

In the Qalandiya refugee camp just north of occupied East Jerusalem, Israeli forces carried out a raid that triggered clashes on Thursday. Two children were wounded, the Palestine Red Crescent Society said.

It said its teams treated a 15-year-old boy who was shot with live ammunition in both legs, and a child, aged 12, who was injured by live-bullet fragments. Both were taken to hospital.

In a separate incident, Israeli soldiers and settlers beat disabled Palestinian Mohammad Ahmad Ayesh Thawabta in the village of al-Maniya, southeast of Bethlehem, the official Palestinian news agency Wafa reported.

In the northern West Bank, Israeli forces detained 13 Palestinians, including three brothers, in raids before dawn on Thursday, the Palestinian Prisoners Media Office and local sources said. Most of the raids were in the Nablus governorate, where soldiers searched homes and harassed families.

In Hebron, Israeli forces continued raids and closures that began last week after two Israeli soldiers were killed in a gun attack. Both Palestinian suspects were also killed.

Hebron Governor Khaled Dudin said on Thursday that 537 families, comprising 3,361 people, have been forced from their homes, Wafa reported.

They include 232 infants, 326 older people, 147 pregnant or breastfeeding women and 213 people with chronic illnesses. Municipal figures put the total number affected, including those under siege, at about 5,000 people from 780 families.

Dudin said the offensive has disrupted education for about 262,000 students across 589 schools in the governorate this week, forcing schools to move to remote learning. Officials estimated daily economic losses at 10 million to 12 million shekels ($3.2m to $3.9m).

The Palestinian Prisoner’s Society said Israeli forces detained 110 Palestinians in the governorate from Friday to Wednesday. Soldiers beat two men in Dura and Wad al-Saman and arrested two brothers while they were ploughing their land east of Yatta, it said. Israeli forces also raided homes in Taffuh and Halhul, and settlers attacked vehicles at the city’s closed northern entrance, it added.

In nearby Masafer Yatta, armed settlers attacked US journalist Jasper Nathaniel and two activists in the village of Umm al-Khair on Wednesday. Russian activist Adele Shoko said on X that the settlers beat and threatened them and stole their equipment and Nathaniel was pepper-sprayed and arrested.

The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) said Israeli forces are still operating in Hebron, blocking access and imposing tight restrictions on movement across the governorate.

They are “disrupting Palestinians’ access to livelihoods, education and healthcare. Most displaced families are staying with relatives,” OCHA said, calling for the prevention of further forced displacements and demolitions.

Israeli forces also demolished memorials to Palestinians in several parts of the Hebron governorate on Thursday. In Dura, south of Hebron city, soldiers fired a heavy barrage of tear gas before bulldozing a mural bearing the images of several of the town’s dead, including Bajis Abu Atwan, who was killed in the 1970s.

The targeting of memorials “appears to be a new pattern recently adopted by Israeli forces across all West Bank governorates – though at a higher rate here in Hebron”, Al Jazeera’s Muntasir Nassar said, reporting from Dura.

Israeli military and settler attacks across the West Bank have intensified since Israel’s war on Gaza began in October 2023.

Marques Ackerman's departure was another peculiar moment in the series. (AP: Halden Krog)

If there was any doubt how much the Cape Town Test matters to Pat Cummins's Australian team, one only needed to clock the cooing pack of them careening towards Cameron Green and Mitchell Starc an hour into the evening session of day three at Newlands.

The catch Green claimed to send his teammates into raptures was straightforward enough, its genesis and aftermath a little more dramatic.

Proteas opener Marques Ackerman, set fair on 62 and attacking everything Australia threw at him, had tried to limbo away from a Starc bouncer that followed him like a heat-seeking missile. Not quite agile enough with his Matrix manoeuvre, Ackerman faintly edged the ball flush into his faceguard, from which it ballooned into the cordon.

Unsure of the deflection and with his ears still ringing from the ding on his grill, Ackerman walked over to his skipper Temba Bavuma to discuss a review of the umpire's call, perhaps as surprised as the Australians when Travis Head arrived on the scene to inquire after his health.

But, in all the chaos and confusion, the crucial 15 seconds had passed before Ackerman could signal for a review. He had to depart.

It has been that sort of series, really; so many periods of ascendancy abruptly terminated by moments of genius, madness and confusion, and so many subversions of the pattern recognition Test cricket followers can normally rely on to impose a sense of order on what they're looking at.

South Africa finished the so-called moving day at 4-140 and 40 runs ahead. It would have wanted to be further in front. It could easily have been far worse off.

Earlier, in the latter stages of its first innings of 429, Australia probably let an extra 100 runs go begging. It sets up a perfect scenario even for those Australian fans who are not normally night owls: a thrilling finish on Friday night into Saturday morning.

On a pulsating day that produced 243 runs and 10 wickets, there was a second Test century for the recalled Josh Inglis and 80 for Alex Carey, both of whom fell to the off-spin of Simon Harmer.

In a Test featuring plenty of runs, only Josh Inglis has reached triple figures. (Getty Images: Grant Pitcher/Gallo Images)

An obscurity to most Australians before this series, and tipped to be dropped after the first Test, Harmer was in the thick of everything again with four wickets, two catches, a busted finger and plenty of chatter.

Youngster Cooper Connolly, looking entirely at home at Test level, made a patient 29 from 83 deliveries and might have kicked on further with better support from Australia's tail; the loss of 6-59 to finish off Australia's first innings was, as Pat Cummins often phrases such things, not ideal.

Yet it was still a 100-run lead, and early in South Africa's second innings you got the sense the game was about to break right open for an early finish.

In quick succession, Aiden Markram, who has been a thorn in Australia's side, lazily slapped a loose one from Starc into the clutches of Head at mid-off, and David Bedingham, seemingly rattled by a Starc bouncer that cannoned into his chest, holed out with another inexplicable hook.

The Proteas were 2-31, still 69 runs behind, and the hyenas were circling.

Via the counter-attack of Ackerman, then in a slow and steady partnership between Temba Bavuma and Matthew Breetzke, South Africa began its rally. Yet there was one last twist in store with barely 15 minutes left in the day's play.

Having tortured Bavuma for a few hours with his sharp turn out of the footmarks, Nathan Lyon removed the Proteas skipper again, this time a dipping off break that snuck between bat and pad to castle the lunging batsman. Bavuma is not quite to Lyon what Daryll Cullinan once was to Shane Warne, but he is not far off.

Proteas captain Temba Bavuma posed a major threat to Australia. (Getty Images: Grant Pitcher/Gallo Images)

On the matter of hoodoos, much has been made in recent days of Cummins's record-breaking streak without a wicket.

It continued today, despite periods in which the Aussie skipper bowled with genuine pace and guile, although his desperation to break his drought was made clear by one ill-advised review. Even when it is 1.6 metre Bavuma in the firing line, it is always difficult to get an LBW call when it hits above the batsman's knee roll.

In truth, the odd brain fade was perfectly excusable due to the ratcheting up of the atmosphere as this game progressed.

Some testiness and conflict in this series was inevitable, but it has manifested in curious ways. Each team's most vocal player — Marnus Labuschagne for Australia, Matthew Breetzke for the Proteas — has been its worst performed.

Breetzke, in fact, finally started to come good late on day three, and will resume tomorrow believing he is the man to turn the game for his country.

Given the way these two teams have echoed each other's efforts in the past few weeks, perhaps that suggests one last tantalising and slightly mad prospect in the Australian chase: how about Marnus Labuschagne as the last-minute hero?

UNITED NATIONS, Oct 29 - Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis said on Thursday that the recently signed maritime deal between Libya's eastern parliament and Turkey was "blatantly illegal, null and void," adding that it "completely disregards the geography."

Mitsotakis, speaking to Reuters as Greece ends its UN Security Council presidency, also called for an urgent "international truce" to protect shipping in the Black Sea after the sinking of a Greek-owned container vessel on Wednesday.

Libya's eastern parliament last week approved a maritime boundaries deal with Turkey, drawing condemnation from Athens, but also Egypt, Bahrain and the United Arab Emirates, as a threat to regional security and international legal order.

"It completely disregards the geography, the reality that there are Greek islands -- they seem to completely disappear in the thinking of Turkey and Libya," Mitsotakis said, accusing them of attempting to create a "fait accompli" that ignores the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea.

Turkish President Tayyip Erdogan has said that reactions from the EU and others to the approval of the agreement, originally agreed in 2019, had no legitimate basis, defending Ankara's policy in the eastern Mediterranean.

Since the 2019 Libya-Turkey deal, Greece has awarded a license to a consortium led by Chevron to look for gas off the island of Crete and has sought a separate agreement with Libya on demarcating their maritime zones.

Mitsotakis assured "there's no risk that these explorations will be in any way challenged."

Referring to Libya and Greece as "natural neighbors," Mitsotakis, who first became prime minister in 2019 and is in his second term, urged the African country to negotiate directly with Athens and take the discussions "very, very seriously."

Mitsotakis, 58, spoke to Reuters after chairing a council debate on maritime security, only hours after a Greek container ship sank after being hit by naval drones off Bulgaria in the latest strike on ships in the western Black Sea this month.

Greek-operated ships are among the world's largest fleets of tankers and are pivotal for trade across the Black Sea region, whose waters are shared by Bulgaria, Georgia, Romania and Turkey, as well as warring Russia and Ukraine, which Moscow invaded in February 2022.

"We do need some sort of international truce when it comes to the freedom of navigation," said Mitsotakis, adding that it was of "utmost priority" to allow for exports of grain from the region into the world through the Black Sea.

"Greek shipowners would be the first to resume trading in the Black Sea, also from Ukraine, assuming a safe passage is guaranteed," he said, stressing the rising costs of shipping as chartering and insurance expenses have "gone through the roof."

It was not immediately clear who had carried out the attack, which added to growing concerns about the impact of Russia's war in Ukraine on commercial shipping in the area.

Mitsotakis said that Ukraine was financially dependent on exports of its products: "They had a bumper harvest, but the grain is now sitting in silos, in danger of rotting."

"Unfortunately, for the moment, Russia doesn't seem to be interested in engaging in a substantive discussion," he said.

Mitsotakis also called on the European Union to bolster its naval mission to protect shipping in the Red Sea called Aspides, which was launched in 2024 to protect from attacks by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis.

Intensifying hostilities between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia have disrupted oil supplies and heightened the risk of a wider regional conflict, eight months since the US-Israeli strikes on Iran.

"My argument is simple: the European Union has an interest in having a concrete presence with hard assets in this part of the world, and we need more countries to participate in Aspides," Mitsotakis told Reuters.

He said Greece, Italy and France have deployed "assets" as part of the mission and that was "clearly not enough." Diplomats told Reuters as of late September there were six warships operating as part of the operation.

The Houthis said this week they had attacked Saudi military aircraft and repelled an advance by Saudi-backed forces near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, an important Red Sea shipping route.

"If we really want to send the message also to the Gulf countries that we take their security seriously, but we also protect the critical arteries and waterways that are absolutely indispensable for European trade, we should be willing to deploy more assets to this part of the world," Mitsotakis said.

Asked about US President Donald Trump's move earlier this month to lift sanctions on Russian diesel exports, Mitsotakis reaffirmed Greek support for sanctions on Moscow.

"We believe that we need to put economic pressure and impose more financial pain on Russia to send a very clear signal that as long as they prolong this war, they will suffer very specific economic consequences," he said. REUTERS

TEL AVIV, October 29. /TASS/. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud party has won the majority of votes in the October 27 parliamentary election, with all ballots counted, according to preliminary figures released by the Central Elections Committee (CEC).

#794. As farm fuel costs rise, clear solar might offer a solution

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Graeme Sandral and Matt Muller are helping lead new research into the use of transparent solar panels. (ABC News: Emily Doak)

Charles Sturt University is investigating transparent solar panels for Australian agriculture.

Researchers say the panels could improve animal welfare, increase production and help farmers save money.

The first results from the research are expected in late 2027.

New research into the use of transparent solar panels in agriculture is being touted as a possible solution for cash-strapped farmers dealing with rising diesel costs.

The panels have gaps between the photovoltaic cells, allowing light to pass through. The Charles Sturt University (CSU) study will look at their use in livestock and horticulture management.

CSU researcher Graham Sandral said in one project, the panels were installed in a sheep feedlot previously reliant on diesel generators.

"[They're] designed to capture energy, which drives the automatic feeders, and allows enough light through so that in winter, sheep who happen to be sitting under the shade are still exposed to light and some warmth," he said.

Transparent solar panels have been installed on a sheep feedlot as part of research into their use. (Supplied: Charles Sturt University)

The sustainability director at CSU's AgriPark, Matt Muller, said apart from animal welfare benefits, there was growing interest from farmers in reducing input costs by switching to renewable electricity.

"We've seen it with the price of diesel this year," he said.

The panels vary in transparency from 10 to 48 per cent, allowing for variables such as location, industry, and generation need.

A secondary project will look at using the panels in vineyards.

The hope is they will act as a greenhouse-like roof over the vines and be movable to allow for pruning.

The company behind the technology, Sharp EIT, said the panels are already being used on farms in France, Austria and Belgium.

Transparent solar panels are used in some European farms.  (Supplied: Sharp EIT)

Mr Sandral said it was important the panels were tested in Australian conditions.

He said the panels had the potential to provide multifaceted benefits for grape growers.

"You're protecting [the crop] from hail and other natural occurrences, which can bring potential damage, " he said.

"But, you're also producing power at the winery, that can be utilised from the paddock, back into the wine production side of things."

The transparent solar panels are double the price of regular solar panels, which retail between $80 and $300 depending on size and wattage.

Farmers for Climate Action outreach officer Peter Holding welcomed the research but said it would be vital it proved the panels delivered a return on investment and productivity gains.

"The cost will come into it, because that's one of the major barriers to people putting them up in the first place," he said.

"If they're twice as expensive, I can't see how a transparent panel would benefit anybody, putting them on a roof or putting them on unused ground."

Mr Holding said while the panels could potentially drive down diesel costs, the electrification of farm machinery such as tractors and headers was where the biggest savings could be made.

Peter Holding said the panels' cost could be a barrier to wider take-up of the technology.  (Supplied: Econnect Communication)

Wine Australia's environmental, social and governance (ESG) manager Julian Marchant said wineries were big users of electricity, so the panels did offer opportunities.

He said there has been an increasing uptake of solar in the sector, but questions remained.

"All the parts that make up the solar panels will need to be tested for their corrosiveness to farm chemicals," Mr Marchant said.

"The sprayers go out quite often, so we'd need to make sure that they're protected from any spray that gets captured there."

A transparent solar panel at CSU's AgriPark in Wagga Wagga. (ABC News: Emily Doak)

Mr Muller acknowledged there were land use tensions in regional Australia when it came to agriculture and renewable energy generation.

"It's important that we demonstrate how they can work together and how the benefits can go for farming," he said.

"I think if we get that, it will prove to individuals that this is something worthwhile to do and you can do it on your own property as well."

Monitoring in the livestock project will start next month, with the analysis of results expected in late 2027.
