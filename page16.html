#283. Dorong Transformasi Bisnis, Pendapatan Garuda (GIAA) Tumbuh 13,2%

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Jakarta, CNBC Indonesia - PT Garuda Indonesia (Persero) Tbk (GIAA) berhasil membukukan pendapatan konsolidasi sebesar US$2,71 miliar di sepanjang sembilan bulan pertama 2026. Jumlah tersebut tumbuh 13,2% secara tahunan atau meningkat US$316,4 juta dibandingkan periode yang sama tahun sebelumnya.

Pertumbuhan tersebut mencerminkan perkembangan positif dari agenda transformasi bisnis yang tengah dijalankan Perseroan, ditopang pertumbuhan pendapatan layanan penerbangan berjadwal, meningkatnya kontribusi bisnis non-penerbangan, serta pemulihan kapasitas dan keandalan operasional. Dari sisi komposisi pendapatan, bisnis penerbangan berjadwal masih menjadi kontributor utama pertumbuhan, dengan kenaikan 12,6% atau setara US$233,2 juta. Sementara itu, pendapatan penerbangan non-berjadwal meningkat 2,1% atau US$6,3 juta.

Direktur Utama Garuda Indonesia Thomas Oentoro menyampaikan bahwa perkembangan kinerja hingga September 2026 memberikan indikasi positif terhadap arah transformasi, khususnya dalam memperkuat produktivitas dan mengoptimalkan potensi bisnis di seluruh Grup. "Keberlanjutan pertumbuhan Garuda Indonesia memerlukan fondasi bisnis penerbangan yang semakin kuat, sekaligus kontribusi yang lebih besar dari seluruh ekosistem usaha Grup. Meningkatnya pendapatan bisnis non-penerbangan mencerminkan potensi pengembangan kapabilitas dan sinergi antarentitas usaha yang terus kami optimalkan sebagai bagian dari transformasi, guna memperkuat ketahanan bisnis dan mendukung pemulihan kinerja keuangan secara berkelanjutan," ujar Thomas dalam keterangan resmi, Kamis (29/10/2026).

Menurut Thomas, optimalisasi potensi seluruh entitas usaha juga menjadi bagian dari strategi transformasi yang dijalankan melalui dukungan berkelanjutan Danantara sebagai pemegang saham, khususnya dalam memperkuat produktivitas dan efektivitas pemanfaatan aset. "Dengan dukungan Danantara, kami terus berfokus pada peningkatan produktivitas, optimalisasi nilai ekonomi dari aset dan kapabilitas yang dimiliki, serta penguatan sinergi antarentitas usaha. Langkah ini diarahkan untuk memastikan setiap lini bisnis dapat memberikan kontribusi yang semakin optimal terhadap pertumbuhan dan ketahanan kinerja Grup," lanjutnya. Di luar bisnis penerbangan, pendapatan dari lini usaha non-penerbangan mencatatkan pertumbuhan lebih tinggi, yakni sebesar 31,3% menjadi US$322,8 juta, atau meningkat US$77 juta secara tahunan. Perkembangan ini mengindikasikan semakin besarnya potensi diversifikasi bisnis Grup dalam memperluas sumber pertumbuhan pendapatan. Kinerja tersebut dicapai di tengah tekanan biaya industri yang masih signifikan, terutama akibat kenaikan harga bahan bakar penerbangan seiring volatilitas pasar energi global dan dinamika geopolitik di Timur Tengah. Sepanjang periode pelaporan, beban bahan bakar Grup meningkat 42,9% secara tahunan atau setara US$309,8 juta, sehingga turut mendorong kenaikan total beban operasional sebesar 15,7%. Meski demikian, pertumbuhan beban operasional di luar bahan bakar dapat dijaga pada kisaran 3,1%, lebih rendah dibandingkan pertumbuhan pendapatan sebesar 13,2%. Kondisi tersebut mencerminkan perkembangan positif dalam pengendalian biaya dan peningkatan efisiensi operasional Perseroan. Dalam periode yang sama, Garuda Indonesia Group mencatatkan laba usaha sebesar US$97,2 juta, sementara rugi periode berjalan Grup secara konsolidasi menyusut 3,0% secara tahunan menjadi US$179,3 juta. Perkembangan tersebut mencerminkan semakin besarnya peluang komersial dari kapabilitas bisnis Grup, sekaligus memperkuat upaya diversifikasi pendapatan untuk melengkapi kontribusi bisnis penerbangan penumpang.Sejalan dengan penguatan pendapatan, inisiatif pemulihan armada turut mendukung peningkatan aktivitas penerbangan dan keandalan operasional Garuda Indonesia Group sepanjang sembilan bulan pertama 2026. #186. Steve Eisman gets more conservative with portfolio as AI trade nears 'tipping point'

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Steve Eisman of "The Big Short" fame thinks the artificial intelligence trade is near a "tipping point," but he's not ready to outright bet against it yet.

"I've gotten much more conservative in my own portfolio. We have shorted against the box a whole bunch of our positions, so that our risk is much, much, much lower. But I'm not, like, net short any," he told CNBC's "Squawk Box" on Thursday.

Eisman's primary concern pertains to the frontier algorithm models, which are exploiting a climate of fear around their technologies, he believes, in order to set up a competitive regulatory moat against more cost-efficient products and to obscure some faltering fundamentals. Bigwigs in the tech industry have issued similar warnings.

Anthropic left its latest revenue figures blank in its initial IPO filing, but Eisman believes they will show a significant deceleration from the prior quarter. Other figures in the report point to capital efficiency issue, he said.

The ratio of revenue-to-equipment-expense was 2.08 in the second quarter and 1.7 in the third, marking a significant change in trajectory, Eisman said, citing an analysis he'd been given.

Yet, Eisman said he was scared to make a definitive bet against the AI trade because of how intertwined it is with the market as a whole, calling it "impossible" for the technology sector to be siloed away from the broader equity market and the rest of the economy.

"So much of the market is on the other side, and it's still reading tea leaves. I think we're getting closer. I think the numbers from Google scared people," he said.

Alphabet's stock tanked after reporting earnings on Wednesday, following a big pullback in its core advertising business as AI queries supplant internet search across its various products.

Google executives said Wednesday they were running experiments to figure out what to do about it.

"It's worth noting the strong growth in AI mode queries had an impact because of the lower monetization we are seeing in AI mode," Philipp Schindler, Google's chief business officer, said. "Our ad experiments have shown promising results."

Portions of Anthropic's recently filed S-1 struck Eisman as "extremely negative" in light of the fact that it didn't contain many metrics for the upcoming quarter that would give a clearer picture of the company's financial situation.

#245. 4 tips on how to get started with consumer-friendly AI agents

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Agentic artificial intelligence is gaining popularity far beyond tech circles, with Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic and other companies going head-to-head in the surging market for consumer-friendly AI agents.

While the leading AI labs have offered tools with agentic capabilities for years, agents that are designed to work proactively on a user’s behalf for tasks from buying groceries to managing to-do lists have taken off this year.

For anyone getting started, it can be overwhelming to understand the differences between the companies’ various offerings and which agents might be best suited for specific tasks.

Here are some pointers on where to begin if you’re looking to leverage agents in your personal or professional life — or both.

Don’t just use one agent; opt for a ‘stack’

Many people in the tech world turn to multiple agents from different AI labs for both their work and home lives. Why? Because “there’s not one holy grail-like agent right now that could do everything,” said Jose Artigas, a business consultant working on AI strategy.

Artigas and many others who use AI agents regularly rely on a “stack” of them, a term that refers to a combination of many tools and technologies.

Each company’s consumer-facing agent has a slightly different target function, and they vary in accessibility. Some are free, some require a monthly subscription and others are still in testing modes or otherwise not available to everyone. There are also restrictions on their availability in countries outside of the U.S.

Experts say it’s best not to rely solely on one agent if you’re looking to maximize their output and productivity.

“What I like to do is to have this system of checks and balances in place where one agent does the work and then I let the other one critique and criticize it, and that way, they find mistakes, correct themselves and become better,” said Clara Gigling, founder of Oudae Labs, a research lab building bespoke tools for agents.

Capitalize on each agent’s strengths and weaknesses

Each of the major AI companies markets their consumer-facing agents in slightly different ways, often focusing on its ability to help manage either a busy work life or a busy personal life.

Anthropic said it built Claude Duet for “professionals and small business owners with more on their plates than they can track.” In contrast, Meta said Muse can help with tasks from “saving money and improving your health to smart shopping and helping you strengthen your relationships.”

Claude Duet, OpenAI’s Dots and xAI’s Grok Bot have each been described as aiming to be like a colleague, or even a chief of staff, whereas Muse and Instinct, from the startup Spear Street Technology, have been described as more like personal assistants. But that’s not to say any of them are incapable of helping with tasks outside those realms.

Artigas said he’d recommend looking at Claude Duet as key for someone working on a side hustle or launching a new business, Dots as a debugger and problem solver if they’re engaging in any specialized technical work, and Instinct for help with things like sending tedious emails or booking reservations and travel plans.

Those are the three consumer-facing agents he uses most often, but Artigas said Muse would be a good place to start for a novice because it’s “very user-friendly,” with many prompts early on to give people ideas of how they can use it.

Trial and error is part of the process

Your preferences for how you want to interact with an agent, how your agent communicates, and your own assessment of the quality of its work should all factor into which agents you’d like to use — and which tasks you’d like to assign to an agent.

Experts recommend testing out agents you’re curious about to help in reaching those determinations.

To begin this testing, Gigling said it can be helpful to think about tasks you consistently do and then choose some to delegate to agents. It is important to be specific — giving the agent a detailed goal and the required context and tools to achieve it, she said.

“Start with one annoying, repeated thing that you keep putting off,” Artigas said, with the caveat that it has to be an online task. An agent can’t handle the pile of laundry you’ve been meaning to fold or the junk drawer that needs organizing. A long email you don’t want to write or a flight you’ve been slow to book are good jumping off points.

“You just have to experience the different agents and see what works best” for you, Artigas said.

Determine your comfort level with sharing personal information

For an agent to most effectively handle routine tasks or act like a proactive assistant, it needs to have access to a slew of information about you.

Your inbox, your calendar, your personal identification information and even your credit card number and bank account are all par for the course if you want an agent to sort through your deluge of emails, book your flight or do some holiday shopping.

This has raised significant privacy concerns for some.

“Curiosity is always a great starting point, just trying and playing around with it,” Gigling said, but it’s also important to “be mindful” when sharing private information.

Many companies offering consumer-facing personal agents have worked to address those concerns, with privacy policies that outline how agents operate on their own dedicated cloud computers and how users can control their memory retention.

#286. Island Energy: No plans to remove gas standing charge

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Islands Energy Group is the primary gas utility and energy service provider in all three crown dependencies

The company which provides energy to households on the Isle of Man said it has no plans to change the charging structure, following announcements in the UK Autumn Budget regarding the removal of standing charges.

On Wednesday, Chancellor John Healy announced the removal of standing charges - fixed daily fees paid regardless of usage - from household gas bills in England, Scotland and Wales, with costs added to the unit rate instead.

Island Energy said any proposed changes would be subject to consultation with the Communications and and Utilities Regulatory Authority (CURA) on the island.

A spokesperson warned removing standing charges may benefit some customers but could see others pay more.

"The cost currently covered by the standing charge would instead be recovered through the unit price of energy," explained an Island Energy spokesperson.

"This means customers who use less energy could save money, while those who use more could see their bills increase."

Islands Energy Group supplies energy to homes and business in Guernsey, Jersey and the Isle of Man.

The gas utility and energy service provider, which serves about 40,000 domestic, commercial, and industrial customers across the three islands, said any change to the charging structure would need to have the clear support of the community, the regulator and government.

Treasury Minister Michael Crowe said his department was reviewing the various measures set out by the chancellor to understand what the implications might be for the island.

"As we look towards the Isle of Man Budget, in February 2027, our priority is to strike the right balance between supporting people and businesses today and maintaining strong, sustainable public finances for the years ahead," he said.

In accordance with the Autumn Statement, the island will extend the reduction in the VAT rate, from 5% to 0% on domestic electricity supplies, until 30 June 2027.

It will also honor the Customs and Excise Agreement with the UK and continue to apply the cut in fuel duty, by 5p per litre, until 30 June 2027.

A reduced duty rate for red diesel, introduced in June until the end of the year, will also be extended to 30 June 2027.

Dengan sasaran tersebut, revitalisasi pada 2026 diharapkan berdampak pada keamanan dan kenyamanan belajar bagi 8.453.369 siswa yang tersebar di 38 provinsi, 514 kabupaten/kota, dan 6.481 kecamatan di Tanah Air. #252. The Amazon rainforest, long known as the ‘lungs of our planet,’ now releases more carbon than it absorbs

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The Amazon rainforest has long been called the “lungs of our planet,” because of how it takes in and stores billions of tons of carbon dioxide. 

But the Amazon has been losing that ability. Years of deforestation, drought, and fires have hindered just how much CO2 the rainforest can absorb. 

Now, the Amazon rainforest often releases more carbon than it takes in, a new study finds. It’s only in the “wettest years” that the rainforest is nearly carbon neutral.

The study, published in the journal Nature, was led by Brazil’s National Institute for Space Research and included an international team of researchers. They used 15 years of atmospheric carbon measurements, which had been collected by research planes.

Those years were then grouped by different characteristics, like the level of human activities—which includes deforestation, logging, or using fires to clear land—and the amount of climate stress—like drought or heat waves. 

What the research found

Some years were wet with low human activity, some had low climatic stress but high human activity, and others had “intense global climate events, such as El Niño” along with intense amounts of human activity. 

In the years where the rainforest stayed relatively wet, and where there was relatively low deforestation and few wildfires, the Amazon was nearly carbon neutral. The forest was still a slight source of carbon into the atmosphere, but it “did not significantly contribute to the global carbon balance” in those years, the researchers write.

When drought or heat were mild but deforestation and wildfires occurred more, the net carbon emissions from the Amazon “jumped dramatically,” according to a statement. 

The years with lots of climate stress coupled with lots of damaging human activity were the worst. But they were still close to the net emissions from the second group, which led researchers to say that most of the carbon emitted by the Amazon comes from human activities, predominantly land-use change.

“Global climate and climate-mediated stressors are important modulating factors, but people’s choices about how to use and live on the land surface in this region are key to understanding the movement of carbon between the land and the atmosphere,” says coauthor Jen Johnson, assistant professor of ecology and evolutionary biology at the University of Kansas, in a statement. 

This isn’t the first paper to point out that the Amazon is not the grand carbon sink it once was. A 2021 study looked at how, as huge areas of the forest are cut down or burned, its ability to capture carbon was shrinking. 

In 2023, a year of extreme drought and high temperatures, researchers showed how the Amazon became a source of carbon emissions, releasing between 10 and 170 million tons of carbon instead of absorbing it. 

Slow burn

We’ve also long known that human activity is threatening the Amazon’s survival. In 2019, there were more than 74,000 fires burning in Brazil, mostly in the Amazon—80% more than the year before.

And though we often think of these as “wildfires,” they’re primarily set by ranchers who are clearing land for cattle to meet the demand for beef.  

The more deforestation, we know, the drier the Amazon becomes, which makes it even more difficult for the remaining parts of the forest to recover. 

“For many decades, there has been concern about the idea of a tipping point, where gradual drying and gradual loss of the forest could feed back on itself in a self-reinforcing way,” Johnson said.

“That could create a transition from an ecosystem type that has stored huge amounts of carbon in living vegetation to drier forests or other ecosystem types that do not store nearly as much carbon,” she continued. “The more carbon from the Amazon that moves into the atmosphere and stays there, the more trouble our climate system is in.”

Though the outlook is dire if these activities continue, the researchers say that policy changes can help. 

“There’s a clear, policy-relevant prescription that comes from this analysis,” Johnson said. “Measures to bring deforestation down to zero are required to manage net carbon emissions from the Amazon . . . It’s not something that’s out of our control because it’s being driven by global climatic factors—it’s something very much under the control of governments and individuals.”
