#691. Empty chairs and a shock promotion as Xi warns purges will continue

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Beijing: In about a year from now, if the weight of expectations holds, Xi Jinping will secure an unprecedented fourth term as China’s paramount leader.

Having already abolished term limits in 2018, and purged factional friends and foes alike, Xi is preparing the ground for the loyalists he wants by his side when he extends his reign for another five years at the 21st Party Congress, due in the second half of next year.

One of those loyalists appears to be Zhang Shengmin. The top general, and Xi’s anti-corruption enforcer inside the military, was promoted to the powerful Politburo during a meeting of hundreds of party officials on Thursday.

Zhang is just one of two remaining members on the party’s Central Military Commission, China’s supreme military decision-making body. The other is Xi.

Over the past two years, Xi has purged the five other generals he hand-picked to serve on the commission, in a near-total decapitation of the leadership of the People’s Liberation Army (PLA). Since 2012, hundreds of other senior and lower-ranked officials throughout the party have been removed or placed under investigation as part of Xi’s campaign to root out corruption and disloyalty.

Zhang’s elevation should see him survive through to next year’s party congress, but nothing is guaranteed.

“It’s hard to read a PLA general’s future these days,” says Shengyu Wang, a researcher of Chinese elite politics at the Asia Society Policy Institute.

“It’s a case of now you see me, now you don’t.”

Zhang’s promotion is the first time in decades the party has restocked the Politburo during the middle of a five-year term. The decision was announced after the conclusion of the “fifth plenum” – a gathering of several hundred delegates on the party’s hollowed-out Central Committee, which meets annually to rubber-stamp Xi’s agenda.

For four days this week, these officials were holed up behind closed doors at a guarded hotel in Beijing to endorse the president’s directives on strict party governance and discipline.

Dozens of faces were missing from this year’s cohort.

Just 279 delegates were present, an attendance rate of 74 per cent — the lowest on record since the Mao Zedong era, and a sign of the sharpness of Xi’s scythe in his long-running anti-graft campaign.

Among the confirmed fallen were 33 officials, including former Politburo member Ma Xingrui and former senior military leader Zhang Youxia, who were expelled from the party.

The message delivered at the plenum, and intended to filter down to millions of party cadres across the country, is that there will be no slowing down.

“We must maintain a high-pressure stance against corruption,” said a communiqué released after the meeting wrapped on Thursday.

The document endorsed the strategy of “not daring to be corrupt, not being able to be corrupt, and not wanting to be corrupt” as key to strengthening party discipline – a slogan that was repeated three times.

For connoisseurs of China’s elite politics, plenums are also an empty chair watching exercise – who’s in, who’s out and who’s been promoted – with extra attention paid this year in the run-up to the big show next year.

“This is a very interesting time for China watchers to see who is actually still sitting in the meeting hall, which means they are still safe at this moment,” says Mingda Qiu, senior analyst of Eurasia Group’s China practice.

“And if they disappear or if they are not attending the plenum without an explanation, then that person might be in trouble.”

Analysts will now study, frame by frame, the shots of the meeting hall aired by China’s state broadcaster CCTV to gauge who was missing and who might be under investigation.

For months, Xi has been sketching out his vision for party building and governance, signalling his frustration that directives from Beijing were not filtering down and duly obeyed by provincial officials.

In a speech published this month, he complained about “two-faced people” who loudly commit to major party decisions “while inwardly resisting, acting arbitrarily, and even disobeying order”. They must be exposed and eliminated, he warned.

China expert Neil Thomas and his colleagues at the Asia Society have likened Xi to Leninist Logan Roy, the patriarch in HBO hit series Succession.

“He presides over an enormous enterprise, exercises commanding authority, and remains perpetually disappointed by the people expected to carry out his wishes,” they said in a recent paper.

Xi has made a point of ensuring he has no successor, a move designed to firm his grip on power.

But it’s left him with another challenge: grooming a new generation of trusted cadres, while filling vacancies in senior leadership positions from a shrinking talent pool.

Police officers carry the body of a killed person at a railway station hit by a Russian drone strike, amid Russia's attack on Ukraine, in Dnipro, Ukraine October 29, 2026. | Photo Credit: Reuters

At least 13 people were killed on Thursday (October 29, 2026) when a Russian drone slammed into a crowded platform at the Dnipro railway station in central Ukraine, where trains were parked, authorities said.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy labelled the strike "horrific" and said the Russians had attacked a platform "where a regular commuter train and a regular passenger train were standing".

"Unfortunately, as of now, 13 people have been confirmed dead," he wrote on social media, posting pictures from the Ukrainian rescue services showing a large hole ripped in a platform strewn with blurred-out bodies, near a burned-out train with smashed windows.

Ukrainian national police said at least 10 people were also injured in the strike.

Ukrainian Railways said a jet-powered Russian drone had hit a platform at the Dnipro train station "where train No. 866 Dnipro-Zaporizhzhia was at the time".

An elevated threat level had been announced, according to the rail company's chief, Oleksandr Pertsovsky.

"But unfortunately there were still a large number of people on the platform," he said on Facebook.

Russia has fired drones at trains across Ukraine since the first days of its 2022 invasion — destroying hundreds of locomotives and killing several railway workers.

In September alone, passengers were evacuated from more than 5,800 Ukrainian train services due to the threat of Russian drone strikes, the national railway operator told AFP.

Mr. Zelenskyy said the attack showed that "fair and decisive actions by America and Europe are needed against Russian escalation".

He has harshly criticised a deal struck this month between U.S. President Donald Trump and his Russian counterpart Vladimir Putin to ease some sanctions on Russia allowing Moscow to release diesel onto global markets.

"How many such terrorist strikes has Russia carried out against our people since sanctions were lifted on some Russian oligarchs and energy sector, instead of de-escalating," he asked.

"Sanctions against Moscow and its war machine must not be weakened as long as this Russian war continues," he said, demanding "stronger sanctions steps by the EU, the United States and the G7".

Russian attacks elsewhere on Ukraine killed at least another five people — including two teenage girls in the northern city of Chernigiv.

Defeated presidential candidate Flavio Bolsonaro sneaked out of Brazil to the US, where he declared himself to be the “true president” of his country.

RIO DE JANEIRO - In a viral wave of mockery, Brazilian popstar Anitta declared herself free of cellulite, one influencer declared himself the “true president” of Twitter, and another social media user proclaimed himself the winner of the next lottery.

The target of the taunts was Flavio Bolsonaro, who – after fleeing to the United States following his election defeat – proclaimed himself the leader of Brazil on Oct 28 in a confusing series of revised statements.

The moves by the 45-year-old senator have been met in Brazil with a mixture of derision, disbelief, and disapproval, even from within his own camp.

The eldest son of far-right former president Jair Bolsonaro was blindsided by his election defeat to leftist incumbent Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva on Oct 25, after leading a first-round vote earlier this month.

In a cloak-and-dagger operation he sneaked out of Brazil via Argentina and Ecuador, after being ordered to surrender his passports due to several ongoing investigations against him.

Hours after his arrival in Florida, he posted a statement on social media declaring himself the “true president” of Brazil, before revising the wording a few minutes later to describe himself as the “leader” of the country.

A Bolsonaro ally who published an earlier version of the statement bearing Brazil’s old coat of arms, abandoned in the sixties, said it had been a ChatGPT error.

The letter was met with outrage in Brazilian media, which described its tone as “coup-mongering,” while politicians from across the spectrum called for the election results to be respected.

A column in the O Globo daily likened Flavio Bolsonaro to a “lunatic asylum Napoleon,” while the Folha de Sao Paulo newspaper slammed the statement as “pathetic.”

On the right, the powerful governor of Sao Paulo, Tarcisio de Freitas, quickly distanced himself.

De Freitas was a former minister under Jair Bolsonaro who had been tipped as the main candidate for the right before the former president chose his son

“We are democrats. The election result is sovereign. What we have seen is the will of the majority of the population, which must be respected,” De Freitas said in an interview with the Estadao daily.

As speculation switched almost immediately to who would lead the right with Flavio out of the country, the governor – a favourite in business circles – said a 2030 run was “a possibility”.

Rogerio Marinho, who co-ordinated Flavio Bolsonaro’s campaign, said there was “no evidence” of electoral fraud, as claimed by the defeated candidate.

Electoral court chief Kassio Nunes Marques, who was appointed to the Supreme Court by Jair Bolsonaro, issued a statement to highlight the “secure electoral process.”

Flavio Bolsonaro also said he would lead the opposition from abroad.

His self-proclamation as Brazil’s leader in exile “is not a misstep, it is a calculated move” intended to maintain his influence over the conservative camp in Brazil, said Geraldo Monteiro, a political scientist at the State University of Rio de Janeiro.

The stakes are high: despite their election defeat, the right scored a strong majority in Congress.

Bolsonaro’s Liberal Party (PL) will hold the largest number of seats from February.

“I do not believe he has the means to lead the opposition from afar, as the newly elected officials have already secured their seats and no longer need him,” Monteiro told AFP.

He said Flavio Bolsonaro’s letter has “drawn a red line” between those who choose “extreme loyalty” and those who “prefer not to embark on another putschist adventure.”

Monteiro said Flavio was following in the footsteps of his father, who was sentenced to 27 years for plotting a coup after his 2022 election loss to Lula.

On Oct 29, PL leader Valdemar Costa Neto visited Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving his sentence under house arrest.

“No one approves” of the letter, Neto said as he left the meeting, adding that the party needed to “move forward.” REUTERS

Conservative US influencers James O’Keefe and Cam Higby are facing felony charges over a video on Minnesota voting practices that allow one to “vouch” for eight others from the same precinct, while not having to show any ID.

Titled ‘I Wore Burqa To EXPOSE Election FRAUD In Minneapolis’, the stunt was shot during the Minnesota primary election in August. It features Higby posing as a Muslim woman dressed up in a full-face veil, with O’Keefe also wearing mock Muslim attire and speaking in a thick accent.

US President Donald Trump has for years alleged that Democrats have committed voter fraud, including by allegedly allowing non-citizens to participate in elections. The Republican has long advocated a standardized photo ID verification for voting.

On Wednesday, the Hennepin County Attorney’s Office announced that O’Keefe and Higby had been charged with unlawful voting and identity theft. According to Attorney Mary Moriarty, the pair had “visited multiple precincts… and misrepresented their identities by giving election judges names of registered voters in the precinct without those voters’ knowledge or permission.”

Speaking during a press conference, Moriarty said that summonses had been issued in both men’s names. Should O’Keefe and Higby fail to appear in court, an arrest warrant will follow, the official clarified.

While the Hennepin County attorney conceded that neither man signed an oath for ‘vouching’, she insisted that they still intentionally misrepresented “their identity to election judges in applying for a ballot.”

The conservative influencers availed themselves of a procedure which allows a registered voter in Minnesota to vouch for up to eight other voters from the same precinct, permitting the latter to cast their ballots without any further verification. Under Minnesota law, a person vouching for someone else must sign an oath and could face charges of perjury if it turns out that they lied on it.

Commenting on the charges filed against him in the Democratic-run state, Higby wrote on X that he was “not afraid of Minnesota.”

In their video, the conservative influencers specifically state they are “not actually going to vote… [and] touch any ballots,” with no intention to “break the law [or] to commit voter fraud.”

The idea was rather to “point out the vulnerabilities in the system and how easily exploitable it is,” O’Keefe and Higby explain.

Having visited three polling stations in different precincts, the pair concluded that “any person who has the address and name of another person can walk into a Minnesota… polling station and vote in that person’s name without ID.”

Additionally, poll workers “were going to allow me to vouch for eight other people so that they could vote the same day, also without verifying their ID,” Higby says in the video.

Late last year, Minnesota was thrust into the national spotlight after the federal authorities launched an investigation into an unrelated alleged fraud scheme involving multiple childcare centers run by locals of Somali background that appeared to be non-operational fronts.

The probe followed an expose by YouTuber Nick Shirley who claimed that the alleged fraudsters had received more than $110 million in state funds for non-existent services.

Francis Manuleleua and Leka Halasima are the new faces of the Samoan and Tongan rivalry.  (Getty Images: Darrian Traynor and Janelle St Pierre)

Ahead of their sold-out Rugby League World Cup match on Sunday, the new faces of the eternal rivalry between Tonga and Samoa didn't quite go straight from the stands onto the field, but they went pretty close.

Four years ago, when the two met in the last World Cup, Francis Manuleleua was hunched over in his bedroom in South Auckland, watching the game on his phone.

He was 18, with the rugby league world at his feet, and after Samoa held on for a pulsating 20-18 victory, he snatched up a flag and marched into the street because his life had changed forever.

"Growing up I was always picked in the Kiwi teams, Under 16s and Under 18s and that, so there was a feeling of wanting to pull on that black jersey in either league or union," Manuleleua said.

"But after that 2022 World Cup, seeing all the boys play, how much it meant to them, that's when it changed for me and I thought to myself 'this is the jersey I'd want to put on if I ever get the opportunity'."

Across the Tasman, Sua Fa'alogo was part of the same celebratory throng, wherever the four winds could take Samoans from their islands.

The parades of the Samoan and Tongan fans have become legendary.  (Getty Images: Darrian Traynor )

The 23-year-old was born and raised in Samoa, in the village of Si'umu, and lived there until he was nine, when his parents moved to Melbourne to find a better life for him and his eight siblings.

Leka Halasima's story was similar: he was born in Tofoa, Tonga, before moving to New Zealand as a boy.

In 2024, a few months after his NRL debut and well before every league fan knew his name, he watched his two nations go head-to-head in the Pacific Championship, cheering on the Mate Ma'a in a 25-24 victory.

Halasima has gone from admiring his heroes to playing alongside them.  (Getty Images: Cameron Spencer )

"Then the next year I was playing with them," Halasima said.

Now the 21-year-old Warriors tyro is doing more than just playing, he is starring alongside the heroes of his childhood and taking forward what they started.

"For a young person like me, I've been a fan since that 2017 World Cup and to go from being in the stands cheering to playing alongside people like Jase (Jason Taumalolo), who I look up to, it's an honour. It's a privilege," Halasima said.

Fa'alogo, Manuleleua and Halasima are not just three of rugby league's brightest young stars; they are part of a new generation inheriting a relationship thousands of years in the making.

The passion of the Pacific shines through in Samoa's rugby league team, and the pride the people have is evident on the island.

The history and culture of Tonga and Samoa are proudly separate and distinct but also irrevocably intertwined by centuries of love and blood and faith, first back in the islands and now within the great Pasifika diaspora.

Today, that relationship plays out in all its colour and splendour on the rugby league field, in grand occasions that prove Tonga and Samoa are a people as much as they're a place.

That place can be wherever either nation plays on their own, but it happens the most when they face one another.

Tonga and Samoa can meet anywhere in the world and their people will find them.  (Getty Images: Michael Steele )

It's been this way long enough that players like Manuleleua, Fa'alogo, and Halasima grew up on occasions like this and dreamed of being part of it.

"Since the draw came out, I've been thinking of this day. I'm pretty sure every islander around the world can't wait for it," Halasima said.

"This is pretty much finals footy, this is what it's all about."

Manuleleua played in the NRL grand final for Newcastle earlier this month in what was the most important night of his young life — until Sunday.

Between them they have a population smaller than Canberra, but when they meet on Monday morning for a shot at the semi-finals, Tonga and Samoa will be the talk of the rugby league world.

"This will probably be the biggest game I've ever been a part of it. A grand final is pretty big too, but the rivalry between Samoa and Tonga runs deeper than footy," Manuleleua said.

"A few of my mates are Tongan, and we know deep down we have that hatred for each other when it comes to coming up against them.

"As soon as we cross that white line there's no friends, but after those 80 minutes we'll get together, have a beer and everything will be all sweet."

These games can happen anywhere, because while the islands are always home, the Pasifika diaspora has taken them to the world, and another home can happen wherever Samoans or Tongans come together.

Last year's Samoa-Tonga game attracted more than 45,000 people.  (Getty Images: Bradley Kanaris)

Four years ago it happened in Warrington, 12 months ago it happened in Brisbane in front of 45,000 people in a game that felt like the State of Origin of the new century, and on Sunday it will happen in Parramatta.

Manuleleua's parents are coming over from Auckland but his siblings, he jokes, will have to make their own way.

Halasima will have his partner and newborn son there, as well as his parents, sisters, aunts, and uncles coming from New Zealand and up from Melbourne.

For Fa'alogo it will be an even more special day because his father, Eti, has never seen him play for Melbourne or Samoa in the flesh but he will be there on Sunday to watch his youngest become a part of history.

Sunday will mark a special occasion for Fa'alogo.  (Getty Images: Darrian Traynor )

"I can't wait. He's done so much for me," Fa'alogo said.

"I just want to give back to him for everything he did, for bringing me over and trying to give me the opportunity for a better life."

The game itself is a coin flip. Tonga is bigger, stronger and more battle-hardened; Samoa is faster and has more strike as proven by their big win last year.

The former is already locked into a semifinal spot, and the latter may well be by kick-off depending on the scoreline between England and Papua New Guinea, but this is a game that is bigger than a World Cup and larger than life.

The fresh faces feel the legacy of the history between the two nations even as they write its latest chapter, and they want to pass it down just as it was passed to them.

Earlier this year, Fa'alogo went back to Si'umu as part of a trip with the Storm.

He went to his old house and his old school and caught up with family.

Wherever he went, children followed. It has been that way ever since he made his NRL debut in 2023 and his Samoan debut a few months later, but Fa'alogo might not ever get used to it.

#733. IN BRIEF: Putin hails Russia-Africa Summit, outlines prospects for cooperation

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

MOSCOW, October 29. /TASS/. The Third Russias-Africa Summit was a success and will give a strong impetus to the development of ties between Moscow and the African continent, Russian President Vladimir Putin said at a press conference following the summit.

The head of state highlighted the significant potential for the further development of relations between Russia and African countries, noting that Moscow welcomed the continent's aspiration to play a greater role in international decision-making. He also outlined his views on global security, dialogue with Europe and the lessons of world wars.

TASS has compiled the key takeaways from the Russian president’s statements.

The Third Russia-Africa Summit was a success and "will undoubtedly give a powerful impetus to the development of Russia-Africa cooperation."

"I would like to point out that this is the third Russia-Africa Summit. Holding such large-scale events has become a good tradition, which clearly indicates the determination of both Russia and African nations to continue boosting and strengthening multipolar ties in the spirit of true partnership and mutual respect for the sake of common growth and prosperity."

The motto of the Moscow-hosted summit was "For Peace, Security and Development," which set the tone and defined the topics of discussions.

"It is certainly satisfactory that the Moscow summit brought together 21 leaders of African nations, along with high-level delegations from most countries of the continent, and the heads of almost all leading African integration structures."

Russia and African countries adopted the 2027-2029 action plan for the Russia-Africa Partnership Forum: "The joint action plan through 2029 that we approved today contains an extensive list of specific goals aimed at developing Russia’s ties with the countries of the continent in the economic, trade, social and humanitarian spheres, as well as at implementing the decisions made at this summit."

As for economic goals, the action plan calls for increasing the volume of mutual imports and exports and improving their structure: "We are confident that the current level of the Russian-African trade turnover, which stands at $27 billion, is far from the limit. It’s safe to say that we are just starting out. However, if we look at our investment, it has reached billions of dollars, so we will definitely be able to increase trade."

The results of productive work at the Russia-Africa Summit are reflected in the Moscow Declaration: "This comprehensive document, designed for the long haul, reaffirms the commitment of all our countries to establishing a fair and democratic multipolar world order based on generally recognized principles of international law and the United Nations Charter."

It has been decided to continue holding Russia-Africa summits on a regular basis: "It’s important that all leaders have unanimously agreed to continue the practice of regular Russia-Africa summits."

The dates and venue of the next Russia-Africa sumit "will be agreed on in due course."

"It has become clear that the positions that Russia and Africa hold on most global and regional issues are not only very close but often coincide."

"Russia sincerely seeks to build a truly strategic, multifaceted partnership with Africa. And it’s important for us that we see the same constructive approach on behalf of our African friends to work together, hand in hand."

Russia has never been a colonial power, so the question about African territory potentially being used as an area for competition with other powers should not be directed to Moscow: "Throughout history, Russia has always viewed African countries only as friends. We have never pursued colonial policies; on the contrary, we helped African nations to break free from colonial dependence."

Russia and African countries "are natural allies in building a new, fairer multipolar world order."

"Of course, African countries seek to play a greater role in international decision-making, and we support that."

The African continent should definitely be represented on the UN Security Council: "Our African friends should figure out the way Africa will be represented there, either as a single voice, or African countries will rotate on the UN Security Council."

The UN needs reform but the process requires broad consensus: "The only question is that these decisions should be made on the basis of, as we say, broad consensus, and that we should not throw the baby out with the bathwater. Not to destroy universal international instruments, such as the United Nations, which are perhaps rightly criticized today, but are singularly unique."

Russia values the trust of African nations and seeks to take a careful approach to problems facing the region, including by "holding consultations with all parties involved in a conflict."

"Africa needs development and, as my colleagues have said, asks for nothing, only makes proposals and offers cooperation options. We are ready to boost our traditionally friendly relations on a completely new, modern soil."

At the meetings in the Kremlin, the African leaders supported the idea of "expanding the network of branches of Russian universities in their countries and creating new educational centers that promote the Russian language, science and culture: Several colleagues talked about opening Russian cultural centers."

Russia will increase the number of students from African countries enrolled in its universities, "including on a free basis."

Training African students is beneficial for Russia because later they will become the people Moscow can rely on in Africa: "I want to say that we in Russia need and benefit from such work [of educating African students]. Why? Because the people who worked with us, studied, received knowledge, acquired this knowledge, later use it in their countries. And, of course, they are guided by their partners in the Russian Federation. These are, relatively speaking, the people we rely on in these countries."

Russia will continue to supply grain to Africa, "we will do this as before, both contractually and free of charge."

Russia will easily adapt agricultural technologies to other climatic conditions, and then Africa will be able not only to produce but also to export its products: "Everything can be transformed, and Africa will not only be a food producer, it will not only fully cover its needs, but will also be able to export to foreign markets."

Russia and African countries intend to deepen their health protection partnership. In particular, joint exercises are planned to combat outbreaks of infections: "We will increase the supply of effective medicines, transfer mobile laboratories, and conduct joint exercises to respond to outbreaks of infections.

"I will mention, in particular, the decision taken to extend the Russian program of assistance to African countries to combat infections until 2029."

Back in the early 2000s, Russia was only a buyer of grain, "and now we top the world as a grain exporter."

The experience of Moscow medicine should not only be implemented in other regions of Russia but also offered for export: "We still have many healthcare problems in Russia. This is clear, and we must spread what is happening in Moscow to other regions of the Russian Federation. But why not offer the same to our friends in other countries, including Africa?"

Russia is one of the few countries in the world that can boast full digital sovereignty. Its IT companies successfully compete in foreign markets: "Russia, along with the United States, the People's Republic of China, is one of the few countries that has full sovereignty in digital technologies. Our platform solutions are very interesting and good. All this is developing not only on the territory of the Russian Federation. Our companies successfully compete in foreign markets.

"Our partners have great interest in small nuclear power plants. Our international competitors, almost everyone, are talking about their readiness to build low-power nuclear power plants, but so far no one has done anything. Only Russia has created such small nuclear power plants, which are working, even in the north they are working. Now we are actually completing two more nuclear power plants."

Of the 32 nuclear power units that are being built by third countries abroad, 28 are being built by the Russian company Rosatom: "The investments are significant. We are investing almost $25 billion in just one power plant in Egypt. There are other such examples. And we are ready to continue working in Africa."

Russia remembers all the tragic pages of history well, including the lessons of both world wars, and for its part does its best to ensure that nothing like this happens to humanity again: "We in Russia remember all this well, including the lessons of WWI and WWII. And we are doing everything to ensure that nothing like this happens on a global scale."

The West has not achieved its goal of inflicting a strategic defeat on Russia: "Many people are talking about preparing for war by 2030. Is this right or wrong? First of all, we are ready, but we don't want any war with Europe."

If it comes to a war between Russia and Europe, "there will be no winners. No one should forget about this."

Russia supports any steps to restore full-fledged relations with European countries: "Either with all of them together in the form of the well-known EU organization, or bilaterally. OK, we don't care, whatever they want."

"Regarding the telephone conversation with the representatives, or rather, with the leaders of the EU countries, with Finland and with Norway. They immediately stressed at the beginning of the conversation that they do not represent the EU as an organization, they are calling me in a national capacity, as they say."

The telephone conversation with Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store was initiated by them: "The conversation took place on their initiative, indeed.

"I know that there was criticism of them later, but it's not our business to assess whether it was correct or incorrect. I think it's right [that Stubb and Store called]. After all, there are the people in Europe who do not want to inflict a strategic defeat on Russia, as some do, but rather want to establish some kind of constructive dialogue."

Russia does not want a situation similar to the Strait of Hormuz and the Persian Gulf one to develop in the Baltic Sea. The issue of security in the Baltic was raised during the telephone conversation with Stubb and Store, who hope that all participants in international communication will comply with international maritime law: "We support this approach. Can it be different? Otherwise, there will be a situation similar to the situation in the Strait of Hormuz. Does anyone want the same situation to develop in the Baltic Sea as in the Persian Gulf or the Strait of Hormuz? I don't know. We don't want that."

Moscow is ready to respond to any challenge in the Baltic Sea but does not want to create tension there: "We do not want this. But we will respond to any challenge."

#724. Last drinks called as council prepares to demolish town's only pub

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The 98-year-old pub is one of Robinvale's oldest buildings.  (ABC News: Nethma Dandeniya)

Swan Hill Rural City Council has announced plans to demolish the remains of the Robinvale Hotel.

The nearly 100-year-old pub was destroyed by fire in 2015 and has since sat derelict in the heart of town.

Demolition could begin as early as next month, despite some residents calling for it to stay.

More than 10 years after a fire gutted the only pub in the Victorian border town of Robinvale, the remains are to be demolished, just two years shy of the building's 100th birthday.

The Swan Hill Rural City Council in north-west Victoria voted this week to step in and demolish the building, after an independent structural engineering report found the site "critically unsafe".

The Robinvale Hotel, built in 1928, had long been an important tourist drawcard for the region, as well as a place for locals to meet and socialise.

Its demise was a loss to the town's estimated 8,000 residents, who have grown increasingly frustrated at the lack of movement at the site.

An independent heritage assessment carried out last year found it was not practical to repair the building and that it was "highly regrettable" that works to stabilise and weatherproof it could not be completed shortly after the fire.

The Robinvale Hotel was extensively damaged by fire and its condition has since deteriorated. (ABC News: Nethma Dandeniya)

Swan Hill Mayor Stuart King said council had worked with the building's owner for a very long time.

"But the time has now come for action," he said.

A safety exclusion zone was erected on Perrin Street earlier this year, forcing residents to take a detour.

Cr King said safety was the biggest priority.

"The building is unsafe, it needs to be demolished and so we will take action to have that work done," he said.

The ABC was unable to reach the owner for comment.

The building's heritage report noted its Spanish Mission influences and Canary Island palm trees. (Supplied: State Library Victoria)

Former local resident Anniemae Goldring launched a petition last year calling for the council to reject the initial demolition application. She spent her formative years at the pub with her grandmother, a bartender and cleaner.

She said it will be a sombre occasion when the building comes down.

"Differences aside, I'm glad that there will be some change for the township," she said.

Ms Goldring remains frustrated that the old pub was not better protected after the fire, and hopes it does not become a vacant lot.

The site was reserved for a hotel when sold in 1924 by Herbert Cuttle, the founder of Robinvale. (Supplied: State Library Victoria)

Just over the border in Euston, NSW, bowling and recreation club chief executive Guy Fielding said he was glad to finally see something happen.

"It's been a long time [coming]," he said.

"The more facilities we have in our community, the better it is for our town and makes it more attractive for not only the locals but for tourists."

He said it would be "fantastic" to see the hotel rebuilt.

The hotel was an important focus for local social activity and recreation. (ABC News: Nethma Dandeniya)

Cr King said the demolition provided a "fresh start" for the town, which has a diverse population of more than 45 nationalities.

"No town wants a burnt-out, half fallen down, derelict building sitting in the middle of the CBD," he said.

Plans for the site are not known. Cr King said any new building would likely need to meet a number of conditions due to the site's heritage overlay.

An assessment will be done on the bottle shop next door after the demolition to decide its future.

#103. Alaska governor orders probe into Democratic campaign's alleged links to same-name candidate

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Republican Governor Mike Dunleavy said he was acting on claims in a news report

The Republican governor of Alaska has ordered an investigation into an alleged "scheme" which led to two men named Dan Sullivan standing in the state's US Senate race.

Governor Mike Dunleavy said he was responding to a New York Times (NYT) story, external which said the team of Democratic candidate Mary Peltola worked with people who tried to recruit spoiler candidates in the Senate race.

The governor's post on X said he would investigate "whether this scheme violated Alaska's laws". He did not say which laws he had in mind.

Dan S Sullivan, the incumbent Republican senator running for re-election, and allies have accused the Democrats of trying to confuse voters. Peltola's team deny any wrongdoing.

"The Alaskans for Mary campaign has no involvement with either Sullivan campaign," the Peltola campaign said in a statement.

They say they did not encourage the second man, Dan J Sullivan, a retired teacher who lives in a remote fishing village, to stand. He has denied any links with the Peltola campaign.

The Alaska race is considered by analysts to be one of the tightest of the upcoming US midterm elections, which will decide which party controls Congress. Americans head to the polls across the country on Tuesday.

Dunleavy's statement, which did not name the Peltola campaign, said the NYT reporting had supported findings by the state Division of Elections earlier this year.

"I am therefore directing the Department of Public Safety to investigate whether this scheme violated Alaska's laws and whether criminal charges or civil action are warranted," he wrote. "The evidence will determine what happens next."

The NYT report alleged that people connected to Peltola's campaign, as well as senior aides at the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee (DSCC), worked to try to recruit Sullivan the schoolteacher to stand in the Senate race.

This was part of a broader effort to encourage other would-be "spoiler" candidates to enter the race and dilute the support of the sitting Republican senator, the newspaper reported.

The paper said it had seen text messages and interviewed 12 people who participated in or were told of the plan, or were would-be candidates who the team tried to recruit. The BBC has not independently verified the NYT's reporting.

"The DSCC is not involved with Dan J. Sullivan," the non-incumbent candidate, said Monica Robinson, a committee spokesperson, in a statement.

The ballot paper is set to list the two men as Dan S Sullivan, who will be described as the incumbent Republican senator, and Daniel J Sullivan Jr.

Senator Dan Sullivan told Fox News on Wednesday night that Peltola should "come clean" and described it as a scandal of the highest order.

His fellow Republican senator in Alaska, Lisa Murkowksi, accused the Democratic campaign of engaging in a "pattern of lies". President Donald Trump has called for Peltola to be barred.

Previous efforts by the Republicans to remove the non-incumbent Sullivan from the ballot have already been rejected by the Alaska Supreme Court which found no state laws had been broken by him running in the race.

Though officials at the state and federal level have called for investigations in the wake of the New York Times reporting, election law experts said it was unlikely to dislodge the second Dan Sullivan from the ballot and it was unclear at this point if any state laws had been violated.

"It turns out that convincing somebody to run for office is not a crime, even if their name happens to be the same as an incumbent," said Justin Levitt, an election law expert at Loyola Law School and a former justice department official.

The non-incumbent Sullivan had a constitutional right to run and have his name on the ballot, added Ruth Greenwood, an election expert at Harvard Law School.

Trump administration sues to block certification of upcoming Alaska election

Alaska's Democratic Senate candidate pauses midterms campaigning after car crash

The Trump administration filed a federal lawsuit last week aimed at preventing the certification of the result if the Trump-backed Sullivan loses, arguing that the presence of both of them on the ballot could confuse voters.

On Wednesday, the justice department amended its lawsuit to include the Times reporting, citing significant sections of the story as well as Dunleavy's announcement.

Some legal experts expressed scepticism of the merits of the lawsuit, and their chances of success given Alaska's courts had already allowed the second Sullivan to remain on the ballot.

The actual legal arguments the justice department made were a "frivolous theory," Rick Pildes, an election law expert at New York University, told the BBC.

He noted the lawsuit did not cite a specific provision that the government wanted to enforce, merely a general preamble taken from a sweeping US voting law.

Alaska election officials asked a federal court to dismiss the lawsuit "because the complaint has no legal basis".

The justice department has pledged continued action in the wake of the New York Times' reporting.

"They are cheating, let's call it what it is," Attorney General Todd Blanche said on Fox News on Wednesday.

In a series of Truth Social posts, Trump too pointed to the report and made a variety of allegations against Peltola, who he argued should be removed from the ballot. He repeated his endorsement for Dan S Sullivan, the incumbent Republican senator, writing: "Dan is a GREAT man, has ALWAYS delivered for Alaska."

Alaska's ranked-choice voting system means that voters could select both Sullivans if they are unsure at the ballot box.

The battle threatens to delay the full picture of nationwide results following the midterm elections. The ultimate question of which party controls Congress will have a significant bearing on the remaining two years of Trump's presidency.

A statement from an Alaskans for Mary spokesperson, written prior to Dunleavy's call for an investigation, said: "Mary got in this race because Alaskans deserve a champion who is fighting to lower their costs and put Alaska first, and no tweet or threat from any DC politician will keep her away from that fight.

"The Alaskans for Mary campaign has no involvement with either Sullivan campaign."

In a statement given to US media, the DSCC likewise denied that it had been involved in any such scheme.

Peltola earlier this week paused campaigning activities in order to recover after she and her family sustained "minor injuries" in a car crash.
