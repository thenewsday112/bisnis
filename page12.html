#342. Laba Bersih Bank Danamon Tumbuh 19% Jadi Rp 3,5 Triliun, Ini Pendorongnya

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

KONTAN.CO.ID - JAKARTA. Bank Danamon Indonesia Tbk (BDMN) membukukan laba bersih konsolidasian sebesar Rp 3,5 triliun, tumbuh 19% secara tahunan (yoy).

Kinerja ini didukung oleh pertumbuhan total kredit dan trade finance konsolidasian sebesar 13% yoy serta peningkatan simpanan nasabah sebesar 12% yoy.

Direktur Utama Danamon Nobuya Kawasaki mengatakan, pertumbuhan intermediasi dan pendanaan tetap terjaga dengan didukung pengelolaan risiko dan kualitas aset yang disiplin.

"Kami akan tetap berfokus pada pertumbuhan yang sehat, kualitas aset yang terjaga, serta penciptaan nilai jangka panjang bagi seluruh pemangku kepentingan," ujarnya dalam keterangan resmi, Kamis (29/10/2026).

Dalam sisi intermediasi, Danamon membukukan total kredit dan trade finance konsolidasian sebesar Rp 232,6 triliun, tumbuh 13% yoy per 30 September 2026.

Pertumbuhan ini ditopang oleh kinerja berbagai lini bisnis, termasuk Enterprise Banking and Financial Institution yang meningkat sebesar 20%, SME Banking yang meningkat sebesar 9%, Consumer Banking yang meningkat sebesar 5%, serta anak perusahaan Danamon yang juga mencatatkan pertumbuhan sebesar 7%.

Pada sisi penghimpunan dana, Danamon membukukan total rekening giro, rekening tabungan (current account and savings account atau CASA) dan deposito meningkat 12% yoy menjadi Rp 190,5 triliun.

Sementara pada aspek profitabilitas, pendapatan operasional meningkat 5% yoy menjadi Rp 19,0 triliun. Penurunan biaya kredit sebesar 5% yoy turut mendukung pencapaian laba bersih setelah pajak (NPAT) sebesar Rp 3,5 triliun.

Sejalan dengan hal tersebut, Danamon membukukan margin bunga bersih (net interest margin atau NIM) sebesar 8,0%.

Pada kualitas aset per 30 September 2026, rasio Loan at Risk (LAR) tercatat membaik 190 basis poin menjadi 7,7%, dibandingkan periode yang sama tahun sebelumnya.

Di saat yang sama, rasio cakupan NPL (NPL coverage ratio) mencapai 282,0%.

"Meskipun kondisi pasar tetap kompetitif dan dinamika ekonomi global masih memerlukan perhatian, Danamon terus berfokus pada pengelolaan risiko yang disiplin, kualitas aset yang sehat, serta pertumbuhan bisnis yang berkelanjutan," pungkasnya.

#347. Survei HSBC: 84% Manajer Keuangan Indonesia Optimistis AI Dongkrak Treasury

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Reporter: Selvi Mayasari | Editor: Avanty Nurdiana

KONTAN.CO.ID - JAKARTA. Sebanyak 84% manajer keuangan korporasi di Indonesia memperkirakan kecerdasan buatan atau artificial intelligence (AI) akan memberikan manfaat besar bagi pengelolaan keuangan perusahaan (treasury) dalam tiga tahun mendatang.

Angka ini jauh di atas rata-rata Asia Pasifik sebesar 72%, menurut survei HSBC bertajuk Redefining Treasury in Asia Pacific: Voices of Treasury 2026.

Optimisme tersebut juga tercermin dari implementasi AI di perusahaan. Survei HSBC menunjukkan, sebanyak 34% manajer keuangan korporasi di Indonesia telah menggunakan AI agent untuk mengotomatisasi proses transaksi treasury. Angka ini hampir dua kali lipat rata-rata Asia Pasifik yang sebesar 17%.

Adapun perusahaan di Indonesia yang belum mengadopsi AI untuk fungsi treasury hanya sebesar 7%, lebih rendah dibandingkan rata-rata kawasan Asia Pasifik sebesar 19%.

Head of Global Payments Solutions Asia ex Greater China HSBC Manoj Dugar mengatakan, pembahasan mengenai AI kini telah bergeser dari sekadar eksplorasi menuju penerapan nyata di perusahaan.

“Pembicaraan mengenai AI telah bergeser dari sekadar rasa ingin tahu menjadi kapabilitas. Pembeda utamanya kini terletak pada implementasi, yaitu membenahi fondasi seperti data, tata kelola, dan kontrol, sehingga AI dapat diterapkan dalam skala lebih besar dan memberikan dampak dengan cepat,” ujar Manoj di Jakarta, Kamis (29/10).

Penerapan teknologi tersebut menjadi semakin penting di tengah volatilitas nilai tukar dan suku bunga. Pengelolaan transaksi keuangan di tengah kondisi pasar tersebut menjadi salah satu prioritas utama korporasi dalam 12 bulan mendatang, baik di Indonesia maupun di kawasan Asia Pasifik.

Meski optimistis terhadap manfaat AI, korporasi di Indonesia masih menghadapi tantangan dalam memperluas implementasinya. Sebanyak 80% responden menyebut integrasi dengan sistem yang sudah ada sebagai hambatan utama adopsi AI, lebih tinggi dibandingkan rata-rata Asia Pasifik sebesar 70%.

Hambatan lainnya meliputi keterbatasan tenaga ahli dan risiko, yang masing-masing disebut oleh 40% responden. Selain itu, 33% responden menyoroti biaya implementasi, sedangkan 27% menyebut kualitas data sebagai tantangan.

Dalam mewujudkan pengelolaan keuangan secara real time, sebanyak 48% manajer keuangan di Indonesia juga menilai persetujuan anggaran untuk implementasi sistem menjadi salah satu tantangan terbesar. Sementara itu, risiko keamanan dan upaya meyakinkan pemangku kepentingan mengenai manfaat perubahan masing-masing menjadi perhatian 37% responden.

“Perusahaan di Indonesia tidak lagi bertanya apakah AI penting, melainkan fokus pada akselerasi adopsi AI. Bank akan semakin berperan sebagai mitra strategis dalam ekosistem manajemen keuangan perusahaan yang cerdas (intelligent treasury management), tidak lagi hanya menjadi penyedia layanan perbankan,” ujar Anne.

Survei HSBC menunjukkan, pembayaran (payment) dinilai sebagai transaksi treasury yang paling terpapar risiko siber, baik di Indonesia maupun di 10 dari 11 pasar Asia Pasifik yang disurvei.

Untuk mengantisipasi risiko tersebut, sebanyak 77% responden di Indonesia menilai perusahaan perlu meningkatkan investasi teknologi guna memperkuat keamanan transaksi. Proporsi ini jauh di atas rata-rata Asia Pasifik sebesar 57% dan menjadi yang tertinggi ketiga di kawasan.

#200. Cruise companies aim to keep fuel costs down. Their strategies for doing so differ

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Carnival, Royal Caribbean and Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings posted strong earnings for their most recent quarters despite rising fuel prices. How the leading cruise companies navigate fuel costs boils down to starkly different strategies.

Oil prices have skyrocketed since the U.S.-Israel war with Iran began on Feb. 28. Brent crude oil reached a six-month high on Friday, settling at $116.72 per barrel. With no clear end in sight for the conflict in the Middle East, the pressure affecting cruise ships' lifeblood seems poised to last into the holiday travel season and beyond.

Both Royal Caribbean and Norwegian practice "hedging," or a strategy of using derivatives to reduce exposure to swings in the price of fuel, to account for sharp increases.

Carnival CEO Josh Weinstein, on the other hand, told CNBC in an interview last month that cutting fuel consumption is the best way to avert the effects of expensive fuel.

"At the end of the day, fuel is an input cost for us. The best thing that we can do to control that cost is to use less," Weinstein said at the time.

Jefferies analyst David Katz told CNBC in an interview that companies that hedge do so primarily to manage the cost of fuel, but also to manage Wall Street expectations and prevent hectic stock trading based on fluctuations in the price of fuel.

"When you're public, the Street will trade your stock around based on the price of fuel," Katz said. "The Street's argument is that volatility puts pressure on your multiple."

"While it is mathematically most likely true that over the long term it is more expensive," he explained. "The benefit of having less fuel volatility in the model and less trading volatility is [it's] good for your multiple."

Despite the potential positives of the strategy, Carnival has maintained the stance it has held since 2019: Cutting fuel consumption works better than hedging.

And Carnival's third-quarter earnings report last month suggests the strategy may be paying off.

Carnival beat analysts' expectations on both the top and bottom lines for its most recent quarter and reported an all-time high net income of $1.9 billion.

Weinstein told CNBC in an interview on "Squawk on the Street" following the release that the company has been "maniacal" about cutting its fuel consumption, saying it's slashed its unit fuel consumption rate by 26% since 2019.

"We estimate we've saved about $700 million in 2026 based on that consumption savings rate going down since 2019," he said.

Carnival spent $615 million, or about 7.3% of its revenue, on fuel in the third quarter, according to its most recent earnings release. That's an increase from the $425 million, or 6.7% of revenue, that the company spent on fuel in the fourth quarter of 2025, before fuel prices spiked.

Carnival projects its fuel costs will rise to $640 million in the fourth quarter, according to its earnings release.

Royal Caribbean similarly beat analyst expectations for earnings per share for its third quarter, reported Tuesday. However, it narrowly missed on revenue.

CEO Jason Liberty attributed the company's beat in part to its hedging strategy during an interview on CNBC's "Squawk on the Street" Tuesday morning.

"We're a little bit over 60% hedged here for 2026, and so that's helping insulate us from some of the impacts on the rising fuel costs," he said.

"It's obviously still impacting us," Liberty said. "[High fuel prices] evaporated a good chunk of our Q3 earnings beat into the full-year guide. But we're continuing to hedge, and we watch."

Royal Caribbean spent about $347 million on fuel in the third quarter, or about 6.2% of its revenue, according to its earnings release. That compares with $293 million, or 6.8% of revenue, spent on fuel in the fourth quarter of 2025, which ended on Dec. 31.

The company projects fuel costs will continue to rise to $379 million in the fourth quarter.

Liberty pointed out that high fuel prices have not dampened demand from consumers for vacations.

"It's not that we don't see points of challenges, but really what we've seen is that even in those points of challenges, the consumer comes roaring back," he said.

Royal Caribbean reported strong bookings for the third quarter and said reservations for 2027 are already up by a high-teens percentage.

Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings beat on both the top and bottom lines for its third-quarter earnings, reported on Thursday, with revenue of $2.9 billion and adjusted earnings per share of $1.12.

The company has hedged approximately 48% of its fuel consumption for 2026, according to the earnings release.

Norwegian spent about $196 million on fuel in the third quarter, or about 6.6% of its revenue, according to its most recent earnings release. That's a lower share of revenue than the 7.4% — totaling $167 million — spent on fuel in the fourth quarter of 2025, which ended on Dec. 31.

The company projects fuel consumption to rise slightly in the fourth quarter, up to 255,000 metric tons from 239,000 metric tons consumed in the third quarter.

The company is in the midst of a turnaround effort. And despite facing added challenges from the macro environment, Chief Financial Officer Mark Kempa said he is reassured by this quarter's results on an earnings call on Thursday.

"These results reinforce our confidence in the appeal of our products and the opportunity to translate stronger revenue performance and disciplined cost management into improved profitability," Kempa said.

"Kami harapannya data center masuk ke dalam kawasan industri supaya mudah pengontrolannya juga, dan juga masalah air dan listrik juga kan di dalam," kata Akhmad.

#190. How to make a big ask of your boss

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

In the past few weeks, I have written about how colleagues can make significant requests of each other and how a boss can approach big requests of one of their direct reports. This week, I’ll complete the cycle by thinking about how you can make a big ask of your boss.

Before discussing some specific issues, there is a general point to make. You’re probably more worried about making this request than you should be. First off, good bosses support their direct reports. If the request is reasonable, then your boss wants to say yes. Rather than thinking of what you’re doing as imposing on your boss, remember an important (if often implicit) part of any supervisor’s job description is to take care of the team.

Second, your boss is likely to be asked to do things all the time from people across the organization. Another request isn’t going to be as big a deal to them as it is to you. And even if they can’t support what you want, they’re unlikely to resent you for asking. 

Do the cost-benefit analysis

When there’s something you want from your boss, it means you’re asking for one of the resources at their disposal. It could be their time, but it might also be a request for political capital (to support something you’d like to see get done), finances, or human resources. So, the request has a cost. Often, that cost is an opportunity cost. The resource given to your request can’t go somewhere else.

And, of course, the request has a benefit. That benefit might be coming directly to you (say, if you’re asking for a reference for a job or the chance to participate in a project). It might benefit the team, or perhaps it may be something that influences the future of the organization.

Before you make the request, map out the costs and benefits. On the cost side, you may need to ask around a bit to figure out what would actually be required for your boss to fulfill your request. Would it require financial support or other people? You don’t want to put in the request until you’re fairly certain you understand what would be entailed for your boss to agree.

Ultimately, you want to shift the cost-benefit calculation in your favor as much as possible. So anything you can do to minimize the costs or expand the benefits increases the chance of getting the outcome you want.

Lower the costs

Once you’ve considered the costs and benefits, you shouldn’t take the list as fixed. You should figure out how to make the cost to your boss as low as possible. 

Take a simple example: If you want a recommendation, make sure you provide all the information they’ll need to complete the task without having to look for it. In some cases, that might involve actually writing a draft of a letter for them. But often it requires giving them a list of key pieces of information they should include in the letter, as well as all the information they might need about the purpose of the recommendation.

More generally, when you frame your big ask to the boss, you want to consider which costs are necessary and which are just nice to have. One way to approach this is to develop “Silver,” “Gold,” and “Platinum” versions of your request. The lowest tier is the bare-bones version that has a minimum cost, but also provides the smallest benefit. The top tier has the highest cost with the biggest benefit. And then there’s a middle option.

There are three advantages to this framing. One is that your boss will see that you’ve considered the range of costs involved in the request. The second is that it will give your boss ideas about how to modify your request to achieve your goals in a different way, while understanding which elements are “nice to have” and which are “need to have.” Third, there’s some evidence that people like a compromise, so giving decision-makers a middle way often gives them a reason to select one of your options.

Determine whose problem you’re solving and why

On the benefit side of the equation, your initial list of benefits was probably framed from your perspective. That is, you have a problem you’re trying to solve, and you’re trying to get your boss to agree to support what you want. 

Remember, your boss is likely to get a lot of requests for support. For your boss to part with any of their valuable resources, the outcome has to hold some worth to them. So when you do that cost-benefit analysis, don’t do it from your perspective. Do it from your boss’s perspective. What problem of your boss’s are you solving with the request you’re making? 

The best way to get what you want is to help your boss see that supporting your request helps them accomplish something that matters to them. That doesn’t mean hiding the reasons why the request benefits you, but it does mean trying to understand any broader organizational perspective that relates to what you’re asking for. Not only does that approach make it more likely your request will be granted, but it also helps prepare you to take on a management role of your own one day. The ability to take an organizational perspective when planning is a critical skill for organizational leaders.

#243. Some investors have midterm election jitters. Here’s what history shows about stocks

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

With the midterm elections approaching, some investors worry about how the results will impact their portfolios. Whether their anxiety is warranted is another question.

Nearly half of surveyed investors — 48% — said they are concerned about the Nov. 3 midterms' potential impact on the stock market, according to Morgan Stanley Wealth Management's latest quarterly retail investor pulse survey, conducted Oct. 1-23 among 953 U.S. investors.

Separately, the Nationwide Retirement Institute, a research and education division of insurer Nationwide, found that 56% of surveyed investors said they feel more anxious because of the elections, and 75% believe the results could meaningfully affect their long-term financial plans. That survey was conducted Aug. 24-Sept. 11 among 2,029 investors with investable assets of at least $10,000.

"This summer and fall, the midterms have been probably the first topic of conversation with almost every [client] meeting that I've had," said John Mullen, managing director, president and CEO of Parsons Capital Management in Providence, Rhode Island. The firm ranked No. 25 on CNBC's Financial Advisor 100 list for 2026.

Read coverage of CNBC's 2026 Financial Advisor 100 list, including stories featuring ranked firms and their advisors:

While it's impossible to predict how the markets will behave following the midterms, historical data offers some perspective on how stocks have performed around past midterm elections.

Voters will be heading to the polls amid broad concerns about affordability and dissatisfaction with President Donald Trump's handling of the economy. The buildout of artificial intelligence through data centers, as well as the Iran war, also weigh heavily on voters' minds at a time when Republicans control both chambers of Congress.

Midterms historically have been unfavorable for the president's party, which has gained House seats in only three such elections since 1906, according to an analysis by Michael Townsend, Charles Schwab's managing director of legislative and regulatory affairs.

Based on polls, Democrats are favored to take control of the House — they lead Republicans on a generic ballot by about 9 percentage points as of Oct. 28, according to FiftyPlusOne, a website that tracks poll results. Democrats are also narrowly favored to win the Senate, according to DecisionDeskHQ, an election forecaster. The new Congress is scheduled to convene Jan. 3.

If Democrats take control of at least one chamber of Congress, the result will be a divided government instead of the unified government — the House, Senate and presidency all of the same party — that's been in place during Trump's second term.

An analysis of the S&P 500 index's performance over the past 50 years by asset manager Janus Henderson shows heightened market volatility when power in Washington is split between Republicans and Democrats, but little difference in the annualized price returns under a unified or divided government — 8.73% and 9.24%, respectively.

"Higher volatility during years with split control may have been caused by policy uncertainty, but a counterargument is that split government potentially insulated voters — and the economy — from some of the most ambitious intentions of a single party," wrote Seth Meyer, global head of client portfolio management for Janus Henderson. "In short, gridlock may be good."

Meanwhile, stock market volatility has tended to run higher in midterm election years, particularly in the months leading up to the election, according to a Capital Group analysis. Since 1970, median annualized volatility — a measure of market fluctuations — during midterm years has been nearly 16% vs. 13% in other years.

Nevertheless, "after the midterms, in almost every case, we've seen a rebound as the market settles into the known and [investors] kind of get the jitters out about what they didn't know before the election happened," said Michael Gibb, president and CEO of Godsey & Gibb Wealth Management in Richmond, Virginia. The firm ranked No. 89 on CNBC's Financial Advisor 100 list for 2026.

In midterm election years since 1974, the S&P 500 has averaged a 5.7% return in the three months right after a midterm election, with positive returns in 11 of the 13 years analyzed, according to the analysis by Schwab's Townsend. Over the six months following the midterms, the index has averaged a 12.4% return, with positive returns in all 13 years.

Separately, in 2024, Fidelity Investments analyzed S&P 500 returns from 1950 through 2023, dividing the presidential cycle into four successive 12-month periods running from Nov. 30 to Nov. 30. Average returns were 8.3% in the first year following a presidential election; 3.4% in year two, which is when the midterms are; 14.7% in year three, right after the midterms; and 9.1% in year four, the next presidential election year. The figures include reinvested dividends.

As for how the economy has performed after a midterm election, it's been mixed. Across 19 midterm cycles from 1950 through 2022, economic growth accelerated in nine cycles, slowed in nine and remained the same in one, according to an Oct. 26 research note by Sung Won Sohn, professor of finance and economics at Loyola Marymount University and chief economist at SS Economics.

And inflation? Another mixed bag, according to Sohn: It declined in 11 of the 19 post-midterm years and increased in eight. Yet average inflation remained relatively the same, slipping from about 3.63% in midterm years to 3.56% the following year.

Nevertheless, some investors may want to shift their holdings by rebalancing their portfolios. After the S&P posted an 18.11% loss in 2022, including reinvested dividends, it had a total return of 88.11% from the start of 2023 through the end of 2025. So far this year, its total return is 14.17% through Oct. 28.

These gains mean some investors whose portfolios started the period with, say, an intentional 80% stock and 20% bond allocation may now have more stocks than they intended.

"If there are investors who are overexposed to stocks because of the multiyear bull market we've been in, then it's not a bad idea to bring yourself back to the risk tolerance you set for yourself in a calculated and non-emotional manner," Gibb said. "But as for clients who are where their allocation should be over the long term, we wouldn't recommend making a change."

Likewise, investors who are nervous and may not be able to stomach volatility may want to reassess their portfolio's risk level to ensure it aligns with their comfort level and long-term financial goals.

"If you're not comfortable with what your asset mix is, if it's too risky for your own level of risk tolerance, then you have to make a change," Mullen said.

"We always tell our clients there's no magic number for percentages in stocks, bonds, cash and alternatives," he said. "It's whatever you're comfortable with, so you don't let your emotions get the best of you and go all to cash when the market's falling."

Additionally, making big moves can upend your long-term investment strategy.

"If you make too many changes and get away from what's worked for you, you run the risk of being disappointed if things don't turn out the way that you're expecting," Mullen said.

This encourages people and businesses to save more, spend less money, and make less investments. It also encourages companies to hire less people or slow the pace of pay rises.

All of this tends to reduce demand and slow inflation.

When inflation is low or if the Fed believes the economy needs a boost, it cut rates.

The idea is that this will have the opposite affect, with banks dropping rates leading to more spending and less saving from people and businesses.

Leaving the rates unchanged means rates on things like new mortgages or business loans are also less likely to change. It also means interest rates on cash savings are unlikely to rise.

US interest rates have historically moved around quite a bit.

The last time Fed rates were as high as they have been recently was in the lead up to and during the start of the 2008 global financial crisis.

The Fed then dropped rates to historic lows to encourage spending and job creation during the economic slump and kept them at those lows for several years.

They picked up again ahead of the pandemic before dropping to as low as 0%-0.25% during the pandemic. They then rose to highs of 5.25%-5% from the middle of 2023 to August 2024 as inflation jumped.

They had been falling steadily since then, until rates were raised last month.

#366. Pendapatan Prodia Diagnostic Line (PRDL) Anjlok 60,9% hingga September 2026

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Reporter: Diki Mardiansyah | Editor: Ignatia Maria Sri Sayekti

KONTAN.CO.ID - JAKARTA. PT Prodia Diagnostic Line Tbk (PRDL) membukukan penurunan kinerja sepanjang sembilan bulan pertama 2026. Pendapatan perusahaan turun 60,9% secara tahunan menjadi Rp21,48 miliar dari Rp54,99 miliar pada periode yang sama tahun lalu.

Berdasarkan segmennya, Kimia Klinik masih menjadi kontributor terbesar dengan pendapatan Rp11,58 miliar. Namun, angka tersebut anjlok 74,4% dibandingkan 9M2025 yang mencapai Rp45,27 miliar.

"Penurunan pendapatan terutama dipengaruhi penyesuaian pola pembelian pelanggan serta perubahan kebutuhan pada sejumlah kategori produk," kata manajemen PRDL dalam keterangannya, Kamis (29/10/2026).

Penurunan juga terjadi pada segmen Hematologi yang turun 32,1% menjadi Rp2,03 miliar. Pendapatan Jasa merosot 64,6% menjadi Rp307 juta, sedangkan Imunologi dan Biomolekuler turun 48,9% menjadi Rp11 juta.

Baca Juga: Prodia Diagnostic Line (PRDL) Siapkan 17 Produk Baru dan Perluas Pasar

Di tengah penurunan tersebut, segmen Instrumen justru mencatat pertumbuhan. Pendapatan dari segmen ini naik 29,5% secara tahunan menjadi Rp7,56 miliar. Kontribusinya terhadap total pendapatan juga meningkat menjadi 35,2% dari sebelumnya 10,6%.

Penurunan pendapatan turut menekan profitabilitas PRDL. Laba kotor pada 9M2026 tercatat Rp6,58 miliar, turun 82% dari Rp36,65 miliar pada periode yang sama tahun lalu. Margin laba kotor pun menyusut menjadi 30,6% dari 66,7%.

Alhasil, PRDL membukukan rugi operasi Rp9,3 miliar dan rugi bersih Rp11,09 miliar pada 9M2026. Pada periode yang sama tahun lalu, perusahaan masih mencatat laba operasi Rp19,84 miliar dan laba bersih Rp13,71 miliar.

Meski demikian, tekanan terhadap EBITDA mulai mereda pada kuartal III-2026. LBITDA sepanjang 9M2026 tercatat Rp4,34 miliar, tetapi pada kuartal III hanya sebesar Rp43 juta. Beban usaha pada kuartal tersebut juga turun 43% secara tahunan menjadi Rp3,3 miliar.

Dari sisi neraca, kondisi PRDL justru semakin kuat. Kas dan setara kas per September 2026 mencapai Rp21,8 miliar, naik dari Rp10,8 miliar pada akhir 2025.

Perusahaan juga telah membayar utang bank sebesar Rp35,8 miliar sepanjang 9M2026. Alhasil, total utang berbunga turun 35,7% menjadi Rp60,6 miliar dari Rp94,3 miliar pada akhir 2025. Rasio utang berbunga terhadap ekuitas juga turun menjadi 0,46 kali dari 1,14 kali.

Ke depan, PRDL akan mengejar pertumbuhan melalui perluasan basis pelanggan, khususnya rumah sakit dan laboratorium swasta. Perusahaan juga memperkuat sinergi dengan PT Prodia Widyahusada Tbk (PRDA), mengikuti tender serta mengembangkan produk baru dan solusi point of care testing (POCT).

Selain itu, PRDL melihat peluang pertumbuhan dari layanan kalibrasi dan pemeliharaan instrumen. Perusahaan juga akan mengoptimalkan fasilitas produksi baru yang memiliki kapasitas hingga empat kali fasilitas sebelumnya untuk meningkatkan utilisasi dan penyerapan biaya tetap.
