#692. This Oddbal Reflective Lava World is Challenging to Understand

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Among Kepler and TESS's many surprising exoplanet discoveries are the ultra-short period (USP) planets. These planets move very quickly and complete an orbit in less than 24 hours. Ancient astronomers thought that Mercury was so fastâit goes around the Sun every 88 daysâthat they named it after the Roman god Mercury, who was the fast-travelling messenger of the Gods. They couldn't have known how sluggish Mercury is compared to the USPs.

Their speed is just one of their remarkable qualities. Since they're so close to their stars, USPs are exposed to the full, blazing force of their stars' powerful radiation. This means these planets should have lost their atmospheres to space.

But one USP named TOI-561 b seemed to be different. Early observations detected hints of an atmosphere, and a 2025 paper baed on limited JWST observations suggested it had an atmosphere made of volatiles like water and carbon dioxide. Now, some of the same researchers revisited the exoplanet. The new work used the JWST to examine the exoplanet's atmosphere in more detail.

An artist's illustration of TOI-561 b. In 2025, JWST observations showed that the exoplanet had an unexpected atmosphere. The researchers thought it had a thick atmosphere above a molten surface. Image credit: NASA / ESA / CSA / Ralf Crawford, STScI.

The research is "A Highly Reflective Atmosphere on the Lava World TOI-561b Revealed by JWST/NIRSpec Phase Curves," and it's published in The Astronomical Journal. The first author is Samuel Boucher from the Department of Physics and Trottier Institute for Research on Exoplanets, at the UniversitÃ© de MontrÃ©al, Canada.

"Our discovery challenges current theories. We would expect a heavily irradiated rocky planet like TOI-561 b to have lost its atmosphere billions of years ago." - Professor Lisa Dang, University of Waterloo.

"One of the most pressing questions in exoplanet science today is whether small rocky exoplanets can host atmospheres," the authors write. If they do, scientists expect these atmosphere to be secondary rather than primordial. Some portion of the atmosphere could come from off-gassing or other exchanges with the exoplanet's interior, and that is evidence of processes that are not visible in remote observations. That has implications for understanding how rocky planets evolve, and how habitability may arise.

In a twist, rocky USPs may be the best place to look for secondary atmospheres and the clues they hold to deeper planetary processes. That's because they're probably magma oceans, at least on their daysides, and they can "be in continuous exchange with and fuel an atmosphere," the researchers write.

"Ultrashort period (USP) rocky planets are expected to be depleted of any substantial gaseous envelope due to the intense irradiation they receive from their host star, making the recent detection of an atmosphere around TOI-561 b particularly surprising," the authors write. "That finding was based on the planetâs bulk density and dayside emission spectrum, but full-orbit phase-curve observations offer a more comprehensive way to constrain the presence and characteristics of a planetâs atmosphere."

In this work, the authors observed TOI-561 b for 37 hours, and because it completes an orbit in about 10.5 hours, 37 hours of observing time was equal to more than three complete orbits. That means the JWST got a really good look at the planet. This generated more fullsome data to understand the exoplanet, its atmosphere, and what that can tell planetary scientists about hidden processes.

The observations of TOI-561 b in previous research showed that the exoplanet's dayside was much cooler than it should be for a bare rock world this close to a star. There could only be two explanations for this: either the heat it was reflected back into space, or it was carried to the nightside by an atmosphere. These new full-orbit observations gave researchers their first look at the exoplanet's nightside.

They found that the nightside wasn't warm enough to have acquired the missing heat. About three-quarters of it must have been reflected into space. To try to understand what was happening, the researchers turned to a General Circulation Model (GCM) and compared it to their observations. "We conclude from the GCM simulations that heat redistribution alone cannot match the observed dayside fluxes or amplitude of the phase curve, and a high-albedo source (Ab = 0.6) indicative of clouds or haze," they write.

âWe measured how the planetâs brightness and heat changed throughout its orbit," said Professor Lisa Dang, project lead at the University of Waterloo, Ontario, Canada. "This technique, known as a phase curve, revealed that the planet reflects a significant amount of light from its star, and that heat is being redistributed around the planet instead of only on the sunlit side. Together, these point to the presence of an atmosphere.â

They found that "multiple possible cloud species can form on the dayside including MgSiO3 and SiO2," and that SiO2 in particular could play a role. SiO2 "could help to explain the high albedo required by the GCM to match the observed dayside emission," the researchers explain. Basically, these clouds are like a sunscreen, protecting the planet from intense heat.

These results are a confirmation of earlier work showing that, against all expectations, TOI-561 b has a significant atmosphere. This challenges the current understanding of how rocky super-Earth USPs evolve.

"Our discovery challenges current theories," Professor Dang said. "We would expect a heavily irradiated rocky planet like TOI-561 b to have lost its atmosphere billions of years ago. Instead, the evidence suggests it still has a significant atmosphere today."

"Atmospheric-escape theory suggests that strongly irradiated super-Earths should be devoid of any volatile envelopes," the researchers explain. "This is particularly astonishing given that the current JWST census of rocky exoplanets has been interpreted as suggestive of a population of bare-rock planets..."

They note that most of those planets are orbiting M-dwarfs, while TOI-561 b orbits a late G/early K dwarf, a more Sun-like star. These red dwarf planets are less irradiated than TOI-561 b, so it's strange and unexpected for it to host an atmosphere. "If rocky exoplanets at intermediate irradiation are completely desiccated, even-more irradiated exoplanets such as TOI-561 b should be, too," the researchers write.

The authors suggest a couple possible explanations for this. It's possible that TOI-561 b started out with a massive amount of atmospheric volatiles, meaning it began as a volatile-rich sub-Neptune and has had its atmosphere whittled away over time. It's also possible that we're seeing its secondary atmosphere, and that primary H/He atmospheres are more easily stripped away, while secondary atmospheres persist.

Migration could play a role, too. If the planet was further from its star while the star was younger and emitting more powerful radiation, then moved closer as the star calmed down, it's been exposed to less radiation overall, so hasn't lost its atmosphere. Lastly, in what they call option d, the authors write that "the originally accreted atmospheric volatiles were sequestered into the interior and later outgassed."

"Why TOI-561 b has an atmosphere may be due to a combination of more than one of these options," the authors write.

Whatever the case, these results are extremely interesting. The fourth possibility, that the exoplanet's original atmsophere was sequestered into its interior during its magma ocean phase and released later, is particularly interesting. "It is an exciting prospect that constraining the composition of a rocky planetâs secondary atmosphere could provide insights into its geochemical evolution (option d), such as the redox state of the magma ocean," the researchers write.

Whether one or more or all of the outlined explanations are responsible for TOI-561 b's atmosphere can hopefully be explained in future work. Part of that work will be comparing the exoplanet to bodies we're more familiar with.

âThe next big question we will seek to answer is whether TOI-561 bâs atmosphere resembles Venus or an extreme version of lo," Dang said. "Observing the planet at longer wavelengths with JWST could give us insights and help explain how a planet this hot has held on to an atmosphere for billions of years.â

#627. Giant Antarctic iceberg approaches remote island after three-year journey

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

A massive iceberg is slowing near an underwater shelf that has trapped and broken apart other Antarctic icebergs in the past.

An enormous iceberg that broke away from Antarctica more than three years ago has traveled thousands of miles across the Southern Ocean and is now approaching a remote island in the South Atlantic.

Known as Iceberg A-81, the massive block of ice has followed a route taken by many Antarctic icebergs. But its journey could soon face an unexpected obstacle.

A new satellite image captured by NASA’s Terra satellite on October 20, 2026, shows the iceberg nearing South Georgia, an island surrounded by underwater terrain that has trapped other giant icebergs in the past.

Iceberg A-81 began its journey in January 2023, when it separated from Antarctica’s Brunt Ice Shelf.

At the time, the iceberg covered approximately 600 square miles (1,550 square kilometers), making it an enormous floating mass of glacial ice.

Despite its size, the iceberg did not immediately head into the open ocean.

Instead, it spent more than three years moving through the Weddell Sea, where powerful ocean currents carried it along the Antarctic coastline.

The movement was largely driven by the Weddell Gyre, a massive clockwise circulation of ocean water that influences how icebergs travel through the region.

Some Antarctic icebergs remain close to their original ice shelves for decades. Others eventually escape into warmer northern waters, where they begin to melt and break apart.

A-81 followed the second path, although its departure was anything but quick.

By May 2026, Iceberg A-81 had reached the northern tip of the Antarctic Peninsula.

From there, it moved into the Scotia Sea, leaving the waters surrounding Antarctica and continuing toward the South Atlantic.

By late October, the iceberg had traveled approximately 1,400 miles (2,300 kilometers) north-northwest of the Brunt Ice Shelf.

Its destination was the vicinity of South Georgia, a remote island surrounded by cold, nutrient-rich waters.

The island lies along a route frequently traveled by icebergs escaping the Weddell Sea.

However, the underwater landscape surrounding South Georgia can dramatically change their movements.

As Iceberg A-81 approached South Georgia, its progress began to slow.

Satellite observations suggested that the iceberg was interacting with the edge of the island’s underwater continental shelf.

This shallow underwater terrain can interfere with the movement of enormous icebergs, particularly when their submerged portions extend deep below the ocean surface.

Jan Lieser of Australia’s Bureau of Meteorology and Christopher Shuman, a retired researcher from the University of Maryland, identified the apparent slowdown.

Their observations suggest that A-81 may have been bumping against the edge of the underwater shelf.

South Georgia has created similar obstacles for other giant icebergs.

In 2025, Iceberg A-23A became grounded on the island’s underwater shelf after its own long journey from Antarctica.

The enormous iceberg eventually broke into smaller pieces, demonstrating how the region can influence the final stages of an iceberg’s life.

Whether A-81 will experience a similar fate remains uncertain.

Although A-81 spent years traveling away from Antarctica, the process that created it began much earlier.

For approximately 35 years, a major fracture known as Chasm 1 remained relatively stable within the Brunt Ice Shelf.

Around 2012, however, the fracture began expanding again.

By 2019, it was growing rapidly enough that scientists expected a large section of the ice shelf to break away.

Yet the ice remained attached for several more years.

The final separation occurred in January 2023, when the expanding fracture produced Iceberg A-81.

This process, known as calving, occurs naturally when sections of floating ice shelves break away and become icebergs.

Although calving is a normal part of Antarctic ice dynamics, scientists closely monitor major fractures because they can reveal important changes in ice shelf stability.

The Brunt Ice Shelf has attracted considerable scientific attention because several enormous icebergs have separated from it in recent years.

In 2021, a fracture called the North Rift produced Iceberg A-74. Two years later, Chasm 1 released A-81.

Then, in 2024, another major fracture known as the Halloween Crack produced Iceberg A-83.

These events have significantly reshaped portions of the floating ice shelf.

The region is also home to the British Antarctic Survey’s Halley Research Station, which has supported research into Earth’s atmosphere, climate, and space environment since the 1950s.

Monitoring the surrounding ice is therefore important for understanding both natural changes and conditions affecting scientific operations.

A-81 is not the only iceberg moving northward from the Weddell Sea.

Iceberg A-74 has already broken apart, while A-83 remains intact and is following a similar route toward the north.

Several other icebergs also appear to be leaving the region, carried by the ocean currents that shape their movements.

Their journeys illustrate how Antarctic ice can travel enormous distances before eventually melting or fragmenting.

For A-81, the next chapter may depend on the underwater terrain surrounding South Georgia.

The iceberg has already survived more than three years at sea.

Now, the same ocean currents that carried it so far from Antarctica have brought it to a place where other giant icebergs have met their end.

#3528. "Jagged" New York house is Aston Martin's first US residence

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Photography by Will Nainis of Sundae Spaces

Automotive company Aston Martin has collaborated with US studio S3 Architecture for the Sylvan Rock residential compound in Upstate New York, conforming the metal roofline to the outcroppings of stone on the site.

Located in the town of Red Hook, Sylvan Rock features a faceted, black-metal roofline that angles with the house, which was built to follow the site’s slope.

At the low end, the roof meets the ground, covering a walkway that leads to the primary entrance.

“When designing, we always let the land speak first and respond to it,” said S3 Architecture partner Christopher Dierig.

“The roofline mimics the jagged edges of the rock ledge reaching down into the earth, as if the home is born of and launching from the landscape,” Dierig continued.

A stairway leads up from the lower entrance into the primary spaces of the house, which has two storeys on top of the outcropping.

Also on the lower level is a garage that forms a plinth for one of two swimming pool areas. Part of the garage was carved into the rockspace and the studio referred to it as “subterranean”.

The main, elevated body of the residence has glass walls interspersed with black metal panelling. Four bedrooms and five bathrooms are spread over this space.

Living and dining spaces are located on the ground plane, in line with the pool areas. A glass bridge connects an auxiliary spa and sauna space.

Luxury wood finishes and stone filter through the interiors, which are mostly monotone.

A floating staircase sits in the middle of the plan and provides access to the upper level, which holds the bedroom suites and features balconies that look out into the wooded landscape.

Wooden ceilings and beams lighten up the space and multiple fireplaces were included in the living spaces, including in the primary suite upstairs.

These interior spaces were designed by the Aston Martin team and also feature one-of-a-kind custom furniture by the brand.

Certain elements have direct reference to the company’s branding, including the brand’s lattice motif covering the wine storage in the primary lounge and a movie theatre that includes seating reminiscent of automobile seats.

The site also includes guest residences, a pavilion and a treehouse. The residences feature similar metal roofs and exteriors.

The house is one of two branded residences by Aston Martin planned throughout the world, with the other located in Japan.

It follows a trend of automotive brands entering the architecture field, directly designing residences and even skyscrapers, especially in the US.

For example, Bugatti unveiled a branded skyscraper design for Miami last month. Aston Martin completed its own skyscraper in Miami in 2024.

When confronted with complaints that the voice on purported call recordings did not match the beneficiaries’ voice, one co-conspirator explained that “some of the calls from overseas would be faked” and used to justify a bogus prescription. The marketing companies then paid medical practitioners to sign prescriptions for orthotic braces without contacting or evaluating the patients, or stole doctor’s credentials to create forged prescriptions, and then sold those prescriptions to Dufort’s and her co-conspirators’ DME companies.

Dufort and her co-conspirators then shipped braces to the Medicare beneficiaries and billed Medicare for millions of dollars in claims, despite the fact that the beneficiaries did not want or need the braces and no doctor had examined the beneficiary to determine if they needed the braces. And, as one medical expert testified, use of these combinations of braces could be “very dangerous” for the elderly Medicare population. When people complained and wanted to return the braces, “they would just tell [them] to just get rid of them or . . . just pass them down” so Dufort and her co-conspirators would not have to pay Medicare back.

Back in 2018, the Farm Bill legalized hemp production and removed qualifying hemp from the federal Controlled Substances Act, helping fuel a new market for hemp-derived CBD and THC products. Hemp and marijuana both come from the cannabis plant, but the law defined hemp as containing no more than 0.3% delta-9 THC by dry weight and included its derivatives, extracts, and cannabinoids.

That definition helped create the opening for today's hemp-derived THC market, although the Farm Bill didn’t automatically legalize every hemp-derived food or drink and the FDA retained authority over consumable products. The THC drink market alone is now projected to reach $5.6 billion by 2035.

The Farm Bill was intended in part to create new legal markets for hemp, including uses like textiles and fibers. But lawmakers have since pointed to the rise of intoxicating hemp-derived products as an unintended consequence of the way the law defined hemp.

Congress has already moved to close what lawmakers call a loophole in the 2018 law. Under legislation passed in 2025, the federal definition of hemp will become much narrower, excluding certain synthetic or manufactured cannabinoids as well as final hemp-derived cannabinoid products containing more than 0.4 mg of total THC and other cannabinoids with similar effects per container—a limit that would sideline most current THC drinks and edibles.

Supporters of the change have pointed to concerns about intoxicating hemp products being sold with inconsistent federal oversight, including products such as gummies, drinks, and candy.

Congress recently delayed most of those restrictions until December 11, which Joe Gerrity, CEO of Crescent Canna, believes buys time to replace prohibition with smart regulation. While restrictions involving certain synthetic cannabinoids are still scheduled to take effect November 12, Gerrity points to two proposed alternatives to the current federal restrictions.

“Lawmakers already have two bipartisan starting points,” Gerrity explains. “The Lawful Hemp Protection Act would set national standards for labeling, age verification, serving limits, and FDA oversight, [while] the Beverage Regulatory Parity Act focuses on THC drinks, with a 21+ requirement, TTB oversight, potency limits, testing, and taxation.”

#1094. Are Young People Overusing Retinol? Dermatologists Weigh In

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Experts explain when the ingredient helps, when it hurts, and why a simple routine may be enough.

Retinol is undoubtedly one of the greatest ingredient breakthroughs in skin care. The vitamin A derivative has powerful wrinkle- and texture-smoothing benefits, but too much of it or improper use can have pretty damaging effects on the skin barrier and overall skin health.

Alongside sunscreen, some form of retinol is often recommended by dermatologists, particularly for more mature skin. Milder doses also work well as a preventative ingredient in younger skin; its popularity across TikTok and other social platforms has raised the question of whether younger skin needs such a strong ingredient, and whether retinol is being overused. Derms have weighed in and offer alternatives that may be less damaging and more appropriate for an early skin care journey.

Dr. Kseniya Kobets, director of cosmetic dermatology at Montefiore Einstein Advanced Care, says some are. “Especially when social media makes them feel they should already be preventing wrinkles. Then they combine retinol, exfoliating toner, and acne treatments and wonder why everything burns. But retinoids can help acne, clogged pores, texture, and pigmentation, so for me it’s less about the birthday and more about the skin’s needs and appropriate use,” Kobets tells ELLE.

There’s no magic age as to when it’s safe to introduce retinol into a skin care routine. What’s most important is understanding its purpose and when your skin may need it.

Board-certified dermatologist and founder of Weiser Skin MD, Dr. Jessica Weiser, says retinol can benefit teens with acne and can be a useful prejuvenation product for people in their 20s and 30s—but it’s best to use it under professional guidance. Weiser notes that in general, retinol shouldn’t be used for antiaging purposes until your 20s or even early 30s. Age isn’t the only consideration either: “For patients with sensitive skin, retinol can be irritating and cause inflammation or dermatitis,” Weiser notes.

Kobets further explains that while retinol is part of the vitamin A family called retinoids, cosmetic retinol differs from acne medications. “OTC adapalene 0.1% is labeled for acne at 12 and older; younger children need clinician guidance. That doesn’t mean every 12-year-old needs it,” says Kobets.

Without a specific concern, Kobets recommends prioritizing cleanser, moisturizer, and sunscreen in your routine. “Adults can consider retinol for texture, pigmentation, or fine lines; you haven’t missed a deadline by not starting at 20,” Kobets adds.

Weiser and Kobets both say retinol has significant skin benefits, depending on what you’re looking to treat. “Retinol is very useful for a few purposes: collagen stimulation to prevent or soften fine lines and wrinkles and also for skin turnover and oil regulation,” Weiser describes. “In this sense, retinol can help to prevent wrinkles from worsening but cannot impact muscle movement and therefore cannot fully prevent lines and wrinkles or aging,” she clarifies.

The ingredient has been shown to support collagen and improve photodamage, although Kobets notes that prescription tretinoin has stronger evidence and OTC formulas vary in efficacy. “Acne-directed retinoids also help prevent clogged pores and breakouts,” says Kobets, adding that for sun-related changes, sunscreen, hats, and shade come first. “We don’t have evidence that children need retinol to prevent wrinkles.”

Perhaps it’s no coincidence that as retinol product options increase, so have solutions for skin barrier support and restoration. “Retinol can cause dryness, flaking, irritation, and skin redness; it can weaken the skin barrier,” says Weiser. Barrier disruption and inflammation can leave dark marks.

Additionally, it can cause irritant dermatitis: redness, dryness, peeling, burning, and stinging from normally tolerated products. “Appropriate use doesn’t permanently thin skin, but pushing through irritation makes things worse. Burning, rawness, or an itchy rash shouldn’t be dismissed as ‘purging,’” Kobets cautions.

Weiser also notes that the effects of retinol are cumulative and can take months or even years to be appreciated. “Retinol can exacerbate redness, rosacea, dermatitis/eczema, and more, so it should be used with caution in sensitive and redness-prone skin. Finally, retinol causes thinning of the epidermis (top skin layer), so it should be stopped before waxing and some types of laser treatment,” Weiser advises.

Key advice from Kobets and Weiser is to start slow. They both recommend using retinol twice a week at the beginning and titrating up as tolerated. Kobets specifies starting with a pea-sized amount for the face and avoiding eyelids, nose, and mouth corners unless the product is intended there.

You’ll also want to take extra caution on the neck and chest where, as Weiser points out, there are significantly fewer sebaceous glands, so skin can become more easily irritated and dry.

How you layer in a retinol can make a difference in lowering irritation, too. “Moisturizer can go before, after, or both; initially separate exfoliating acids onto other nights. Results take weeks to months, and collagen-related changes take longer,” says Kobets.

Additionally, for sensitive skin, a sandwich technique can be used where clean skin is treated first with moisturizer, then with retinol, and then with another layer of moisturizer to sandwich the retinol between two layers of emollients.

The good news is that irritation usually improves not long after you stop using a retinol product. If you do feel like your barrier is compromised and skin is more irritated or worse damage has set in, Kobets says to pause retinol, acids, and scrubs and replace them with a gentle cleanser, fragrance-free moisturizer with glycerin or ceramides, and sunscreen. A little petrolatum can help dry areas. Wait until comfortable before restarting more slowly, possibly with a different product. Swelling, blistering, crusting, or persistent irritation should be evaluated by a dermatologist. Also keep in mind that residual dark marks can take longer to fade. Weiser adds that barrier repair creams or lipid-replenishing creams can be useful.

As with any ingredient, choose based on the concern you want to address. “Healthy younger skin may only need cleansing, moisturizer, and sunscreen. Salicylic acid or benzoyl peroxide can help acne, and azelaic acid can address breakouts and pigmentation, but all can irritate,” says Kobets. So, just like retinol, start slow and see how your skin responds.

If you’ve already started using retinol and want a gentler alternative, Kobets also recommends bakuchiol, a plant-derived active with some retinol-like effects. It’s not actual retinol. However, she adds a caveat: the testing of bakuchiol to treat pigment issues has been rather limited, and broader analyses haven’t established clear pigment-reducing benefit. “It’s an option for some adults who don’t tolerate retinol, not an equally proven replacement, especially for pigmentation, or something children need. Plant-derived doesn’t mean allergy- or irritation-free,” Kobets clarifies.

Final Cut Pro, Apple’s professional-grade video editor, is getting built-in MotionVFX. That should bring more visual and graphic effects tools to content editors on Mac. Logic Pro is getting a new beta AI assistant (powered by AI models built by Apple and OpenAI) for music editors in the US, along with a redesigned browser that uses Apple’s new Liquid Glass aesthetic. Pixelmator Pro is getting a kind of generative fill tool that uses AI to resize and fill in the gaps in new images; it sounds like it may be similar to Photoshop’s popular generative fill.

The Apple news has been fast and furious this fall. The Cupertino-based company dropped the new iPhone 18 Pro lineup, along with its first folding phone, the iPhone Duo. Then it refreshed its entire smart home lineup, complete with a new HomeView device. We’re expecting even more Apple hardware news to drop on Wednesday, the in-person day of its Pro and Tell event that experts say could bring another long-awaited Apple first: a touchscreen MacBook. CNET is tracking all the Apple shenanigans this week and testing the new iPhone Duo — by folding it over 75,000 times and counting.
