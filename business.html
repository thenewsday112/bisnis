"Kita ingin ya produk-produk yang kita kurasi itu tampil pertama awalnya tampil di ruang tamu Mendag dulu. Nanti kan bisa saya kenalkan ke tamu baik dalam negeri maupun luar negeri ya. Salah satu produk lokal yang juga mencetak sukses di pasar ekspor saat gelaran TEI 2026 adalah kerupuk ikan produksi Palembang.

"Kerupuk ini ikut trade expo. Dapat transaksi dari Belanda, China, Taiwan, dan Arab. Dari Belanda aja US$10 juta (setara US$181,75 miliar). Ini UMKM, kerupuk dari Palembang, namanya Awan Kerupuk," kata Budi.

As a six-time CEO who has served on 23 corporate boards, I’ve watched the same story unfold in nearly every sector. An ambitious founder builds a business, reaches the point where they want to step back from daily operations, hires a CEO and moves into the chairman role, assuming the hardest work is behind them. The founder feels the new leader is moving too slowly or making different choices, while the CEO feels micromanaged and stripped of authority. Once that trust breaks, the transition meant to unlock the company’s next chapter becomes a source of friction and lost momentum.

Having navigated this handoff from both sides of the table, I call this dynamic “the chairman’s gap”: the gray zone between providing governance and interfering. Staying on the right side of that line doesn’t happen by accident. It requires unlearning the operational habits that made you successful in the first place.

When founders ask me how to make the move from founder to chairman, they usually expect a tactical playbook on board meetings or governance structures. The mechanics matter, but I’ve found the real challenge is psychological.

As an operating CEO, your daily survival depends on answering “how.” You inspect details, solve bottlenecks and drive execution. As chairman, you have to give up ownership of the “how” and focus on the “what” and “why”: setting strategic direction, securing capital and holding your CEO accountable for results.

I put this into practice when I co-founded a luxury vehicle business. I knew from day one that I wouldn’t run daily operations, so I brought in an industry expert I had mentored for five years as president and CEO. Different isn’t wrong; it’s just different. When a chairman accepts that there are multiple routes to a goal, the partnership thrives.

IRS CEO Frank Bisignano has donated to Trump Accounts for certain kids in Brooklyn, the agency announced Thursday.

The Bisignano Family Fund donated $250 apiece to Trump Accounts of eligible children ages 2 to 8 living in 11234, Bisignano's hometown ZIP code, according to the release.

"My life was shaped by the people, schools, and neighborhoods in Marine Park, Mill Basin, Bergen Beach, and Flatlands," Bisignano said in a statement. "This donation is an investment in the children of Brooklyn, giving them a boost in their personal wealth building journeys."

Within that ZIP code, fewer than 10,000 U.S. citizens are under 10 years of age, according to CNBC's analysis of the most recent population estimate from the U.S. Census Bureau.

The contribution will be invested in a diversified index fund, the release also said.

Expanding access to Trump Accounts has been a Trump administration priority. In July, Bisignano was tasked with leading that effort.

The latest Trump Account news comes weeks after the Treasury Department auto-enrolled more than 60 million children.

Trump Accounts, also known as 530A accounts, are open to any U.S. citizen under 18 with a Social Security number. Children born from 2025 through 2028 may also claim a one-time $1,000 pilot program contribution from the Treasury.

Families must still activate their child's Trump Account to receive contributions if eligible, according to Treasury guidance.

Bisignano's commitment comes on the heels of news that Elon Musk plans to give every kid in America a share of SpaceX in 2027.

Altimeter Capital CEO Brad Gerstner, founder of Invest America and an architect of Trump Accounts, described Musk's gift as "the tip of the iceberg" for philanthropic gifts on CNBC's "Squawk Box" Oct. 21. Gerstner has committed about $35 million to help fund investment accounts for kids in his home state of Indiana.

More gifts are in the pipeline, Bisignano told CNBC's "Squawk Box" on Oct. 8. "They range from small to mega," he said.

After claiming an account, families can track deposits in the Trump Accounts app, which was designed in partnership with Robinhood.

"Trump Accounts is direct philanthropy, extremely efficient, direct to children...," Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev said on CNBC's "Squawk on the Street" on Oct. 28.

"Excited to see Elon donation," he said. "I think there will be many others as well."

The Treasury Department in September released temporary regulations allowing Trump Accounts to hold donated individual stocks, a shift from previous guidelines that permitted only diversified, low-cost index funds. The Trump administration said the move could pave the way for more multimillion-dollar donations, with a number of philanthropists poised to participate.

The latest donation comes just days before the midterm election, and Republicans fight to keep slim congressional majorities in the Senate and House.

#302. Inside the scramble that moved Thursday's WNBA Finals game to 12:30 p.m. ET

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The CNBC Sport newsletter with Alex Sherman brings you the biggest news and exclusive interviews from the worlds of sports business and media, delivered weekly to your inbox.

Moving Game 6 of the WNBA finals to a 12:30 p.m. ET time slot on Thursday puts a spotlight on a league that's ready for a bigger stage — even if the bigger stages aren't always ready for the WNBA.

Game 6 between the Atlanta Dream and the Golden State Valkyries will be played Thursday at 12:30 p.m. ET/9:30 a.m. PT — a shocking time for a finals elimination game. A Golden State win would give them the series, 4-2. A Dream win would send the series back to Golden State for Game 7 on Saturday, Oct. 31.

Earlier this week, it became clear there was a good chance the series was going to run into a timing problem for Game 6. The Dream has been playing its playoff games at Atlanta's State Farm Arena, the home of the Atlanta Hawks, which seats 17,000. State Farm will be the permanent home for the Dream beginning next season.

Game 6 presented an issue — the Hawks also are playing at State Farm tonight. The Dream plays its regular-season games at Gateway Center Arena, which seats just 3,500 people.

Earlier this week, a multiparty discussion began between the teams' ownership groups, WNBA Commissioner Cathy Engelbert, NBA Commissioner Adam Silver, Atlanta Hawks CEO Steve Koonin, and Versant, which owns the Game 6 media rights. All parties began brainstorming ideas about where and when to play the game.

The Dream and Hawks have different ownership. The Dream put out an open letter Tuesday saying it had raised concerns about potentially conflicting games two months ago to the WNBA, and that those concerns were ignored. Sources close to the league said it's the team's responsibility to secure an arena in case of conflict. Playoff dates were issued by the league in Dec. 2025.

The Dream never secured another arena to play the game other than Gateway. The NBA was brought in to help problem-solve just days ago, according to two people familiar with the matter. The WNBA has its own scheduling division separate from the NBA.

Earlier this week, the teams, the league, and Versant executives began throwing out solutions. Many different ones were considered, including moving the Hawks game to a different time, keeping the game at Gateway and playing a Game 6 in front of 3,500 people. They also weighed moving Game 6 to Wednesday, Friday, or Saturday at State Farm and then moving Game 7 in San Francisco, or finding another local arena for the game.

All options involving shifting the day had problems. The Hawks also played Wednesday. USA Network already had WWE's Friday Night SmackDown during the Friday primetime TV slot and the Breeders' Cup on Saturday afternoon. State Farm had a concert featuring rapper-singer Young Miko already on the schedule for Saturday night. Moving Game 7 to Tuesday would bump up against Election Day.

The Hawks game couldn't be moved to 12:30 p.m. ET given existing NBA collective bargaining agreement rules, which don't allow teams to play games without a 22-hour break. Both the Hawks and the Cleveland Cavaliers play tonight at 7 p.m. ET.

There was even discussion of moving the Hawks game to another month, but given the density of the NBA schedule, too many other teams' schedules would also need to shift for this to be feasible, according to people familiar with the matter.

Both teams and their players did not want to play at Gateway, given the importance of the game — a sentiment echoed by Golden State Warriors coach Steve Kerr this week, who said moving the game was "better than having it at the YMCA."

Both teams felt uncomfortable playing the game at a non-professional basketball arena such as Mercedes-Benz Stadium, where the Atlanta Falcons play, due to potential safety concerns and lack of familiarity with the venue, according to people familiar with the matter.

The Dream has been selling out State Farm and has already sold out today's game. Golden State plays its games at San Francisco's Chase Center, which has seating for 18,000 fans. The atmosphere for the Valkyries' games throughout the playoffs has been on par with almost any NBA Finals game. Golden State calls its arena "Ballhalla."

Versant initially favored keeping the game at Gateway to maximize primetime viewership, according to people familiar with the matter. A skilled sports TV production team can make a game with only 3,500 fans seem much bigger — and louder — to a television audience.

Moving the game to 12:30 p.m. ET will likely lead to an audience loss in the hundreds of thousands, according to two media executives who asked not to be named for fear of retribution from the league. Game 2 averaged 1.33 million viewers on USA Network and Peacock. Game 6 in primetime would almost certainly have averaged more.

This is the background to what led to moving the game time. The game will air on USA and two other Versant networks, E! and Syfy. It will also be rebroadcast at 8 p.m. ET — its original start time — on USA Network and CNBC.

Still, it's possible the amount of attention this scheduling shift has gotten in recent days could boost awareness of the game. "The daytime window isn't perfect from a TV audience perspective, but the league had to make a really difficult decision taking a lot into account across multiple stakeholders," a USA Network spokesperson said. "What was important to us was to ultimately be a good partner and to be part of the solution here."
