#841. Suicide deaths in Germany surge to 27-year high

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Germany has seen its highest annual suicide toll in nearly three decades, with 11,334 deaths recorded last year, according to the Federal Statistical Office. Experts suggest that the surge may be linked to a loosely regulated assisted-suicide system, which allows even healthy adults to seek help ending their lives.

The data set released earlier this month and analyzed in greater detail on Wednesday by Germany’s National Suicide Prevention Program (NaSPro) found a rise of 962 suicides, or 9.3%, in 2025, taking the annual toll to its highest level since 1998.

The total was greater than the approximately 7,677 people killed in traffic accidents, homicides, illegal-drug-related deaths and AIDS combined, the report said, adding that men accounted for 69.2% of suicides.

Since 2021, the annual total has climbed 23% though the number of suicides remains below its historical peak of 18,825 in 1981, NaSPro said.

While men accounted for most suicides, the increase was disproportionately concentrated among women, whose deaths rose 17.9%, compared with 5.8% among men. Most suicides (73.7%) also occur in the over-50 age group.

Although hanging remained the most common method of suicide (4,145), it was followed by medication (2,848 cases), which saw a striking surge compared to 2024, when it accounted for 2,002 deaths. This category alone accounted for nearly 88% of the entire annual increase.

The latest figures also point to an increase in suicide deaths among the German youth: among girls aged 15 to 19, deaths surged from 52 in 2024 to 84 in 2025, while the overall toll among teenagers rose from 188 to 208. Suicide accounted for approximately 18% of all deaths among people aged five to 24.

While the German government did not offer a definitive explanation for the dramatic increase, multiple experts linked it to the expansion of assisted suicides, which are not separately identified in official mortality statistics. Three major organizations providing such services reported 1,287 assisted deaths last year, up from 977 in 2024 and just 346 in 2021; medication is also the most common method used in assisted suicides.

Germany’s legal framework permits assisted suicide even for people who are not terminally ill or suffering from incurable conditions. Individuals willing to go this path are not required to provide any justification, and in principle, even a physically healthy young adult can legally seek assistance if the decision is voluntary, informed, and enduring. However, nobody is obliged to assist, and euthanasia performed directly by another person remains illegal.

Ulrich Hegerl, chairman of the German Depression Aid and Suicide Prevention Foundation, criticized what he described as the country’s “extreme position” on the matter, warning that “when assisted suicide becomes normalized as a service, this threshold is lowered, and the number of tragic, preventable suicides rises.”

Suicide researcher Ute Lewitzka of Goethe University Frankfurt, meanwhile, has offered another explanation, describing the surge as a crisis phenomenon. She suggested that financial hardship and uncertainty can intensify suicidal thoughts among vulnerable people, while social media could also contribute. Lewitzka also criticized what she described as Germany’s inadequate access to psychological care.

#1085. Russia’s international reserves up $6.4 bln over week — Central Bank

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

MOSCOW, October 29. /TASS/. Russia’s international reserves increased by $6.4 bln over the week and totaled $729.6 bln as of October 23, 2026, the Central Bank said.

"International reserves amounted to $729.6 bln as of the close of business on October 23, 2026, having increased by $6.4 bln or by 0.9% over the week, largely due to positive revaluation," the regulator informed.

Reserves totaled $723.2 bln as of October 16.

Russia’s international reserves are highly liquid foreign assets available with the Bank of Russia and the Russian Government. They comprise foreign currency, Special Drawing Rights (SDRs), a reserve position in the International Monetary Fund and monetary gold. The target level of the Central Bank for international reserves is $500 bln.

#1087. Mission in Alyoshki and unacceptability of colonial practices: Putin’s statements

MOSCOW, October 29. /TASS/. The Russian business community rejects colonial methods and conducts business with African partners on respectful terms, investing in their countries, Russian President Vladimir Putin stated at the Russia-Africa summit.

It is hardly possible to speak of socio-economic development without guaranteeing security, he noted.

Putin added that a humanitarian operation is currently underway in Alyoshki in the Kherson Region to provide assistance to the civilian population.

TASS has compiled the key statements by the head of state.

It is impossible to discuss socio-economic development without guaranteeing security: "Naturally, without resolving these problems, it is impossible to speak of any kind of economic or social development. It is self-evident. Of course, the UN Security Council must devote the necessary attention to this."

A humanitarian operation is currently underway in Alyoshki in the Kherson Region to provide assistance to the civilian population: "Regarding the zone of the Russian-Ukrainian conflict: an operation is scheduled right now, over these next few days, today or tomorrow, with the support of the First Lady of the United States [Melania Trump] by the way, to provide aid specifically to the civilian population."

Ukrainian forces have mined the approaches to Alyoshki in the Kherson Region, bringing the settlement closer to a humanitarian catastrophe: "The situation had been developing for a long time."

Russia is exploring options for a peaceful settlement of the conflict in Ukraine: "We are working on this as well, just as we are addressing issues related to a peaceful settlement of the Ukrainian crisis itself."

Russia and Africa are natural allies in building a multipolar world system free from dictates: "Russia and Africa are united by truly strong bonds of friendship and close partnership. We are natural allies in building a multipolar and truly democratic world order. A world order free of diktat and double standards, based on respect for the norms of international law and fundamental principles of the UN Charter and, what's more, in their entirety and interrelation."

The positions of Russia and African states on the most pressing issues of global politics are similar or coincide: "We advocate for an equitable dialogue and for taking into account the interests of all states."

Russia-Africa summits bring significant added value, as they give a powerful impetus to the development of mutually beneficial ties: "Such summits offer us a valuable opportunity to exchange views on the current state of Russian-African relations and give a powerful boost to our multifaceted and mutually beneficial ties."

Moscow remains interested in seeing African countries develop, protect their sovereignty, and achieve sustainable economic growth: "Russia has been and remains interested in seeing African nations protect their sovereignty, achieve steady growth, and strengthen their economies and social sectors. To address these goals, we are ready to continue working together with you across all these areas."

Africa will be able not only to feed itself but also to become a major food exporter in the future: "We are convinced that through the application of modern agricultural technologies and the proper organization of agricultural production, as you have noted, dear friends, Africa will be able in the future not only to feed itself but also to become a major exporter of various types of food products."

Russia’s trade turnover with Africa has grown 1.5-fold since the previous summit, reaching $27 bln: "The momentum is good, but the true potential of our collaboration is significantly greater, of course," Putin added.

The Russian business community rejects colonial methods and conducts business with African partners on respectful terms, investing in their countries: "Our business community always approaches cooperation with African partners with honesty, respect, and good faith, conducting business on equal terms. Rather than siphoning off profits in the spirit of the colonial practices of former imperial powers, they invest what they earn in the development of your countries’ economies, eagerly share advanced technologies and expertise, and extensively engage local subcontractors, creating new production facilities and jobs."

Russian businesses in Africa demonstrate social responsibility by caring for the well-being of local employees and the local environment: "What is important, Russian businesses operate in Africa in a socially responsible manner: they look after the well-being of their employees and their families, fund and participate in the implementation of significant humanitarian programs, pay close attention to ecological and environmental issues, and show respect for internal political processes."

Moscow has established an uninterrupted channel for mutual settlements with African countries, with the ruble accounting for 85% of those transactions: "All of this helps to shield our economic exchanges from external influence and makes them more resilient to fluctuations in the global economic and political landscape."

Russian companies are interested in relocating some of their production facilities to African countries, especially in the current sanctions environment: "I won’t say anything unexpected, but this is entirely in Russia’s interests, especially in today’s environment, with numerous sanctions being applied to the Russian economy and Russian enterprises. Forty-seven countries on the continent are eligible for these benefits, with 32 of them enjoying completely zeroed-out customs duties."

Initiatives to introduce advanced Russian services are underway in Africa with the participation of Russian businesses: "Promising initiatives to introduce advanced Russian digital services are being actively implemented on the continent with the participation of Russian businesses."

Russia is doing much to help African countries maintain internal stability and security, and to strengthen their defense capabilities: "As a permanent member of the UN Security Council, our country makes a significant contribution to resolving ongoing conflicts and preventing new ones on the African continent. That said, we consistently proceed from the fundamental principle that you yourselves have proclaimed: ‘African solutions to African problems.’ Russia is doing a great deal to assist African states in maintaining internal stability and security, and in strengthening their defense and security capabilities."

Russia’s diplomatic presence on the African continent is being expanded systematically: "I must mention the systematic steps being taken by the Russian side to expand our diplomatic presence on the continent. Since the previous summit in 2023, eight new Russian embassies have begun operations in African states, bringing the total number to 48. At the same time, a network of trade missions is being developed; there are currently only six of them, but their activities cover 16 African countries."

Botswana, Seychelles and Togo are planning to open their embassies in Moscow soon, and Russia supports their intention: "We render all necessary assistance to African diplomatic missions operating in Russia. Their number is also growing. We also know about the intention of Botswana, the Seychelles Islands and Togo to open their embassies in Moscow soon and, naturally, we support them."

One must not lose sight of the problems facing Palestine, which continues to suffer amid developments in the Persian Gulf: "Of course, amid the events in the Persian Gulf, we must not forget the problems of Palestine, which continues to suffer."

Russia believes a definitive resolution to the Palestinian question can only be achieved through the implementation of the principle of establishing two states: "Russia supports and believes that a final resolution to the Palestinian question can only be reached through the implementation of the well-established two-state principle, which includes the creation of a fully-fledged state on Palestinian territory, a Palestinian state."

Thomas Markle Sr strongly denies the allegations of possessing images of child sexual abuse

Afghan farmers harvesting sap from poppies in a field in Badakhshan province. | Photo Credit: STR

Opium poppy cultivation in Afghanistan more than quadrupled in 2026 despite a Taliban government ban, the United Nations said Thursday (October 29, 2026), urging that impoverished farmers needed alternatives to the lucrative crop.

Cultivated areas reached 43,000 hectares (around 1,06,200 acres), up from 10,000 hectares in 2025, while estimated opium production jumped fivefold from 300 tonnes to 1,500 tonnes, the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) said in a report.

Long the world’s leading producer of poppies, Afghanistan was overtaken by Myanmar after a 2022 Taliban government ban crushed the trade in the country. But rampant poverty has driven some farmers to return to the trade.

“Without opium, we cannot even afford basic food,” Mohammad, a 22-year-old farmer in Afghanistan’s northeast, told AFP.

Afghanistan’s 2022 crackdown drove cultivated areas down from more than 2,00,000 hectares to 10,000 hectares in 2025.

Myanmar cultivated 53,000 hectares in 2025, with no 2026 figures available. The Taliban authorities claim to have destroyed more than 22,000 hectares.

But UNODC said many poppy plots were concealed among cereal crops or behind walls, making them difficult to detect.

Mr. Mohammad lives with his parents in Badakhshan which has become Afghanistan’s main opium poppy-growing province.

“The Taliban threaten us and do not allow us to grow opium. We cultivate it with a great deal of fear,” he said, asking only to be identified by his first name.

“I want to buy a car, build a house and get married. Without opium, I cannot achieve these things,” he further explained.

In May, two people were killed in clashes that erupted as Afghan security forces moved to destroy opium poppy fields in Badakhshan.

Another 35-year-old farmer named Mohammad, who ploughs his poppy field with an ox, said: “If we grow wheat, it is not enough to sustain our lives. We are forced to grow poppies to earn a good income.”

Mr. Mohammad said that he is threatened by the authorities and sometimes his crops are destroyed but he will continue.

“It is the only way to cover living expenses,” he said.

One hectare of wheat generated just over $770 in 2023, compared with $10,000 for a hectare of poppy, according to UNODC.

“Opium prices slightly decreased from US$ 570 per kilogramme in 2025 to US$ 500 per kilogramme in 2026, remaining well above pre-ban levels of less than $100 per kilogramme,” UNODC said in a press release.

“The fourfold increase in opium poppy cultivation shows the difficulty in sustaining decreases in illicit opium cultivation when poor farmers have no alternatives to feed their families,” said UNODC Executive Director Monica Juma.

The Taliban government acknowledged that the ban was not a “sustainable and long-term solution” without alternative livelihoods and economic support for farmers.

The challenge comes as Afghanistan faces the return of 6.6 million Afghans since 2023, climate disasters, dwindling aid and banking isolation since the Taliban movement returned to power in 2021.

With support from Japan and the European Union, UNODC has funded programmes to help farmers find alternatives.

Through the NGO Afghan Aid, Zikrullah, 35, who supports an extended family of 12 in Badakhshan, received improved wheat seeds and fertiliser.

“We’re satisfied, it takes less effort than opium and we’re more at ease,” he told AFP, also asking to be identified only by his first name.

A third farmer named Mohammad, 30, who has converted 80% of his land to wheat, told AFP, that the lack of water in recent years has affected crops other than poppy.

To curb opium production sustainably, alternative crops alone are insufficient, UNODC Country Representative for Afghanistan Polleak Ok Serei told AFP in an interview in Kabul.

“Sustainable alternative livelihood requires more than thinking in terms of alternative crops,” he said.

Farmers “need access to markets, including international markets through trades. They need infrastructure, roads, irrigation, storage”.

UNODC estimated in 2025 that the approach would require approximately $500 million, but “only 10%” had been funded by donors and the Taliban authorities, according to Ok Serei.

“Thailand spent 15 years investing in alternative livelihood before starting eradicating,” Ok Serei said.

The problem extends far beyond Afghanistan, he said.

“It truly is an international problem,” Mr. Ok Serei added, explaining that Afghan drugs were consumed across other continents.

Afghan opium is the main source of heroin in the European Union, where approximately 8,50,000 people used opioids in 2024, according to the European Union Drugs Agency.

Addressing the problem requires international cooperation, the UN representative said.

Mr. Ok Serei called for a “drug demand reduction strategy, not only in Afghanistan but also outside of Afghanistan”.

#84. Record number of purges as Xi emphasises 'party discipline' at China political gathering

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

This is the first time in Xi's 14 years of leadership that plenum attendance fell below 300

A record 33 members have been expelled from China's Communist Party as it concluded a major annual political gathering, which had the most empty seats ever seen.

The fifth plenum was attended by 279 Central Committee members, compared to 315 at the fourth plenum in October 2025 and 364 at the third one in July 2024.

While recent plenums focused on economic reforms or social and development issues, party discipline took centre stage at this week's gathering.

Xi Jinping has made the fight against corruption a central pillar of his rule since he took the reins in 2012, calling it the "biggest threat" to the party.

Sixteen of the 33 members expelled are from the military, according to a communique issued on Thursday, which marked the end of the four-day closed-door gathering held at a hotel in Beijing.

It's unclear what specific charges these members face beyond "serious disciplinary and legal violations". Such broad allegations have become routine under Xi's anti-corruption campaign.

It has now become "an effort to consolidate and maintain authority", says Chong Ja Ian, a non-resident scholar with Carnegie China.

"In this case, the campaign will continue and expand depending on how secure Xi feels rather than any real issue with corruption."

In its statement, the party said it has "squarely confronted the grim reality that party leadership had at times weakened or hollowed out" and that it will continue to "enforce strict party self-governance, including turning the knife inwards to... curb disorder".

The latest expulsions bring the total number of members removed under Xi's third term to a record 51, up from 35 and five in his first and second terms respectively.

Beyond the plenum, China's top anti-corruption watchdog reported that 680,000 people across the party apparatus have been disciplined in the first nine months of this year.

This is also the first time in Xi's 14 years of leadership that plenum attendance fell below 300. The current Central Committee started its term in 2022 with more than 370 members.

Observers argue that Xi views corruption not just as graft but also ideological impurity and, crucially, disloyalty.

In January, he warned against "two-faced cadres" who profess political loyalty but defy orders and act in their self-interest.

Among the high-ranking officials expelled are former Xinjiang party chief Ma Xingrui, and ex-top generals Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli. Official statements announcing their removal this year said they "seriously violating [the party's] discipline and rules", which is widely read as a euphemism for corruption.

The culling of the military's top brass has been particularly significant, with Neil Thomas, Lobsang Tsering and Shengyu Wang from the Asia Society Policy Institute describing it as "in a category of its own".

"It raises serious questions about organisational leadership and combat readiness, even as China continues to unveil formidable new military hardware," they wrote.

Zhang was also vice-chairman of the Central Military Commission, which controls the armed forces. The seven-member commission has since been whittled down to just two members: Xi as chairman and Zhang Shengmin vice-chairman.

Ma and Zhang Youxia were former Politburo members. Their removal, along with former general He Weidong in 2025, meant the powerful 24-man Politburo was at one point left with 21 members.

Zhang Shengmin was promoted to the top decision-making body at the fifth plenum, bringing the number to 22.

Former prominent diplomat Liu Jianchao, who disappeared from public view in July last year, was also named among those who have been purged.

"Xi has few qualms about removing people he promoted," Chong says. "That he does so at will with little opposition underscores how unassailable his position is at the moment."

A leadership reshuffle is due in 2027 and many analysts expect Xi to hold on to the top job for a fourth term and beyond. No leader has served that long except Mao Zedong, the founder Communist China.

Xi has reason to "scrutinise the next cohort more closely" given the fall of so many officials from the current committee, Thomas says.

"Today's rising stars tend to be relatively young, boast technocratic backgrounds, and have connections to Xi's political network," he says, adding that "provincial leadership ranks are a promising place to look".

Observers say that ultimately, Xi is expected to dominate personnel selection at the 21st Party Congress, which will further cement his grip on the party and the country.

"Xi's focus is further consolidating power around himself... The readout from the plenum suggests he intends to be at the centre of China and the Communist Party for the foreseeable future," Chong says.

"However, further down the road, such concentration of power may pave the way for a more serious power vacuum when Xi ages and slows down."

Iran has used a secret network of more than 5,000 military agencies to import at least $2 billion worth of equipment, explosives and heavy-duty trucks since January 2025, bypassing Western sanctions and the US naval blockade, a Reuters investigation published on Thursday found.

One agency alone, identified as MOD 346428, imported $2.3 billion in military-related goods between January 2025 and June 2026, according to shipping records examined by Reuters.

The shipments included rocket fuel ingredients, explosives, aircraft parts, industrial machinery and hundreds of heavy-duty trucks that could be adapted for military use, with goods recorded as originating in China, Russia, Germany and other countries.

The findings provide details of how Iran has continued to obtain materials used in missile and drone production during the US-Israeli war, which began in February, despite attacks on its weapons production facilities.

Thousands of agencies linked to defense ministry

Reuters identified more than 5,000 agencies whose names begin with MOD, referring to Iran's Ministry of Defense, and which share the Tehran address of the Ministry of Defense and Armed Forces Logistics.

Although their registration records list February 11, 1979, as their founding date, most were established in 2013, when Iran was facing severe economic sanctions, the investigation found.

Reuters and Israeli-American risk consultancy 240 Analytics traced the agencies through Iranian banking records, corporate filings and shipping documents, including around 19,000 import records.

Some entities were linked to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) and large Iranian foundations known as bonyads.

The agencies use intermediaries and overseas companies to purchase goods with proceeds from oil sales that bypass sanctions, according to the investigation.

In 97% of the shipments reviewed, transactions were recorded as settled "without currency transfer," allowing purchases to be made using funds held outside Iran.

Banking records also showed that 36 agencies held about $1.2 billion in foreign currency deposits at the end of 2024, while the wider network held around $1.8 billion in local currency, Reuters reported.

The news agency said it found no evidence that the numbered agencies identified in the documents had been individually sanctioned by Western governments.

Missile materials and hundreds of trucks imported

The investigation found that Iran continued importing materials that could be used in missiles and drones after the war began on February 28.

In the first six months of 2026, deliveries included detonators, explosives, aircraft engine components and rocket fuel ingredients.

In February, shortly before the war, Iran imported 93 metric tons of Chinese titanium and hundreds of tons of chemicals that could be used in rocket propellants and explosives, according to customs records.

Between January 2025 and June 2026, MOD 346428 also imported 435 Iveco trucks, some of which matched models used as disguised mobile missile launchers, Reuters reported.

The same agency imported thousands of Toyota Hilux and Land Cruiser vehicles, as well as Toyota Tundra trucks, models that Iranian forces have used for military purposes.

The investigation also traced imports of chemicals to the port of Bandar Abbas, where an explosion in April 2025 killed at least 70 people and injured more than 1,200.

Reuters said it could not determine whether chemicals in one of the shipments it identified were responsible for the explosion.

It also could not establish that all imported goods were intended for weapons production, noting that many materials and machines have both civilian and military uses.

Imports continued during war and blockade

The materials entered Iran through several routes, including cargo vessels crossing the Caspian Sea from Russia, trucks traveling from Central Asia and freight aircraft arriving from China, Reuters reported.

Monthly imports by the identified military agency fell by 47% to $54 million in March, shortly after the war began, but recovered to $77 million by May.

The investigation identified five shipments of Russian detonators worth $7.6 million since January 2025, including a $1.9 million delivery in June 2026.

Since the war began, $27 million worth of goods purchased by Iran's military were recorded as originating in the United Arab Emirates, mostly arriving by sea.

The Reuters data also listed hundreds of millions of dollars in trucks, aircraft components and electronics as coming from Germany and other European Union countries.

Reuters cautioned that some customs records identified shipments only through product codes and declared countries of origin, which it could not always independently verify.

"We firmly oppose attempts to maliciously link regional hostilities to China or smear China's reputation," the ministry said.

Italian truck manufacturer Iveco said it had safeguards to prevent its vehicles from reaching prohibited users and destinations but could not control products resold by third parties.

Toyota said it did not conduct business in Iran and had no knowledge of the purchases described in the investigation.

#834. Pompeii cupboard opens window onto life before Vesuvius erupted

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Sign up now: Get ST's newsletters delivered to your inbox

ROME, Oct 29 - A cupboard sealed for almost 2,000 years in Pompeii has yielded an intimate snapshot of one resident's life before Mount Vesuvius destroyed the ancient Roman city, from make-up utensils and perfume to coins, cooking pots and childhood amulets.

The once-thriving city of Pompeii, near Naples, was buried by a volcanic eruption in AD 79, preserving buildings, objects and graffiti under metres of ash.

Archaeologists pieced together bits of the wooden cupboard from traces uncovered in a house on Via dell'Abbondanza, one of the busiest streets of ancient Pompeii, the site said in a statement.

"We cannot reconstruct with certainty the identity of whoever lived in the house, but these fragments bring us closer to their daily life," said site director Gabriel Zuchtriegel.

Among the objects stored inside was a necklace comprising at least 24 tiny objects made of bone, glass paste, earthenware and rock crystal, including images of the gods Bes and Mercury, as well as miniature hands, feet, pine cones and bunches of grapes.

Such necklaces, known as crepundia, were traditionally given to Roman children both as protective amulets and as rattles whose sounds helped adults keep track of them.

They were sometimes kept into adulthood as mementos of childhood, offering a particularly personal connection to the unknown inhabitant of the house.

Other finds tell a more prosaic story of daily life.

There were tweezers, a small glass perfume or ointment bottle and an incense burner, alongside bronze, glass and ceramic vessels and cooking pots. Lead weights in a wooden box suggested that someone in the house was involved in trade.

A small woven container held 38 bronze coins.

The fact that both money and objects with apparent sentimental value remained behind suggests the owners had no time to retrieve them after Vesuvius erupted.

"Pompeii is not just a place of great works of art. It is also a place where the most ordinary objects restore the human dimension of the past," Zuchtriegel said.

"The value of this discovery lies in the possibility of reading together the different traces of a life: personal habits, work and memories."

The house, in a central area of Pompeii, is tentatively associated with a man named Cornelius Maximus because of inscriptions discovered there. Its facade was first excavated in 1912, but archaeologists have only now investigated some of the rooms behind it as part of a wider restoration project.

The excavation also provided evidence that some problems of urban living have changed remarkably little in two millennia, with archaeologists finding the fragmented skeletons of two young mice inside an amphora kept on a raised storage area. REUTERS
