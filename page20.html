#87. It's a 'socialist' bank — and North Dakota Republicans love it

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

BISMARCK, N.D. — In 1918, an offshoot of the progressive farmer-labor movement known as the Nonpartisan League swept into power in North Dakota, riding a populist groundswell of anger directed at big banks and industry barons in Minneapolis and Chicago.

The group was organized by former Socialist Party member Arthur C. Townley, a failed flax farmer who barnstormed the state in a borrowed Ford Model T calling for public ownership of key industries, including banks, mills and factories. By 1919, the state legislature authorized a state-owned and -operated bank.

Over a century later, the Bank of North Dakota still stands, and the state is reaping the benefits.

The Bank of North Dakota is the nation's only surviving state-owned and -operated bank. It has persisted as Great Plains politics has shifted from prairie progressivism to deep-red conservatism, and it now manages about $11 billion in assets while enjoying broad support from the state's current political leaders.

"I think it's weird, right? We're one of the most conservative states in the union, and we have this state-run bank that was started by first-generation Norwegian immigrants," North Dakota Gov. Kelly Armstrong, a Republican, told CNBC. "I don't think we could set one up now, but I'm sure glad we have it."

In addition to Armstrong, the bank's present-day boosters include the state's U.S. senators, Kevin Cramer and John Hoeven — who is a former president of the bank, and former North Dakota governor and current Interior Secretary Doug Burgum. All are Republicans in a party where "socialist" is a pejorative on the 2026 campaign trail.

At its core, the Bank of North Dakota is a bank. It has a governance structure, holds the state's deposits and provides loans. But it is also a unique institution that melds the political and financial arenas to advance the state's industrial and economic goals. Those efforts can range from saving a coal plant on the verge of going belly-up to buying down interest rates on loans for agriculture, energy or housing.

"I've always said it's a very interesting concept; obviously it's a successful one up here," Don Morgan, the Bank of North Dakota president and CEO, told CNBC. "It's one that if you use it wrong, it could be a little bit dangerous. But if you use it right, it can be a really powerful financial tool."

The bank also stands as a vestige of the country's last populist socialist uprising, as the U.S. is now gripped by another ahead of the 2026 midterm elections. The bank, which is in a state President Donald Trump won by more than 36 percentage points in 2024, has been used as an example by Democratic, progressive and democratic socialist candidates and lawmakers from across the country who are trying to expand public banking.

CNBC traveled to North Dakota to talk with leaders of the bank, which rarely grants such access to national media, and with state political officials and business leaders to learn how the country's only state-owned bank operates and about the interplay with the state it serves — and whether its century-old idea could support a new groundswell of state-run banks.

"For a long time people have scoffed at it — 'Oh, that's their experiment with socialism over there' — but I think it's important to note that we live in an era of populism," Cramer said. "Others have looked at it a little bit skeptically, but more recently they've looked at it and gone, 'You know, we could use a tool like that.'"

Roughly a dozen states have had state-run banks in the past, but the Bank of North Dakota is the only one operating today. Most failed by 1900, following the enactment of laws allowing private banks to issue notes backed by Treasury bonds or due to turbulent economic stretches.

Still, some politicians hope to use the Bank of North Dakota as an example and bring public banking back to the national conversation. And a wave of progressive populism could create an environment where policymakers return to public banks as a solution for a litany of economic ills.

Most public banking supporters are Democrats, including progressive democratic socialists who have surged in popularity after New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani was elected in 2025.

Mamdani, who was a New York State Assembly member from 2021-2025, cosponsored a bill in the assembly that would have allowed municipalities and local governments to stand up public banks. Wisconsin gubernatorial candidate Francesca Hong, who narrowly lost to Democratic nominee David Crowley in August, included a call for a state bank modeled on the Bank of North Dakota in her official platform. Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, D-N.Y., a potential 2028 presidential contender, led a bill in Congress with Rep. Rashida Tlaib, D-Mich., to make it easier for states to create public banks.

"We're populists, whereas in other parts of the country Republicans aren't as populist as they are conservative," Cramer said of one of the nation's most conservative states being emulated by democratic socialists. "It's somewhat natural that a more liberal political bent is attracted to this sort of experiment that's been going for 108 years in North Dakota that looks more appealing on the surface to the left than it might to the right."

Several Democratic-led states, meanwhile, have already taken steps to give public banking a try. One of those states is Washington, where a work group of officials is set to deliver an implementation plan to the legislature by mid-2027.

"I think the people deserve to be in control of their own tax dollars that they're putting forward," said Washington state Sen. Bob Hasegawa, a member of the work group who has spent years pushing for a state bank. Hasegawa said he began advocating for the idea after the 2008 financial crisis.

"We're allowing big Wall Street banks to use our tax dollars, leverage it, and make tons of money for their shareholders and themselves," he said. "And in the meantime, if we want to do anything like build a bridge or water system or whatever, we have to go back and sell bonds. So we're essentially borrowing what otherwise would be our own dollars back just so we can build infrastructure or finance other programs."

Massachusetts, New Mexico and California are also pursuing their own proposals to create public banks.

A monetary expert says public banking makes good sense in the design of the monetary system.

"For me, it came from a design perspective. Why don't we do this obvious thing? Why doesn't the public use the same power of banking that private institutions use?" said Christine Desan, the Leo Gottlieb Professor of Law at Harvard University, who teaches about the political economy of capitalism and the monetary system.

"The reason banks are such great lenders is that they operate in a network," Desan said. "In effect, the way a public bank would operate is it would be saying, we're just going to join the network; the public, the state of Massachusetts, will join the network, and then we'll have the same advantages in lending."

Major players in the banking industry aren't sold on public banking as a concept.

The American Bankers Association opposed eventual laws in California that allowed municipalities to set up public banks and set up state checking accounts for individual citizens. The group also opposed a ballot measure in Los Angeles that would have allowed the creation of a public bank.

"History has shown that public banks are a solution in search of a problem and present significant risks to taxpayers," ABA spokesman Josh Britton told CNBC. "America's banks are already highly regulated and well-capitalized and reliably serve communities, businesses, and state and local governments across the country, and misguided political interference would only harm consumers and the economy."

The state getting involved in banking decisions is also one of the greatest political risks facing public banking. A public bank could theoretically pick winners and losers based on the political bent of elected officials, disadvantaging some industries while advantaging others.

Morgan said strict prohibitions on competition with local financial institutions, along with a sound governance structure, could help other states set up public banks with as few pitfalls as possible.

"Our founding documents stating that we're never to compete with [North Dakota financial institutions] is a very powerful statement, because I've always said the minute we start competing with them, then our model falls apart," he said. "I believe any state can do it. But how you found it originally is critically important. That original governance framework is critically important."

Morgan noted the bank does "serve at the pleasure" of the North Dakota Industrial Commission and legislature and will follow them "as long as it does not jeopardize and move against our founding documents or jeopardize the safety and soundness of the bank."

Running the bank as a financial institution more than as a government institution serves as a "backstop that we can fall back on, because at the end of the day, we have to run the bank and protect the bank because we're protecting the state's deposits," Morgan said.

Morgan, who travels the country to speak with public banking advocates, said a public bank could serve its state in any way the state wants, so long as it is a bank first. Other states have proposed using the bank to fund new housing, finance infrastructure and reduce inequality.

"Your bank can do whatever your state wants it to do, as long as it does not get in the way of being a healthy bank," he said. "And then when you're ready, you can do those things that strategically align with your state, which will be different in every state of the Union."

Today, the bank of North Dakota acts as a "bankers' bank," backing small community banks in financing projects they may be too small to finance themselves.

The bank is helmed by the Industrial Commission, a government body created by the bank's founding statutes. The governor, attorney general and agriculture commissioner make up the commission, which acts as a board of directors for the bank. The bank also has an advisory board and an executive committee, the latter of which runs its day-to-day operations.

"I really attribute that governance framework to the 107-year history and why we're still around, [while] many other public banks have failed out up to this point," Morgan told CNBC at the bank's Bismarck headquarters.

Morgan said the bank's core business consists of participation loans, commercial real estate loans and agricultural loans. The bank also engages in other areas where the state's privately owned banks don't already compete, including offering direct student loans at a low interest rate for North Dakotans hoping to attend college.

It will also quickly stand up disaster relief programs. The bank pumped $500 million in loan support to farmers in 2026 amid challenges from Trump's tariffs, low commodity prices and high input costs. In decades past, it has gone further — selling foreclosed farmland back to families under asking price after the Dust Bowl and ensuring teachers got paid in full during the Great Depression.

The bank does not have a traditional deposit base of individual customers stashing their cash. In fact, the bank discourages individual deposits so as not to compete with the state's community banks.

Instead, the bank is the primary depository of state funds. Morgan said it draws its core deposit base from the general fund, a mix of state revenue from taxes and fees. The state legislature can then appropriate bank profits back into the general fund for the state's budget, or the profits can be used to fund the bank's investments to benefit the state's industries.

"We take that average balance of about $6.6 billion — that's our deposit base, our core deposit base," Morgan said. "We use those deposits [for the bank's operations], work through our 91 financial institutions in the state, do our participation loans, do our disaster loans, do our student lending, and then we make a nice healthy profit back to the general fund."

The state's other banks don't see the Bank of North Dakota as competition, according to Rick Clayburgh, president and CEO of the North Dakota Bankers Association. Instead, they view it as a tool that allows community banks to "punch above their weight class."

"I think if we were trying to create a state bank here in North Dakota today, our association would probably be opposed to it," Clayburgh said. "But the fact that we know what it is now and that it's developed over the 107 years, it is a wonderful tool for institutions."

An example of the bank's investment activity — and one progressives might do differently — is found in McLean County.

There, in the middle of the rolling plains between Underwood and Washburn, Rainbow Energy Center's Coal Creek Station burns lignite coal to generate 1,151 megawatts of electricity per hour for roughly 1.7 million customers in Minnesota via a hulking transmission line. The plant employs about 200 people directly, and the coal mine next door employs hundreds more.

In 2020, however, the plant was on a much different trajectory. Its then-owner, Great River Energy, said the plant was losing money and that it planned to retire it by the end of 2022, replacing its generation with wind farms and natural gas.

Then-Gov. Burgum, a vocal supporter of coal, along with Cramer and Hoeven immediately demanded that Great River find a buyer for the plant and its accompanying transmission line, a tall order, as the country was moving toward renewable energy and coal's economic advantages were declining.

"It would have been a huge detrimental impact to this area," said Ethan Vaagene, the plant's director. "It was probably the worst feeling I've had in my life ... I still remember this day when the prior owner announced to all the employees that they were going to be closing the facility. It was a complete shock to all of us."

Burgum led an all-of-government effort to find a buyer. And while one did emerge, the complex financing of the sale would have proven difficult to execute without the Bank of North Dakota.

Morgan said the Bank of North Dakota was able to "quarterback" the complex sale of the plant and its transmission line from Great River to its eventual buyer, Rainbow Energy. Rainbow Energy Center bought the coal plant, and Nexus Line purchased the transmission line that moves the power to Minnesota. Both companies are affiliates of Rainbow Energy Marketing Corporation.

The deal included a financing package in which the North Dakota Transmission Authority sold bonds for the first time, netting $150 million in proceeds to fund a loan to Nexus Line to purchase the transmission line. The Bank of North Dakota purchased NDTA bonds through a $150 million Match Program loan, the bank's program that supports large-scale economic diversification projects.

The CEO of the company that bought the power plant said that without the Bank of North Dakota, the deal would not have materialized.

"I start all our meetings with the Bank of North Dakota [by saying] that we wouldn't be here today without you," Rainbow CEO Stacy Tschider told CNBC, standing on the coal plant's roof. "If I was going to go outside the state, it would be more difficult to basically get other banks' understanding because ... with prior administrations, there has been a war on coal."

After the bank's investment, Rainbow began expanding the plant's use.

CNBC toured the plant with Tschider and Vaagene. Tschider said he envisions the plant as a futuristic "energy park."

An example of the plant's evolution already exists. Underneath the soaring spires releasing steam from electricity generation, computers mining cryptocurrency hum away. Coal Creek now redirects the electricity it generates on site to mine bitcoin during periods of low grid demand to offset lost profits.

Tschider said the plant is in talks with artificial intelligence hyperscalers to build a data center directly next to the plant, bringing in more revenue. A smattering of anti-data center yard signs dot roads near Coal Creek, indicative of local opposition to the facilities that is plaguing the AI build-out nationwide.

"We're in the process right now of working with several hyperscalers," Tschider said, adding that he expects to begin construction over the next six months.

The Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library, nestled in the canyons of the badlands in Medora, North Dakota, is built into the geography, jutting out of the land where a young Roosevelt visited seeking solace after his wife and mother died on the same day.

On the day CNBC visited the library, the Bank of North Dakota — which extended a $70 million line of credit to the library to begin construction while it awaited private financing, allowing it to open for the nation's 250th anniversary — was celebrating the launch of its first stablecoin there. Dubbed the Roughrider Coin, it will allow North Dakota's network of community banks to execute near real-time bank-to-bank transfers.

Roosevelt looms large over the ethos of North Dakota, and the library has big ambitions: It aims to host a presidential debate and serve as a national hub for conservation work in the U.S. Roosevelt also has the unique distinction of being the favorite president of modern political figures on both sides of the aisle for his populist, progressive appeal, trust-busting history and staunch nationalism.

The bank, in many ways, was a result of the movement in American politics that Roosevelt ushered in.

"The Bank of North Dakota has very deep roots in the state of North Dakota; [it's] very much tied to the prairie populist movement," said Robbie Lauf, the library's interim CEO. "That kind of happened after the Progressive Era, of which Theodore Roosevelt was one of the most important characters of a fundamental piece of American history."

Lauf, who previously worked in Burgum's office and in the office of former Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell, rejected any idea that the bank clashes with free-market ideals just because it was founded out of a prairie populist, progressive movement.

"Anywhere where agriculture and rural communities are formed, there's a deep populist heritage, and there's a misunderstanding of what populism means, as well," Lauf said. "What it really is trying to do is help us achieve self-determination as a region."

"It is anything but an embrace of any type of state ownership of means; it's a partnership with private enterprise," he said. "This institution does not work without private enterprises and private banks ... For me, that's deeply free market."

Asked about the bank's socialist roots, Morgan said the bank, whatever its roots, was simply a good idea.

"There's good ideas on all sides of the political spectrum," he said. "We get so hung up in the debate or the letter by your name or the title assigned to you. There are good ideas."

#462. Pemerintah 5 Negara Minta Warga Siaga Darurat, Stok Makanan-Air

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Jakarta, CNBC Indonesia - Sejumlah negara kini meminta warganya untuk menyetok makanan dan air untuk keadaan darurat. Tak hanya itu warga juga diminta menyiapkan obat-obatan penting.

Warga juga diminta menyiapkan senter, baterai, serta rencana alternatif jika listrik atau komunikasi tiba-tiba terputus. Kelima negara itu adalah Jepang, Jerman, Inggris, Taiwan, dan Amerika Serikat (AS).

Mengutip laman AS, Newsweek, dan Kanada, Philippine Canadian Inquirer Kamis (29/10/2026), negara-negara tersebut memperkuat persiapan untuk menghadapi bencana alam, ancaman keamanan, serta gangguan terhadap layanan penting. Inggris menjadi pelopor di 22 Oktober lalu dengan mengimbau setiap rumah tangga untuk bersiap menghadapi setidaknya tiga hari tanpa akses normal terhadap layanan penting.

Negara-negara lain juga menerapkan rekomendasi serupa, meskipun panduan mereka berbeda sesuai dengan risiko yang dihadapi masing-masing. Mulai dari gempa bumi dan badai hebat hingga serangan siber dan potensi konflik bersenjata.

Namun pesan yang disampaikan tetap sama. Yakni rumah tangga harus mampu bertahan untuk sementara waktu ketika bantuan dari luar terbatas.

Kampanye baru pemerintah Inggris, Be Ready, mendorong keluarga untuk bersiap menghadapi kemungkinan gangguan selama tiga hari terhadap pasokan listrik, air, komunikasi seluler, dan layanan internet. Diluncurkan pada 22 Oktober, inisiatif ini merupakan bagian dari upaya peningkatan kesadaran masyarakat selama tiga tahun agar rumah tangga lebih siap menghadapi keadaan darurat.

Pemerintah Inggris merekomendasikan warga untuk menyimpan air minum kemasan, makanan yang tidak mudah rusak, senter, pengisi daya portabel (power bank) yang terisi penuh, obat-obatan penting, serta radio bertenaga baterai atau radio engkol. Menteri Keamanan Dan Jarvis mengatakan kesiapsiagaan dapat membantu mengurangi beban petugas tanggap darurat sehingga mereka bisa memprioritaskan orang-orang yang paling membutuhkan pertolongan.

Kampanye ini diluncurkan setelah badai hebat dan kebakaran hutan mengganggu kehidupan masyarakat di Inggris. Inisiatif tersebut juga melanjutkan panduan kesiapsiagaan rumah tangga yang telah diterbitkan pemerintah sejak 2024.

Di Jepang, negara yang berulang kali menghadapi ancaman gempa bumi, tsunami, topan, dan aktivitas vulkanik, kesiapsiagaan darurat telah lama menjadi prioritas. Pemerintah Jepang merekomendasikan warga untuk memiliki persediaan makanan, air minum, dan kebutuhan pokok lainnya yang cukup untuk setidaknya tiga hari, atau idealnya satu minggu.

Keluarga juga dianjurkan menyiapkan tas evakuasi, toilet portabel, senter, dan peralatan komunikasi. Kantor Kabinet Jepang menekankan bahwa bencana berskala besar dapat mengganggu transportasi, layanan utilitas, dan pengiriman pasokan.

Karena itu, rumah tangga perlu bersiap sebelum petugas darurat dapat menjangkau seluruh warga. Rekomendasi tersebut merupakan langkah keselamatan menghadapi bencana yang telah lama diterapkan.

Jerman merekomendasikan penduduk untuk menyimpan makanan, air, dan kebutuhan lainnya yang cukup untuk memenuhi kebutuhan hidup hingga 10 hari. Meski demikian, Kantor Federal Jerman untuk Perlindungan Sipil dan Bantuan Bencana (BBK) meminta warga menyetor persiapan untuk tiga hari sebagai langkah awal.

Panduan terbaru lembaga tersebut mencakup cara menghadapi pemadaman listrik berkepanjangan, cuaca ekstrem, misinformasi, serta keadaan darurat keamanan, termasuk situasi yang melibatkan konflik bersenjata. Pemerintah Jerman menekankan bahwa kesiapsiagaan harus disesuaikan dengan kebutuhan setiap rumah tangga, ruang penyimpanan yang tersedia, dan kondisi keuangan masing-masing.

"Tujuannya adalah membantu warga tetap aman ketika layanan penting untuk sementara tidak tersedia," kata BBK.

Panduan kesiapsiagaan darurat juga dirilis Taiwan. Ini dilakukan untuk melatih warga akan kesiap-siagaan bencana maupun kemungkinan keadaan darurat militer.

Buku panduan keselamatan publik Taiwan merekomendasikan rumah tangga untuk menyimpan kebutuhan pokok yang cukup untuk setidaknya tujuh hari. Persediaan air minum yang dianjurkan minimal tiga liter per orang setiap hari.

Warga juga disarankan menyiapkan tas evakuasi darurat terpisah yang berisi kebutuhan pokok untuk satu hingga tiga hari. Persediaan yang dianjurkan mencakup makanan, obat-obatan, perlengkapan kebersihan, peralatan medis dasar, dan barang-barang pribadi yang penting.

Langkah kesiapsiagaan Taiwan mencerminkan kerentanan wilayah tersebut terhadap gempa bumi, cuaca ekstrem, serta ketegangan dengan China. Panduan itu dirancang untuk meningkatkan kesiapan warga sipil dalam menghadapi berbagai keadaan darurat, bukan untuk memprediksi bahwa konflik atau bencana akan terjadi pada tanggal tertentu.

Di Amerika Serikat, Badan Manajemen Darurat Federal (FEMA) terus mendorong kesiapsiagaan rumah tangga melalui program Ready.gov. Lembaga tersebut merekomendasikan persediaan makanan yang tidak mudah rusak dan air minum untuk setidaknya tiga hari.

Jumlah air yang disarankan sekitar satu galon atau 3,8 liter per orang setiap hari untuk minum dan sanitasi. Persediaan lain yang disarankan meliputi kotak pertolongan pertama, senter, baterai, radio, obat-obatan resep, serta dokumen penting.

Keluarga juga didorong untuk menyusun rencana komunikasi darurat dan mempertimbangkan kebutuhan anak-anak, lansia, penyandang disabilitas, serta hewan peliharaan. Panduan tersebut mencakup berbagai keadaan darurat, termasuk badai tropis, kebakaran hutan, banjir, gempa bumi, dan pemadaman listrik berkepanjangan.

Di luar masing-masing negara, Uni Eropa (UE) juga mengambil langkah untuk memperkuat kesiapsiagaan masyarakat. Sebelumnya, pada Maret 2025, Komisi Eropa memperkenalkan Strategi Uni Kesiapsiagaan (Preparedness Union Strategy).

Strategi ini mencakup berbagai risiko, mulai dari bencana alam dan serangan siber hingga ketegangan geopolitik serta gangguan terhadap infrastruktur penting. Salah satu tujuannya adalah menyusun panduan yang membantu masyarakat memenuhi kebutuhan sendiri setidaknya selama 72 jam ketika terjadi keadaan darurat.

Strategi tersebut juga mendorong edukasi publik, sistem peringatan dini, serta koordinasi yang lebih erat antara pemerintah, masyarakat, dan dunia usaha. Pejabat Eropa menekankan bahwa kesiapsiagaan merupakan tanggung jawab bersama yang melibatkan lembaga publik maupun setiap rumah tangga.

#464. Dunia Gonjang-Ganjing, Anindya Bakrie Beri Petuah Ini ke Pemerintah

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Jakarta, CNBC Indonesia - Ketua Umum Kamar Dagang dan Industri (Kadin) Indonesia Anindya Novyan Bakrie, mengingatkan pentingnya memperkuat ketahanan ekonomi Indonesia di tengah ketidakpastian global. Sejumlah negara mulai menyiapkan cadangan darurat untuk menghadapi berbagai risiko, termasuk potensi gangguan pasokan energi dan bencana.

Anindya mengatakan, kondisi global saat ini menuntut setiap negara memiliki kesiapan dalam menghadapi situasi darurat. Dia mencontohkan Amerika Serikat yang disebut menyiapkan cadangan bahan bakar minyak (BBM) untuk 90 hari, sementara Inggris meminta warganya memiliki persediaan kebutuhan untuk bertahan selama 72 jam.

"Mungkin sudah sadar, bahwa dunia sedang membangun cadangan darurat, dan ini bukan negara-negara kecil. Amerika Serikat mengatakan 90 hari untuk cadangan BBM. Lalu Inggris meminta warganya 72 jam untuk bisa istilahnya berdiri sendiri atau swasembada, dan siaga bencana 3 hari, setiap rumah mesti punya stok," kata Anindya dalam pembukaan Rapat Pimpinan Nasional (Rapimnas) Kadin 2026 di Jakarta, Kamis (29/10/2026).

Menurutnya, Indonesia juga perlu terus memperkuat ketahanan pangan, air, dan energi untuk menjaga kedaulatan nasional di tengah situasi dunia yang tidak menentu.

"Bersamaan, Indonesia juga tentunya membangun ketahanan pangan, air, dan energi, di tengah dunia yang tidak pasti ini. Karena kita mengerti bahwa ketahanan daripada pangan, air, energi adalah bagian daripada kedaulatan Indonesia," ujarnya.

Anindya menilai ketahanan pangan, air, dan energi merupakan bagian penting dari kedaulatan ekonomi Indonesia. Dia pun mengaitkan hal tersebut dengan konsep Trisakti yang pernah disampaikan Presiden pertama RI, Soekarno.

"Ini juga sering dikumandangkan oleh Pak Presiden Prabowo, dan juga oleh Presiden Soekarno dengan Trisakti-nya, yaitu kedaulatan ekonomi, kedaulatan politik dan juga kebudayaan," tutur dia.

Di tengah ketidakpastian tersebut, Anindya menilai fundamental ekonomi Indonesia masih cukup kuat. Dia menyebut Indonesia tetap mampu mencatatkan pertumbuhan ekonomi positif dibandingkan sejumlah periode krisis sebelumnya.

"Nah, kenapa? Karena kita resilien atau mempunyai daya tahan. Daya tahan berdasarkan data dan juga daya tahan berdasarkan alam yang diberikan," ucap Anindya.

Dia kemudian membandingkan kondisi ekonomi Indonesia saat ini dengan beberapa periode krisis, mulai dari krisis finansial Asia 1998, krisis keuangan global 2008, hingga pandemi Covid-19 pada 2020.

"Kita lihat tahun 1998 minus 13%, tahun 2020 waktu (pandemi) Covid-19 minus 2,1%, sedangkan sekarang plus 5,1%. Jadi dapat kita sampaikan. melihat data, fundamental kita kuat dan selamat tentunya untuk pemerintah di bawah pimpinan Pak Presiden Prabowo," tuturnya.

Meski demikian, Anindya tidak menampik adanya tekanan di pasar modal dan keuangan Indonesia. Dia menyebut tekanan tersebut perlu diwaspadai, tetapi tidak seharusnya membuat pelaku usaha mengabaikan kondisi fundamental ekonomi.

"Rp124 triliun keluar dari pasar modal, IHSG minus 40%, lalu dolar Rp18.170. Tapi bersamaan kembali ke fundamental," katanya.

Anindya juga menyoroti sejumlah indikator yang menurutnya masih menunjukkan ketahanan ekonomi Indonesia, di antaranya realisasi penanaman modal asing (PMA), pertumbuhan laba bersih emiten, cadangan devisa, dan pertumbuhan kredit.

Although Japan has increasingly marked Halloween, external in recent years, many Japanese people take ghosts seriously all year round. The country has a word, "yuurei", which means a lingering, troubled spirit.

These are the ghosts of people who died in violent or tragic circumstances - those who were murdered, killed themselves, or simply were lonely and died alone. If they died at home, then the belief is that their yuurei won't go away.

For house hunters who are not superstitious or squeamish it means there are big bargains to be found. There is a whole category of dwellings called jiko bukken, which means "stigmatised" or "incident" properties.

Under guidelines issued by the government in 2021, Japanese estate agents must disclose suicide, murder, death by fire, and any death where the body was not discovered for an extended period of time.

For rental properties this information must be disclosed for three years, and it must be revealed indefinitely for any property offered for sale.

By contrast, in the UK there is no legal requirement, external to reveal that a person died in a property. "The worst thing is if a suicide has happened in the apartment. Even if somebody has jumped from the roof, many renters will move out. Other issues are a serious crime or a lonely death."

Jakarta, CNBC Indonesia - Kepatuhan masyarakat dalam membayar pajak kendaraan bermotor di Indonesia masih tergolong rendah. PT Jasa Raharja mencatat, dari total 116 juta wajib pajak kendaraan bermotor di seluruh Indonesia, baru 49,25% yang tercatat patuh membayar kewajibannya hingga September 2026.

Artinya, tingkat kepatuhan tersebut masih berada di bawah 50%. Kondisi ini menjadi perhatian karena pembayaran pajak kendaraan bermotor tidak hanya berkaitan dengan penerimaan daerah, tetapi juga mencakup komponen Sumbangan Wajib Dana Kecelakaan Lalu Lintas Jalan (SWDKLLJ) yang digunakan untuk memberikan santunan kepada korban kecelakaan lalu lintas.

"Sampai September ini, kami merekam dari 116 juta wajib pajak pemilik kendaraan bermotor di seluruh Indonesia, tingkat kepatuhannya baru sekitar 49,25 persen," ujar Direktur Utama Jasa Raharja Muhammad Awaluddin di Kementerian Perhubungan, dikutip Kamis (29/10/2026).

Salah satu komponen yang dibayarkan bersamaan dengan pajak kendaraan bermotor adalah SWDKLLJ. Dana ini dihimpun dari pemilik kendaraan dan dikelola secara kolektif untuk memberikan santunan kepada korban kecelakaan lalu lintas maupun ahli waris korban meninggal dunia sesuai ketentuan yang berlaku.

Besaran iuran SWDKLLJ berbeda berdasarkan jenis kendaraan. Pemilik sepeda motor dikenai iuran sebesar Rp 35.000, sedangkan pemilik mobil sebesar Rp 143.000.

Awaluddin menjelaskan, dana tersebut merupakan bentuk kontribusi bersama masyarakat untuk membantu mereka yang mengalami musibah kecelakaan di jalan raya. Karena itu, pembayaran kewajiban kendaraan bermotor juga berkaitan dengan keberlangsungan mekanisme perlindungan bagi korban kecelakaan.

"Uang yang dikumpulkan atas dasar gotong royong masyarakat itu, di dalamnya ada komponen SWDKLLJ," katanya.

Melalui mekanisme tersebut, masyarakat yang mengalami kecelakaan lalu lintas dapat memperoleh santunan sesuai ketentuan yang berlaku. Apabila korban meninggal dunia, santunan juga dapat diberikan kepada ahli waris yang berhak.

Rendahnya tingkat kepatuhan pembayaran pajak kendaraan bermotor menjadi tantangan tersendiri dalam memastikan kewajiban tersebut dipenuhi oleh masyarakat. Dengan jumlah wajib pajak mencapai 116 juta, peningkatan kepatuhan membutuhkan koordinasi berbagai pihak yang terlibat dalam administrasi kendaraan bermotor.

Awaluddin menegaskan, Jasa Raharja tidak menjalankan tugas tersebut sendirian. Pengelolaan administrasi pajak kendaraan bermotor melibatkan tiga instansi yang bekerja bersama melalui Sistem Administrasi Manunggal Satu Atap atau Samsat.

"Jasa Raharja di dalam konteks pajak kendaraan bermotor dan SWDKLLJ itu tidak bekerja sendiri, karena ada tiga pilar besar yang kita sebut dengan Samsat atau Sistem Administrasi Manunggal Satu Atap," tegas Awaluddin.

Samsat melibatkan unsur pemerintah daerah melalui badan pendapatan daerah, kepolisian, serta Jasa Raharja. Masing-masing memiliki peran dalam pelayanan administrasi kendaraan bermotor, mulai dari pengelolaan pajak daerah hingga pemenuhan kewajiban SWDKLLJ.
