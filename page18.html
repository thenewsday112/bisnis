InfoQ Homepage News Chrome Opens Trial for the Email Verification API: Letting Sites Verify Without Sending Emails

Oct 28, 2026 2 min read

Google has opened a Chrome origin trial for the Email Verification API, a WICG proposal that lets a browser confirm a visitor owns the email address they typed, without sending a single message. The trial began in Chrome 150 on desktop, with Gmail taking part as the first participating provider.

The idea replaces the familiar one-time code or magic link. Today a user enters an email, leaves for their inbox, copies a code, and returns, a context switch that often loses them. The proposal uses a three-party model where the browser sits between the site (the "verifier") and the mailbox provider (the "issuer"). If the user is already signed in to their provider, the browser quietly fetches a signed token and drops it into the form, and the server validates that token on submission. Google notes the issuer never learns which site made the request.

For developers the front end implementation is fairly straightforward. The email field needs an autocomplete hint, plus a hidden input carrying a session-bound nonce:

The heavier work sits on the server side, where the token's signature is checked against the provider's keys. Resend's Phil Nash, who wrote a walkthrough of the flow, published an npm package and a Next.js example to handle that parsing and validation.

On Hacker News a developer said there is "literally no worse context switch" than hopping to an inbox mid sign-up, and that email verification had cost them "dozens, maybe hundreds" of registrations they never finished. Another pointed out that verification mail is frequently blocked or filtered to spam, with no visible difference between delayed and lost messages, so removing it is a genuine win.

One commenter argued the privacy claim is thin:

Make everything what works complicated. There is no advantage.

"User privacy is enhanced as the issuer does not learn which web application is making the request as the request is mediated by the browser." Every web application nowadays send you a welcome, onboarding, reminder after the verification. (No user privacy enhancement)

So we get a new process that solves nothing, but makes everything complicated. (And complicated helps the big and hurt the little in th long run)

Not verified but feels like a Google draft that closes the web.

Another called the issuer's blindness to the requesting site a "functional liability" rather than a feature. Developers who rely on per-recipient addresses, such as name+site@example.com, noted the flow still forces a fallback whenever the provider cannot attest those aliases. Others likened it to FedCM, folding identity primitives into the browser as password managers once did.

Outside Chrome with a signed-in Gmail account, sites drop back to the classic confirmation email, and the established syntax, MX, and SMTP checking services still cover the addresses this API cannot.

Teams already testing should track breaking changes. Chrome's October update added Android and third-party origin trials in Chrome 154, renamed the Sec-Fetch-Dest issuance value to email-verification, and from Chrome 156 returns the email exactly as submitted. The verifier and issuer guides track the current contract.

The Email Verification API remains an experimental proposal, best used today as a progressive enhancement while the WICG gathers feedback from sites, providers, and users.

#4409. ICE Is Running One of the Largest Anti-Left Surveillance Operations in US History

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Attorneys for 15 Minnesota residents facing federal conspiracy charges have obtained evidence of a sweeping, possibly unconstitutional spying operation carried out by Immigration and Customs Enforcement against left-wing social movements in Minnesota and beyond.

The high-profile Minnesota 15 prosecution makes clear that the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), flush with federal funds and newly hired personnel, is now President Donald Trump’s primary tool against dissent, with a powerful array of post-9/11 investigative authorities brought to bear against everyday Americans organizing their communities.

Nothing of this scale has been seen in the United States, experts say, since the federal government’s counterinsurgency against the New Left and the anti-war movement in the 1960s and 1970s.

Among the records revealed during the discovery process in these cases are dozens of reports, audio transcripts, emails, intercepted Signal chats, photographs, database queries, and more. They reveal the inner workings of the domestic terrorism investigations, running under the names Project Whipple Shield and Operation Puppet Master, authorized by the controversial National Security Presidential Memorandum 7.

Published following Charlie Kirk’s assassination in September of last year, NSPM 7 instructs federal law enforcement to use every tool available against organizations and people who criticize the administration's policies and espouse "anti-Christian" ideas. The full scope and extent of the investigations it authorizes remains unknown.

Defense attorneys for the Minnesota 15 claim that the prosecution stems from a broader nationwide surveillance operation aimed at opponents of the Trump administration’s immigration policies. One attorney in the case directly compares the DHS-run campaign to the FBI’s infamous Counterintelligence Program, or COINTELPRO, which unconstitutionally surveilled, infiltrated, and blackmailed racial justice, civil rights, and anti-war movements from the 1950s through the early 1970s.

Three former law enforcement officials who reviewed filings concur, telling WIRED that the case is almost certainly part of a wider intelligence-gathering operation that began long before the administration invaded Minnesota in December 2025.

Dueling motions in the case over discovery of DHS’s surveillance program reveal that personnel with Homeland Security Investigations—one of ICE’s two components—repurposed funding mechanisms and investigative tools for serious criminal probes and turned them on domestic dissenters. Operation Keyhole, the HSI undercover program that bankrolled the Minnesota 15 prosecution, dates back to 2008. As of June 2026, it was underwriting a half dozen active undercover investigations in the Minneapolis–Saint Paul area.

More than 120 HSI agents, many drawn from specialized units across the country that have served as the leading edge of the administration’s militarized immigration enforcement operations, were assigned to the case—including 11 undercovers. They used highly invasive surveillance techniques, including the deployment of Reaper drones and Patriot Act tools usually restricted to money laundering and terrorism investigations. Undercover operatives also infiltrated churches in apparent violation of a court order.

The Minnesota 15 indictment stems from the Trump administration’s demand that the Justice Department “go big and go loud” to crack down on community response networks during last winter’s Operation Metro Surge. Twin Cities activists organized volunteers to monitor DHS agents and their vehicles using encrypted Signal groups, homebuilt vehicle databases, and a centralized dispatch system. Government prosecutors claim that this amounts to a criminal conspiracy to block immigration enforcement, unlawfully identify and stalk federal agents, and even assault them.

Their defense attorneys say the heavy charges filed against the Minnesota 15 don’t align with their alleged conduct. “This is the first time I’ve had a client who’s accused of a conspiracy and went on a speaking tour,” says Kevin Riach, who represents defendant Isaac Sant.

An internal ICE document laying out the scope of Operation Puppet Master—which led to the indictment of the Minneapolis 15—ends by noting, “This case has also been accepted under CUC Op Keyhole.” According to prosecutors, Operation Keyhole is a long-standing program created by HSI’s Saint Paul office to fund “undercover activity”—primarily equipment purchases and personnel costs—in a region covering Minnesota, North and South Dakota, Iowa, and Nebraska. The program has funded at least 77 investigations into “narcotics trafficking, child sexual abuse, financial investigations, and counter-proliferation” since it started in December 2008.

The CUC prefix, per an internal 2008 DHS handbook unearthed by the independent media outlet Unicorn Riot, refers to Certified Undercover Operations, a formal designation with clearly defined oversight mechanisms, procedures, and reporting requirements. Operation Keyhole’s parameters are reviewed every six months by a committee comprising senior DOJ and DHS officials. As of June 2026, when it was last renewed, Keyhole funded six active investigations, including Project Whipple Shield, which was initiated in February to probe claims of assault, stalking, and doxing of DHS agents and other federal employees, and underpins the Minnesota 15 indictment.

At least 11 undercover agents working under Operation Keyhole infiltrated Twin Cities anti-ICE activists, participating in martial arts and know-your-rights trainings, marches, general strikes, and community meetings. They also infiltrated activists’ communications network for identifying possible undercover ICE vehicles and dispatching activists to tail them through the Twin Cities, and penetrated several activist group chats on Signal, transmitting their contents back to other HSI colleagues for analysis and eventual inclusion in the indictment.

Activist conferences as far away as Seattle, Michigan, and New York City were placed under surveillance as part of the investigation, and HSI teams photographed and ran the license plates of every car parked in the lot of a meeting location or seen transporting activists. There is also drone footage from unarmed Customs and Border Protection Reaper drones of the activists moving around the Twin Cities area.

The undercovers were part of a much broader group of more than 50 HSI agents, including at least two dozen from HSI’s little-known Special Operations Group, that took part in the investigation and surveillance of the defendants, both digital and in-person. Feds with decades of experience on complex financial crimes, drug cartel investigations, human trafficking, and organized crime were pulled into the Minnesota 15 investigation.

Several of the Minnesota 15 case agents are from out of state and appear in court records for the prosecutions of anti-ICE activists in other cities. This includes Los Angeles–based HSI agents Robert Kurtz III, who specializes in financial fraud, and Ryan Ribner, who allegedly assaulted SEIU California president David Huerta at a June 2025 protest of an ICE enforcement operation, arrested him, and then claimed the then 58-year-old Huerta attacked federal law enforcement. (Huerta’s charges were dropped in July after videos contradicting Ribner’s account surfaced.) HSI records show Ribner was also involved in at least two prosecutions of Southern California residents who physically confronted ICE and Border Patrol agents last year.

Other agents from the conspiracy investigation have also been involved in recent high-profile incidents. Tim Gerber, the “team leader” of an HSI Special Operations Group squad, led the investigation of former CNN host Don Lemon for reporting on a protest at a Minneapolis Church where an ICE agent was a pastor. Fellow SOG agent Yue Her was present at the scene of a 2025 incident when Renee Good’s killer, ICE Special Response Team agent Johnathan Ross, was dragged by the car of a man he tried to apprehend.

“This operation does not seem in any way limited to Minnesota,” says Spencer Reynolds, a former DHS attorney and counterterrorism lawyer who now works at the NAACP Legal Defense Fund. “As we saw with the boots on the ground in the streets, with Greg Bovino leading CBP and Border Patrol special forces units on sweeping raids across the country, this is the intelligence and undercover investigative apparatus that is running in parallel to the door-kicking.”

Mike German, a retired FBI agent who worked undercover domestic terrorism cases for years before becoming a sharp critic of post-9/11 federal law enforcement, likens the Minnesota 15 case to federal law enforcement’s long history of suppressing left-wing social movements, including the CIA’s Operation MH/Chaos, which surveilled the anti-war movement during the Vietnam War for evidence of foreign influence. One difference is the sheer scale—this time, it’s bigger.

According to a 2011 book by former CIA officer Frank Rafalko, the CIA secret investigation into suspected foreign influence on the New Left and anti-Vietnam War movement had 42 officers working the case at its peak. Court records show 62 HSI agents worked on the Minnesota 15 case alone.

“Broad monitoring of social media, communications, and financial transactions are used to link an ever increasing number of people as potential subjects of investigation and prosecution,” German says. “These are exactly the kind of intelligence activities J. Edgar Hoover adopted to suppress the Civil Rights and anti-war movements in the 1960s.”

The key difference today, German says, is the vast pool of data available to the feds.

HIS used highly invasive measures—including scrutiny of wire transfers, PayPal reimbursements, and other financial transactions; forensic digital analysis of website traffic; 15 terabytes worth of infiltrated Signal chats; and information from a commercial data broker—to identify individuals under investigation.

After a local activist uploaded a license-plate search tool on Fly.io, a developer-focused public cloud platform, to an infiltrated Signal chat, HSI agents reviewed website DNS queries and Stripe data along with data purchased from a commercial broker. This gave HSI access to SSH session logs, outbound connection records, the Fly.io account’s signup IP, and a Stripe card fingerprint on an account that had never completed a payment. Agents fused that with Minnesota DVS records and commercial data from the Thomson-Reuters CLEAR platform to produce a driver's license number, Social Security number summary, years of address history, phone numbers, property records, and jointly registered vehicles.

Documents concerning financial records requested through the Treasury Department’s 314(a) suspicious activity reporting program—created by the Patriot Act to enable speedy tracking of finances for terrorism and money-laundering investigations—show that the feds targeted records of 29 “subjects” during queries conducted on March 31, 2026. Emails viewed by WIRED between several HSI agents based in Los Angeles and Arizona show that batches of records were turned over pursuant to those inquiries, including some dealing with SEIU that a senior Minnesota HSI agent sent to a LA-based colleague to be filed “wherever these records are stored.”

HSI investigators used similar workarounds to warrants and grand jury subpoenas to obtain phone records for several journalists who covered Operation Metro Surge, as first reported by The Guardian. Court records show the feds used an administrative summons request reserved for violations of customs and taxation requirements, which does not fit the activity that made up the criminal charges filed by the Minnesota US attorney against former CNN anchor Don Lemon and others.

The Minnesota 15 case is the clearest example yet of DHS pursuing the Trump administration’s efforts to crack down on what have long been upheld as First Amendment–protected activities. But it’s unlikely alone.

Riach, the defense attorney, says the HSI reports show the agency is complicit in what he called “the new COINTELPRO.”

“What we saw during the immigration surges last year was that ICE is the administration’s paramilitary force, the projector of strength,” Riach says. “Here, we see that HSI are taking the FBI’s place as the investigative secret police—it’s the classic one-two punch of authoritarian regimes around the world.”

#4307. Neocloud thinks AI agents - not meatbags - will be its main customers

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Quake AI is developing APIs to let autonomous agents access its services more efficiently

Newly formed Quake AI is a cloud provider that expects its users will be mostly agents rather than humans.

It was created after US-based Rumble Inc acquired German datacenter operator Northern Data in June. The parent then rebranded as RUM Group, a holding company for Quake AI (formerly Northern Data) and the Rumble video platform.

Quake AI is an Nvidia Cloud Partner (NCP) operating a 22,000 GPUs estate, making it a neocloud: a specialized infrastructure provider dedicated to AI workloads.

Yet where other neoclouds are building a full stack of services around their GPU farms, Quake AI wants to make its infrastructure discoverable to AI agents, which may soon be traversing the internet in hordes on behalf of their owners.

RUM Group's chief financial officer Mike Massey told The Register that "Cloud 1.0 - let's call it applications - is like a user interface of how you interact with a machine of some kind."

He added: "Now, if you think about agents, they don't use vision. That's not how they interact with systems. They interact with APIs and with information effectively, and that means they want highly discoverable, highly data-driven platforms..

“So, to us, the things that make GPUs and AI compute easier to consume for humans, things like model-as-a-service, dashboards and one-click settings, are actually orthogonal to, if not detrimental to what agents are going to want, agents are going to want homogeneous compute… to understand and know what your data and your expectations are in terms of performance and uptimes, and then they're going to want to consume that compute as a utility,” he claimed.

In other words, Quake AI believes that, at some point, the customers for its compute services are going to be AI agents rather than people, but carrying out tasks on their behalf.

“I think that is the case, and it's the vision that we are driving towards, and you know ultimately agents are in service of people, right?” Massey said.

In its view of things, agents won't go through an intermediary, they'll go to the compute to retrieve the answers they need. "So all of these tool frameworks and all of these things that we have set up, I think, are a hindrance or an encumbrance to what the agents are going to want to consume, which is a utility," he explained.

"Yes, we're a little crazy in this regard," Massey added. "It's different. But I think our worldview is very grounded in the data and the realities. Like, we're a technical team. We understand the engineering, and at the end of the day, I think that most would validate our hypothesis," he continued.

John Hoffman, CEO of Northern Data and acting CEO of Quake AI, compared this with a solution he had been shown of an AI that was developed to navigate the internet by itself.

"I told it I wanted pizza. It went on to DoorDash's website. Typed pizza in the search box, clicked on the pizza with the highest ranking, went to add to order, knows I like pepperoni, and it clicked through all of this," he said.

"Don't get me wrong, this was a good solution that solves the problem. But the amount of compute that you are using to try to get an AI agent to act like a human that has eyes that can see web links is insanity compared to if DoorDash just created an API that is queryable," Hoffman claimed.

Done that way, the compute usage would be one thousandth of the way that the problem was actually accomplished, Quake AI claims, but it will require that everybody embraces the idea of how agents interact.

"And they don't interact with interfaces or applications. They interact with data and APIs. Just give me the raw data. That's what it wants," Hoffman told us.

"I would imagine DoorDash is developing an API for agents," he added. "It just makes sense, and that’s why we're doing the same exact thing."

Earlier this year, TollBit, an outfit that tracks AI bot traffic, said that agents were gradually taking over the internet. Its State of the Bots report found that by the end of 2025, there was roughly one AI bot visit for every 31 human visits to a site, up from just one for every 200 human visits at the start of the year.

Last year, consultants McKinsey & Company produced a report saying that the neocloud operators had to evolve beyond being just a provider of GPUs-as-a-service and move up the AI stack to sell services on top of bare metal, or risk being commoditized into competing on price.

Analyst firm Gartner was also reading the last rites for much of the neocloud industry recently, forecasting that AI services are unlikely to deliver enough revenue to cover the expected capital expenditure on IT infrastructure between now and 2030, and that consolidation in the provider market was inevitable.

Quake AI's gamble is that AI agents will be a reality, and that by tailoring its APIs to make it easier for these autonomous software bots to access its resources as needed, it will be one of the winners.  ®

#4514. GTA 6 is a massive 210GB download on PlayStation 5

There wasn't really any chance Grand Theft Auto 6 wasn't going to be a big download. Thanks to some new information, though, we now have a hard number.

PlayStation Game Size, which tracks PS Store game releases by poking through its backend, has revealed that GTA 6 has a chonky 210GB download size. While the actual space it's going to occupy on your console's SSD may be smaller, everyone will have to download over 200GB to play Rockstar's latest.

@OP - I note you are in New York City, so I assume you are a tax resident of New York State. If you leave the US from NY, be sure to officially change your domicile to another, more friendly state tax jurisdiction (either one with no income tax, or one that does not tax residents abroad). NY is one of the "sticky states". It's not easy to detach oneself from their tax jurisdiction, and they will continue to tax you, even when you live abroad unless you can establish that you have ended your NY residency. Keeping a property in NY, or just an address (for brokerage purposes) or driver's licence can maintain NY state domicile for tax purposes. Many US taxpayers overlook this when moving abroad from NY or another sticky state. Another thing US expats often don't know is that many US states don’t recognize the FEIE, so excluded federal income can still be taxable at the state level.
