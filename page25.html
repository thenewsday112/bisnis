#378. 7 Marathon Routes From Australia's International Gateways In 2026

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Merrillie Redden | Shutterstock, Simple Flying

Long-haul commercial aviation out of Australian international gateways has entered an extraordinary phase of expansion, brought on by geographical necessity, advanced engine thermal efficiency, and dynamic airframe range limits. Positioned thousands of miles from the world’s major economic centers, Australian international hubs rely heavily on ultra-long-range twinjets and quad-jets to cross vast expanses of ocean in single non-stop sectors.

For passengers, these marathon flights offer unmatched point-to-point convenience and test the boundaries of human endurance, cabin environment optimization, and modern widebody dispatch reliability. To highlight the longest non-stop commercial sectors departing from Australian airports, this list takes a closer look at seven remarkable routes based on geographic flight distance, maximum scheduled block times, and operational network strategy.

Spanning transpacific corridors to Texas, seasonal links to Nevada, and direct bridges into European airspace, these long-haul sectors demonstrate how major global carriers maximize widebody range and payload economics.

Starting December 29, 2026, Qantas will launch Australia's first-ever direct non-stop air link to Harry Reid International Airport (LAS), running a seasonal schedule through March 12, 2027. Operating three times weekly as flight QF55, the non-stop route connects Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport(SYD) directly to LAS. The new transpacific flight removes the need for traditional intermediate transfers, cutting up to five hours off previous one-stop itineraries.

Flown using long-range Boeing 787-9 aircraft, the 8,144-mile (13,107 km) sector carries a maximum scheduled block time of 13 hours and 55 minutes on the eastbound leg, stretching to nearly 15 hours on the westbound return. The service caters specifically to high holiday passenger demand surrounding major global events, including the annual Consumer Electronics Show and peak North American winter travel seasons.

The seasonal Nevada link will be Qantas's fifth mainland destination in the United States, joining established hubs in Los Angeles International Airport(LAX), San Francisco International Airport(SFO), Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport(DFW), and New York JFK Airport(JFK). With the West Coast coastal hubs being skipped over on this route, the Australian carrier can now offer a seamless direct gateway for more than 250,000 Australian residents who visit Las Vegas each year.

Operating as flight QF5, Qantas's non-stop service between Perth Airport(PER) and Rome Fiumicino Airport(FCO) stands out as the carrier's main direct link into continental Europe. Covering 8,278 miles (13,322 km), the seasonal route connects Western Australia and Italy across a scheduled 16-hour and 25-minute westbound block time.

The Australian airline deploys two-class 787-9s on the corridor, outfitted with 42 custom business suites and 166 long-haul economy seats. To be able to keep up with surging European summer travel demand, Qantas expanded the route's seasonal schedule from four weekly flights up to daily frequencies running through late October, as per Qantas press releases.

With other long-haul European routes undergoing temporary routing adjustments due to active Middle East airspace closures, the Perth–Rome sector has become a vital direct conduit for travelers seeking to avoid traditional intermediate hubs across Asia and the Middle East.

Launched as the longest non-stop flight in American Airlines' global network, the seasonal service between Brisbane Airport (BNE) and Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport covers 8,299 miles (13,356 km). Operating as flight AA7, the eastbound transpacific sector takes advantage of favorable high-altitude jet streams, while the westbound return reaches a maximum block time of 16 hours and 50 minutes.

American Airlines assigns its own flagship 787-9 aircraft to the route, equipped with 30 Flagship Business suites and 21 premium economy seats. The non-stop connection gives Queensland passengers direct entry into American's massive Texas megahub, enabling rapid single-stop connections to over 200 destinations across North America, Latin America, and the Caribbean.

The route's operational feasibility is greatly dependent on the 787-9's advanced composite airframe and high-efficiency General Electric GEnx engines, which allow the aircraft to carry full passenger payloads over 8,000 miles (12,874 km) without taking on any severe weight and balance structural penalties that typically plague other aircraft types.

Spanning 8,578 miles (13,805 km), Qantas's daily service between Sydney and Dallas/Fort Worth is famous for being the airline's sole ultra-long-haul North American route operated exclusively by the double-decker Airbus A380-800. Flown as flight QF7, the westbound return sector from Texas back to Australia features a maximum block time of 17 hours and five minutes.

The superjumbo, due to its immense size, provides an increased passenger capacity across four distinct cabin tiers, housing 14 first class suites, 70 business class lie-flat seats, 60 premium economy seats, and 341 economy seats, as per Qantas seat maps. Landing directly at American Airlines' main international hub, the route forms a crucial commercial artery for corporate and leisure traffic connecting Australasia with North America's vast domestic air network.

Operating a four-engine aircraft over 8,500 miles (13,679 km) means that fuel density management and heavy-weight runway performance planning need to be at their best. The A380's massive upper-deck cabin ratio allows Qantas to capture high-yield premium ticket demand as well as offering robust lower-deck cargo capacity for transpacific freight that is especially important on this route.

Covering 8,596 miles (13,834 km), United Airlines' non-stop connection between Sydney Kingsford Smith and Houston George Bush Intercontinental Airport(IAH) holds the title of the longest single non-stop flight across United's global network. Flown as flight UA101, the westbound sector from Texas to New South Wales carries a maximum scheduled block time of 17 hours and 30 minutes.

United operates the ultra-long route using its long-range 787-9 Dreamliner fleet, featuring 48 United Polaris Business Class suites and 21 Premium Plus seats, as shown in Simple Flying cabin analysis. Due to prevailing Pacific headwinds, the return flight to Houston takes approximately 15 hours and 45 minutes, creating an operational block-time differential of nearly two hours between directions.

The route connects Australia directly with United's dominant Gulf Coast hub, serving as a critical commercial pipeline for global energy sector executives while providing seamless connections across two continents. With this route, United gives travelers from Sydney access to over 90 domestic destinations and more than 50 international cities across Central America, South America, and the Caribbean basin.

Covering an immense distance of 8,990 miles (14,468 km), Qantas's non-stop service between Melbourne Airport and Dallas/Fort Worth is the Australian carrier's longest active transpacific route in scheduled service. Flown as flight QF21, the westbound leg from Texas back to Victoria reaches a scheduled block time of 17 hours and 35 minutes.

Qantas uses its three-class 787-9s on the three-times-weekly service, featuring a custom cabin configuration designed specifically for extended passenger comfort over 17-hour flight durations. The direct flight allows travelers from Victoria to forget about stopping over at congested West Coast gateways in Los Angeles and San Francisco, though at the expense of a day and a half in the air.

For Qantas, the idea of connecting Melbourne directly to Texas came directly from the desire to use its oneworld alliance cooperation with American Airlines to capture lucrative corporate travel markets and to offer unprecedented single-stop access to major population centers across the central and eastern United States.

Covering a distance of 9,010 miles (14,498 km), Qantas's flagship QF9/10 service between Perth Airport and London Heathrow Airport remains Australia's most famous ultra-long-haul flight. Designed as a non-stop link in both directions with a maximum block time of 17 hours and 50 minutes, the route represents the modern evolution of the historic Kangaroo Route.

Due to active airspace restrictions across the Middle East, Qantas temporarily altered the outbound Perth-to-London leg to include a technical refueling stop in Singapore as flight QF209, as noted in Simple Flying coverage. However, the eastbound return leg, operating as flight QF10, continues to fly non-stop from London Heathrow directly back to Western Australia in under 16 hours and 30 minutes.

Despite operational rerouting challenges caused by international conflict zones, the route is still seen as a pioneering benchmark for long-haul commercial aviation, showing what is really possible when the boundaries of range and airliner endurance are pushed to their limits. It provides a real-world testbed for ultra-long-range passenger economics as airlines prepare for the next generation of non-stop travel, with Qantas aiming to push the limits even further with Project Sunrise. Qantas will use the first of its kind Airbus A350-1000ULR to fly directly between Sydney and London, as well as New York City, and when this route is finally inaugurated, it will overtake every route on this list to stand out on top.

#24. King acknowledges 'suffering of the past' as Caribbean tour begins

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

King addresses 'suffering and injustices' in Bahamas past

King Charles III has begun his tour of the Caribbean by acknowledging the "great suffering of the past" as he faced calls for slavery reparations.

Speaking an event in the Bahamas celebrating local youth, the King said the country's culture was "born out of darkness", and it was "only right" to acknowledge historic injustices.

His nine-day tour will culminate in the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM), where the King has confirmed leaders will discuss the region's legacy of slavery.

The visit, his first as monarch, comes in the wake of renewed calls from Caribbean nations for Britain to apologise for its role in selling enslaved people for profit.

Prior to the trip, Buckingham Palace said the King would not shy away from "colonial legacy" and the impact of the slave trade during his visit but, in line with the UK government position, he will not apologise for Britain's role in the slave trade or offer reparations.

Upon his arrival on Tuesday, the King met Governor General Dame Cynthia A. Pratt and its Prime Minister Philip Edward Davis, before speaking with members of the Royal Bahamas Defence Force as he inspected a guard of honour.

King Charles, wearing sunglasses and a grey suit, was then met with cheers as he arrived for his first engagement at a youth rally at Clifford Park - where he attended independence celebrations for the Bahamas as Prince of Wales in 1973.

The young people in the crowd had been given time off school for the event. "It's my first time seeing him in person out of TikTok so he's out there, it's cool," one student told the BBC.

A small group of protestors gathered close to the rally shouting "pay us what you owe us" and "return artefacts you've stolen."

The group of around a dozen protestors was kept outside the main venue as they called on the King to help change the UK government's position on apologising and offering reparations.

"He's a man of influence. The Crown still has influence. And if they do not do it then we will become a republic and we will turn our backs on England if you do not show respect to the former colonies," said one protestor, Sharon Purcer-Cooper.

During his speech in Nassau, the King acknowledged his 50-year-long commitment to fighting climate change and highlighted the challenges faced by low-lying Caribbean islands, saying that "our world cries out for healing".

He went on to call on young people at the rally to "take up the sacred task to play your part" as "in years to come, this will be surely be the measure of every person - how they answered the call to care for planet earth - the source of our life, our prosperity and our security".

The King resumes engagements in the Bahamas on Wednesday, where he will hear how vulnerable the low lying islands of the Caribbean are to climate change.

He will also learn more about the Bahamian tradition of Junkanoo - a cultural festival and street parade.

Buckingham Palace said opportunities for the King to reflect on the legacy of the slave trade will be built into the schedule of his nine-day tour of three Caribbean countries with visits planned to memorials and other places of reflection and remembrance.

A spokesperson for the palace told the BBC that the King's programme on the tour "will primarily focus on shining a light on the many modern partnerships and opportunities for the future", specifically those which are "having a transformative effect on the region".

For the King, the spokesperson added, the "tour is both a new journey but also the continuance of a lifelong one, in which he has sought to engage, understand and educate about the most shameful aspects of our past".

Those themes will be reflected by the King through the nine-day visit, the palace said.

The visit has been planned alongside the governments of the Bahamas, Guyana and Antigua and Barbuda with an emphasis on honouring the past but also looking forward to the ambition and possibility for future generations.

The King will move to Guyana later this week where his engagements will focus on the environment and biodiversity.

He will then visit Antigua and Barbuda, where he will be joined by Queen Camilla, for the CHOGM and his keynote speech as leader of the Commonwealth.

The biennial summit will bring together leaders of 56 Commonwealth countries to address shared challenges and advance cooperation.

#878. More Pieces, More Possibilities: How to Merchandise Maximalism

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

This season, even quiet luxury all-timer The Row showed gorgeous, beaded, colorful tops, while the stark whites and creams that still dominated the collection were paired with chunky beaded necklaces and lace and crochet detailing.

It’s a signal of the balance fashion brands now need to strike between the minimalism of the past five years and the maximalism this season was heralded for bringing back. Almost every buyer’s takeaway this season was that the collections offered looks that were deconstructable; pieces that are easy to wear and pair together (or apart), without being overly prescribed. Looks are buildable, pieces interchangeable — and the best ones had an edge.

Sure, in a sense, that’s just getting dressed. But in an era where doing so has become increasingly formulaic, guided by pre-packaged aesthetics like quiet luxury and prescriptive micro-trends, consumers have grown fatigued. Now, they want clothes they can build an easy wardrobe out of while showcasing a level of idiosyncrasy. And brands delivered, integrating funky, off-kilter — yet wearable — clothing into the mix for Spring/Summer 2027, which emerged as a pushback against the algorithm-driven sameness that fashion has bristled against in recent seasons. In her SS27 trend report, Vogue Runway’s Laird Borrelli-Persson wrote that “offness, which feels anti-AI, is the turn-on”.

“While the SS27 runway featured an eclectic mix of aesthetics, wearability and self-expression ran throughout almost every collection, with designers reimagining narratives through layered, buildable styling,” says Venetia Rothwell, senior retail analyst at retail intelligence platform EDITED. “Maximalist layering and clashing prints dominated collections, a departure from the overly polished and refined looks of recent seasons.”

The Row toed the line between minimal and maximal.

Even the more pared-back looks were more layered and lived-in than past seasons.

This in-between is also commercially smart, at a moment where brands are ever-cognisant of their need to sell. Buyers applauded SS27 collections’ balance of commerciality (positive) and creative nuance, as designers expressed awareness of the need to speak to a broader clientele. “You have to speak to a much broader clientele than Martin [Margiela, the brand’s founder] used to have,” Glenn Martens said in regards to his SS27 Margiela collection. “We have to speak to the world, so it’s a bit difficult to go as hard on concepts.” In this vein, brands amped up the accessories, styling their layerable, buildable looks with belts and scarves galore, injecting more entry-level options for shoppers keen to tap in without breaking the bank.

It was a season that cemented what a product strategy should be in 2026: buildable and layerable, approachable but nuanced enough that it’s an offering shoppers feel they can’t get elsewhere, and with in-roads at entry-level price points beyond the usual wallet and sunglasses. Both the 6397 team and namesake designer Julie Kegels spoke about the women in their lives when discussing what goes into their product mix, and how they teeter between wearability and creativity. “I always try the clothes on myself and the women around me,” Kegels says. “A piece can be very exciting in your imagination, but once you put it on, do you actually want to leave the house in it?”

This balance of pieces that toe the line between minimalist and maximalist is reflective of a product strategy that’s been building over recent seasons, as designers find ways to work smarter, not harder, and devise product strategies that build on one season to the next, designed to layer across collections. (Recall the mixing and matching of last season’s “small plates-ification” of American sportswear.) Designers like Celine’s Michael Rider and Dries Van Noten’s Julian Klausner have nailed this approach. A string of independent brands including TWP, Julie Kegels and 6397 are adopting a similar strategy.

It may sound like a no-brainer, but hitting the sweet spot is no small feat, and when many customers are more cash-strapped than ever, getting it right can make or break sales.

Cinched waist accessories were (literally) big at Celine.

The smartest product offerings in 2026 are buildable: both within collections via accessories and layers, and across seasons. There’s a desire for imperfection and artfully undone styling, EDITED’s Rothwell says, noting that retailers bought into this aesthetic early on; arrivals of 2-in-1 layered tops — like the aqua and teal tanks in 6397’s Look 5 — rose 244% year-on-year in early SS26.

Tal Waksal, founder and designer of New York ready-to-wear brand All Three, has taken this approach since starting the brand less than two years ago. “I call it the All Three Lego system,” she says. “I’m never just thinking about the collection in front of me. I’m thinking about the one before it, the one after it, and the woman who’s been collecting pieces along the way.” When she designed SS27, she already had a blue leather jacket from All Three’s FW26 in mind, she says. “I introduced new shades of blue and different textures that would work with it.”

Pick-and-mix clothing is also reflective of the way people shop today. Typically, they’re not buying the head-to-toe look, says Substack writer and brand consultant Zara Wong — “they’re buying the piece that slots into what they already own”. Even when people are buying full looks — which, Waksal says, her customers often do, before coming back for the same items in new colors — they’re styling them with their own spin. This is something 6397 founder Stella Ishii has noticed as more celebrities have worn her label of late. “Greta Lee, Alexa Chung, Gracie Abrams, Amy Sherald and Jack Innanen have each injected their own attitude to 6397,” she says. “All have worn full looks, but not how we showed them, which is the coolest thing. It can be dull for things to be repeated in the real world.”

This ‘with a twist’ logic also applies to accessories, which offer a way in at a price point typically lower than a top or pant — even if you’re not buying the full look. For TWP’s Trish Wescoat Pound, belts — which she says are flying off the shelves — were a logical next step. “We saw our customer needed finishing pieces with the same attention to detail as our clothing, adding both edge and refinement,” she says.

All Three’s ‘Lego system’ involves layering clothes and accessories across seasons.

Technical fabrics in bright colors backstage at TWP’s SS27 show.

All Three’s Waksal thinks of belts in a similar fashion; the brand refers to them as the “third piece”. “Belts are the basics of accessories — I’ve collected them forever,” Waksal says. “I love how something so simple can change an entire proportion.” Kegels agrees: “A belt can be a small detail, but it can give the whole silhouette a particular attitude.”

For Kegels, accessories are a fun way to play with coordination — and a way to stretch a material further. “I love choosing one leather and seeing it become a shoe, a bag and a belt — red shoes, red bag, red belt,” she says. “It reminds me of opening a Polly Pocket box and finding a whole little world, with everything belonging together.” Shoppers can then, of course, choose to pull one out, or buy all three.

This influx of accessories like belts and scarves reminds Wong of the ’90s and early aughts magazine ‘tips and tricks’ pages. “I’m seeing this sort of ‘service journalism’ — fashion tips and tricks — on social media and platforms like Substack, where people can share how they use belts and accessories to do something different with their outfits,” she says. Scarves paved the way, with the scarf skirt (a scarf tied around the waist) emerging as one of summer 2025’s biggest trends. This has since graduated to actual scarf skirts, which sold out at Dries Van Noten, Wong notes. This season, waist accents developed further with the rise of the cummerbund on and off the runway.

“Accessories have become an easy way for shoppers to add personality to looks without overcommitting to maximalism,” Rothwell says, noting that statement buckle belts were one of the strongest runway trends of the season, with cult styles like the Celine Maison 13mm and the Dior Médaillon dominating street style outside the shows. “Mass-market brands are already capitalizing on the popularity of the oval buckle trend in fall 2026 assortments, with entry price points up to 95% above the category baseline,” which refers to the entry price point for a standard belt at mass-market brands, she adds.

Matchy-matchy accessories at Julie Kegels.

Experts wouldn’t go so far as to say minimalism is over — even if we’re tired of hearing about quiet luxury — but this buildable logic is reflective of the shift away from the ultra-pared-back look. “Designers have had to be very creative in how they express minimalism. They can’t just dial it in anymore,” Wong says, putting this partly down to how easy it is for mall brands to mimic the aesthetic. Brands need to inject an extra something into their clothing to differentiate. “Minimalism hasn’t gone anywhere, but it’s more expressive than it was,” Rothwell agrees, highlighting how designers are giving pared-back looks individuality through tactile fabrics and an injection of color.

The Row presented a twist on the minimalism the brand is known for — which, Wong argues, resulted in more humanized and wearable pieces of clothing. “This season, they introduced even more craft and touchable elements — it’s closer to how the [founding] Olsen twins actually dress, which I am far more excited about,” she says.

TWP’s Wescoat Pound feels that the maximalist element of her line is becoming more important for the brand. She invested in more technical and colorful fabrics in SS27, for a “sense of edge”. But this doesn’t take away from the minimalist themes on which the brand was built, she adds. “I’ve always liked playing with highs and lows and that mix — adding color and texture along the way without compromising wearability.”

This has long informed 6397’s Ishii and her design partner Lizzie Owens’s approach to design. “We’re all working women on our team and need wearable clothes, but without them being boring or predictable,” Ishii says. “We want the things we make to trigger an emotional response and excite people, while remaining relatable and adaptable. In our collections, there is always a color, finish, or fabric that feels slightly strange in a satisfying way, balanced with pieces that feel essential.”

At a moment where shoppers are being extra careful with their dollars, this essentialness is key. “The purpose of fashion is to provide newness and excitement, but the consumer nowadays has access to so much they’re getting increasingly skeptical; they don’t want to feel like they’ve been taken for a ride,” Wong says. “So it needs to be new, but also be wearable, and feel like there’s value in it.”

#545. PhD student built software to catch streaks in Euclid images, and it flagged the telescope’s first comet discovery

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Euclid's first new comet, C/2026 S1, was flagged by a Helsinki student's software in 16 exposures taken on September 16-24, 2026.

On September 29, 2026, Mikko Pöntinen, a PhD student at the University of Helsinki, reported a moving object in Euclid space telescope images. His own tracking program had flagged it as a streak. On October 8 the Minor Planet Center (MPC) listed it as a new comet.

The European Space Agency (ESA) built Euclid to photograph galaxies. It points away from the band of sky where most comets move.

ESA announced on October 19 that C/2026 S1 (Euclid) is the first previously unknown comet the telescope has found. The agency also said that this comet is not a suitable target for its Comet Interceptor mission.

Euclid took the images between September 16 and 24 in a northern survey area called Euclid Deep Field North. The comet appears in frames from the VIS camera, which records visible light.

Three of the VIS frames were released by ESA as an animation. They were taken at 00:41:07, 00:58:53 and 01:16:39 UTC on September 17. Each exposure lasts 560.52 seconds, long enough for the comet to leave a short dash.

From the first detections, Pöntinen predicted where the object would be in later frames and checked them. He found it in five fields of view over several days. That came to 16 exposures, two to four per field.

In ESA’s release he said he was “excited to see my software flag a ‘streak'” in the data. Further checks showed “a fuzzy cloud around it”, which pointed to dust and gas coming off a comet.

The team then made a cleaner image from eight shorter exposures of 89.5 seconds. They aligned the frames on the comet and combined them. Background stars and galaxies were then removed, leaving the core and its coma.

The object matched no entry in the catalogs of known comets and asteroids. Pöntinen reported for the Solar System Objects Science Working Group of the Euclid Consortium. The MPC, which works for the International Astronomical Union, then alerted other observers.

In his report, Pöntinen gave the coma a width of 10 arcseconds, a tiny angle next to the full Moon’s roughly 1,800. The MPC circular lists eight Euclid measurers, from M. Pöntinen to B. Y. Irureta-Goyena. Euclid itself is observatory code 273, a 3.9-foot (1.2-meter) reflector.

Ye measured positions from the Zwicky Transient Facility in California, dated September 22 to October 8. On October 5, M. Jaeger’s team in Martinsberg, Austria, used a 16-inch (0.40-meter) reflector. They measured an 11-arcsecond coma.

H. Sato reported a coma of 8 arcseconds on October 6 with a remote iTelescope instrument at Beryl Junction, Utah. Other positions came from Romania, Switzerland, Pennsylvania, Hungary and the National Research Council of Canada.

On October 7, R. Weryk and R. Wainscoat used the 11.8-foot (3.6-meter) Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope. They reported a tail about 12 arcseconds long. MPC circular 2026-T146 came out at 15:40 UT on October 8 with an orbit from 54 observations.

ESA gives the distance from Earth as about 810 million miles (1.3 billion kilometers). From the Sun, that is roughly the zone between the orbits of Jupiter and Saturn.

The MPC elements give an inclination of about 85 degrees to the plane of the planets. ESA describes the comet as currently “straight up” from the Sun’s point of view. Its orbit will take thousands of years, and the agency’s orbit visualization tool shows the path.

Perihelion, the closest point to the Sun, is listed for September 2, 2027, at 8.75 times the Earth-Sun distance. On October 7 it was about 8.96 times that distance away, so it gets only slightly closer.

Comets have been caught active this far out before. ALMA, a radio array in Chile, picked up carbon monoxide from the largest Oort Cloud comet ever detected beyond a billion miles out. A team led from Florida found a frozen object beyond Jupiter venting carbon dioxide.

ESA says Euclid avoids the band along the Solar System’s plane because dust there adds glare to deep images. That band holds most comets and asteroids.

In its “step-and-stare” pattern, the telescope usually covers each section of sky once. As a result, a comet cannot simply be revisited on demand to confirm what it is, the agency says.

Animation of how Euclid scans the sky with its ‘step-and-stare’ method, imaging one patch at a time before shifting to the next. Video: European Space Agency, ESA

Euclid’s main task is to study dark matter and dark energy. Over six years it will record the shapes and motions of billions of galaxies. Deep Field North was one of three deep fields in a first data release that mapped 26 million galaxies.

Comets and asteroids leave trails and extra light in those images that have to be removed. In 2023 Pöntinen led a paper in Astronomy & Astrophysics on spotting asteroid streaks in simulated Euclid images with deep learning. It estimated that up to 150,000 asteroids would show up in Euclid’s data.

Four of his co-authors on that paper also signed the comet report. Earlier Euclid side results include seven new rogue planets in the Orion Nebula.

Bruno Altieri, Euclid archive scientist and a member of the discovery team, said very few comets are expected from Euclid. He described this one as “a happy coincidence” of the comet’s orbit and the telescope’s scanning on those days.

Comet Interceptor is an ESA mission with Japan’s JAXA, due to launch in late 2028 or early 2029. It will be parked in space, then sent to a pristine comet, where the main craft will release two probes.

ESA wrote that the quick turnaround shows Euclid can find comets while they are still far from the Sun. Valeria Pettorino, Euclid project scientist, called the discovery and alert “a real test case” for the two mission teams.

The first orbit uses positions from September 16 to October 8. ESA says the MPC will refine the orbital period once more data are collected. No period has been published yet.

There is also no published size for the nucleus. Euclid’s measurements give a brightness of about magnitude 20.6, far too faint for the naked eye or binoculars.

Further tracking now depends on telescopes on the ground, since Euclid cannot revisit the comet on demand. The 16 Euclid exposures from September 16 to 24 hold the streak that Pöntinen’s software first flagged.

The information was obtained from the European Space Agency and the Minor Planet Center.

That’s the text on the single post that lives on Lush’s TikTok account. To its 344,900 followers, the beauty brand’s TikTok is practically a ghost town. It’s been that way for years.

It has been five years and Lush hasn’t been back to those platforms — neither in terms of posting nor in paid advertising, Julia Hamfelt, head of marketing for Lush North America, told Digiday. Instead, marketing dollars are earmarked for things like Lush’s mobile app, brand collaborations, content creator and in-person events. She did not provide exact spend figures.

“We don’t engage in any paid advertising on social media, in that we don’t invest in paying the platforms for advertising,” Hamfelt said. She added, “We do still engage a large community of creators and influencers, but our philosophy really is to invest in people over platforms.”

To modern media planners and direct-to-consumer marketers, leaving social media may sound self-sabotaging — abandoning millions of potential shoppers, losing brand visibility and a cultural connection.

“Paid social is really about driving reach,” said Jeremy Ekes, vice president of client service and strategy at January Digital. He added, “That is the pro. You’re buying a reach vehicle.” The tradeoffs of abandoning that vehicle, as far as Ekes is concerned, are losing eyeballs and having to find alternative ways to reach shoppers.

For Lush, the risk was worth it. “There was a period of time where we had to rebuild avenues and ways to directly connect with our customers,” Hamfelt said.

To adapt, the beauty brand turned its attention to creators and in-person events. Lush also invested in its own channels, like its mobile app, which currently has 2.8 million users, and email marketing, which reaches more than 6 million people globally, Hamfelt said. In comparison, Lush Cosmetics North America’s Instagram page has 3.6 million followers alone.

Lush has also increased its cross-brand collaborations, generating around £34 million (about $47.9 million) in sales, driving retail traffic and brand exposure, per the brand’s June earnings report. Popular collections included Wicked, Hello Kitty, Minions, Minecraft and most recently, The Grinch, though the brand did not provide exact sales figures.

Lush hasn’t exactly shut itself off from the social stratosphere — it launched its own Substack account and joined Bluesky this year. Lush also maintains an active presence on YouTube (where they post at least once a month) and Pinterest. They have an organic, unpaid approach to those platforms, Hamfelt said.

“There are some hard lines in the sand,” Hamfelt said, “but there are also benefits in continuing to have a presence with and partner with platforms that are taking the right steps to change and protect user safety.”

Lush isn’t interested in joining its peers — which could be seen as effectively, paying to rent audiences on Meta, Snapchat, TikTok and X. The strategy hasn’t put it out of business. In fact, Lush is growing.

For Lush, 2025 was a strong year with 11 consecutive months of “solid growth,” per the earnings report. The company points to things like new product launches, cross-brand collaborations and online customer forums for its success. Lush has not yet reported financials for 2026.
