#698. Hubble Will Give the Roman Space Telescope a Helping Hand

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope (NGRST) is on its way to its station at the SunâEarth L2 point, where it will begin its projected 5-year mission. It has several goals, including measuring dark energy's effect on the Universe, the growth of structure, and general relativity's consistency over time. Gravitational lensing plays a big role in its work.

There are two kinds of gravitational lensing, strong and weak. In strong gravitational lensing, a massive foreground object like a galaxy cluster lines up with a background object and produces magnified and distorted images of the background object, making the background object open to observations. It can produce Einstein rings, arcs, and multiple images of the background object.

These distortions let scientists measure the mass of the foreground galaxies. Since visible matter isn't enough to cause the distortion on its own, scientists can do some fancy math to calculate the amount of dark matter that must be present. That, in turn, will help them understand dark energy, the growth of structure, and general relativity.

In this image, the red foreground galaxy produced two arcs of light that are distorted, multiplied images of a background galaxy; a large arc on the top and a smaller one on the bottom. With images like this, astronomers can measure the total mass in the foreground galaxy and the degree of magnification of the background galaxy. By comparing the two, they can determine the amount of dark matter present in the foreground galaxy. Image Credit: NASA, ESA, and X. Huang (University of San Francisco); Processing: Gladys Kober (NASA/Catholic University of America)

The Hubble is the Roman's older brother, in a way, and it works differently. It has something to offer the NGRST's mission. The Roman sees mostly in infrared, with a little bit of visible light. The Hubble sees in mostly visible and UV light, with a little infrared. But the pair of telescopes differ in another important way, too. While the Hubble can examine individual objects, that's not how the Roman works. It has a much larger field of view.

This image compares the Hubble's small, focused field of view and the Roman's much larger one. The difference is stark, but it lets Hubble do preparatory work that will help the Roman's weak gravitational lensing observations. Image Credit: Background image: Digitized Sky Survey and R. Gendler; Moon image: NASAâs Goddard Space Flight Center and Arizona State University

That means that while Hubble can examine individual gravitational lenses, the NGRST cannot. That's because the Roman is a wide-field survey telescope. Here's how the Hubble will help its more modern counterpart.

The Hubble examines a single strong gravitational lens at a time. Astronomers can use Hubble's detailed observations to understand the lenses better. That model will be used to help make sense of the Roman's gravitational lenses.

It comes down to what's called the point-spread function. Telescopes don't image a point source as a point. The source produces a pattern across the detector, and the PSF pattern always has aberrations, diffraction spikes, and other problems. Since the Hubble looks at each gravitational lens separately on a single detector, the PSF is easier to understand and account for.

The problem the Roman will have also concerns the point spread function. (If there's an astronomy paper that doesn't mention PSF, Universe Today hasn't seen it.) The Roman will detect most of its gravitational lenses in its High Latitude Wide Area Survey (HLWAS). The HLWAS will cover about 5,000 degrees2 of the sky (about 12% of the sky).

The Roman's High-Latitude Wide-Area Survey should detect about 160,000 gravitational lenses. But only about 500 of them are predicted to be good for follow-up observations that will reveal their substructure. Image Credit: NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center

That means Roman's images are 200 times larger than Hubble's, and spread across multiple detectors, each with a different focal-plane position. In a 2025 paperr, researchers forecast the number of strong gravitational lense the NGRST will detect. "For a fiducial case of single 146-second exposures, we predict around 160,000 detectable strong lenses in the HLWAS, of which about 500 will have sufficient signal-to-noise to be amenable to detailed substructure characterization," they wrote. The HLWAS is expected to detect 15 strong lenses per exposure.

That number, 15 strong lenses per exposure, makes understanding the PSF more difficult. Its images are detected across multiple detectors, each with a different focal plane. So it's harder for the Roman to deal with the PSF and do its job: measuring dark matter.

Astronomers are preparing for the Roman's gravitational lens detections by building a computer model with Hubble's observations. Hubble's PSFs are being used to help decipher the Roman's PSFs.

Hubble will help in another way, too, by performing follow up imaging of the lenses that the NGRST discovers.

This shows that even though the Hubble is technologically outclassed by newer telescopes like the JWST and the NGRST, it still plays an important role. It is, after all, still a powerful space telescope. It's as if the venerable space telescope is passing the baton to a new generation, even though it's still part of the team.

But while the Roman's mission is just about to begin, the Hubble Space Telescope is approaching its finish line. The Hubble is gradually losing its stabilizing gyroscopes, and with no repair mission planned, the telescope will probably die with its last gyroscope.

Nobody can say for sure when that will happen, but up until then, it still has important work to do by supporting its more modern counterparts.

#671. An extra year of school may lower dementia risk, even when genes raise it

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

A decades-old school reform offers new evidence that additional education may reduce dementia risk, even among people with a higher genetic risk.

In 1972, Britain changed how long teenagers had to stay in school.

More than 50 years later, researchers found a possible benefit that reached well beyond the classroom: fewer dementia diagnoses among people covered by the rule.

The biggest drop appeared among people whose genes put them at higher risk.

The finding suggests that education could help change someone’s chances of developing dementia, even when they inherit a greater risk.

Silvia Barcellos, a health economist at the University of Wisconsin–Madison’s La Follette School of Public Affairs, led the study.

She is also affiliated with the University of Southern California’s Center for Economic and Social Research, where three of her four coauthors work.

Researchers often list low education as a major risk factor for dementia. People who spend more years in school tend to have better memory and thinking skills later in life, and fewer of them develop dementia.

The catch is that those same people often differ in other ways.

They may have grown up with more money or carry different genes, and even a trait like self-control could shape both how long someone studies and how they live afterward.

“We have known for a long time that people with more education are less likely to develop dementia, but it has been difficult to know whether education itself is responsible,” Barcellos said.

With dementia cases worldwide projected to reach 131.5 million by 2050, knowing whether extra schooling actually lowers risk matters for dementia prevention.

The 1972 law raised the minimum school-leaving age from 15 to 16 in England, Scotland and Wales. It applied to students born on or after September 1, 1957.

Someone born on August 31 could leave at 15, while someone born a day later had to stay another year.

Comparing people born close to that birthday helped the researchers separate the effect of schooling from other differences in their lives.

Most teenagers already stayed until 16. Still, the first group covered by the law was 14 percentage points more likely to do so than students born just before the cutoff.

Across the group covered by the law, schooling increased by a little over two months on average. That average includes people who would have stayed anyway.

The team analyzed up to 107,200 people born within four years of the cutoff.

They came from UK Biobank, a study of 502,363 volunteers who enrolled between 2006 and 2010, aged 40 to 69.

The researchers checked whether people on either side of the birthday cutoff were comparable. Their parents were equally likely to have had dementia, and the participants had similar genetic risks.

Dementia diagnoses came from hospital records, death records and participants’ own medical histories.

Hospital and death records allow researchers to follow people who stop answering surveys, something people developing dementia are more likely to do.

An earlier check found that at least 84.5% of the recorded diagnoses were correct. The diagnoses also showed links with blood proteins associated with dementia.

Among people born before the cutoff, about five in every 1,000 had a dementia diagnosis by December 2022. Those born just afterward had roughly two fewer diagnoses per 1,000.

That sounds like a small difference, but dementia was still uncommon at these ages.

Because the law added only a few months of schooling on average, the researchers also estimated the effect of a full extra year.

Their preferred estimate was a drop of about 0.88 percentage points per year of education.

The researchers said this was too large to be realistic, because it exceeded the group’s dementia rate to begin with.

The team cautioned that the estimate was imprecise because relatively few participants had developed dementia.

The findings suggest a protective effect, but its exact size remains uncertain.

The researchers gave each participant a genetic risk score combining many small DNA differences linked to Alzheimer’s disease with the APOE gene.

People who inherit two copies of the APOE4 variant face a substantially higher risk of developing Alzheimer’s disease.

In the higher-risk half, 58% carried at least one APOE4 copy, compared with 0.02% in the lower-risk half. The law could not change anyone’s genes, and it increased schooling by the same amount in both groups.

The gap in dementia diagnoses between the two groups fell from 0.18 percentage points before the cutoff to 0.02 points afterward.

The decline was clear only in the higher-risk group, but there were too few cases to rule out an equal benefit in both groups.

Study co-author Leandro Carvalho is an associate professor of economics at USC.

“For people who know they have a higher genetic risk of dementia, this is a hopeful finding,” said Carvalho.

“Genes help shape disease risk, but they do not necessarily determine the outcome.”

The study could not establish exactly how additional schooling might lower dementia risk.

The authors suggested that it might lead to more mentally stimulating jobs, higher incomes that make healthy living easier, or spending time with healthier peers.

People covered by the law were 2.3 percentage points more likely to have a household income of at least £31,000.

They were also 0.64 percentage points less likely to have had a stroke or heart attack, with that drop concentrated in the higher genetic risk group.

However, they were more likely to have the mildest stage of high blood pressure, a finding members of the team had reported previously.

A Swedish study of 1.3 million people found that a similar school reform had no effect on dementia and little effect on income. The British reform raised incomes, which the authors suggested might help explain its different results.

The oldest people covered by the law were only 65 at the end of 2022, before the ages when dementia becomes most common.

UK Biobank volunteers tend to be healthier and better off than the wider population, and the genetic analysis included only people of European ancestry.

The findings also concern people who would otherwise have left school early, so they may not apply to other levels of education or other places.

The difference in diagnoses grew clearer as the researchers added each of the last four years of health records.

“Understanding which interventions can produce these protective effects is an important direction for future research,” Carvalho said.

Following these participants for longer will help researchers see whether the difference holds as they reach ages when dementia becomes more common.

The full study was published in the journal Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

Admits a 'small number' of iPhone Duos have display problems, and no, you're not folding it wrong.

Apple has confirmed problems with the folding screen on its flagship iPhone Duo after reports of misbehaving displays spread among its customer base.

A company spokesperson told The Register, "We are aware of an issue impacting the inner display on a small number of iPhone Duo units."

"If users experience issues with their iPhone Duo display, they should contact AppleCare for a replacement."

That's the least the company can do. A borked screen on a brand-new device is something any customer would expect the warranty to cover.

The iPhone Duo went on sale last week and sold out almost immediately as loyalists rushed to hand over cash for Apple's foldable device. UK shoppers can expect to pay from £1,999 to £3,199 for the Duo, depending on configuration.

Complaints soon surfaced on social media, with a small number of customers reporting horizontal lines on their screens, and a crunching noise from the hinge. Some reports were undoubtedly the work of AI images, though iFixit speculated in a weekend teardown that dust ingress could be interfering with the complex hinge mechanism and stressing the screen.

Apple is far from the only foldable maker to stumble over production creases in its foldable phone hardware. Samsung's Galaxy Fold was delayed in 2019 after reports of dust creeping under the screen and borked displays, prompting swift design tweaks.

Francisco Jeronimo, veep of Worldwide Devices at IDC, said: "The early reports of inner display failures on the iPhone Duo show that even a company with Apple's engineering resources and supply chain discipline faces a steep learning curve in foldables."

Jermonimo noted Apple had not yet shared any figures or the scale of the problems. "Some of the images circulating online are recycled or altered, so caution is needed before drawing conclusions about the whole production run."

However, Samsung's "fast and transparent response" limited damage to the category, he added, and how Apple, "handles this first-generation issue will shape buyer confidence in the category well beyond its own sales."

Apple's statement to The Register indicates taking the problem seriously, and it will be eager to avoid a repeat of the iPhone 4, which was marred by the infamous "you're holding it wrong" furor.

#1704. What McDonald’s dropping its FIFA sponsorship means for sports marketing

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Let Marketing Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The fast-food giant’s marketing model is evolving, which could have downstream implications for QSR competitors’ marketing efforts.

McDonald’s will no longer be an official sponsor of the FIFA World Cup, following a partnership that spanned more than three decades, the chain announced late last week. The company described the move as an evolution of its marketing model and how it invests in global partnerships, potentially disrupting the QSR sports sponsorship space.

“Looking ahead, sport and culture will remain important ways we connect with our fans as we sharpen our focus on creating distinctive McDonald’s experiences that deepen those connections year-round,” Morgan Flatley, McDonald’s global chief marketing officer and head of new business ventures, said in a statement.

McDonald’s began its official sponsorship of the global soccer tournament in 1994. The chain this year rolled out its largest-ever World Cup effort, reaching more than 100 markets with a campaign that included soccer stars collaborations, collectible cups and experiential elements.

The campaign provided a lift to McDonald's Net Promoter Score and its social media buzz among young consumers, per Kantar data shared with Marketing Dive. McDonald’s saw 0.8% same-store sales growth in Q2 2026. A drop in traffic during the period was a drag on performance, with the company attributing one-third of the traffic loss to its World Cup campaign.

“While the campaign provided a lift to the business and generated excellent system excitement, the campaign underperformed versus our expectations,” CFO Ian Borden said on an earnings call.

The end of McDonald’s FIFA partnership could speak to broader changes in the sports marketing and media landscapes than simply the underperformance of this year’s campaign. While sponsoring a league like FIFA represents one of the few opportunities for marketers to reach billions of consumers around the globe, imperatives around dynamically deploying content across platforms have become more costly.

“The rights holders are providing the platform, but in a lot of cases, the onus is on the brands to activate against that effectively,” said Bob Lynch, founder and CEO of SponsorUnited. “You've got to spend another probably $70 to $100 million dollars, just to bring those things to life outside of the spend with FIFA… do they feel like they're set up to do that most effectively in that environment?”

While it will no longer sponsor the World Cup, the chain continues to invest in soccer and sports more broadly, including its sponsorship of Ligue 1 in France and a tie-up with MLS team Chicago Fire FC that will give the chain its first naming rights partnership for a major pro sports stadium in the U.S. In addition, McDonald’s invests in its long-running All-American Games program and around Formula 1 in Latin America.

McDonald’s has hundreds of partnerships with individual teams and athletes and is the second-largest spender in the QSR category within North American team sports behind Chick-fil-A, per SponsorUnited data shared with Marketing Dive. The post-FIFA focus on smaller partnerships could allow the chain to be more nimble with its investments after it committed nearly $80 million towards its World Cup partnership for the 2026 cycle alone.

“They may be able to A/B test at scale different programs and things that they can do, but it's also going to require a tremendous amount of work because you're going to be doing it in a distributed fashion,” the executive said. “It probably creates a little bit more chaos if you're sitting on the marketing teams.”

McDonald’s is actively exploring other opportunities, according to the company. The chain is operating a new marketing model that focuses on creating fan-first experiences, leaning into its own brand and IP, securing year-round engagement and spanning both local relevance and global scale. The marketing model is part of a strategic plan that McDonald’s unveiled in June and detailed during a September investor day.

While McDonald’s FIFA exit leaves a gap in the organization’s sponsorship roster, the outcome will likely be more complicated than seeing one of its QSR competitors slide into the role, due to the large investment that could entail — even if such a brand was able to negotiate from a position of strength. The move will likely have other downstream implications in QSR marketing for competitors in the space, now that McDonald’s potentially can spend marketing dollars previously earmarked for FIFA elsewhere.

“Maybe [there’s] a space where other QSR companies have been in, now all of a sudden [McDonald’s is] able to deploy capital into those environments as well,” Lynch said. “I do think it creates some disruption in the category.”

#681. Eggplants have been secretly recruiting bacteria for thousands of years

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Scientists have discovered how eggplant genes influence the bacteria living around their roots, revealing a hidden connection between evolution and agriculture.

While farmers were selecting eggplants for bigger fruit and better flavor, the plants were doing something else entirely.

Their genes were helping determine which bacteria thrived around their roots. This process was not entirely random.

Specific genetic variations were linked to particular bacteria, and some of those variations appear to have been shaped by the eggplant’s history of cultivation and geographic expansion.

A new study caught them at it, and the findings are stranger and more useful than anyone expected.

The research comes from Nanjing Agricultural University and the Guangxi Academy of Agricultural Sciences.

The team worked with 432 eggplant varieties from around the world. They sequenced their genomes and cataloged the microbial communities living around their roots.

This is the largest study of its kind in eggplant, and the scale was necessary.

These are not simple one-gene-one-microbe relationships. Most of the plant traits that influence microbial communities are controlled by dozens of genes acting in concert.

That’s exactly why the picture had been so hard to assemble before now.

Between 9% and 39% of the variation in individual microbial taxa comes down to host genetics. That is not a marginal effect.

The microbes most central to community stability – the core of the root microbiome – are under the tightest genetic control of all.

This suggests that eggplant genetics play an important role in determining which bacteria become established around its roots.

The team ran microbial genome-wide association studies and found 1,235 genetic variants linked to 46 core microbial taxa.

What those variants turned out to be is where things get odd.

The gene MYB113, which helps produce the deep purple pigment that makes eggplants recognizable, is associated with Stenotrophomonas.

This bacterial group includes species that produce growth hormones such as auxin, gibberellin, and abscisic acid.

The connection suggests that a gene associated with fruit color may also influence the root microbiome.

The gene BLH9, which governs organ development, is linked to Mycobacterium, which promotes root growth through its own auxin production.

A locus on chromosome 5 is associated with multiple bacteria from the order Rhizobiales. This is a group best known for fixing nitrogen in legumes.

“Plants are not passive recipients of their microbial partners – they actively manage them through their genetics,” the authors wrote.

“We found that eggplant has been shaping its microbiome not by accident, but as part of its adaptation to new environments.”

The sharpest finding in the paper is also the most conceptually satisfying.

The loci most strongly associated with microbiome composition sit in regions of the eggplant genome that show clear signs of directional selection.

These regions changed as eggplant moved from Southeast Asia into Europe and China over centuries of trade and cultivation.

Different geographic populations carry different combinations of alleles at these loci. Those combinations shape which bacteria show up at the roots.

So the spread of eggplant across the world was not just a story of farmers picking fruit they liked. It was also, invisibly, a story of plants assembling new microbial communities to match new soils and climates.

The agricultural history and the microbial history were running in parallel the whole time, and nobody knew.

“The same genes that helped eggplant spread across the globe also helped it recruit the right microbial allies along the way,” the authors explained.

“Plant-microbiome partnerships are not just ecological accidents – they are co-evolutionary outcomes.”

Crops that recruit beneficial bacteria could eventually require less fertilizer or fewer chemical treatments. But researchers still need to demonstrate those benefits under real farming conditions.

These are not small prizes. They sit at the center of every serious conversation about reducing agriculture’s chemical footprint without sacrificing yield.

Microbiome-assisted breeding would mean selecting varieties not only for what the plant does but for which microbial communities it assembles around itself.

The findings may also help researchers understand similar genetic relationships in other crops.

Gene editing, metabolomics, and field trials could help determine whether these discoveries translate into practical agricultural benefits.

But all of that requires knowing where in the genome to look. That, more than anything, is what this study provides.

The study is published in the journal Horticulture Research.

#382. American’s Citibank Business Card Annual Fee Goes To $150—But Two People Can Earn Elite Status From One Card’s Spending

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

American Airlines and Citi are raising the annual fee on the Citi / AAdvantage Business card from $99 to $150, adding travel insurance, an inflight credit and more mileage earning. And it’s making its most valuable feature, which has been officially targeted, official: letting the same purchases earn credit towards elite status for two people. For instance, $125,000 in eligible spending on an employee card can produce 125,000 Loyalty Points for the employee and another 125,000 Loyalty Points for the primary cardmember. That’s enough for both to earn Platinum Pro status. On an American Airlines personal card, only one person would earn this status. It’s leverage no other airline’s card offers. This is the business card’s killer app. Honestly I should be doing this for my wife – I have the card myself, need to get an employee card in her name, and put significant spend on her card so that we both earn status. It would be helpful for us to both be oneworld emeralds. For instance, if we’re flying business class back from Sydney we’d be able to bring our family into the Qantas first class lounge (since we’d each get a guest) rather than being relegated to the business lounge (since I only get one guest and we’re four people). One Purchase Can Earn Status For Two People Frontier Airlines was the first carrier to offer ‘one dollar spend equals one qualifying point’. American Airlines leveraged the idea with a better loyalty program. They still offer a better card spend proposition for elite status than Delta or United. American’s business card earns redeemabe miles that go into the company’s AAdvantage Business account, which are also transferable to individual AAdvantage accounts and you can choose where they go later. That’s amazing flexibility. Meanwhile, loyalty Points go to the individual cardmember making the purchases. The additional benefit gives the primary cardmember one Loyalty Point for each dollar of eligible employee card spending, too. American lists this as valid through December 31, 2028 but that doesn’t mean it won’t be extended. Those customers now gain access. We completed the migration of former Barclays business cardmembers into the Citi AAdvantage Business program this weekend, making them eligible for the Loyalty Point match promotion. As a result, all Citi / AAdvantage Business cardmembers are now included. The Executive Card Makes This Even More Valuable The recently-refreshed Citi Executive card now awards 10,000 additional Loyalty Points at each of four thresholds: 50,000, 90,000, 165,000 and 240,000 Loyalty Points in a qualifying year. Those thresholds are triggered by Loyalty Points earned across your AAdvantage account. They don’t require spending a single dollar on the Executive card. I’m a primary Citi Executive card holder. If I get one for my wife, then $105,000 on her business employee card would produce 105,000 Loyalty Points for each of us plus 20,000 in Executive card bonuses apiece. $105,000 spend becomes enough for two Platinum Pro and oneworld emerald statuses (without loyalty points from any other source, including Citi Globe’s ‘flight streak’ bonus). New Benefits For The $150 Annual Fee Existing cardmembers receive new benefits starting January 24, 2027 when card renewals begin to charge the higher $150 annual fee, up from $99. New accounts still have no annual fee for the first 12 months. There’s moderately-improved points-earning. 3 miles per dollar on eligible bookings through AAdvantage Hotels and AAdvantage Cars. 2 miles per dollar in one chosen category: U.S. shipping, office supply stores, or U.S. advertising. The existing 2 miles per dollar on eligible American Airlines purchases, gas stations, car rentals, telecommunications, cable and satellite providers remain. While the consumer card gets 2 points per dollar on EV charging, the business card doesn’t. And over $400 in inflight spending meant the old discount was more valuable. Most people don’t spend $400 a year inflight. American doesn’t board enough food for sale in coach for that. Lost or damaged luggage: Up to $5,000 per covered trip, secondary to airline or other applicable coverage. The full transportation cost must be charged to the card or paid using a combination of the card and AAdvantage miles. Rental car coverage: Pay the entire rental cost with the card or a combination of the card and AAdvantage miles, and decline the rental company’s collision/damage waiver. The coverage includes collision or physical damage and secondary personal effects insurance. An employee flying alone doesn’t independently get the primary cardmember’s bag and boarding benefits just by holding an employee card. The Status Benefit Gives Citi A Better Shot At Winning Spending American’s new Citi relationship is better understood as a joint venture than a traditional arrangement to sell miles. Bilt Cash is becoming more flexible, but you'll need to take action to continue earning 4% back.

Bilt just announced several changes that are largely consumer-friendly, most of which are coming in 2027.

The biggest update is to Bilt Cash, the secondary rewards currency introduced during the Bilt 2.0 rollout. In a win for cardholders, Bilt Cash will now expire 12 months after it’s earned, rather than at the end of the calendar year, according to the program's announcement on Oct. 27, 2026. The catch: Bilt is retiring the ability to earn 4% back in Bilt Cash on eligible non-housing purchases unless you opt out of the change before your card account anniversary.

At the same time, Bilt announced several other changes to the Bilt Rewards program, including new benefits when you hold Bilt status, a change to the hotel credits that come on select Bilt credit cards and a new “Daily Rewards” program when you pay for housing or transact through Bilt’s partners.

Bilt Cash is a separate rewards currency from Bilt Points. It was introduced in January 2026 during the fairly chaotic Bilt 2.0 launch. It isn’t traditional cash back; expiration dates, redemption caps and merchant-specific rules make Bilt Cash harder to use. You can spend it on eligible purchases through the Bilt app or on bilt.com.

Until now, cardholders could choose one of two options to earn rewards:

Option 1: You don’t earn Bilt Cash on credit card spending. Instead, the more non-housing-related spending you put on the card, the higher your “points multiplier” is for earning Bilt Points on housing costs (up to 1.25 points per $1). For example, if you spent $2,000 on rent and spent an extra $2,000 on the card, you'd earn 1.25x points on that rent payment.

Option 2: You earn 4% back on eligible non-housing spending in the form of Bilt Cash. You can use Bilt Cash to unlock points earnings on housing payments, among other options.

Now, less than a year after it was introduced, Bilt is removing Option 2 unless you opt out of the change before your account anniversary. New cardholders and those who don’t opt out won’t have the option to earn Bilt Cash through everyday credit card spending. Additionally, cardholders who choose to opt out will no longer have the ability to switch between the two earning structures.

But that doesn’t mean Bilt Cash is going away. You'll still earn $50 of Bilt Cash for every 25,000 Bilt Points you earn. And the currency is becoming easier to use.

So should you opt out? If you're willing and able to handle the added complexity that comes with balancing two rewards currencies, I'd say opting out of the change and continuing to earn 4% back in Bilt Cash is the more valuable option for most people. But if you want a simpler rewards option, I'd go with the new program. If you want help deciding, see our guide to the Bilt cards.

Under the current system, all but $100 of Bilt Cash will expire at the end of the year. But now, all Bilt Cash will expire 12 months after you earn it, at the end of that last month. For example, Bilt Cash earned in November 2026 will expire Nov. 30, 2027, rather than Dec. 31, 2026.

This is a welcome change. I’m a Bilt Palladium Card holder who will opt out and continue to earn Bilt Cash at a 4% rate on eligible non-housing spending with my card, so that means I earn $40 in Bilt Cash for every $1,000 I put on the card. Until now, I was worried about how I’d use the Bilt Cash I earn during the winter holiday season before it expires. But with this change, that year-end expiration is no longer a concern.

When you redeem Bilt Cash, it's used on a first-in, first-out basis, meaning your oldest Bilt Cash is used first. The new expiration policy is already live and you can check your Bilt Cash expiration dates from the wallet tab of the Bilt app.

Effective on your next cardmember anniversary, unless you opt out, annual hotel credits on the Bilt Palladium Card and Bilt Obsidian Card will be issued as Bilt Cash. They'll also be doled out by anniversary year rather than calendar year. This broadens the credit’s use while still letting you redeem Bilt Cash for hotel stays in Bilt’s travel portal. It’s really a change in the credit's form and flexibility, since the overall dollar amount stays the same.

There's a downside, though: Effective Jan. 1, 2027, you'll only be able to redeem Bilt Cash for hotels booked through Bilt twice annually. Currently, you can redeem Bilt Cash for hotels booked through Bilt on a monthly basis. As before, there's a two-night minimum when you use Bilt Cash for these hotel redemptions.

The amount of Bilt Cash you can redeem toward these hotel stays will be based on your Bilt status or card. Blue and Silver members can redeem up to $50 in Bilt Cash per redemption, while Gold and Platinum members can redeem up to $100. Eligible Bilt Palladium Card holders can redeem $200 per redemption.

If you opt out of the changes, you'll retain your existing benefits. If you have the Bilt Palladium Card, you'll still earn semi-annual hotel credits and the ability to stack that credit with $100 of Bilt Cash from Bilt Gold status when you use the credits (up to twice a year).

The positives outweigh the negatives here, in my opinion, unless you redeem Bilt cash for hotel stays more than twice a year. You still have the option to redeem Bilt Cash for more valuable options, like the Point Accelerator, which lets eligible cardholders earn an extra Bilt Point per $1 spent on everyday spending categories on up to $5,000 in spending, up to five times a year. You can then redeem those extra points for hotel stays, avoiding those redemption caps.

I already have hotel credits on a handful of other credit cards, so seeing these Bilt cards maintain the dollar value of these credits while adding additional flexibility is a welcome change.

Coming in 2027, Gold and Platinum members in the Bilt Rewards program can redeem Bilt points for hotel bookings in the Home Away from Home program, a curated collection of more than 2,000 luxury properties. A two-night stay is required to receive VIP perks similar to AmEx Fine Hotels and Resorts or Chase’s The Edit, including:

Early check-in and late check-out (subject to availability).

Eligible travel redemptions through Bilt give your points a value of 1.25 cents each (a $1,000 stay would require 80,000 points). That's 25% better value for your points than the 1 cent each you’ll get when you use Membership Rewards points to book a stay with Fine Hotels and Resorts through AmEx (a $1,000 stay would require 100,000 points).

Beginning Jan. 15, 2027, Bilt is introducing a Daily Rewards program for all qualifying members, not just cardholders. It will offer bonuses for each on-time housing payment and daily qualifying transaction you make with a Bilt neighborhood partner, in addition to the chance to win larger prizes.

According to Bilt, each Daily Reward will reveal two rewards and you choose one to claim. Rewards range from instant prizes like Bilt Points and Bilt Cash to collectibles. For cardholders, this is in addition to the points you earn for housing payments or with neighborhood partners.

While we still need to see the value that Daily Rewards will add, the ability to earn more points and prizes is a good thing.

Bilt has introduced product changes for its credit cards, allowing you to swap an existing Bilt card for a higher- or lower-tier card. Previously, Bilt didn't offer these product changes.

Say you have the Bilt Palladium Card and no longer want to pay the $495 annual fee. You can now downgrade that card to one of Bilt’s lower-cost options, including the Bilt Obsidian Card or Bilt Blue Card, without submitting a new application.

This is important because you can switch to a card that might be more suitable for your spending habits without triggering an additional credit inquiry.

You can upgrade to a card with a higher annual fee through the Bilt app. To downgrade your card to one with a lower annual fee, you’ll need to call the customer service number on the back of your card.

There are a few actionable takeaways for Bilt Rewards members.

Opt out if you want to continue earning 4% Bilt Cash. You will no longer have the ability to earn 4% back in Bilt Cash on eligible non-housing purchases, unless you opt out of the change before your account anniversary. You can do this by navigating to your account's card settings. Personally, I’m planning to opt out so I can continue to earn Bilt Cash. That extra 4% back I earn in Bilt Cash is like a cherry on top of the points I earn with my Bilt Palladium Card. And now that I don't have to worry about most of my Bilt Cash expiring at the end of the calendar year, I value it even more.

Switch to a different card, if you want. Instead of closing your Bilt account, consider changing to a card that better suits your spending habits. That could mean upgrading or downgrading your card with no credit inquiry.

I rarely close a credit card and instead opt to downgrade less-used cards to versions with no annual fee. This allows the account to stay on my credit report, adding to the length of my credit history and preserving my available credit, which keeps my credit utilization low. You might also consider upgrading your card if you think you’ll take advantage of the added benefits.

Next year, apply for your car rental status match. This won’t be available until 2027, but be sure to apply for a status match between Bilt and SIXT or Hertz when you can. Status matches can sometimes take a little bit of time to go through, so be prepared.
