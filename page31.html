At least 14 people have been killed after a Russian jet-powered drone hit a crowded platform at the main railway station in the central Ukrainian city of Dnipro, local officials have said.

Dnipropetrovsk regional head Oleksandr Hanzha said at least 14 others were wounded in the attack, including an 11-year-old boy.

Ukraine's state railway operator, Ukrzaliznytsia, said a passenger train headed for the southern city of Zaporizhzhia was at one of the platforms in Dnipro when the drone hit.

Russia's military has not publicly commented. It has repeatedly hit Ukraine's large railway network, but Moscow denies deliberately targeting civilians.

In Dnipro, an elevated threat level was announced ahead of Thursday's strike, according to the railway operator chief, Oleksandr Pertsovsky.

"But unfortunately there were still a large number of people on the platform" when the drone struck, he said.

Dnipro Mayor Borys Filatov said the incident was a tragedy, adding that a drone hit an electric civilian train.

Pictures from the Ukrainian rescue services show a large hole in a platform that is strewn with blurred-out bodies, near a blast-damaged train with smashed windows.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said "the Russians used a drone to attack a platform where a regular commuter train and a regular passenger train were standing."

He called for further US, European and G7 sanctions on Russia and repeated his criticism of a controversial deal that was struck earlier this month between Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin, which eased some sanctions on Moscow, allowing the US to import Russian diesel.

Elsewhere in Ukraine, two girls aged 15 were killed in a Russian missile attack on the centre of Chernihiv city, in the north of the country, regional head Vyacheslav Chaus said. At least 25 others, including four children, were injured, he added.

A day of mourning has been declared for Chernihiv and Dnipro on Friday.

In the city of Kremenchuk, in the central Poltava region, three people were killed and 28 injured in a Russian drone attack that hit residential buildings and a school, regional head Vitaliy Diakivnych said.

At least 25 people were injured in the Russian strike on Kremenchuk, local officials said

The mayor of Moscow said 613 Ukrainian drones were launched overnight towards the region, most of which were shot down during their flight.

Russia's defence ministry has always denied targeting Ukrainian civilians and says its attacks on railways target the infrastructure used to transport military personnel and equipment.

However, the dangers to passengers are very real, and attacks on Ukraine's train infrastructure have risen sharply.

Of the 521 locomotives hit since the start of Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022, almost 60% have been hit this year, according to statistics released by Ukrzaliznytsia supplied to AFP news agency.

Overall attacks on railway facilities have risen from 1,206 in 2025 to 1,700 so far this year.

There appears to have been a wider assault on Ukraine's infrastructure in recent weeks, with two bridges attacked in the capital Kyiv, and a metro train hit during one of the strikes.

With nearly 93% of votes counted, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s nationalist-religious bloc held a modest lead in seats.

JERUSALEM – Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu alleged on Oct 29, without providing evidence, that Arab parties’ record gains in an election this week were driven in part by voter fraud, a charge rejected by Arab leaders, who accused him of incitement.

The allegation represents an escalation of anti-Arab rhetoric by Netanyahu and his far-right partners after high Arab turnout in a national ballot on Oct 27 effectively blocked a smooth path to power for either him or his main Jewish rivals.

Israeli election officials said on Oct 29 that there was a probe into fraud allegations, though the election’s integrity was not “systematically compromised”.

Analysts and critics say that by stepping up attacks on Arab parties, Netanyahu may be trying to ensure his main rival, Gadi Eisenkot, feels unable to even consider forming a minority government with Arab help that could oust him from power.

Netanyahu’s goal is “delegitimising any Arab-Jewish cooperation”, said Eytan Gilboa, professor of politics at Reichman University.

Election officials were expected to finish tallying votes later on Oct 29, in a tightly fought election that has produced no clear winner and is likely to be followed by weeks of protracted negotiations over a new government.

With nearly 93% of votes counted, Netanyahu’s nationalist-religious bloc held a modest lead in seats over a bloc of right-wing and centre-left parties led by Eisenkot, with both men’s coalitions short of the 61 seats needed for a majority in Parliament.

Parties representing Israel’s 21% Arab minority were projected to win an unprecedented 19 seats, leaving Eisenkot’s bloc, with 48 seats against Netanyahu’s camp of 53, with no realistic path to power without broad Arab backing, an option Eisenkot has so far ruled out.

However, Eisenkot’s camp could also try to form a minority government with Arab support from the outside, without including them in a governing coalition.

Ahmad Tibi, one of the leaders of the Arab Joint List party, said Netanyahu was on an orchestrated, toxic campaign meant to undermine the election results.

“This entire campaign is directed against Eisenkot, to prevent him, intimidate and scare him off forming a government that relies on external support from the Joint List or Ra’am,” Tibi told Reuters.

During and after the Oct 27 election, Netanyahu and his allies warned against rising Arab turnout, and his right-wing Likud party issued statements accusing the Arab parties of “stealing the vote”.

It demanded police and the Shin Bet domestic intelligence service investigate incidents of suspected election bribery and intimidation at ballot stations in Arab towns. It said it possessed evidence of such activity but has not presented it to the public.

The police say they have arrested at least seven people since Tuesday in Israel’s south, where many Arabs live, on suspicion of voter fraud. Israel’s public broadcaster Kan said they were suspected of attempting to bribe voters to cast ballots for the Arab-dominated party Ra’am, which has Islamist roots.

On Oct 29, the head of Israel’s Central Election Committee, Supreme Court jurist Noam Sohlberg, said he had asked the Shin Bet to investigate suspicions that unidentified foreign players had tried to intervene in the election.

Likud seized on Sohlberg’s request, alleging an attack on Israel’s democracy, “an Islamisation of Israel and the toppling of the right-wing in order to bring about IDF withdrawal and defeat on all fronts, foremost Iran”.

Likud said Qatar was behind the alleged election intervention, which Qatar later denied.

Ra’am head Mansour Abbas said on Oct 29 that his party was not involved in the reported suspicious conduct and urged a full investigation. He said that Netanyahu was on an incitement campaign against Israel’s Arab citizens.

“We have one message in response to the campaign of incitement and delegitimisation he is waging against Arab citizens: Nothing will come of it, because there is nothing there,” Abbas said in a statement.

In a letter sent to a parliamentarian and published by the Central Election Committee, the committee said that there was “no base for claims that there was systematic harm to the election’s integrity”.

After vote tallying is completed, Israeli President Isaac Herzog will consult with party chairs and choose a candidate who he thinks has the best chance to form a government.

The coalition that last toppled Netanyahu, in 2021, relied on Abbas’ Ra’am party. But Eisenkot’s underperformance means the opposition would now need backing from the Arab-dominated Joint List, which includes an explicitly anti-Zionist party. Most Jewish leaders have shunned formal alliances with Arab parties since Israel’s founding.

Eisenkot called for an investigation that would protect election integrity but said Netanyahu was exploiting the suspicions to “spread chaos”.

He said in a statement that Netanyahu’s aim was to “challenge the official election results, in which the Israeli public voted against the government”.

Herzog said the allegations must be investigated.

“But even at a moment like this, we must not tarnish an entire community that sought to exercise its right to vote and take an active part in democracy,” Herzog said in a statement. REUTERS

#507. Iranian parents enroll children in foreign online schools, defying ministry warnings

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Some Iranian families are paying thousands of dollars to put their children in foreign online schools, buying an international education from home and stepping outside the Islamic Republic’s school system, despite the education ministry’s warnings.

The government-affiliated newspaper Iran reported on Thursday that institutions offering foreign curricula and diplomas from countries including Canada and Britain were attracting Iranian students.

A mother whose 15-year-old daughter attends an online high school based in Toronto told the newspaper her family pays $7,000 annually to prepare her daughter for admission to Canadian universities without leaving Iran.

Another father cited shortcomings in Iranian schools’ approach to practical skills and creative thinking as reasons for enrolling his son in a foreign online college alongside his education in Tehran.

Mohammad Salimi, head of the Education Ministry’s Center for International Affairs and Overseas Schools, warned that the foreign online institutions in question lack authorization to operate in Iran and that their diplomas are not recognized by the domestic education system.

Students attending only these institutions are not registered in Iran’s official education databases and could face difficulties continuing their studies domestically, Salimi told Iran daily.

For families planning to send their children to universities abroad, however, recognition by institutions in the destination country may matter more than approval from Iranian authorities.

A diploma’s lack of recognition in Iran does not necessarily make it invalid internationally. Its acceptance depends on the school’s accreditation and the admission requirements of individual universities.

The expansion of international online schools allows students to follow American and British curricula without relocating.

International Virtual Learning Academy (IVLA), a private American school, provides online education from elementary through high school, allowing students to select courses and progress at a pace suited to their circumstances.

Annual high school tuition starts at $2,679 for its basic program, rising to $3,799 or $5,279 for packages offering additional educational support.

Britain’s King's InterHigh offers live and recorded online classes, with subjects including computer science, economics, psychology and film studies.

For the 2026–2027 academic year, annual tuition is £7,250 for students aged 14 to 16 and £7,585 for the pre-university program covering three principal subjects.

Crimson Global Academy also provides American and British curricula online. Annual full-time tuition for its American high school program ranges from $22,375 to $25,500.

The differences show that foreign online schooling does not carry a uniform price. Costs vary according to grade level, course selection and the educational services provided.

Ideological education and alternative choices

Some families are turning to foreign education in a country whose school system was restructured around the Islamic Republic’s religious and political objectives after the 1979 revolution.

UNESCO has documented how Iranian textbooks were revised after the revolution to incorporate Islamic teachings, with religious and political content extending beyond religious education into subjects including history, social studies and Persian literature.

Iran’s Fundamental Reform Document of Education also identifies the cultivation of Islamic-Iranian identity and religious values among the education system’s central objectives.

In a study published in July 2025, education researcher Golnar Mehran examined Iranian social studies textbooks and the role of Islamization and politicization in their content.

By contrast, the international online schools examined offer greater flexibility in course selection, different academic pathways and preparation for universities abroad.

The YouTube generation and English-language learning

The spread of the internet and social media has also exposed Iranian students to English-language material and international educational resources from an early age.

YouTube, online gaming and English-language social media content have expanded opportunities to learn beyond formal classrooms, despite extensive internet filtering in Iran.

Reuters identified more than 5,000 agencies whose names begin with MOD, referring to Iran's Ministry of Defense, and which share the Tehran address of the Ministry of Defense and Armed Forces Logistics.

Although their registration records list February 11, 1979, as their founding date, most were established in 2013, when Iran was facing severe economic sanctions, the investigation found.

Reuters and Israeli-American risk consultancy 240 Analytics traced the agencies through Iranian banking records, corporate filings and shipping documents, including around 19,000 import records.

Some entities were linked to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) and large Iranian foundations known as bonyads.

The agencies use intermediaries and overseas companies to purchase goods with proceeds from oil sales that bypass sanctions, according to the investigation.

In 97% of the shipments reviewed, transactions were recorded as settled "without currency transfer," allowing purchases to be made using funds held outside Iran.

Banking records also showed that 36 agencies held about $1.2 billion in foreign currency deposits at the end of 2024, while the wider network held around $1.8 billion in local currency, Reuters reported.

The news agency said it found no evidence that the numbered agencies identified in the documents had been individually sanctioned by Western governments.

The investigation found that Iran continued importing materials that could be used in missiles and drones after the war began on February 28.

In the first six months of 2026, deliveries included detonators, explosives, aircraft engine components and rocket fuel ingredients.

The same agency imported thousands of Toyota Hilux and Land Cruiser vehicles, as well as Toyota Tundra trucks, models that Iranian forces have used for military purposes.

The investigation also traced imports of chemicals to the port of Bandar Abbas, where an explosion in April 2025 killed at least 70 people and injured more than 1,200.

Reuters said it could not determine whether chemicals in one of the shipments it identified were responsible for the explosion.

It also could not establish that all imported goods were intended for weapons production, noting that many materials and machines have both civilian and military uses.

The materials entered Iran through several routes, including cargo vessels crossing the Caspian Sea from Russia, trucks traveling from Central Asia and freight aircraft arriving from China, Reuters reported.

Monthly imports by the identified military agency fell by 47% to $54 million in March, shortly after the war began, but recovered to $77 million by May.

Reuters cautioned that some customs records identified shipments only through product codes and declared countries of origin, which it could not always independently verify.

"We firmly oppose attempts to maliciously link regional hostilities to China or smear China's reputation," the ministry said.

Italian truck manufacturer Iveco said it had safeguards to prevent its vehicles from reaching prohibited users and destinations but could not control products resold by third parties.

Toyota said it did not conduct business in Iran and had no knowledge of the purchases described in the investigation.
