Government workforce has shrunk to smallest number of employees since 2013, with Black workers disproportionately impacted by job cuts, according to a new analysis

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

The U.S. government has lost nearly one in five of its employees since President Donald Trump returned to power in January 2025, with Black workers more likely than other groups to have been impacted, according to a new analysis.

Trump took office with Elon Musk at his side and swiftly tasked the world’s richest man with cutting wasteful expenditure from the federal bureaucracy through DOGE.

A Washington Post study of Federal Workforce Data collected by the Office of Personnel Management now reveals that 426,000 full-time employees have left their posts over the last 18 months, accounting for roughly one-fifth of the 2.2 million people Trump inherited from Joe Biden’s administration.

By contrast, the proportion of workers leaving the government during Biden’s single term between 2021 and 2025 was closer to one in seven and it was around one in eight in Trump’s first term from 2017 to 2021.

The Post notes that only 32,000 of the total were directly fired while 85 percent resigned voluntarily or retired. There was a sharp spike in departures in September 2025 when the administration’s incentive programs encouraging early exits came into play, at which point almost 121,000 people left federal service.

When subsequent new hires and reinstatements are taken into account (the latter sometimes forced on a judge’s orders), the 426,000 figure falls to 255,000, taking the total size of the workforce to 1.94 million – a decline of 12 percent – which is roughly where it was at the end of Barack Obama’s first term in January 2013.

Asked about the findings by The Independent, OPM Director Scott Kupor denied that Trump had carried out mass firings and said: “The vast majority of individuals who left government since the beginning of the administration did so voluntarily – through early retirement or the deferred resignation program, which provided eight months of severance to participating employees.”

Of the disproportionate impact of job cuts on Black employees, the Post notes that, as of September 2024, the demographic accounted for 428,000 employees or 19 percent of the federal workforce, giving the group an outsized representation given that it makes up just 12 percent of the total U.S. population.

It adds that while the Trump administration has broken with recent precedent by ceasing to publish demographic information on government employees, it was able to use data collected by the Census Bureau for the Bureau of Labor Statistics to identify a 30 percent decline in Black employment between November and December 2024 and the same period a year later.

DOGE placed a particular emphasis on removing “DEI” employees – meaning people supposedly hired in the interests of promoting diversity, rather than on merit – and a planning guide cited singled out 225 offices for closure and described how their staff might by removed via firings, administrative leave or reassignment.

The Post identified 200 workers to whom the document’s instructions applied and found that 52 percent no longer work in government and that more than half of those targeted were Black, whereas a third were White and Asian and Hispanic people accounted for 13 percent and 8 percent respectively.

It also looked at a website called “DEI Watchlist” created by the American Accountability Foundation that named 93 specific federal employees to be removed from their posts, finding that 50 still worked for the government, 15 had since found jobs elsewhere and the remaining 28 were unemployed. Twenty-three of the last category were Black.

A White House official told The Independent: “The government complies with all Equal Employment Opportunity Commission rules regarding employment discrimination, including those governing disparate impact alone and discriminatory purpose.”

DOGE only operated from January 20 until July 4 last year but claimed to have saved taxpayers $215 billion, or $1,335.40 per person. Those figures have since been hotly disputed and are well short of the $2 trillion in “waste, fraud and abuse” Musk promised to eradicate.

#945. Odessa rejects ban on Russian-language content

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

MOSCOW, October 29. /TASS/. The Odessa City Council in southern Ukraine has voted against a moratorium on the public use of Russian-language cultural content, the Strana news outlet reported.

Authorities in several Ukrainian cities, particularly in the west of the country, have already introduced similar bans. However, according to the news outlet, only 18 of the 64 members of the Odessa City Council voted in favor of the proposal. Two abstained, another 21 did not take part in the vote, and the remaining members were absent.

Since 2014, Ukraine has pursued a policy of openly pushing out the Russian language. In 2019, the country adopted a law "On ensuring the functioning of Ukrainian as the state language," which significantly restricted the use of Russian and minority languages in the country. Local authorities have imposed blanket bans on Russian-language works of art, books, films, theater productions and songs. They have also banned the study of Russian in schools and universities and required schoolchildren to speak only Ukrainian during breaks. At the same time, Ukrainians continue to use Russian widely in everyday life, which regularly becomes a source of conflict.

An antiterrorism court in Pakistan has sentenced prominent human rights lawyers Imaan Mazari and her husband, Hadi Ali Chattha, ⁠⁠to 10 years in prison on terrorism-related charges.

Judge Tahir Abbas Sipra announced the verdict on Thursday, following their trial inside Adiala prison in the garrison city of Rawalpindi.

The couple were found guilty of putting “the security of [the England and Wales Cricket] team at risk”, the judge wrote, adding that it “clearly shows the intentions and objective of the accused to create a sense of terror”, according to a copy of the court judgement obtained by the AFP news agency.

At the same time, the ruling conceded that the security breach was “a very common nature ordinary type of incident apparently of removal of a barrier”.

The case relates to an October 2024 incident in which the couple removed road barriers in the capital, Islamabad, along a route designated for the touring England cricket team.

The couple were indicted on October 7, accused of obstructing police and inciting public interest, and pleaded not guilty.

In statements seen by the Reuters news agency, the couple denied the charges, while calling themselves “prisoners of conscience”, adding that ⁠⁠they were being targeted for their human rights work.

In their defence document, the couple had earlier said they were “being made an example to further cement the chilling effect against those who stand (or want to stand) against tyranny, oppression and injustice”.

Shireen Mazari, who is Imaan Mazari’s mother and served as Pakistan’s human rights minister under incarcerated former Prime Minister Imran Khan, said it was a “sham trial without any due process”.

Mazari told the Reuters news agency the couple “were not permitted to defend themselves through their own lawyer, and were not provided with key legal ⁠⁠documents”, adding that the defence would file a petition against the verdict in the Supreme Court.

Amnesty International called the convictions “an indictment of Pakistan’s justice system.”

“It was a test of the judiciary’s ability to uphold the principles of justice and the right to fair trial. It has failed that test miserably,” said Amnesty’s Yasmine Ahmend.

“It is clear that these ‘anti-terrorism’ charges are being used to punish Imaan and Hadi for their human rights advocacy,” she added.

Mazari and Chattha have been in custody since January 23, in a separate case over their social media posts.

A lower court sentenced ‌‌them each to 17 years the next day under Pakistan’s Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, which critics have said is designed to quash antigovernment dissent online.

Although the Supreme Court later suspended those sentences, police re-arrested ‌‌the couple the same day in the security breach case.

“Imaan and Hadi’s re-arrest dispels any pretence that the charges against them are anything other than a politically motivated attempt to punish them for their human rights work”, said Babu Ram Pant, Amnesty International’s Deputy Regional Director for South Asia, adding that the case demonstrates once again the authorities’ willingness to weaponise the legal system against critical voices.

Pakistan’s ⁠⁠law minister, Azam Nazeer Tarar, has repeatedly said the country’s judiciary is independent.

#530. The rainfall England needs between now and April to escape drought, according to experts

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Despite rain brought by storms Austen and Boelo, swathes of the UK are still in drought

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

Storm Austen and Storm Boelo swept across the UK in quick succession. Rain lashed windows, river levels rose and the clocks went back. It feels like winter has arrived all at once.

Yet most of England and Wales are still in drought. Parts of Cornwall, Devon, Dorset, Somerset and Sussex are in severe drought, the highest level the Environment Agency can declare.

South-west and south-east England have had their driest April to September in over 100 years.

But how can this be when it is cold, dark and pouring with rain? Drought tightens or loosens its grip over months, not just a few days.

It will be the rainy or dry nature of the months ahead that will decide whether the drought ends, or more worryingly, if it stays into next summer.

Summer droughts grab the headlines and public attention. When we have a dry period over the summer it’s everywhere, with brown lawns, hosepipe bans and cracked reservoir beds.

But winter is when the UK refills its water stores.

Between October and March, with shorter and colder days, less water evaporates and plants drink far less, so rain finally has the chance to refill our reservoirs.

Rain can soak deep into the ground to top up groundwater, the water held in chalk and sandstone rocks beneath our feet, like a giant sponge.

Groundwater supplies much of the drinking water in southern and eastern England, but it fills slowly, over months and years.

Think of it like a bank account. A hot summer is a big splurge with lots of withdrawals. But a dry winter means the deposits needed to repay that spending never arrive.

That’s why a dry winter can do more damage to our water supplies than a heatwave.

This year the account is badly overdrawn. Southern England has just had its driest April to September in more than a century, alongside record summer heat.

The Wimbleball reservoir on Exmoor, which supplies around 400,000 people, was full in March, after an extremely wet winter.

Then the weather shifted from wetter than normal to dryer than normal. By October, water levels at Wimbleball were down to about 13%.

Droughts that carry on into winter are rare in the UK. Usually the autumn rain arrives, soil gets wetter and water stores recover by Christmas. Every instinct suggests that a stormy autumn should fix things.

In the 2010 to 2012 drought, two dry winters in a row left groundwater in south and east England so low that hosepipe bans came in during spring 2012. Then the wettest April on record arrived, and parts of the country swung from drought to flood within weeks.

What stands out now is how quickly droughts are stacking up. This is the third drought in England in five years. The stores barely have time to recover before the next one begins.

England and Wales can be in drought and experience flooding simultaneously. After months of dry weather it takes a lot of rain to wet the ground all the way through. Storms such as Boelo and Austen bring bursts of heavy rain.

Drains are overwhelmed and rivers rise quickly, sometimes spilling out onto their floodplains. But the reservoirs and groundwater that supply our taps respond far more slowly, and need lots of water to fill up.

So the type of winter rains make a big difference.

Floods can hit fast. But the key parts of the water cycle are slow.

Hannah Cloke is the Regius Professor of Meteorology and Climate Science at the University of Reading.

This article is republished from The Conversation under a Creative Commons license. Read the original article.

Rain has to wet the soil all the way through before it can trickle down into the rock, and groundwater can take months to respond. The Environment Agency estimates England needs around 120% of normal rainfall between September and April for every area to come out of drought.

We would need half a year with consistently more rain than we would expect at this time of year to break this drought.

If rain falls short across the whole winter, then England could start next summer in drought, with even less room for manoeuvre.

But climate scientists don’t expect there will be a dry winter every year, either now or in decades ahead as fossil fuel burning continues to heat the planet.

Climate projections suggest UK winters will get wetter on average. But averages can hide the danger.

Summers heated by the burning of fossil fuels dry out the soil and drain reservoirs faster, leaving a bigger hole for winter to fill. Winter rain is increasingly arriving in heavy bursts that are harder to capture and more likely to flood. And natural swings in the weather will still deliver dry winters from time to time.

Put those together and the chance of a drought rolling into winter, or across two winters, goes up. This is a trend that will keep getting worse as long as we keep heating the planet.

Preparing starts with how we think about water. It is precious, not endless. Yet we have built a system that lets a wet winter rush away to the sea, then rations what is left through a dry summer.

As with other parts of the UK’s water system, we are paying the price now for years of chronic underinvestment.

Water companies need to move much faster on fixing leaks, which still waste billions of litres every day. New reservoirs are being planned in England, which will help deliver more storage. But they will take more than a decade to build, cost billions of pounds, and cannot be the whole solution.

There is also a low-tech local answer, that would need regulation and a programme of retrofitting to make a big difference. Rainwater harvesting and water reuse in new and existing homes, sustainable drainage in our towns, and encouraging wetlands and floodplains that hold water like a sponge.

Changes to our landscape and homes can help prevent both problems.

Each of us can help. Take shorter showers, ask your water company about a meter and and fit a water butt to catch rain for the garden. These habits add up.

Autumn storms have started to replenish water reserves, but it’s not the finish line. We also need to get ahead of what is likely to happen in years to come.

#548. Trump’s Cabinet members are violating federal law by joining him on campaign trail, experts allege

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Please refresh the page or navigate to another page on the site to be automatically logged inPlease refresh your browser to be logged in

At least nine of Trump’s 15 cabinet secretaries have been accused of violating the Hatch Act over the past few months, according to a report

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

Ethics experts have accused some of President Donald Trump’s Cabinet of violating federal law by joining him on the campaign trail to rally for Republican candidates ahead of the midterm elections.

At least nine of Trump’s 15 cabinet secretaries have been accused of violating the Hatch Act —a federal law prohibiting federal employees from engaging in certain partisan political activities in their official capacity — over the past few months, according to The New York Times.

The president and vice president are exempt from restrictions under the 87-year-old law, but it applies to all other civil employees of the executive branch.

Some cabinet members have been accused of using their official government titles while taking part in campaign rallies and activities, with nonprofit groups filing complaints with the Office of Special Counsel to investigate.

If the office deems the Hatch Act has been violated, penalties can range from a formal warning, suspension and demotion, to termination of employment and being blacklisted from future federal jobs.

Interior Secretary Doug Burgum, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and FCC Chairman Brendan Carr were named in a formal complaint brought by Democracy Defenders Fund after they joined Trump at a midterms campaign event in Alaska to rally for GOP Senate candidate Dan Sullivan.

Trump introduced Lutnick and Burgum to the stage by using their official government titles.

Their attendance at the Oct. 13 event raised “serious questions about whether they violated federal laws prohibiting federal employees from using their official authority to influence an election,” the nonprofit said in an Oct. 19 news release.

Carr said he was speaking in a personal capacity, but the nonprofit noted that his remarks “focused on FCC business related to Alaska, including committing to work with Senator Sullivan to get every person in Alaska connected to high-speed internet.”

The Office of Special Counsel declined to comment when reached by The Independent.

“It has long been established that senior administration officials are allowed to discuss current events, policies, and the candidates involved with those policies in order to advance the agenda of the administration,” said White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis.

Elsewhere, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy endorsed Senate candidate Mike Rogers in Michigan and Attorney General Todd Blanche landed in hot water at a New York rally in August when he appeared to endorse the GOP candidate for governor.

#569. FACT FOCUS: Misinformation about voting spreads less than a week before Election Day

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Please refresh the page or navigate to another page on the site to be automatically logged inPlease refresh your browser to be logged in

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

False and misleading claims about the voting process are being spread online as voters cast their ballots in the 2026 midterms, with Election Day less than a week away.

The posts often focus on alleged election rigging or voter fraud. However, closer scrutiny of the claim typically reveals a logical explanation.

Holes in California ballots are not nefarious

CLAIM: A punched hole in a Ventura County, California, ballot return envelope is proof of election rigging.

THE FACTS: This is false. The ballot return envelopes have two holes, according to election officials. They help election workers confirm that the ballot has been removed and act as an accessibility feature.

A video spreading online shows the front of a return envelope from Ventura County that has a punched hole on one side. A man explains that if ballots are inserted with a certain side facing the front of the envelope, the bubble for Democratic gubernatorial candidate Xavier Becerra is visible through the hole.

The man warns: “Be very careful how you place your ballot in your California envelope.” A popular X post claimed that “California is rigging elections,” using the video as alleged proof.

Simone Seydoux, spokesperson for the Office of the Ventura County Clerk-Recorder & Registrar of Voters, explained that the envelopes follow a design standard recommended by the California secretary of state. Both holes can be seen from the back of the envelope. They appear on each side of the spot voters must sign, acting as a guide for those who are vision impaired. One of these holes goes through the entire envelope so that election workers can ensure it is empty after the ballot has been removed. Workers also secure stacks of emptied envelopes for storage by inserting zip ties through this hole.

“The holes are NOT positioned in order to see the vote on a particular contest,” Seydoux wrote in an email. “The envelopes are ordered ahead of time and used from one election to another if possible.”

Ventura County's 2026 general election ballot consists of three double-sided ballot cards, according to Seydoux. She added that, “voters are welcome to fold the ballot and place it in the envelope in any order.”

The California secretary of state's office confirmed earlier this month that there is nothing untoward about such holes.

“The small holes on some county ballot return envelopes are an accessibility feature to allow sight-impaired voters to orient themselves to where they are required to sign the envelope,” the office wrote in an Instagram post. “If a voter is concerned their ballot choices might be visible through these holes, they can insert their ballots into the return envelope in various ways where all choices are hidden.”

New York City ballots are not missing candidates, may be more than one page

CLAIM: A New York City polling site gave a voter two ballots, one of which was missing the conservative candidates.

THE FACTS: This claim was originally made in a TikTok video by a user who says in the comments that she voted at the Borough of Manhattan Community College in New York City. A sample ballot for that location shows that it consists of two doubled-sided pages. One side of the second page includes judicial races, in which no conservative candidates are running.

“They’re betting on your stupidity and they’re betting on your ignorance that you are not going to see that they’re giving you a ballot that is missing half the candidates,” the woman in the video says. “That’s actually some real foolery and real trickery right there. So honestly, wake up, wake up, wake up, wake up.”

There are four judicial races on the ballot. Democratic candidates are running in all of them and Working Families Party candidates are running in one. There are no Republican or Conservative Party candidates listed since there are none in the race.

“The only reason there would be no candidate on a party line in a contest on the ballot is because no individual was nominated by that party for that particular office,” Kathleen McGrath, a spokesperson for the New York State Board of Elections, wrote in an email. “Of course, all contests offer voters a space for write-in candidates, so voters can write in another person if they do not like the choices printed on the ballot.”

She added that New York City has had multi-page ballots for many years.

The woman in the video also highlights the fact that she was told she didn’t need to show her ID before voting. In New York, voters provide their Department of Motor Vehicles number or the last four digits of their social security number when registering to vote. They may also use another form of valid photo ID or a government document that shows their name and address, such as a utility bill or a bank statement. Voters will be asked for ID at the polls if their identify cannot be verified before Election Day, according to the state’s registration form.

McGrath explained that voters must only verify their identity at the polls via signature if additional identification, such as a photo ID, has already been verified. Their signature is reviewed and approved by two bipartisan poll workers at the site to ensure it matches what is in their voter record.

“Unfortunately, much mis- and disinformation about elections and voting spreads quickly online,” she wrote. “Worse yet, many social media posts are made solely as efforts to troll or get clicks (with no concern for truth or impact). Sometimes, it is simply the individual not understanding the process — which is run securely, transparently, and always by bipartisan officials in New York State. Boards of elections remain the trusted sources for accurate information.”

Alleged vote flipping is attributed to user error

CLAIM: Touchscreen voting machines in multiple states are flipping votes from Democrats to Republicans.

THE FACTS: Election officials say the touchscreen challenges are almost always the result of user error or an easily fixed screen miscalibration, and say the number of complaints this year isn’t higher than it has been in years past, despite outsized attention on social media. They emphasize that voters can select a candidate as many times as needed, consult a poll worker for help and review selections before submitting their ballot.

“Almost all of the claims of vote flipping are understandable human error,” said David Becker, executive director of the Center for Election Innovation and Research, who noted disinformation purveyors often seize on well-meaning concerns to sow doubt in the voting process. “Is there one of us who hasn’t had a butt dial or mistyped something on our phones? It is not nefarious.”

Voters across the country cast ballots in various ways, including with ballot marking devices that print a paper record or with paper ballots they fill out by hand.

The counties where most of the recent vote-flipping social media reports are concentrated use ExpressVote ballot marking devices from a company called Election Systems & Software. The machines let voters make their selections on a touchscreen. Then, a paper record is typically printed for review before the votes are tabulated.

Katina Granger, senior manager of public relations for the Nebraska-based ES&S, said the company works closely with election officials and provides technical support when needed but has not been made aware of any widespread issue with voting machines this cycle.

She said when a voter is having difficulty, they should ask a poll worker for assistance. If they’ve already printed a ballot that doesn’t reflect their intended selections, “they should notify a poll worker rather than cast it so the jurisdiction can follow its procedures for replacing the ballot.”

Marking ballots is normal procedure in many jurisdictions

CLAIM: Ballots marked by poll workers will be disqualified in the 2026 midterm elections.

THE FACTS: Voting procedures vary by jurisdiction, but it is not unheard of for poll workers to mark ballots as part of the process. There are multiple reasons a poll worker might write on a ballot, according to election officials in multiple states. Experts advise voters to check what the rules are in their district and to avoid making stray marks of their own.

In many North Carolina counties, for example, election workers write “P” for provisional or “A” for absentee in a box in the upper left corner of a ballot so that they can be identified if they are put into a tabulator instead of sealed in an envelope as they are supposed to be. Ballots in Illinois are initialed before they are issued to voters.

“I always like to say, get your facts straight from the source, and the source is your local election official,” said Pamela Smith, president and CEO of Verified Voting, a nonpartisan group focused on election technology. “Not your friends, cousin, uncle, on Facebook.”

Smith encouraged voters to seek out information on the websites or social media accounts of those officials. She also suggested asking a poll worker who marks a ballot why they’ve done so if a voter has concerns.
